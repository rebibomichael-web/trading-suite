**Halftime Report: Brad Gerstner on NVIDIA, the Anthropic IPO and the AI Trade (Mon, Nov 16, 2026)**

*I inferred the date from "yesterday's Sunday shows" and "earnings tomorrow." The prompt gave none, so check it. Mike Santoli hosted the committee segments, and Scott Wapner interviewed Gerstner from Silicon Valley.*

**Overview:** NVIDIA fell to a new intraday low after CEO Jensen Huang was pushed out amid a scandal and export-control concerns. Altimeter's Brad Gerstner urged the board to take its time and said he is putting in a $5B order for the Anthropic IPO. The panel debated how bad NVIDIA's worst case is and previewed Disney and Palo Alto earnings.

**Breaking news and context**
- **Fed Chair Kevin Warsh (via Steve Liesman):** AI may be "the most transformational period in my adult lifetime" and could lift growth well above its long-run average. He laid out three scenarios:
  - gains shared between labor and capital;
  - gains going mostly to capital, meaning higher unemployment but still high growth;
  - AI disappointing, which could mean a slowdown or a notable rise in unemployment.
- **Market:** S&P 500 was down about 1.2% and held under 7,500. Nasdaq was down 1.6%, with semis the main pressure point.
- **Seema Modi:** A source says NVIDIA's board is not considering bringing Huang back and is focused on the investigation.
- **Sam Altman (via Kate Rooney):** Huang "has long been a hero of mine. Heroes have flaws." He has empathy from his own 2023 firing. He said Anthropic going public first is not a worry, and OpenAI plans to go public at some point in 2027. NVIDIA has invested about $30B in OpenAI.

**Brad Gerstner (Altimeter), Halftime Headliner**
- **NVIDIA board and Huang:**
  - He says he is not in any "war room" and made no demands of the board. His letter, co-signed with Gavin Baker, asks for a slow, deliberate, fact-finding process with all options open, including a possible Huang return.
  - He did not sign the online "save Jensen" letter, saying a quick return isn't in Huang's interest.
  - Huang texted him back: "I'm deeply sorry about what I've done."
  - He worries the board could "succumb" to pressure from the White House, Congress and the Treasury, which wants answers within 10 days.
  - If the facts show Huang can't run the company in line with national security interests, he would agree with the Treasury Secretary's call for an outsider.
  - He expects Huang to be called to testify publicly.
- **Interim CEO and successors:** He backs Colette Kress in the interim and says 2027 and 2028 are "all baked" on fab and delivery schedules. The real question is vision beyond that. Arm CEO Rene Haas "could be capable" of leading the company, and "legends recruit legends."
- **NVIDIA position:** NVIDIA is down about 36% top to bottom this year, but up 18x in three years. He has sold NVIDIA. He went from a large exposure to a small one, is de-grossing the fund, and doesn't know whether he sold more since the news. He called the AI trade overdone on May 28 and July 2, with about 20% downside.
- **Export rules and competitors:**
  - Commerce and Treasury issued a new chip-distribution framework this morning, with added licensing for Singapore, Malaysia and Indonesia. Three bills are also pending. He sees all of it as a headwind for AI semis.
  - He owns little or no AMD or Broadcom (at most 1–2%), and thinks they fall with NVIDIA if the whole trade is pressured.
  - Anthropic reportedly used Claude to port optimizations to AMD GPU kernels over a weekend, which penetrates the CUDA moat. He doesn't think CUDA was NVIDIA's only moat.
  - He owns Amazon but not Google.
- **Compute forecast:** The consensus for 2027 compute was 43 GW, and he has said about 30 GW. He also expects 5–10 GW less data-center buildout than forecast, citing regulation and capital limits.
- **Anthropic IPO:**
  - He is placing a $5B order, more than 5x his largest prior IPO order. If allocated and he can add in the aftermarket, it would be Altimeter's largest position.
  - He expects pricing around $1.4–1.5T. A pricing of $1.75–1.8T would be "risky."
  - He compares this to "Google 2005," where one of 20 search engines captured 99% of the upside. He thinks Anthropic and OpenAI will do the same, and he is a major shareholder in both.
  - Frontier labs' cheap models (Haiku and others) undercut open source, though open source won't die.
  - Before the midterms his exposure was the lowest in years, outside April 2025's tariff trade, because everything was in the "too hard bucket."
- **China:**
  - The US is ahead and extending its lead by making model distillation harder and releasing frontier models more slowly under the new regulatory framework.
  - He favors engagement with China while still winning, and calls the game "infinite."
  - He noted Trump is meeting Xi four times this year.

**Jenny Harrington**
- **NVIDIA:** She calls herself a potential future shareholder, not a current one. Huang made a horrible professional decision that "incinerated" over $1T and his credibility. She worries about the ecosystem's circular financing if it starts to retract.
- **Valuation:** She doubts that a cheap valuation matters to the market. She would consider buying once there is a new CEO and visibility into next year's earnings. She cited the "GE parable": the price you say you'll buy at isn't always when you actually will.
- **Disney (owns):**
  - Theme parks are about 30% operating margin and underappreciated.
  - She expects 8% earnings growth this year and 12% over the next two.
  - It trades at 16x earnings with a 5% free cash flow yield.
  - Management is targeting 20% cost cuts and landed a good new CEO.

**Joe Terranova**
- **NVIDIA:** He thinks quant models are exacerbating the downside. Valuation is "incredibly cheap" but the story is complicated, and the real headline is the lack of clarity on whether China got advanced chips. Money is flowing to Apple, the most expensive Mag 7 name, instead. He is confident in Kress, CFO since 2013, but she can't remove the complexity. The earnings call tomorrow night matters.
- **Sentiment:** Hedge funds are pulling back on risk with the 2026 calendar closing. The optimism he had been representing is "quickly fading."
- **Palo Alto:** Earnings come this week with a 9% implied move versus a 5% average. He expects 33%+ revenue growth, triple-digit EPS growth and strong margins. He worries about the market's reaction, and CrowdStrike and Okta report Dec 1.
- **Disney:** He sees the cost cuts as financial engineering.

**Jim Lebenthal**
- **NVIDIA worst case (owns it):** He sized a worst case. Up to 20% of next year's roughly $700B in sales could be China-related, so $140B comes off. Treating that as all profit, net income falls from about $450B to $310B. The stock has lost over $1.5T, so he thinks the market is pricing something worse than that.
- **Pushback on the bear case:** The next six quarters are baked in, since nobody cancels chips mid-build. Anthropic's IPO could pull $60–80B of new capital that goes largely to chips. He also noted AMD and Broadcom were up 4–5% on the day.
- **Disney (owns):** He wants streaming profitability to offset legacy declines. He also wants confirmation that parks and cruise ships are healthy and not just riding price hikes. He rejects the "financial engineering" label and calls it a new CEO cutting fat.

**Mike Santoli**
- He argued that whatever NVIDIA's demand looked like a month ago, the addressable pool may now be smaller. He also noted reports of NVIDIA's anxiety over market share and over-commitment.
- He pointed out that NVIDIA can resume buybacks after earnings.

**Final Trades**
- **Jim Lebenthal:** Berkshire Hathaway (BRK) — a defensive name with a large cash pile to deploy.
- **Joe Terranova:** Eli Lilly (LLY) — strong healthcare momentum, "a port in a storm."
- **Jenny Harrington:** Cisco (CSCO) — 5% free cash flow yield, down 8% on the tech selloff despite strong earnings last week.
