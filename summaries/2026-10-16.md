**AI Debt Fears Fuel Tech Volatility: The Investment Committee's Strategy – 10/16/26**

**Overview:** Leslie Picker filled in for Scott Wapner. The panel focused on a rough week for tech, driven by worries over AI financing costs, higher yields and a weak showing from CoreWeave and the other neoclouds. They also covered bank earnings, a new AbbVie buy, Netflix's leadership shakeup and the growing appeal of alternatives.

**Market snapshot:** The Nasdaq was down 1.4% near session lows, the S&P down 0.6% and the Dow near flat. The 10-year yield was about 5.098%. Tech was on pace for its worst week since June. CPI and PPI both came in hotter than expected.

- **Kevin Simpson:**
  - Called this week an AI "reality check" and said it's healthy for investors to ask for returns on capex.
  - Expects "haves and have-nots" in AI.
  - Thinks yields haven't peaked and could rise another ~30 bps from here.
  - Said the market has turned defensive (staples, international, health care) and expects chop.
  - **Committee move:** bought **AbbVie (ABBV)**, a 1% starter position he wants to build to 5% on pullbacks.
    - The stock is up ~25% YTD and hit a record high yesterday.
    - He likes the 2.5% dividend and the double-digit growth.
    - Skyrizi and Rinvoq, plus neuroscience, have replaced the lost Humira revenue.
  - Would use options to manage risk, such as covered calls and put-spread collars.
  - On the Brad Gerstner clip: understands the argument for buying the dip, but says "down off highs doesn't mean cheap." He owns six semis across two portfolios and expects them to get cheaper.
  - On Netflix: well positioned, added in March. Peter Rice starts on the 26th, and he is being patient.
- **Rob Sechan:**
  - Financing will be available "at a price," and AI growth rates can absorb it.
  - Tech trades at only a ~9% premium to the market (versus ~100% in the dot-com era), and many high-quality names are at ~20x earnings.
  - Wouldn't try to time the semis and will wait for fundamentals to deteriorate. He called AI "the theme of a generation."
  - Is building fixed income (Treasuries, munis) as a shock absorber.
  - Said Anthropic may have repriced its IPO target, and that credit stress is showing up in triple-C debt.
  - Said the broadening trade is helped by owning high-quality earnings.
  - Is way overweight Meta, which reports next week, because it is cheap and owns customers. Owns Apple as a neutral weight, since being underweight has been "one of the most dangerous trades."
  - Likes alternatives. Said "alts" shouldn't be treated as a single asset class and cited private placement life insurance for tax efficiency.
  - Likes Netflix.
- **Malcolm Etheridge:**
  - Said sentiment toward AI is the problem even though fundamentals are strong. Wants to wait for big-tech earnings and capex guidance before buying beaten-down tech.
  - On chips, favors owning only the core names: Nvidia, AMD, ASML and TSMC. He thinks speculative names carry more risk.
  - Has been buying Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley and JPMorgan after 10%+ selloffs, and thinks the market is too focused on net interest margin.
    - He cited a JPMorgan exec's remark about 130 AI projects that could save ~$700M next year.
    - He sees bank AI spending as temporary and the payoff as more immediate than for infrastructure builders.
  - On Netflix: surprised by the selloff. He sees the hire of a live-TV/sports executive as a step toward competing with YouTube, and he owns the stock.
- **Jason Snipe:**
  - Wants companies that can absorb higher capital costs and aren't leveraged to the capital stack. Prefers capex-light names with buybacks and strong balance sheets, such as Apple.
  - Likes Blackstone ($1.35T AUM) and Apollo for their diversified revenue, including Apollo's insurance business. Sees private credit redemptions continuing and might add in a few quarters.
  - Likes AbbVie as a diversifying biopharma (immunology, oncology, neuroscience).
  - On Netflix: down ~25%, attractively priced, and the stock's drop is probably due to the timing of the shakeup before earnings. Notes the ~6% staff cut (~1,000 positions), and earnings are Tuesday.
- **Mike Santoli (Market Memo):**
  - Yesterday's intraday bounce is being retested today, and the action looks erratic and forced, as if levered positions are being blown out.
  - Equal-weight S&P is barely down.
  - Thinks credit markets will decide whether this is a mini-panic.
  - Hyperscaler earnings are the next anchor.
  - Called CoreWeave a potential "canary in the coal mine." Said financial conditions have tightened, and that's "uncomfortable" but not unhealthy.
- **Featured segments:**
  - **Robert Frank (Goldman survey):** 38% of millionaires plan to raise their alternatives allocation, and only 7% plan to cut. 93% are happy with their alts. Nearly two-thirds still call alts high or very high risk, which is up from last year. Half of millennials plan to increase allocations, compared with 18% of boomers. Warburg Pincus CEO Jeff Perlman said market concentration (10 companies are ~50% of the S&P) will push investors toward private equity.
  - **Options Action (Oliver Renick):** Energy options flow was one-sided and bullish. About 94,000 of 200,000 trades were calls, and volume was 10x the average. The most popular trade was in Petrobras (up 115% YTD). In gold, GLD call buying was heavy, and someone sold $11M of mid-January puts.
  - **CNBC news update (Contessa Brewer):** The NCAA is suing Ohio's governor over a fifth-season order. Diddy was moved to a Pennsylvania facility. Trump is campaigning in Georgia.
- **Final Trades:**
  - **Malcolm:** ServiceNow (NOW). He likes its agentic governance and AI passport tool, plus analyst upgrades and target raises.
  - **Rob:** JPMorgan (JPM). He used the headline miss as a chance to buy a world-class franchise.
  - **Jason:** Eaton (ETN). Orders are up 40%.
  - **Kevin:** Buy SentinelOne (S) on today's ~9% drop in cybersecurity.
