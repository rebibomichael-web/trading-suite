**Stocks Hit Record Highs: Your Next Move – 10/6/26**

**Overview:** The S&P 500 and Nasdaq hit record highs after a rough September of a Fed hike, a 10-year yield near 5.3% and rising oil. The panel debated whether the rally can broaden beyond the Mag 7 and AI leaders, and whether the Fed is "one and done" or in a longer hiking cycle. Other topics were semis (Marvell, AMD, Micron), dividend stocks in a rising-rate world, and several stock calls (SpaceX, Disney, Netflix, Northrop).

- **Market backdrop (Scott):** Every sector was green and yields and oil were down. The 10-year was around 5.26%. The Mag 7 were up 5.5% in September while the S&P was flat. Nvidia's market cap is near $6T. Microsoft is up 38% over three months, Nvidia 24% and Meta 23%. Wells Fargo says buy hyperscalers over semis and set an Alphabet target of $417.
- **Stephanie Link:**
  - Wants a broadening trade. XLI, XLF, XLU and materials were each down 7–8% in September, which she sees as value. She pointed to nine power deals worth over $25B this year, including today's Constellation/Google announcement.
  - Expects broadening to be paused during earnings season. Q3 earnings growth is expected at 25–27%, with energy above 100% and tech up 67%, and Nvidia plus Micron are about a third of S&P earnings growth. She would revisit broadening in November–December.
  - Marvell (held): It raised its long-term revenue target to $70–90B by 2031 (Street was at $47B) and sees a $400B TAM. She trimmed after a 150% run in three months and rotated into Nvidia for the first time. She puts earnings power at about $15/share by 2028, up from her earlier $10.
  - Micron: JPM target $1,540. She sees about $40/share earnings power, so the stock trades near 5x earnings. Demand should exceed supply into 2028, with $100B+ in visibility through 2030.
  - Cyber: Loves it but says it's pricey (Palo Alto went from 11x to 24x sales). She holds Palo Alto and CrowdStrike. ServiceNow is her better value at about 25x forward earnings, down 10% year to date, and she would add on a bad day.
  - SpaceX: Bought on the way down, at 40%, 30% and 25% below the high. Revenue grew 92% and EBITDA 191%. She likes the connectivity business, with a 250M subscriber goal by 2030. Goldman target is $230.
  - Northrop: Rothschild & Redburn call. Down 16% year to date, 16x earnings, 5% FCF yield, about 10% earnings growth, $105B pipeline and the B-21. She owns it, bought 13 years ago and up 400%, and calls it a permanent compounder rather than a war-trade double.
- **Jenny Harrington:**
  - Dividend strategy requires a 5%+ yield. She noted the 10-year was also at 5.2% when she started in 2001, and the strategy did fine.
  - Bought Alphabet convertible preferred (6.4% yield, matures 2029, trades like the common). She values Google at 17x earnings and thinks it wins if LLM players fade. If Google is at $250 in 2029 her return is –9.7% versus –26% for the common, thanks to about 18% in income. At $500 she gets 34% versus 46% for the common.
  - Dividend names she added to: Bristol Myers, Dominion (4.5% yield) and Sabra (about 6.5% yield, about 7% earnings growth). These are down 13–15% from their highs.
  - Says high-dividend stocks outperformed in 7 of the last 10 rising-rate periods since 1960. The exceptions were short, fast spikes, which is the open question now.
  - Also named Home Depot, UPS, Comcast (bought at $22) and Western Union as beaten down.
  - Disney: Bull calls from BofA, JPM and Bernstein with targets of $125–129. She cites 14x earnings, 6.1% FCF yield, 17% EPS growth this year and 8% next, and theme parks as a tangible asset less exposed to AI. Her cost basis is $118 after six years.
- **Kevin Simpson:**
  - Has been defensive. He sees a scenario where energy and inflation fall and the Fed is one and done after a December hike, in which case 5.3% on the 10-year could be the high-water mark. A longer hiking cycle would leave the leadership with big tech.
  - On Alphabet, he thinks agents bypassing search is the main overhang. He still likes the diversified business.
  - Dividend strategy is growth-focused (dividend aristocrats/kings) with no minimum yield, which is why he owns Nvidia now that it pays one.
  - Owns AMD (Citi target $800, Mizuho $705) and calls the run hard to argue against.
  - Sold IBM into Nvidia. He loves Red Hat, AI and quantum, but the guide cut, 5% software growth, flat consulting and a 7% infrastructure decline worried him.
  - Bought more UNH, first buying around $370. He is buying ahead of next week's report, with the guide raised to $19.50–20.
  - Netflix: Goldman cut its target to $90 from $94. He is adding a small position and is down about $10 on it in six weeks. He hopes sports and live entertainment turn the story around.
- **Bryn Talkington:**
  - Bullish on Microsoft. She cites Azure acceleration, Amy Hood's pledge not to go free-cash-flow negative, and a model-neutral Copilot/Cowork. She says it's risky to turn negative on hyperscalers.
  - Holds a DRAM basket (Micron 26%, Samsung 25%, SK Hynix about 16%) and sells 2–3 month calls 10% out of the money. She thinks the group is resting after a big move and compares it to Nvidia's range between April and now.
  - Owns RSP and CBRE, which fell sharply. She thinks multiple rate hikes would kill the broadening story.
- **Michael Santoli (Market Memo):**
  - The breakout above 7,800 and the August highs is tentative. Nvidia remains the dominant theme.
  - Late-cycle signs include wider credit spreads, sovereign debt issues and the shift from easing to tightening.
  - The market is discerning within semis. It doesn't want pure memory and favors names with stories and renewed momentum (Marvell +5%).
- **Options Action (Oliver Renick):** Pepsi is down more than 10% this year but has rallied after four of its last five earnings reports. About 2,600 calls versus 550 puts traded today, and 70% of the $5M in options was in calls. The options market implies a 3% move. One trader bought $500K of $130-strike puts expiring next September.
- **Final Trades:**
  - Bryn Talkington: GPIQ (10.5%+ income plus Nasdaq exposure)
  - Kevin Simpson: UNH (turnaround story with improving fundamentals)
  - Jenny Harrington: Clearway Energy (down 26% in a few months, 6.3% yield, double-digit earnings growth)
  - Stephanie Link: EQT (natural gas winner)
