**Will Rates & Oil Derail Stocks? The Investment Committee Shares Their Strategy – 9/28/26**

**Overview:** Stocks are about 1.75% off record highs, but the 10-year yield (about 5.26%) and WTI crude (about $95) have the panel debating how much risk they pose. The panel also covered Nvidia's record $150B buyback, Micron earnings on Wednesday, Berkshire's Lennar buying, and Disney vs. Netflix.

- **Steve Weiss:**
  - Cautious as rates approach 5%. He sees a path to 6% or 7% on the 10-year and thinks oil is likely to stay high, since he says continued conflict serves Iran's interests.
  - He sold Cisco, which he bought after its last quarter and hoped would bounce. He also trimmed Meta to a "right size," and it is still his largest position.
  - He is about 25% cash and may go higher.
  - He is full in Alphabet, has a decent Microsoft position, and calls buybacks better than dividends for stock prices.
  - He said the flattening yield curve is bad for banks, and that all eight recessions since 1960 were preceded by inversion.
  - On Meta, he called hiring the MongoDB CEO a signal of an enterprise push, and said he was surprised the stock fell about 4%. On Micron, he noted nearly 50% of its contracts aren't long-term, which could hurt margins.
  - On Netflix, he bought in the low 70s, added lower, and can't imagine a third straight miss. He will play it through the quarter and exit if wrong.
- **Jim Lebenthal:**
  - Bullish. He cites good growth, strong labor markets (4.1% unemployment) and excellent profit growth, and expects earnings to drive the market higher. He would put new money into Cisco.
  - He says the S&P forward P/E fell from about 22 to 18.5, with a PEG well below 1. He expects the rally to broaden and calls this an equal-weight S&P time, while conceding the last month narrowed sharply.
  - He likes energy (XLE roughly 10% below its high, with SPR refills and rebuilding ahead), healthcare and pharma.
  - He thinks the 2-year is overshooting by pricing at least four rate hikes, and that the curve could steepen. He is keeping cash well above 10%.
  - He added back the half of Qualcomm he trimmed earlier in the year.
  - He has a meaningful Micron position, bought after the last report and added on the dip. He expects strategic long-term contracts, rising pricing and buybacks at about 6.5x forward earnings around the CHIPS Act anniversary in December.
  - On Berkshire's Lennar stake (now over 10%), he called it a bet that rates fall and housing recovers. On Disney, he is holding a stock at 14x forward earnings and likes Josh D'Amaro as CEO for the next 10 years. He said Bob Chapek was likely undermined by Bob Iger, but the stock didn't perform under him.
  - Farmer Jim, a horse named for him by Barry Schwartz, finished sixth of eight in its first race.
- **Joe Terranova:**
  - He said positioning got caught offside in the last 30 days. People expected lower oil and suppressed yields going into the midterms, and got neither.
  - Rate-sensitive areas (real estate, utilities, discretionary, financials) are in clear downtrends. He said a broadening needs a Middle East resolution or 10-year yields back below 5%.
  - He said mega-caps act as both offense and defense, and reiterated his 7640 S&P target. He sees $250B buybacks coming.
  - He noted that multiples on these names have compressed: Nvidia is at 19x vs. a 26x high in May, Amazon 26x vs. 32x, and Apple about 37x.
  - He worries about narrow, 2023-style leadership and says Micron's report is a read on earnings season. Semi-equipment names are down sharply quarter to date (LAM -29%, KLA -39%, AMAT -34%).
  - He sold Berkshire on momentum earlier.
  - He said Disney's streaming profits are partly financial engineering and cost-cutting. He sees Netflix's live and sports push as a margin challenge, though it is trading at about 20x, its cheapest in over a decade.
  - On gold and silver, he said they are washed out and near a 52-week low. He wouldn't buy here, but wouldn't bet on further downside either.
- **Nvidia:** It announced a $150B buyback, the largest ever, ahead of Apple's. The panel called it a positive, and Weiss noted it is funded with cash rather than debt.
- **Market moves:**
  - Oil turned lower midday on a CNN report that the president is open to Iran sanctions relief for "concrete progress on nuclear issues," and S&P futures moved higher.
  - Options flow (Oliver Renek): GLD fell 4% but call buying was twice put buying, and SLV calls were more than double puts. HYG put volume was over 3x average, with one trader spending about $4M on the $78 November puts.
- **Other news:**
  - Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei dined privately with President Trump, and an IPO in November is still possible. SNL also spoofed him.
  - ETF Edge: Janus Henderson's Nick Cherney discussed tokenized funds offered offshore to institutional investors.
- **Final Trades:**
  - Weiss: UnitedHealth
  - Lebenthal: AbbVie (new Parkinson's drug approved today)
  - Terranova: refiners and cybersecurity as the two anchor themes
