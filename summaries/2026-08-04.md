**Navigating a Stock Market at Record Highs — August 4, 2026**

Overview: Stocks hit fresh record highs (S&P, Dow, Nasdaq all up sharply) as blowout earnings from Palantir, Caterpillar, Microsoft, and the chip sector drove a broad, healthy rotation rather than multiple expansion. The committee framed July's sharp semiconductor/momentum selloff as a technical deleveraging reset (not a growth scare), leaving the AI capex and earnings story intact and setting up several names — Palantir, SK Hynix, financials, industrials — to keep rebuilding positioning into new highs.

- **Joe Terranova**: Called this an earnings-driven bull market — 10 of 11 S&P sectors posting double-digit earnings growth, blended rate near 29%, 85% beat rate vs. 77% five-year average. Said July's semi/momentum washout (SOXX, Sandisk, KLA, Marvell, Lam, Applied, Micron all down 25-46%) was a deleveraging/margin-call event (cites Korean/Kospi margin calls), not a fundamental top — forward S&P multiple cheapened from 23.3x to 19.8x. His ETF bought Palantir at $16 (Jan '24), rode it to $123, and framed its 27% earnings pop (93% vs. 80% expected revenue growth) as a rebuild-of-positioning story; multiple fell from 247x to mid-60s. Sold Apple, bought SK Hynix two weeks ago on a rerating. On Cummins: beat-and-raise but gross margins light (26.1% vs. 27.1% est) — vulnerable post-earnings. Stayed positive on ONEOK and EQT (natural gas), noted the Williams deal ($5.5B). Sold Disney in the last rebalance — sees no fundamental catalyst, calls its momentum activism-driven only. Healthcare: ETF healthcare weighting now 11.7%, added Vertex (raised forecast), Eli Lilly, Quest Diagnostics, Humana; already owns Amgen. Final trade: JB Hunt (just added).
- **Jim Lebenthal**: Agreed July's rotation/deleveraging (not peak-earnings) explains the selloff, and no one in AI capex is "blinking" on spend. Added to Cheniere ("Shaneer"), betting on long-term US LNG exports to Asia/Europe. On Disney (sold half his Apple pre-earnings previously, still owns Disney): called it "a value trap" and admitted he's "tired," seriously considering harvesting the loss, but holding into tomorrow's earnings — a rare on-air admission he may be wrong. Final trade: Alphabet, which snapped back after a rough post-earnings week.
- **Rob Sechan**: Liked Microsoft as a "garpy value" play at ~24.8x earnings given its AI data-center buildout. On SpaceX ahead of earnings/lockup expiry, flagged four things to watch — Starlink connectivity/margins, capex guidance, free cash flow, AI monetization timeline — and said full price discovery isn't there yet, though passive index buying could offset lockup supply. Called Amgen's GLP-1 entry exciting and cheap; likes Gilead at 16x with a strong oncology/HIV (Biktarvy) franchise. Final trade: an under-the-radar industrial dominating the turbine market ("Met Aerospace" per transcript).
- **Josh Brown**: Called this "one of the healthiest tapes" in his 29-year career — sector rotation absorbing pullbacks (Microsoft, Meta) without sinking the market. Tied resilience to a younger, dip-buying investor base ("I have another $40,000, how fast can I get it in?"). New buy: Marriott, bought the 14% pullback (Middle East-related guidance shave) while the company raised full-year guidance. New overweight: QXO ($35 PT per Morgan Stanley), small position, actively adding. Best Stocks in the Market picks: Incyte (crushed earnings, $1.67B revenue vs. estimates, raised guidance; watch $109/50-day, $101/200-day) and Biogen (beat top/bottom line, eyeing a breakout above $218, support at $190/$183). Long Uber (16x forward P/E) despite its post-earnings drops, says the market won't believe the robotaxi-partnership strategy until autonomous fleets are visibly on the road. Final trade: still long Apple despite the post-earnings dip.
- **Calls of the Day**: Walmart downgraded to Perform from Outperform at Oppenheimer (pharmacy headwinds, peakish valuation, capital rotating back into Target); eBay downgraded to Market Perform from Outperform at Citizens JMP (Depop acquisition risk/reward concerns).
- **Featured segment — Amazon antitrust lawsuit (Dom Chu)**: New Jersey AG Jennifer Davenport filed suit alleging Amazon uses its power to unlawfully control Delivery Service Partner (DSP) drivers; Amazon (via spokesperson Steve Kelly) called the claims "not grounded in fact." Committee agreed the stock's weakness is unrelated, driven instead by AI-trade sentiment after crossing $3T market cap.
- **Featured segment — Uber options action (Oliver Renick)**: Calls outnumbering puts nearly 2:1 (7,700 vs. 3,500) ahead of earnings; options pricing a ~6% post-earnings swing; biggest single trade was a $200K+ bet on June 2027 $75 calls, implying a 20% rally target.
- **Final Trades**: Josh Brown — Apple; Rob Sechan — turbine-market industrial ("Met Aerospace"); Jim Lebenthal — Alphabet; Joe Terranova — JB Hunt (just added).
