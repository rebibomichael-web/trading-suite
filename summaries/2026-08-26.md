Looking Ahead to Nvidia Earnings Tonight: Your Investment Strategy — August 26, 2026

**Overview:** The Investment Committee spent the hour previewing Nvidia's after-the-bell earnings, debating whether the report can revive a stalling momentum/AI trade and whether a bigger buyback or diversification story could finally re-rate the stock. They also covered a landmark Vanguard-Altruist wealth management deal, calls on TJX and utilities, and options positioning ahead of the print.

- **Joe Terranova:** Nvidia has fallen after earnings in 6 of the last 8 quarters, so the bar is a blowout; Q3 guidance is key — whisper numbers are $110-120B (UBS $110B, Jeffries $108B) versus the roughly 15% price hikes Nvidia has telegraphed on GPUs due to memory costs; if pull-forward demand drives a beat, it "hits the refresh button" on the AI/momentum trade. Also flagged Salesforce: traded out of CRM into ServiceNow, took a tax-loss, still likes Salesforce's enterprise stickiness. Final Trade: Valero (bought after a 10-day consolidation working off overbought conditions).
- **Kerry Firestone (Carrie):** Sees ~75% odds Nvidia beats the ~$209 estimate and talks up a strong Q3 (~$110B) plus Rubin demand at higher prices — that combo could spark a big move given sideline cash. Doesn't think Nvidia needs a bigger buyback (not "mature" like Apple was); notes NVDA up 1,700% Oct 2022–Oct 2025 and now basing near its 100-day average. Likes Oracle after adding two weeks ago (target range $125-150, cites backlog, EPS growth 20%+, no new 2026 debt). On Microsoft, sees an open-source Chinese AI model coming to Copilot as a potential catalyst. Owns Schwab; says Schwab/Fidelity have deep tech resources to fight Altruist competition. Final Trade: Flowserve (industrial pumps/power plants).
- **Bill Baruch:** Watching the Rubin ramp, Blackwell demand, margin pressure from memory costs, financing-deal commentary, and non-GPU products (AI models, CPUs) as potential positive surprises. Bought Block (~50% down over the year) at end of July, up ~10%, says software is a hedge against an AI-momentum unwind. On CrowdStrike (held, outside top 5 now): didn't trim despite CTO departure and profit-taking, cites 97% gross retention and strong ARR — thinks bar is now lower into earnings. Likes Vistra, considering adding — nuclear/gas contracts, ERCOT positioning, Helix partnership with KKR/Nvidia, CEO bought ~$250K in stock. Final Trade: not explicitly given (segment ended before his final trade was shown).
- **Rob Sechan:** Frames Nvidia as "the center of the AI trade," dismisses Cisco-2000 comparisons since Nvidia has real pricing power; sees monetization of the ~$1.5T AI infrastructure buildout as still early innings; notes his own firm's rising AI/Anthropic spend is boosting productivity, not yet cutting headcount. On momentum: prefers a broadened basket (Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, financials, healthcare) over concentrated bets; expects volatility either way. Bullish on utilities exposed to data-center power demand (NRG, Vistra) despite re-rating to ~11x — sees room to run given the energy bottleneck; also mentioned Nextera and American Water as "scarce resource" plays. Final Trade: Micron ("building momentum," expects Nvidia to blow out and lift Micron and other names).
- **Christina Partsinevelos (CNBC correspondent segment):** Nvidia trades at a forward P/E just above 20x, its lowest pre-earnings multiple in 5+ years, and one of the widest valuation gaps versus the SOX index on record; explained Wall Street's worry that Amazon/Google in-house chips are eating share, and that Morgan Stanley now treats Nvidia's bonds like triple-B credit rather than AA given its vendor-financing exposure; flagged the WSJ's "$1.5 trillion question" on whether AI capex ever pays back, and Nvidia's push to diversify beyond the four hyperscalers into AI startups, enterprises, sovereign AI and its own VC-style investing.
- **Buyback debate:** Bank of America reiterated Buy with a $350 price target, calling an "Apple-style" buyback pivot an under-appreciated catalyst; Kerry pushed back that Nvidia isn't mature enough for that yet, while Bill said a bigger buyback wouldn't be bad given ~$360B run-rate free cash flow.
- **Calls of the Day:** TJX cut to Hold at Jeffries, target cut to $145 from $180 after weak Barmax division results (stock at a 52-week low); Barron's made the case for buying historically unloved utilities (yield-proxy pressure plus data-center backlash) — Rob and Bill both defended Vistra/NRG-type names.
- **Options Action (Oliver Renick, CBOE):** Options imply a 5.5% move for Nvidia shares post-earnings (more than double the average recent-quarter swing); calls are pricier than puts, open interest clustered near the $220 strike, with $200 seen as key support to hold.
- **Landmark deal segment (Josh Brown, phone):** Vanguard is buying wealth-management platform Altruist for $4 billion; Josh (an Altruist shareholder/advisor) called it an "earthquake" that lends Vanguard's brand/scale to a venture-backed startup and its AI tool "Hazel," pressuring Schwab, Fidelity and wirehouses like Morgan Stanley; noted Altruist CEO Jason Wink is staying on a multi-year deal and the team isn't relocating. Rob Sechan (New Edge) said no custodian has a monopoly on good ideas and sees a "middle layer" enabling advisors as the model of the future.
- **Michael Santoli (midday word):** Says the market mainly wants to get past the Nvidia print to resume an underlying uptrend; Nvidia's valuation is de-risked with indifferent buy-side sentiment versus still-bullish sell-side; little macro reason for the Fed to shift September expectations.
- **Final Trades:** Rob Sechan — Micron; Kerry Firestone — Flowserve; Joe Terranova — Valero.
