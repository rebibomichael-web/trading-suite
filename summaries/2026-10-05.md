**Nasdaq and Yields in Focus: The Investment Committee's Strategy, 10/5/26**

**Overview:** Stocks hit new highs on the Nasdaq even as the 10-year yield sits near 5.3%. The panel debated how long equities and bonds can coexist at these levels, and why the market has become so concentrated in mega-cap tech. They also covered the case for beaten-up industrials and cyclicals, a set of analyst calls, and Josh's third-quarter review of his Best Stocks list.

**Panelists and segments**

- **Scott Wapner (host):**
  - The 10-year is up about 50 bps since Sept 5, when it was 4.78%. Over the same stretch the equal-weight S&P is down 4.5% and the Nasdaq is up about 5%.
  - Evercore notes extreme sector divergences: energy is up about 40% YTD and tech about 30%, while financials are down 2%. Only 8 times since 1990 has the gap been this wide.

- **Joe Terranova:**
  - Stocks and bonds can keep coexisting. Last week's White House meeting with tech CEOs and Micron's earnings ended worries about higher yields hurting AI capex and confirmed demand. 10 of 11 sectors were higher.
  - He will not buy the cyclical reset. His strategy is momentum-driven, and cyclicals have lost momentum. Any turn depends on oil and yields falling, and he won't invest on that hope.
  - He is skeptical of Capital One and Synchrony after Barclays' notes (Cap One target $237). He calls Synchrony a clear momentum breakdown with a fee-driven retail model that struggles as rates rise.
  - He still holds Valero, bought last year at $144. He keeps thinking about trimming but can't bring himself to, given domestic and European diesel tightness.
  - He calls First Solar a "never again" name: Chinese polysilicon costs are far lower than expected, and it is hurt by rates and housing.
  - He has new holdings in Nu Holdings (digital banking in Brazil), up about 14% today. On the EWZ rally, he likes Brazil for demographics rather than politics. It is the seventh-largest country and 10th-largest economy, with 63% of it in services.
  - He bought back Cboe in June at $266. TD Cowen upgraded it to Buy with a target of $334 (from $315). He cites the S&P licensing renewal and the potential tokenization of SPX options.

- **Stephanie Link:**
  - She is bullish on the economy. S&P services PMI is the best since July 2021, today's ISM is the best in 4.5 years, and weekly claims are the best since 1969. Hourly earnings and core PCE came in better than expected, and 3-month CPI is running in the 2s.
  - She has been adding to Mag 7 names (Meta recently, Nvidia about two months ago) and expects strong tech and comm services earnings. She says 8 of 11 sectors posted double-digit earnings growth last quarter.
  - She likes industrials, which she says are down 18–30% from highs with backlogs up 30–40% and multiples compressed. She notes Morgan Stanley calls them the best risk/reward, and BMO is Outperform on Vertiv with a $329 target (about 31% upside). Vertiv is on track for about 31% organic growth this year. She owns Dover as a short-cycle play and is not buying housing-related names. Starbucks has been great and Amazon disappointing.
  - **Wells Fargo:** Morgan Stanley upgraded it to Overweight. She sold out earlier and bought it back after the CEO's Sept 9 CNBC interview, where he said net interest margin had bottomed and the asset cap lift (June 3 last year) is helping. It trades at 11x earnings and 1.4x book, and has lagged the big six banks by about 14% YTD.
  - **SpaceX Call of the Day:** the stock is up 20% since the IPO. Adam Jonas at Morgan Stanley has a $300 target against a price of about $167. She owns it as a long-term hold. Last-quarter capex rose 65% with a one-year payback, and the stock is still down 17% from its highs.
  - **Estée Lauder:** Barclays upgraded it to Overweight with a $108 target. She sees the "Beauty Reimagined" turnaround plan delivering organic growth, cost cuts and better earnings. The stock is still down 74% from its January 2022 high.
  - She owns CRH and is not selling. Interest rates explain the drop. It carries about $19B of debt against a market cap of about $54B, and its P/E fell from 18x in July to 13x. She sees it as a long-term compounder tied to infrastructure and data centers.

- **Jim Lebenthal:**
  - He is uncomfortable at these levels and would be more so if yields rise. Debt-heavy sectors face higher interest costs. The Mag 7 are less affected, since 50 bps won't derail data center plans.
  - He says the equal-weight S&P led the cap-weighted index six weeks ago and could rotate back, but yields need to ease first.
  - He thinks financials are held back by a thinner IPO calendar, with Anthropic still expected but the window shaky, rather than by a credit scare.
  - He questions consumer discretionary, particularly travel and leisure (maybe Disney if the Iran situation resolves), because there is no catalyst or timing.
  - On energy, the transcript doesn't name the speaker, but the remarks fit Jim. The Saudi Aramco CEO says refilling global inventories will take two years or more. Exxon, Chevron and XLE are in uptrends, and at $75 WTI these companies "mint money." Oil would need to fall to $60 to hurt, which he doesn't expect.
  - He supported the Spotify view: UBS has a $675 target (from $690), and Spotify trades like Netflix and is range-bound.

- **Josh Brown:**
  - The S&P's YTD return "is lying." Everything changed in March with the Iran attack. Defensiveness now means owning mega-cap tech, and only 41 S&P stocks are above their 200-day moving averages.
  - Mag 7 is up 14.1% against 13.8% for the S&P 493. The market trades at 19.1x forward earnings against 21.6x for tech. Nasdaq forward EPS estimates are up 32.5% YTD, and the Nasdaq's RSI is 65 with only 2.85% of its stocks above 70.
  - He expects the status quo with no end to rate pressure in sight, and says there may be more Fed hikes.
  - Nvidia hit a record high for the first time since May, at a $5.7T market cap. He called it the most important chart last week. He also cites Apple's product cycle, Meta's multiple expansion and a Mizuho (likely) upgrade of Microsoft to Buy. Those names could drive a 20%+ S&P year. A 7% 10-year would be too much, but the 1990s averaged about 5%+.
  - He says Spotify "might be one of the best businesses in the world" but is technically in no man's land.
  - **Best Stocks in the Market (Q3 review), winners:** Intel is up 163% since Jan 26 and another 39% since Apr 27, and about 200% YTD. Valero has doubled, and with Phillips 66 and Marathon it is among the three best XLE performers.
  - **Best Stocks, losers:** CRH was stopped out and is down 33% since January; the lesson is don't change your rules because the market tells you you're wrong, since the first sale is often the best. First Solar he avoided, because he disliked its parabolic run and said not every "best stock" is a buy.

- **Options Action (Oliver Renick):** EWZ is up 13% today and more than 40% YTD, on volume 15x normal with implied volatility collapsing. It looks like profit-taking on in-the-money calls rather than a bearish signal. Less than $10M of about $250M in premium was in puts. Brazil's election runoff is Oct 25.

- **ETF Edge guest, Priya Misra (JPMorgan Asset Management):**
  - She calls high-quality bonds yielding 6–7% a "generational opportunity," with record inflows into bond funds and ETFs.
  - She recommends a diversified, manager-selected mix: credit, structured/ABS, hyperscaler secured debt and some duration.
  - The rise in yields is driven by real rates (a supply shock) rather than inflation fears. She says the Fed has hiked once, with three more priced in. She thinks the 10-year peaks around 5.3–5.5%.

- **Final Trades:**
  - **Josh Brown:** Nvidia — he doesn't think it's done going up.
  - **Jim Lebenthal:** Cheniere (LNG exports) — a buying opportunity after it wasn't added to the S&P 500. The transcript garbles the name, so this is an inference.
  - **Stephanie Link:** Antofagasta — copper, well-run, good execution, her Latin America play.
  - **Joe Terranova:** Steel Dynamics.
