**High Bar for Q3 Earnings? The Investment Committee Debates, 10/12/26**

**Overview:** Leslie Picker, filling in for Scott Wapner, led a debate on whether expectations are too high as Q3 earnings begin, with stocks just below record highs. The panel focused on narrow market breadth, AI capex and debt financing, and upcoming bank earnings. Other segments covered Calls of the Day, energy's record run, and bond-ETF flows.

**Who said what:**

- **Jenny Harrington:**
  - Earnings numbers won't be the issue. Guidance and the pace of growth are, with 2027 earnings expected up about 15%. She thinks the market's reaction will say more than the data, as with Nvidia.
  - Her Q3 client letter notes the S&P's 2.3% gain was misleading because only a narrow group drove it.
  - She cited an FT piece on AI borrowing slowing and Broadcom's Hock Tan calling access to capital the biggest obstacle for AI.
  - On banks, she pointed to JPM at 13x with about 5% YTD, BofA at 10x and flat, and Schwab (owned) at 12.5x and -2%. All have double-digit earnings growth estimates, and she thinks capital could rotate back into banks.
  - Regeneron (owned): BofA raised its price target to $908 from $891, about 20% upside. It trades at 12x earnings with a 6% FCF yield and double-digit EPS growth for about 3 years. The drug behind the upgrade is only a roughly $2B product, and the stock was up 2.6% (about -0.8% YTD).
  - On energy, she avoids refiners because of political and economic sensitivity and a risk that oil falls back to $68. She owns midstream (Enterprise Products, Energy Transfer, Kinder Morgan), Flex LNG, and Total and Shell (both up about 50%).

- **Jim Lebenthal:**
  - He expects earnings to be fine. The question is whether hyperscaler capex plans stay intact, and he sees no sign of anyone pulling back.
  - Non-tech sectors have lagged because of rates, with the 10-year steady near 5.25%. Good bank earnings and stable CPI/PPI could help them.
  - He argued Nvidia has to invest in the ecosystem given about $300B of free cash flow.
  - He sees saturation in the debt market: investment-grade spreads are tight while triple-C spreads have widened. He doesn't think this is yet a credit-worthiness problem.
  - He likes Citi (about 10x forward earnings, a decent dividend, and catalysts including consent orders and the Banamex spin-off).
  - Adobe (owned): Wells Fargo double-downgraded it to underweight. He called it 9x forward earnings, with sales up about 50% and EPS up 80% in four years. Revenue growth was 12.5% last quarter and is projected at 11%. The share count is down 7% in nine months and it has no debt. He said AI hasn't eaten its lunch in four years. The stock is down about 32% YTD.
  - On energy, he owns ExxonMobil, Cheniere and Transocean (the highest-beta play), and doesn't expect crude to collapse.

- **Steve Weiss:**
  - He disputed the "market at new highs" framing. Most stocks are below their 50-day average and more than half are below their 200-day.
  - He said tech must deliver both results and guidance, and a pattern of misses would be a problem.
  - He sees no Fed move before the election and says "another tightening in December." He is cautious but still in a bull market.
  - He called Nvidia's vendor financing a "major house of cards" and said such financing is unprecedented in scale. He cited a roughly $250M investment in another cloud company and a reported deal with Reflection AI.
  - He named Micron and SK Hynix as stuck below prior highs.
  - On banks, he's watching trading desks, credit quality and capital markets. He noted M&A is hurt by rates and the IPO market is weaker than expected, though he hopes Anthropic comes next month.

- **Leslie Picker (host):**
  - She owns eBay, bought just under $100. Morgan Stanley named it a top pick, and it rose 4.2%. She likes its expansion into collectibles.
  - She noted the KBE bank ETF has underperformed the S&P by about 1,200 bps since June 30 and has sold off five weeks straight. The last comparable gap was in January 2025, and banks rallied on earnings then.
  - Jefferies proxy data show advisory/M&A and debt capital markets lower, with equity capital markets a bright spot.

- **Bill Baruch (futures trade):**
  - Energy is at a record high, up nearly 50% YTD. The top three XLE performers are refiners (Marathon Petroleum, Valero, Phillips 66), each up more than 100%.
  - He attributed the move to diesel and heating-oil crack spreads, driven by Russia/Ukraine and then the US attack on Iran in March.
  - Refiner utilization is running hot. He said it's crack spreads, not AI, driving the trade.
  - He noted Trump's unconfirmed Russia-Ukraine energy ceasefire talk.

- **Featured segments:**
  - **Mike Santoli's Market Memo:** The market is deeply split. The equal-weight S&P is 4–5% below its highs while the S&P 500 is near a record. Semis fell after South Korea's market, and software and Meta rose in contrast. Low volatility is encouraging people to keep their exposure.
  - **Fed news:** Cleveland Fed's Beth Hammack said restrictive policy is appropriate and wants quick, smaller rate adjustments. She called inflation broad-based and sees an "inflationary mindset" taking hold. This could set up an October dissent.
  - **ETF Edge:** Nate Jiracy of Novodeus Wealth Management reported $75B of fixed-income ETF inflows in the past month and a record $525B this year. Some investors are moving to managed-futures ETFs as a diversifier.
  - **Options Action (Oliver Renick):** About $5M of SNAP options traded, over 120K contracts and four times the average, mostly calls. The biggest bet was the $8 December call, which needs about a 30% rally. The stock rose 3% to its highest since February and is down about 92% over five years.

- **Final Trades:**
  - **Steve Weiss:** Netflix. He thinks the stock has found its lows and the Street is too negative, so risk/reward is attractive.
  - **Jim Lebenthal:** The ticker was garbled in the transcript, so I can't name it. He said it's a solid company he's owned for years, at an all-time high with strong momentum.
  - **Jenny Harrington:** DocuSign. It trades at 14x earnings with nearly a 10% FCF yield. She's holding it in the growth strategy and doesn't think AI will eat its lunch.
