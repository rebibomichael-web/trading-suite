**Mega-Cap Earnings Aftermath — July 31, 2026**

Overview: Amazon and Microsoft roared higher on blowout cloud earnings while Apple slid on rich valuation and supply-chain cost concerns, driving a mega-cap-led, small-cap-lagging market. The desk also dug into a sharp bond-yield backup under new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh and flagged fresh M&A chatter in chips (NXP/Ambarella).

- **Jim Lebenthal**: Called out AWS's 37% growth (just below Azure's 43%) and Amazon's <3-year payback on data-center capex vs. 30-year depreciable life as the key bullish data point, contrasting it with Meta's murkier profitability path. Sold about half his Apple position ahead of earnings on rich valuation (mid-30s forward multiple) and weak Qualcomm-signaled smartphone demand; now holds roughly half-market-weight (~6.5%). Likes eBay into Wednesday's report — GameStop bid optionality, strong consumer trends, buybacks, high-margin/high-ticket focus (collectibles, event tickets).
- **Amy Raskin**: Skeptical the AI capex story is fully resolved — spending is still financed via equity, circular deals and debt, not AI revenue itself; expects the "rotational" winners/losers narrative to keep looping. Worried about margin-call-driven forced liquidations (Korean retail accounts, Citadel-linked unwinds) distorting the memory-chip trade; thinks Micron's ~6x forward earnings/14% FCF yield is unreasonably cheap given execution risk, but flagged December's CHIPS Act buyback eligibility as a catalyst. On rates, she's concerned rising yields could start pressuring equities (yield/equity matrix in "red territory"). Still likes Apple's moat despite the pullback (bonus point: incoming CEO John Ternus is a Penn engineering grad like her). Very overweight energy — bought back a trim, sees low inventories creating a floor under oil/energy stocks; likes EOG into earnings.
- **Rob Sechan**: Framed the earnings divergence as a story of "clarity" — companies articulating a clear AI monetization path (Amazon, Microsoft) rallied, while Meta's murkier framing hurt it. Trimmed Apple ahead of earnings, moved to neutral/market-weight given rich valuation despite strong FCF and ecosystem. Views the recent volatility (Micron's ~10% weekly drop, mega-cap swings) as a "positioning unwind," not a fundamental growth scare — leverage and momentum flows unwinding, not a derating of the AI story. Bought SK Hynix in the international portfolio as an AI-unwind/memory-recovery play alongside TSMC, citing high-bandwidth memory's high value-add. Final trade: Monolithic Power (SLB?) — actually final trade was SLB, playing an energy-exploration theme tied to the Valaris merger update next week.
- **Stephanie Link**: Called Amazon's quarter "spectacular" — acceleration across AWS, retail and advertising, record 13.7% operating margin, AWS backlog of $496B plus $225B in Trainium commitments; sees AWS growth of at least 25% for five years even if backlogs are cut in half. Raised 2026 industry capex estimate to $900–950B, heading toward $1.6 trillion next year. Bought more Starbucks on an operating-margin inflection thesis — North America comps of 8.1%, operating margin of 14.4% (beat by 240bps), targeting a return toward the 20.4% 2021 peak and ~$6 EPS power. Still holds NVIDIA/Broadcom over Micron for memory exposure. Highlighted Vertiv (referred to as "verdict" in transcript) as a buy-the-dip after its 17% post-earnings drop despite raised guidance and backlog growing from $15B to $20B — calls it a "conversion problem, not a demand problem." Final trade: Target, trading at 17x earnings with comps at 5.6% and traffic up 4.5%, calling the turnaround "definitely working."
- **Rate debate segment (Oliver Renick, Cboe)**: Options flow has flipped bearish on bonds — 10-year yield at highest since Jan 2025, TLT puts (75-strike, June 2027 expiry) dominating volume, implying another ~10% downside in the ETF; LQD saw 120,000 of 121,000 contracts traded in puts.
- **NXP/Ambarella news alert (Christina Partsinevelos)**: FT reports NXP Semiconductors in talks to acquire Ambarella (~$4B market cap), sending Ambarella shares up 18%; deal would bolster NXP's auto-chip/camera-AI compute platform.
- **Final Trades**: Stephanie Link — Target; Rob Sechan — SLB; (Jim Lebenthal and Amy Raskin's final trades were not explicitly restated in the closing segment, having been covered earlier as eBay and EOG respectively).
