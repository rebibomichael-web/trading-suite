**Is the AI Trade in Trouble? 11/5/26**

**Overview:** The Nasdaq is in a full-blown correction, with semis and AI infrastructure names getting crushed. The worst day for the chip group since March 2020 follows the midterm results and fears of a data center backlash. The panel debated whether the AI build-out has peaked, whether the bull market can continue without semis, and what this means for the coming Anthropic IPO.

- **Scott Wapner (framing):** The Nasdaq was down about 4.5-5% and the semis ETF down about 10%, its worst day since March 2020. From highs, Micron is down 45%, Marvell 57%, Lam 55%, Qualcomm 42%, and Vistra 42.5%. Bespoke noted that semis have fallen this fast in five days only in major crises. Nvidia fell 8.5%, AMD almost 15%, Micron 14%, Marvell 14%, CrowdStrike almost 10%, Palo Alto 9% and Oracle almost 8%. Dan Ives now sees about 10% of data center plans not happening and capex peaking in 2027 instead of 2028-29. Brad Gerstner said AI infrastructure drives about 75% of GDP growth, and a pullback could cost at least 50bps of growth next year.
- **Josh Brown:** He blamed leverage and hot money in AI hardware. Most of these stocks peaked in July, and forced selling is now taking over. He said it is not a market-wide event: Berkshire is at a record high, staples are flat and energy is green. He and Rob had urged rebalancing since October, since a risk-free rate above 4.5% means there is no need for 80% US stocks. Nvidia at 14x forward earnings shows the market never believed the 2028-29 numbers. He expects people to buy SpaceX if the dust settles. For his Best Stocks in the Market pick, he chose **MSCI**. It sold off in the "SaaS apocalypse," then rallied hard after its Oct 20 quarter. AI agents use its data, and it sells index licenses to ETF providers. He wants it to pull back and come to you, with a weekly close below $600 as the line in the sand. He added that Okta already stopped out about 10 days after they discussed it, so exits matter.
- **Malcolm Etheridge:** He is skeptical of the "rotation" optimism. About 40% of the S&P is mega-cap tech, and about 75% of GDP growth is tied to hyperscaler spending. Earnings growth rests on OpenAI and Anthropic compute promises and on Nvidia, Broadcom and Micron. He argued hyperscalers like Microsoft may use this window to walk back capex. He **bought Qualcomm** (QCOM) after earnings, with the stock down about 20% in a month and 40% from its 52-week high. He called it a "show me" story. Non-handset revenue is guided for about 60% growth, and automotive grew about 89% year over year. The stock trades at 12.5x forward earnings with a good balance sheet and a dividend. The SpaceX/Starlink revenue doesn't show up until 2028. He **sold American Express** (bought April 2 at $295). Despite card member spending up 8%, revenue up 9% and an earnings beat, the stock sold off, and he doesn't want consumer exposure. He cited Wells Fargo's "we're buyers" call on AmEx as he sold.
- **Sirat (Seti):** He argued that the AI build-out got harder but demand is not gone. Azure is growing 48%, and it is capacity-constrained rather than demand-constrained, so existing compute becomes more valuable. Incumbents benefit while those without capital access, like CoreWeave (at new lows), suffer. Nvidia reports Nov 17. He said the market can still rise without semis, with healthcare as the obvious winner, and his portfolio is 18% healthcare. He **bought Medline** (10% organic growth, 30% margins) and **sold UnitedHealth** (up over 20% this year, limited upside).
- **Rob Sechan:** He said the IC had urged taking allocations back in October, with hedging and fixed income now working. He noted that energy is only 2% of the S&P and has still rallied, and that healthcare's rally began months ago. Anecdotally, he said Uber's CEO told investors that one reason private capital backs autonomous fleets is that it is "not a data center." He also pointed to the UPS pivot away from Amazon as an example of a company reinventing itself, as a parallel to Qualcomm. On SpaceX, he said that despite the market's reaction to the election, it is a political story. SpaceX has gone from 93x sales to 53x, and the lockup unwind could bring tax-loss selling, so timing matters. Musk's political stance may put him in the crosshairs, with about $500B of combined SpaceX and Tesla market cap lost since the election.
- **Cristiano Amon (Qualcomm CEO, guest):** The stock is down 11% on the day. He said the handset worries are familiar, and the company is a new entrant in data centers. Its targets are $5B in revenue in fiscal 2027 and $15B in fiscal 2029. He said the $5B is "all supply bound," not demand-bound, and could be higher with more supply. The focus is low-power inference and decode, with 0.3 pJ/bit, better than the advertised 0.5. He said Starlink's nationwide terrestrial network is one of the largest build-outs in wireless and will show up in revenue as a fiscal 2028 story.
- **Kate Rooney (CNBC):** The Democrats swept both chambers and won the Texas governor's mansion, which is a data-center backlash risk for the AI labs. OpenAI released a policy blueprint with community commitments, including more disclosure and scrapping NDAs. Anthropic's S-1 flagged tougher regulation as a risk. The Anthropic IPO is expected in a couple of weeks. An investor said a $1.5T valuation may be a stretch, versus $2T talk a month ago. Gerstner expects it to price at the low end of its range, and he plans to be a big investor.
- **Shake Shack (Rob's earnings call):** Q3 revenue was up 15.2% to $423M, same-shack sales rose 2.5%, and it has had 23 straight quarters of positive comps. Guidance was good, but the stock was hit anyway because of the day.
