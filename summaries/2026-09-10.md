Protecting Your Portfolio Against Rising Oil Prices and Yields — 9/10/26

**Overview:** Oil above $100 and the 10-year yield near 4.90% pressured stocks and home builders, with a hotter PPI report raising fears of sticky core PCE inflation. The committee debated whether September seasonality and rate/oil anxiety are creating buying opportunities (Apple, Netflix) even as they praised Apple's new iPhone/foldable lineup and Restaurant Brands' technical breakout setup.

- **Stephanie Link:** Sees September volatility as an opportunity to "pick your spots"; stays bullish into Q4 on strong bank commentary (Wells Fargo, BofA CEOs), tight credit-default swap spreads, and resilient consumer spending (Amex +11% U.S. spend, Capital One volumes +26%). Sold her Apple position — won't add at 33-36x forward PE despite liking the new products, citing margin-compression risk from rising memory costs.
- **Malcolm Etheridge:** Cites Citadel's Scott Rubner (highs in for the month, weakness starting mid-September) vs. Goldman's view that sentiment may be too cautious; calls Apple the top dislocated opportunity, arguing the new iPhone/foldable lineup (with on-device AI infrastructure) is an underappreciated super-cycle catalyst, aided by Apple's lease program to soften pricing.
- **Bill Baruch:** Downplays PPI/PCE hawkishness, thinks a Fed rate hike next week is overpriced (~70% probability too high), sees a "Kansas City shuffle" from Fed governor Warsh; bullish on industrials (overweight) and selectively on consumer discretionary, cites Challenger Gray layoffs down 41% YTD and JOLTS up 3% YTD as labor-market positives.
- **Josh Brown:** Bullish on the new Apple lineup, says the A20 chip enables real on-device AI/agentic workflows and pushes back on claims Apple lacks an AI story; argues Apple's 34x PE reflects earnings stability, not just growth, and that Apple/Google are uniquely positioned to monetize AI via their ecosystems regardless of which LLM wins. Also touched on Uber (praised CEO Dara Khosrowshahi's ~141,000-share personal stock purchase and new autonomous-taxi partnership in Spain with WeRide) and Service Titan (stayed with the stock despite a brutal ~50% round-trip move this week, calling the AI-platform transition to "Max" the "right thing" long-term despite a near-term EBITDA/subscription-revenue hit). Sold Marriott and Citizens Financial (CFG), explaining both were trades, not investments, and he moves stops up to protect gains rather than let winners turn into losers.
- **Best Stocks in the Market — Josh Brown on Restaurant Brands International (QSR):** Bought the stock after flagging it since April at $75; cites 8%+ U.S. comps (best in QSR), passing Wendy's as #2 burger brand, 21 straight quarters of positive international comps, a beat-and-raise quarter ($1.07 EPS, dividend +5% to its 11th straight raise), and Bill Ackman's Pershing Square adding 14% to its stake. Technical setup: cup-and-handle pattern with $82 resistance — wants a weekly close above $82 to confirm breakout, plans to buy the breakout with a stop near $73 support.
- **Starbucks discussion (Steph/Josh):** Two years into Brian Niccol's turnaround, Steph sees the "real" earnings power story beginning — comps guided to 5% next year, ~4% in 2028, margins expanding from 11.7% toward ~15%, implying ~$6/share earnings power. Josh notes partner turnover at a record low (under 50%) and global comps swinging from -7% to +8% under Niccol.
- **Apple bear case (Steph citing Laura Martin/B of A):** Laura Martin (hold rating) flagged iPhone revenue concentration (>53%), margin risk from memory costs, reliance on Google's Gemini for AI, slow organizational velocity, and capital-allocation questions; B of A's Mellius called the foldable "Duo" a potential hit but trimmed price target to $370 from $380 on margin concerns.
- **Meta:** Highlighted as the only mega-cap up on the week (~7%) after a JPMorgan upgrade to overweight with price target raised to $820 from $640, following strong reception of the new "Muse" AI agent; Bill Baruch (owns the stock) says Meta's compute buildout could become a 2027 monetization story if it rents out excess capacity.
- **NFL/Netflix segment (Alex Sherman in Melbourne):** Netflix streaming the Rams-49ers game live from Australia; NFL commissioner Roger Goodell hinted at potentially repackaging/reselling media rights when the current deals' opt-out arrives (as soon as after the 2029-30 season). Netflix content chief Bella Bajaria described the strategy of turning select games into "eventized," zeitgeist moments.
- **Netflix trade discussion (Malcolm):** Stock down ~18% YTD and 7% over three months; Malcolm thinks the market isn't crediting Netflix's push into live events/podcasts, sees it as a way to monetize its existing subscriber base rather than a pure subscriber-growth story.
- **Mike Santoli's Market Memo:** Notes the S&P cracked its 7,600 support level from a five-week trading range; flags yields as overbought and the equal-weight S&P as oversold/outperforming; energy stocks (equal-weight, -2.3% week-to-date) aren't benefiting further from rising crude, suggesting the market may be sniffing out a peak in oil.
- **Final Trades:** Josh Brown — Snowflake (rally not over) and Tempest AI (new small healthcare/AI starter position); Joe Terranova — Nvidia (cheap at ~16-18x, continuing to buy).
