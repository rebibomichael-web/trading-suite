**How to Trade the Market Resilience: The Investment Committee Shares Their Strategy — August 14, 2026**

**Overview:** Stocks hovered near record highs as the committee digested a cooler CPI/PPI but soft retail sales, largely dismissing the miss as an Amazon Prime Day timing quirk rather than consumer weakness. The bigger debate was AI infrastructure spending — last month's semi sell-off was reframed as deleveraging (notably Korean margin calls) rather than "peak spending," with hyperscalers raising 2027 capex guidance reinforcing the bullish case, alongside fresh moves in energy/refiners, industrials, and materials.

- **Jim Lebenthal:** Says the Fed likely stays on hold through September (Jackson Hole, more CPI/PPI ahead); retail sales miss is an Amazon Prime Day pull-forward, not consumer weakness. Flat on Micron after averaging in post-earnings. Holds ExxonMobil as his energy pick (2x S&P annualized returns over 5 years), plus refiners' crack-spread strength. Adding to Cisco after the "price for perfection" pullback (holder since 2015, low beta/market-beating returns). Sticking with Disney despite frustration — streaming just turned profitable. Owns materials plays CRH (cement/aggregates tied to data-center buildout) and Cleveland Cliffs ("coming out of the penalty box"). Final trade: Lockheed Martin.
- **Kevin Simpson:** Sees the Fed on hold, equal-weight S&P and earnings strength as bullish setup. Holding Microsoft (added in the 360s-370s after the Feb selloff) — now a hold, writing calls given the run to ~$500. Adding to Caterpillar on the recent AI-trade-linked pullback; will write covered calls if volatility spikes but calls it a long-term holding, not a trim candidate. Active week harvesting call premium on Micron, SanDisk, Intel. Added to Goldman Sachs on a ~10% pullback (~$21 EPS, 23.5% ROE, raised dividend). Getting more bullish on Netflix after multiple compression (40x to ~21x forward) if content/pricing/advertising improve. Bought CF Industries (11x earnings, cheap domestic natural gas advantage, 20% dividend hike). Final trade: Visa ("toll road for global commerce"), near new highs.
- **Steve Weiss:** Flags a bifurcated economy — wealthy investors fine, working-class consumers pressured by inflation/oil. Sold Caterpillar and Micron at higher levels (thinks Caterpillar overvalued). Bought Meta on a dip near $535, added to Nvidia last week (undecided whether to hold through earnings). Bought Leidos on its post-earnings quarter, likes its cyber/AI-vulnerability business. Bought Valero on the refiner-capacity story (tip from Joe Terranova) and Cisco after analysts overreacted to a slight margin miss. Also holds Chevron and Marathon Petroleum. Final trade: UnitedHealth, calling the recent dip unwarranted with turnaround intact.
- **Bill Baruch (phone):** Added to Nvidia ahead of earnings (moved from ~7.25% to 8.25% portfolio weight), citing the $500B Nvidia financing deal, 20x forward multiple against 100%+ earnings growth, and technical setup near highs. Also added to Oracle (18x forward), calling it the premier "Neocloud" — thinks resistance is at $155-165. Bullish on Meta as a "2027 compute story" and likes smaller compute plays Nebius and CoreWeave.
- **Featured segment — Bezos/Liverpool (Alex Sherman):** Fenway Sports Group is selling a minority stake in Liverpool FC at a $7.1B valuation to the "1892 Holdings" consortium, with Jeff Bezos contributing over $1B; contract language could let the group take controlling ownership within 12 months at ~$8B.
- **Featured segment — Disney/D23 (Julia Boston interviews CEO Josh D'Amaro):** D'Amaro says he inherited a strong business from Bob Iger and wants more speed/urgency; plans to grow streaming and parks to turn around the stock, is launching a free ad-supported Disney+ tier, and does not want to spin off ESPN or ABC. Says calls about AI partnerships have picked up since the OpenAI/Sora deal ended.
- **Featured segment — Mike Santoli's Midday Word:** Calls the rally a "subtraction of all fears" — AI capex fears, a feared semis/memory bubble burst, and Fed-tightening fears have all eased, pushing the S&P to marginal new highs; cautions the market may be overcrediting an uninterrupted earnings trajectory.
- **Final Trades:** Steve Weiss — UnitedHealth; Kevin Simpson — Visa; Jim Lebenthal — Lockheed Martin.
