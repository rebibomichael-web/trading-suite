**The AI Race Deepens as Access to Capital Shrinks: Your Next Move, 10/22/26**

**Overview:** The Nasdaq fell about 2 to 2.5% (more than 500 to 660 points) as AI names sold off, led by Meta, while the Dow held up better. Staples, healthcare and utilities were the best sectors, a defensive tone. The panel's main debate was whether capital is becoming scarce for AI spending: Meta's cash-flow-negative guidance, CoreWeave and neocloud funding worries, and Lam Research and other semis selling off.

- **Market setup (Scott Wapner):**
  - Nasdaq down about 2 to 2.5%.
  - Staples, healthcare and utilities were the top sectors, and Procter & Gamble had its best day in 6.5 years.
  - Meta's selloff (lows down about 7.5%) reflects a raised midpoint of the spending range, no 2027 spending guidance, negative free cash flow, expenses growing faster than sales, and lower operating margins. The Street doesn't expect a return on the spending until about 2029.
  - Other AI-linked names also fell: SpaceX about 7%, AMD, Micron, Hynix, Marvell, and the power names (one down 11%, another almost 10%).
  - Wapner noted Meta moved its earnings call up a week and asked whether a debt or equity raise is coming.
- **Rob Sechan:**
  - Owns Meta and isn't worried short term. He says the core business is "firing on all cylinders" and AI monetization is proven.
  - He sees the new Meta share class as a way to keep control while financing growth.
  - He calls negative free cash flow next year "uncharted waters" and says the AI or superintelligence trade underlies the whole economy.
- **Josh Brown:**
  - Meta's filing said it wanted to "secure capital before competitors saturate various options." It cited recent raises at Alphabet, SpaceX, Anthropic and OpenAI.
  - Capital will be scarce, "but not for Meta." Demand for AI doesn't care which data center runs the workloads, so the question is who gets the economics: the builder with the lowest cost and fastest buildout. He named SpaceX as likely best, with Oracle and Google Cloud as open questions.
  - Meta could "play the card" of entering the cloud compute business, and he thinks the stock would jump on that news.
  - Anthropic's revised S-1 shows hundreds of billions of dollars in compute commitments. SpaceX must keep delivering and the Anthropic IPO must open well to calm the AI complex.
  - Spotify: added about 30% to his position after 306M paid subs (a beat). Revenue growth is guided at 14%, gross margin 33%, monthly active users 788M (+11%), with guidance of 818M MAUs and 316M premium subs. He thinks it is breaking away from Netflix.
  - Best Stocks in the Market: IQVIA (IQV), a roughly $25–26B company that has run clinical trials since 1954 and holds a major private healthcare data set. It was hit in the "SaaS apocalypse." Adjusted EPS rose 12% and R&D bookings rose nearly 20%, which he sees as a sign it will be the go-to for AI drug discovery. The chart shows higher highs and higher lows, and the stock is up from about $155 in the spring. Levels are on CNBC Pro.
- **Malcolm Etheridge (attribution inferred from context, not stated in the transcript):**
  - Adding to Cerebras, his most speculative holding. It fell from about $180 on Oct. 1 to about $110, down 37% this month and 71% from its high.
  - The risk is that revenue is tied to OpenAI. His thesis is that its low-latency chips suit agentic cybersecurity (CrowdStrike is its major partner), and more cyber partners could turn the stock. It reports at the end of the month.
- **Jim Lebenthal / Stephanie Link-style commentary (speakers not clearly identified):**
  - Semis: Lam Research (LRCX) fell about 9–10% on a guide only slightly ahead of expectations. Demand is robust but supply is the constraint, which is a better problem than weak demand (companies that missed on demand fell 20% or more). Applied Materials and KLA fell in sympathy, and DRAM names are about 40% off highs. Revenue was a record and EPS grew 74%.
  - The panelist said the selloff is macro and risk-off, not a reason to dump the investments.
  - Zero tolerance for hyperscalers going cash-flow negative, which could be what's hitting Meta.
- **Other individual-stock comments (speakers unattributed):**
  - IBM: beat and raised, with six target raises. The stock is still scarred by a roughly 25% one-day drop last quarter. One panelist owns it, bought around 12x earnings, and is "being patient." Another sold in July.
  - One panelist noted CFOs are reining in AI and token spending (JPMorgan was cited) and moving to cheaper models. This raises questions about hyperscaler returns and weighs on neoclouds like Nebius and CoreWeave.
  - Netflix: downgraded to Peer Perform at Wolfe. One panelist sees it as a likely tax-loss-sale candidate, and another has defended it but is bothered by declining ad sales.
  - CrowdStrike: Mizuho target raised to $275 from $250. The panelist called it "the best stock in the world" and expects more cyber use as AI spending grows. CEO George Kurtz is on CNBC tomorrow at 11.
  - Lockheed Martin: raised its annual forecast. Called a good way to play global tensions, not expensive, and F-35 issues are rectified.
  - Union Pacific: higher profit and revenue on volume growth. Called an oligopoly with pricing power, though sensitive to economic slowdowns.
  - Alibaba (BABA): still held, bought in December 2023 and up about 50%. It suffers from the same capital-intensity concerns, but valuation is more reasonable.
- **Healthcare discussion:** One panelist is massively overweight healthcare (AbbVie, Amgen, J&J, Lilly and others) and sees AI as a forward benefit. Another is warming to the space as a place where AI deployment shows up (drug discovery, faster time to market).
- **Mike Santoli's Market Memo:**
  - The 10-year yield broke below 5% for the first time in a month, and advisors and ETF flows have been buying fixed income at those yields.
  - AI capex overheating helped push yields up, and the market is tightening financial conditions itself.
  - There isn't literally a scarcity of capital, but the market is pulling back on its enthusiasm for open-ended spending and wants a path back to positive free cash flow.
  - Market breadth is "bad breath," with defensive leadership. The S&P held its uptrend from the July low.
- **Options Action (Oliver Renick):**
  - The KWEB and EWZ call buyers are buying the dip. KWEB had 64,000 calls versus 10,000 puts, at twice its 30-day average volume.
  - EWZ (Brazil) had over 1 million contracts, about 80% calls, eight times the 30-day average. The popular strikes were 43, 46 and 48 for Dec. 18, and the 48 needs a rally of about 17%.
- **Late-show discussion:**
  - Meta firmed up its capex guidance without raising it, which is the first time in about 12 quarters, but it was a no-win situation. Raising it would have meant more debt.
  - The "haves vs. have-nots" idea: Meta is not CoreWeave, and CoreWeave's debt terms are much shorter.
  - Banks were mostly red (JPMorgan, Goldman down about 1.7%, Citi, Morgan Stanley), with Bank of America and Wells Fargo slightly up.
  - American Express reports tomorrow, with about $20B in revenue expected (+9%). Capital One's report was strong.
  - Robinhood fell about 5.5% and Coinbase almost 5%.
- **News alerts:**
  - Skydance fell about 3% on a Semafor report that Elon Musk invested about $1B.
  - A judge ordered Trump to sit for a deposition in his $10B BBC defamation suit before Dec. 11.
  - Quest Diagnostics disclosed a cyberattack in which patient records were accessed.
- **Final Trades:** The transcript doesn't clearly attribute these. The picks named were Zscaler, ServiceNow and Spotify ("beast mode").
