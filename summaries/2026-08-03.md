**The Post-Tech Earnings Rotation: Your Next Move — August 3, 2026**

**Overview:** The Investment Committee dug into what tech earnings season really means for the market's momentum trade, with Microsoft and Amazon crowned the big winners and Apple and Meta the relative disappointments. Debate centered on whether Microsoft's fundamentals now justify a "buy," whether Apple is "done" for a quarter, and whether beaten-down Oracle offers real value or just more risk — alongside a look at energy's dominant year, cybersecurity's comeback, and SpaceX's lockup-and-earnings week.

- **Joe Terranova**: His ETF's quarterly rebalance forced sales of Meta, Microsoft, Oracle, Palantir, and Synopsys as momentum rotated away from Mag7/software — a rules-based outcome he personally regrets on Microsoft given its "best cloud growth in four years." Said he's weighing pulling the rebalance date to the third week of August to capture earnings. Framed the hyperscaler takeaway as "A+B=C": strong ROI (Amazon recoups data-center spend in <3 years) plus continued capex commitment ($750B this year, ~$1.2T next) equals investors rewarding the group — but flagged debt/financing as the wildcard to watch. Personally reestablished CrowdStrike and added to Palo Alto Networks and Fortinet as cyber momentum returned. Added Exxon and other energy names; sees momentum shifting from AI/software into "hard assets" like energy.
- **Jim Lebenthal**: Called Microsoft the classic "show me" stock that finally answered the monetization question with margin and cloud growth — an "embedded, hard to displace" name he's adding to on weakness. Keeps Meta on a watch list (not selling, but no cloud lever like peers). Stuck with Oracle despite the ~$636B backlog's OpenAI-funding risk and 200bp five-year CDS, seeing a 17.3x forward PE with room for a new all-time high within 12 months if hyperscaler ROI proves out; sized appropriately, hasn't added in six months. Took energy to a 10% portfolio weighting, favoring the Valero refiner trade — praised for calling that early, noting Exxon is also a major (underappreciated) refiner.
- **Stephanie Link**: Sold half her Apple position pre-earnings, calling it "priced for perfection" (mid-30s valuation vs. Microsoft's mid-20s, which she doubled down on in February) — Apple is "done"/dead money for the next quarter, though she says it eventually reaches $400. Trimming energy exposure after a big run, keeping a smaller position as a portfolio hedge rather than a growth bet.
- **Steve Weiss**: Sized up Meta considerably into the earnings dip (~11x EBITDA, 17 PE — cheap, and trusts Zuckerberg to pull back AI spend if it's not working) and bought Microsoft on the post-earnings pop; added to Alphabet on cloud-demand strength; bought a small, "ill-timed" Apple trading position ahead of new Siri/iPhones (Sept. 18). Pushed back hard on Jim's Oracle thesis, arguing its forward PE is already above Meta's and near Microsoft's, seeing no clear valuation edge. Also likes energy for the ongoing need to refill strategic petroleum reserves.
- **A+B=C hyperscaler framework** (Joe Terranova): Wells Fargo sees hyperscalers proving ROI, BofA sees capex appetite staying strong, and Deutsche Bank/BofA flow data show tech pulling in $15.7B of inflows last week — net result, investors are rewarding the group, with financing/debt issuance the risk to monitor.
- **Kate Rooney**: Reported Anthropic will attend a White House AI-industry meeting this week to discuss the June executive order framework; Meta and Google expected to be represented too, following Sam Altman's and Jensen Huang's visit last week.
- **Morgan Brennan**: Previewed SpaceX's first lockup expiration (Thursday, 911M shares eligible) and first earnings as a public company (Wednesday after the bell) — public float could triple by September and reach 6.4B shares over time; short interest is 32% of tradable float; options imply a ~14-15% post-earnings move; watch Starlink subscriber growth, CapEx, and Starship guidance.
- **Phil LeBeau**: Reported the FAA has certified Boeing's 737 MAX-7, clearing deliveries of the ~20 in inventory and setting up the more important MAX-10 certification expected by year-end.
- **ETF Edge (Dom Chu with Todd Rosenbluth, TMX VettaFi)**: Small caps are outperforming large caps by 1,000+ bps this year on broadening strength (industrials, energy, financials); highlighted the Victory Shares Small Cap Free Cash Flow ETF (SFLO) as outperforming on quality/free-cash-flow screens.
- **Options Action (Oliver Renick)**: SpaceX options flow leans bullish by volume (53k calls vs. 38k puts) but big put activity near the money; bears target the 95-strike put (needs a 17% drop), bulls the 130-strike call (needs a 23% rally); heavy sellers unwound a prior 330-strike call bet that needed the stock to triple.
- **Energy sector desk debate**: Best-performing sector YTD, up 32% (12.5% in July). Jim and Steve Weiss are bullish (SPR refill demand, refiner exposure), Joe is trimming his outsized personal energy weighting after a strong run while keeping the ETF's energy holdings, and Stephanie Link is also taking money off the table, viewing further upside as contingent on either a stronger economy or worsening geopolitics.
- **Final Trades**: Steve Weiss — Dick's Sporting Goods (adding on the decline); other panelists closed with Apollo Global, Steel Dynamics, and a third name heard as "Transnite" (unclear exact ticker in the transcript).
