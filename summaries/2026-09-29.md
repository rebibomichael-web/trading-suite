**Halftime Report: Sam Altman Live at OpenAI Dev Day — 9/29/26**

**Overview:** Sam Altman spoke live with CNBC's Kate Rooney ahead of OpenAI's developer keynote. He covered AI safety, a scrapped model release, and why he is in no hurry to IPO. The panel weighed regulation and safety against the AI trade. They also covered Anthropic's S-1, Nvidia, Meta, and Micron's earnings due tomorrow. The 10-year yield was at 5.28%, and the 30-year was at its highest since 2002.

**Bullets:**

- **Sam Altman (OpenAI CEO, interview with Kate Rooney):**
  - OpenAI shelved a new model over safety concerns. He called it "abundance of caution": the model was slightly worse on a few evals, with "not a big scary thing."
  - He said "pacing doesn't always mean slowing." Alignment, safety, monitoring and security "have to stay way ahead of capabilities."
  - On the Hugging Face incident, he said nothing else as serious is known. He said Jensen Huang's safety software is good but "not a full solution," and that AI safety is also a science problem, not only an engineering one.
  - He expects a "multi-level" liability framework, and used an auto-industry analogy.
  - He promised 20+ announcements at Dev Day, including new models, cost and developer tools. He said no open-source model beats OpenAI on cost-price comparison.
  - He said he is not threatened by Zuckerberg's Muse ("No"). On the Jony Ive hardware, he said "I'm not worried about being first," and quality will take a while.
  - On the IPO, he has "no particular timeline." He said "barreling forwards with an IPO while things feel so in flux" isn't the priority, and it's "safety and mission first."
  - He called the movie trailer about him "an awful movie," but said the mockery doesn't bother him.
- **Kate Rooney (CNBC):**
  - Labs are self-policing because Washington is doing little. She sees no Section 230-style blanket protection, and enterprises demand trust.
  - Altman is playing to several audiences: the public, the administration, and enterprise customers.
- **Aiman Javers (CNBC, White House):**
  - Tech CEOs arrived for a luncheon with President Trump, including Dario Amodei, Satya Nadella, Zuckerberg, Bezos and Musk.
  - Alex Karp of Palantir was confrontational and said AI "has to work for the warfighter, has to work for enterprises."
  - Jensen Huang sat next to Trump, with Musk on the other side. Huang is the "go fast" voice, and Treasury Secretary Bessent says Trump is in lockstep with him.
- **Joe Terranova:**
  - The market is treating AI as a "security blanket." He expects the coming earnings to match the last five or six quarters, and cybersecurity is his top investment off the theme.
  - He doesn't expect guardrail legislation after the midterms.
  - His trade-risk worry is that circular financing for OpenAI could hit chip demand if capital is redirected to security.
  - He noted Altman's IPO hesitancy versus Dario Amodei's willingness. Anthropic's filing says "catastrophic or existential risk to humanity."
  - He also defended Nvidia's move to insurers as logical.
  - On Micron, he expects historic revenue and margins in the mid-80s. He is unsure how the stock will react, but he needs the semi-cap equipment names to rebound on strong capex commentary. Micron is up about 14% in a month and still below its June high.
- **Stephanie Link:**
  - Hyperscalers are spending about $1 trillion this year and will keep going. They have not explained the growth, jobs and tax benefits of data centers.
  - She noted 16 states have paused some data center projects. She thinks regulation would extend the cycle.
  - On the Anthropic IPO she is hesitant: a quarter of revenue comes from two customers, and many large clients aren't locked into long-term contracts. She likes the $518B in cloud and infrastructure commitments and prefers to "own the food chain."
  - She still likes SpaceX, which is down 26% from its highs, and said "a lot of ways to win."
  - On Micron, she expects DRAM ASPs up 60% and NAND up 80% last quarter, plus $100B in contracts through 2030. She said the stock isn't expensive and she would buy a pullback. She cited $40 in earnings power and about 7x forward earnings.
- **Jason Snipe:**
  - He is bullish going into earnings, which start in two weeks: a third straight quarter of about 25% earnings growth.
  - Only 12% of S&P stocks are above their 50-day and 24% above their 200-day. The 10-year is up about 50 bps in 30 days.
  - He would be interested in the Anthropic IPO, comparing it to early Nvidia.
  - On Micron, he acknowledged the memory business is a cyclical stock, but said a structural shift is happening.
  - On Netflix, the stock is down about 25% this year. He is focused on its AI-driven ad targeting, which he sees as accretive long-term, and on live sports, which are expensive content.
- **Josh Brown:**
  - Private-market money is rewarding Anthropic's heavy spending. The S-1 shows a $42B loss in 2025.
  - He said Altman is clearly getting media training.
  - The utilities ETF (XLU) is in a big drawdown, which he reads as investors pricing in tougher sledding for politically unpopular data center projects.
  - **Nvidia:** he called a breakout above $235, the April high, the most important chart. It has been seven months in the making, and a break would change the narrative.
  - He defended Nvidia's approach to insurers over neocloud loan risk. He cited $96B in free cash flow this year and possibly $375B next year on about $680B in revenue, and a $230B buyback authorization.
  - **Best Stocks in the Market:** Meta made the list for the first time, on technicals. He said "I'm not telling you to buy the stock here" (about $750). Risk management is at $685, the April and July ceiling. He wants the RSI to cool toward 60, and the 200-day is $626.
  - **Netflix:** his average cost is in the low $80s. He admitted he has been wrong on it and called it "dead money" for now. He would almost rather buy above $80 than below $70. Deutsche upgraded it to Buy but cut its target to $95 from $100.
  - He also referenced the Disney job cuts and their ongoing Netflix-vs-Disney bet.
- **Mega-cap moves (last two months):** Microsoft is up 30%, Meta up 23.5%, and Nvidia up 21%.

  - **Stephanie Link:** SpaceX.
  - **Jason Snipe:** ADBE (the transcript reads "add V").
  - **Josh Brown:** AMD, a holding in the Reformed Broker portfolio and another Best Stock pick.
