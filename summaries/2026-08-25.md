**AI Trade in Focus with Nvidia Looming — 8/25/26**

**Overview:** The committee previewed Wednesday's Nvidia earnings, calling it the most-watched report of the season even though Nvidia's own post-earnings stock reaction has historically been muted — the real stakes are the guidance implications for the entire AI/semi/data-center trade and the growing data-center political backlash. They also covered Marvell/Broadcom ahead of Marvell's Thursday report, rising Treasury yields pressuring hyperscaler debt issuance, several portfolio moves, and a rough day for Dick's Sporting Goods on a Foot Locker-driven miss.

- **Josh Brown**: Says Nvidia is historically "not a great earnings stock" — average next-day move since Q1 2020 is only +2%, and flat if you strip out two outlier quarters (May 2023 +24%, Feb 2024 +16%); calls Jensen Huang the "AI Fed" whose guidance matters more to the broader market than to Nvidia's own stock. Opposes any Nvidia dividend (double taxation vs. buybacks); wants cash reinvested into robotics/automation/space. On Broadcom's lag: cites $91B debt load, rising competition from Marvell, and 30x forward valuation vs. cheaper Nvidia. On data-center backlash, compares it to cell-tower NIMBYism that ultimately got resolved via federal legislation. Best Stocks in the Market segment: highlighted **Merck** (up 30% YTD, all-time high ~154, breakaway gap off 137, resistance/support at 145/128) and **Amgen** (six growth drivers, raised guidance, pivot at 390-400).
- **Joe Terranova**: Flags the WSJ story on Nvidia potentially becoming "the banker to the AI boom" via vendor financing, calling it "dangerous ground" as pricing nears an elasticity ceiling. Wants Huang to address data-center backlash on the call (data-center construction now 2.5% of all U.S. construction, exceeding infrastructure). On Broadcom: says Marvell isn't yet taking share (Broadcom's Alphabet TPU deal runs through 2031) but sees credit/CDS risk as a real story. Bought more **XBI**, building since ~$101 last year, eyeing a break above the Feb 2021 intraday high of 174.79; prefers XBI over IBB to avoid diluting biotech exposure. Holds Amgen, Gilead, and Vertex as "three giants" in his ETF; sold Merck from the ETF on quality/valuation concerns despite personally liking it.
- **Stephanie Link**: Expects Nvidia to beat and raise, pushing back on the idea of a bigger buyback signaling slowing growth — says free cash flow should hit ~$200B this year, ~$300B next, with 50% already committed to shareholder returns. On Marvell (2nd-most-important report this week, Thursday): still holds a smaller position after trimming for profits, sees $10 in earnings power from optical plus custom chips, but prefers Nvidia and Broadcom on valuation/sentiment. Says data-center backlash is a 2-3 month noise issue, not a durable story; likes the power/infrastructure food chain (Quanta Services earnings growth ~70%, Vertiv +60% YTD, GE Vernova +43% YTD). Trimmed **Palo Alto Networks** (up ~145% since Feb) purely to take profits/rebalance, funding a new buy in **EQT** (cheap at 5.8x EV/EBITDA, largest U.S. natgas producer, Appalachia power-to-data-center thesis, potential $70 stock if gas prices firm into winter). On Dick's Sporting Goods: Foot Locker integration "a disaster" (SG&A up 65%, same-store sales -3.6% vs. expected -1%, margins down 200bps), core Dick's business fine (+4.9% comps); says 30% stock drop is overdone but recovery will take time — comparing it cautiously to her past Target experience.
- **Karen Firestone**: Bought **Vertex Pharmaceuticals** — cystic-fibrosis competitor Sienna's Phase 2 failure de-risked the category; Vertex does $13B/year in highly profitable CF drugs, plus a non-opioid pain drug, renal/autoimmune pipeline, and a "holy grail" insulin/diabetes drug; sees ~15% earnings growth over 10 years and potential for the stock to double at a 20x multiple (stock up 175% over 5 years, at record highs). On Amgen: notes AI-driven drug development benefits, highlights MariTide (GLP-1 diet drug) plus deep oncology/hematology/immunology franchise, and a strong BTK biotech index trend.
- **Nvidia/Marvell earnings preview segment**: Committee agreed Nvidia's Q3 guidance (bulls want $110B+ revenue) and margin trajectory (mid-70s gross margins) matter more than the print itself; framed the semis trade as "tired" and unlikely to be reignited broadly by Nvidia alone. Marvell targets cited: $300 (Rosenblatt), $265 (Susquehanna); stock up ~170% YTD.
- **Mike Santoli (Midday Word)**: Market is holding steady between the prior high and waiting on PCE data and Nvidia earnings; Nvidia hasn't historically been a huge market-moving catalyst; notes stocks like Micron swinging on no news while the broader tape stays controlled.
- **Final Trades**: Josh Brown — added to Delta; Karen Firestone — Thermo Fisher; Joe Terranova — (Interactive Brokers, per Steph's sign-off cue); Stephanie Link — Interactive Brokers.
