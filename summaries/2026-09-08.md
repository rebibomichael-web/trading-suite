**Trading Ahead of the Apple iPhone Event & the CPI Report — September 8, 2026**

**Overview:** Stocks are contending with rising oil (WTI near $94, Goldman flagging $120 potential), rising bond yields, and a looming CPI report/Fed decision, but the panel argued strong earnings momentum is outweighing those macro risks for now. Apple's iPhone event tomorrow and Oracle's earnings this week loom as the next catalysts, with healthcare stocks whipsawed by Novartis trial failures and Amgen selling off in sympathy.

- **Joe Terranova:** Says momentum (semis, optical, infrastructure) is rebounding after last week's washout; today's weakness is mostly Amgen (down ~9%, 5% of the Dow) dragging the index. Argues earnings strength outweighs the risks from oil, yields, and Fed uncertainty — a 25bp hike "would do literally nothing" for inflation but would further hurt the housing recession. Thinks oil stays elevated/keeps climbing into the election given the Goldman $120 call. On Apple, thinks the market is too quick to dismiss a foldable iPhone/iPad as a long-term traction story, even if the stock is a "sell the news" this week and September is seasonally weak. Likes Qualcomm's AWS data-center deal as a structural diversification move but won't chase it in September. Final trade: Cheniere Energy (LNG) — down on a technical factor, calls it an opportunity.
- **Jim Lebenthal:** Pushes back on the earnings-outweighs-everything view — flags diesel (not crude) at record highs as the real inflation risk feeding through supply chains, crop prices, and shipping, arguing there's a "no man's land" for stocks in September with no earnings catalyst until October. On Apple, believes the stock's malaise is about last quarter's earnings/valuation reset, not the Tim Cook succession, and doesn't expect the iPhone event itself to move the stock — trimmed Apple at $330 and isn't in a rush to re-add. On Amgen/Novartis news, notes GLP-1 drugs pulled big-cap money out of traditional pharma (Pfizer/Merck/J&J) into biotech (Amgen, Gilead, AbbVie), and floats that GLP-1 adoption may be structurally hurting quick-serve restaurant demand. Owns AbbVie (mid-teens multiple, ~3% yield, diversified via Skyrizi/Rinvoq) and Qualcomm (trimmed in half around $230; tempted to buy the AWS-deal pop but waiting out September). Final trade: Pinnacle Financial Partners (regional bank).
- **Brian Belski:** Bullish on the broad market, citing small-cap earnings/free-cash-flow strength and a "revival" of the Mag7 (matching, not needing to beat, the S&P) as valuations remain supportive — NVIDIA is his top pick within the group, ~19x forward earnings on 70% (supply-constrained) revenue growth guidance, within a hair of an all-time high; sees Microsoft's sentiment recovering off sub-$400 lows. Says small-caps rallying despite higher oil/yields reflects genuine earnings/valuation support, and expects strong Q4 revisions across financials (wealth management, deal flow) and communication services. Owns Oracle as a core tech-value holding, wants no further CapEx raise above $70B and 33% y/y top-line growth in Thursday's report as the key metric. Notes the "commodity trade" (copper, ag, gold) is one of the market's strongest right now, and likes Exxon Mobil as an integrated play on refining margins. Final trade: Freeport-McMoRan, looking for a move into the $80s.
- **Calls of the Day:** RBC initiates Shake Shack outperform, $89 PT — Belski calls Shake Shack "Chipotle for Gen Z" with margin help from kiosks/drive-thrus, rates McDonald's a steady value play and Starbucks a genuine operating turnaround under Brian Niccol; Lebenthal counters that GLP-1 drugs may be a hidden structural headwind across quick-serve/restaurant names. Airbnb upgraded to outperform, $200 PT (already on Josh Brown's Best Stocks list) — Terranova cites two raised revenue forecasts this year, best post-earnings stock reaction since 2020, resilient travel demand, and a World Cup tailwind.
- **Healthcare in focus:** Novartis shares near a record-worst day after its muscle-wasting drug (tied to the ~$12B Avidity acquisition) and a cholesterol drug both missed trial goals; Amgen fell in sympathy (~10%) on GLP-1/obesity-drug competition (BMO cut Amgen to Hold from Buy, PT $450). AstraZeneca posted strong COPD trial data, with its CEO telling CNBC the drug could reach $5B+ in peak sales. Goldman/Belski flagged J&J and Merck as best-positioned healthcare names into year-end, cautious on Pfizer.
- **Mike Santoli's Market Memo (new newsletter launch):** Watching the "QTOP" (top 30 Nasdaq 100 names) as a broader AI proxy — still ~4% below its record high and without a new high in three months — as the tell for whether mega-cap AI reasserts leadership; says oil and yields are this week's dominant story ahead of CPI.
- **Options Action (Oliver Renick, CBOE):** Mixed/two-way flow in USO and XLE despite oil's rally; bullish outlier is Exxon Mobil, with heavy buying of the $170 calls expiring Friday (needs a ~6% weekly rally), while Bloom Energy (BE, up 11%) saw explosive call buying — nearly $350M in options volume, more than SpaceX.
- **Final Trades:** Joe Terranova — Cheniere Energy; Jim Lebenthal — Pinnacle Financial Partners; Brian Belski — Freeport-McMoRan (target move into the 80s).
