**Halftime Report: NVIDIA Leadership Shakeup, AI Trade Fallout and OpenAI Valuation, 11/12/26**

Overview: Jensen Huang resigned as NVIDIA CEO after a report of a second alleged affair with a direct report. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO and Harvey Jones is the first board chairman. The panel debated whether to sell or hold NVIDIA and where the money is rotating (AMD, Broadcom, Apple, power names). The show also covered OpenAI's $15B raise at a $1.25T pre-money valuation and the coming Anthropic IPO. Frank Holland hosted in place of Scott Wapner.

**Breaking news and reporters**
- **Seema Modi:** NVIDIA's board met Wednesday and agreed on Huang's resignation that night. Kress is interim CEO, with a company-wide meeting Nov 18 and earnings less than a week away. Two hedge funds that own the stock praised Kress as pragmatic.
  - Later headlines: Huang agreed to testify before Congress before Thanksgiving. There is also a report of an investigation into whether he smuggled Super Micro products into China.
  - Trump posted on Truth Social that Tim Cook should take the NVIDIA job.
- **Kate Rooney:** OpenAI has raised $15B (up from $11B reported earlier) at a $1.25T pre-money valuation. The round is a bridge to a 2027 IPO.
  - OpenAI's Q3 revenue was about $10B, up nearly 50% quarter over quarter. Its operating loss was about $10.9B and its adjusted operating margin was -109%, improved from -150%.
  - Anthropic lists on the Nasdaq next week with about +15% adjusted margins, though those figures include adjustments.
- **Mike Santoli:** The market is finding places for the money leaving NVIDIA. Broadcom and AMD were up about 25% in three days off washed-out semiconductor levels.
  - The S&P has been stuck near 7,620 since early June, even though forward earnings are up about 12%.
  - He saw liquidity ebbing: gold, silver and Bitcoin were down 3%, and yields were up big.
- **Steve Liesman:** The Fed's October 2027 meeting will focus on strategy with no rate decision. There will be seven rate-setting meetings in 2027 and six in 2028.
- **Oliver Renick:** AMD was up 6%, about 10% below its record high, and about $50B of market cap has shown up there. Retail is buying far out-of-the-money calls (750 strike for February, 600 strike for tomorrow). Alphabet call flow is about 4x puts.

**Panelists**
- **Stephanie Link:** She sold NVIDIA yesterday after buying at about $204 on July 8. She said the fundamentals are strong, with $300B in free cash flow expected next year, but investigations and leadership questions mean a trading range for 6–12 months. She would buy it back once there's a great leader.
  - She added to Micron, which is down 35% from its high, trades at 5x earnings, and may announce a buyback at its early-December earnings. The CHIPS Act buyback lock-up ends Dec 9.
  - She still owns Broadcom (AI revenue above $100B, possible $20/share earnings power) and likes AMD.
  - She likes power and grid names (Vertiv, Quanta, GE Vernova, Vistra) with about $1T of grid spending through 2030, and thinks natural gas is the long-term winner.
  - She passed on Apple over valuation and slowing services growth.
  - She added to Meta last week, at 18x earnings with revenue growth of about 26%.
- **Malcolm Etheridge:** He sold NVIDIA after hours. He called it a "broken company, not a broken stock," because everything tied to NVIDIA went with Huang, and he raised national-security concerns.
  - He doesn't think Kress can fix it, since reporting says Huang overrode her.
  - He prefers diversifying through the ETF (SMH) and wants Qualcomm. He sees no real CUDA or Rubin replacement before 2027.
  - He calls Apple the safety trade since July, up about twice as much as the next-best Mag 7 name.
- **Josh Brown:** He is holding NVIDIA with a very low cost basis, bought in the 90s. The stock is down 23% in 10 days, a bottom-2% drawdown, with about $1T of market cap erased. He cited the HP/Mark Hurd (2010) and Intel/Brian Krzanich (2018) precedents, where the successor's choices mattered more than the scandal.
  - He called Apple "the trade of the year" and the "anti-AI large-cap tech trade."
  - He pointed to a hammer pattern on NVIDIA's daily chart.
  - He said NVIDIA price-target cuts, including Evercore at $186 and a Melius cut to $185, are unusually telling.
- **Bill Baruch:** He is holding NVIDIA (trimmed at $217 three weeks ago, now about $165) and says the selling has been flushed out. He sees a move back toward $190 if communication improves, and calls the hammer pattern constructive.
  - He owns Apple and Alphabet, whose TPU is a long-term story despite a revenue miss outside cloud. He likes Vertiv (stable above $200) and Vistra, and may add to Vertiv.
- **Group:** They agreed that OpenAI holding its valuation is good for public investors, and that the Anthropic IPO is likely priced at $1.1–1.3T with retail demand. They split on whether Tim Cook would want the NVIDIA job.

**Final Trades**
- **Bill Baruch:** Micron, about 4x earnings with significant buybacks coming. The transcript attributes this to Bill, but it may have been Stephanie's.
- **Malcolm Etheridge:** Digital Realty, "the least dirty sock in the data center hamper."
- **Stephanie Link:** Natera, down 26% from its highs after a beat-and-raise quarter, which she called an overreaction.
- **Josh Brown:** Moody's, which has joined his "best stocks in the market" list (written up at CNBC Pro).
