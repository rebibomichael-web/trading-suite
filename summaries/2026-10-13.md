**Halftime Report Live at the CAIS Summit — 10/13/26**

**Overview:** Live from the CAIS Summit in Beverly Hills, the panel debated Paul Tudor Jones's "1999 all over again" warning and an AI-driven, top-heavy market, with the 10-year yield at 5.22%. The hour also covered the boom in alternatives and private credit, and mixed bank earnings.

- **Paul Tudor Jones (clip):** Said 2027 will be "really challenging" and that he'll be short all year. He called the market a "two-speed market" like 1999, split between companies adopting AI and those that aren't.
- **Shannon Saccocia:** The picture is more complex than PTJ's split. The S&P is at all-time highs, but 39% of its members sit 20% below their highs, and so do 56% of the Russell 2000. AI-adoption questions are hitting "with full force" against rates. People are leaving the broadening-out trade because of rate and energy sensitivity, and earnings comparisons get tough next year. She said evidence of demand (power, chips) hasn't declined. She also noted that spreads were tight and covenants light before February's "SaaS-pocalypse," and that the reset has improved lending quality. Her advice was to diversify, for example into high yield, which has less AI exposure.
- **Rob Sechan:** The bifurcation is the way things are for obvious reasons, because that's where earnings growth, revisions and contracted revenue are. Mag 7 plus chips are about 44–45% of the market. The risk is a wobble in financing or capital-raising for the build-out. He thinks that's "not today's problem" but could be next year's. It would also hit energy and the consumer through the wealth effect. His view: "the stock market isn't the economy, but maybe now it actually is." Other companies get squeezed by higher rates, and he said the highest-conviction bet is still AI. He thinks it's not time to get off the train yet.
- **Josh Brown (remote):** Asked what happens to the S&P 493 if rates fall. Over 17 years, narrow leadership with weak internals has usually resolved with the rest of the market catching up, not with a crash. Possible catalysts are Middle East de-escalation and lower oil, a dovish Fed speaker, or something overseas. He said, "Let's not be so sure of how this definitely has to resolve." On the BofA fund manager survey, he noted 37% are overweight tech, the most since August 2020.
- **Joe Terranova (remote):** Two forces are pushing against each other: robust tech earnings against higher oil and yields. He said technology is the economy, and that the trillion-dollar companies (Apple, Meta, Microsoft) provide resilience. If yields stay high, the broadening trade doesn't come into play through 2027. He called the market "unhealthy but resilient." He cited the BlackRock downgrade: US stocks to neutral, short-term Treasuries upgraded.
- **Scott Wapner (framing):**
  - JPMorgan's Sembelist: 42 AI stocks account for 60–80% of S&P earnings growth, returns and capex since January 2024. 60% of S&P stocks are down 20% or more from their highs.
  - Goldman's Pascarella: tactical setups net positive, but a market at highs attracting "so little chase."
  - WSJ: single-stock options volume near $700B a day, close to 2021 meme-stock levels.
  - Upcoming: a live "AI in America" show from San Francisco on Thursday.
  - Closing Bell guests from the summit: Robert Smith (Equity Partner CEO), Matt Brown (CAIS CEO) and Drew McKnight (Fortress CEO).
- **Halftime Headliner – Glenn August (Oak Hill Advisors founder/CEO, $100B+ under management):**
  - AI build-out: calls it the biggest capex investment ever ($3T, $5T or $10T). He expects spreads to widen as capital needs grow. OHA is financing power generation, data center construction and chips. It recently did a turbine deal for a data center that was 2x over-collateralized, bought at a discount, with a targeted mid-teens return.
  - Risk: "I'm not sure they're all going to get built," given local opposition and capital constraints. He focuses on roughly five-year exposure backed by investment-grade companies. Asked about the Jefferies note (about $2.3T of contracted backlog across Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Oracle, about half owed by OpenAI and Anthropic), he agreed there is longer-term credit risk with OpenAI and Anthropic as end users. He sees much less risk with hyperscalers over five years.
  - Credit health: no imminent recession. About 5% of high yield trades at stress levels (a 20-year low), and about 9% of bank loans do (5% excluding software). About 10% of the market is distressed, with roughly $150B of triple-C debt coming due. In software, 75% of loans trade above 90 and 20% below 80.
  - Rates: short-term expectations have moved about 200bps, from heading to 3% to heading to 5%. The economy can handle it. He sees high-single to low-double-digit returns in credit.
  - Retail: institutions allocate 25–35% to alternatives, individuals 0–15%. OHA launched senior loan and multi-strategy credit funds for the wealth channel.
- **Alts discussion (panel):** Saccocia said her firm (about $120B in assets) has 20–30% alternatives targets, but implementation is nearer 14–15% because headline risk has scared investors off. She sees continued "drought of distributions" and a need for secondaries and co-investments. Large PE has more capital and fewer opportunities, while small PE and secondaries look better. Private companies now mature before IPO, so value creation happens before then.
- **Best Stocks in the Market – Josh Brown:** The pick is Expeditors International (EXPD). Q2 earnings rose 51%, 21% above consensus, and air freight revenue rose 57% on chip shipments to hyperscalers. As a customs broker, it also benefits from tariff paperwork. Five of 17 analysts rate it sell or strong sell, with an average target of about $159, well below the price. The stock is up 30% this year and trades at 28x. The technicals show a range of $183–$195, a rising 50-day near $187 and a breakout above $196, with RSI at 57. His stop is $183, with $177 (the August post-earnings low) as the anchor. Johnson Controls (data center cooling) was a secondary mention: it has already broken out and is testing the breakout on low volume.
- **Oliver Renick (SIBO, Chicago):** Options markets are a competitive threat to alts. Box spreads, buffer ETFs and structured products offer yield without private-credit risk. He cited Goldman's purchase of Innovator Funds and said alt managers are nearing a two-year bear market.
- **News alerts:**
  - Google is broadening access to Gemini "Argon" (shares up about 1%).
  - Meta debuted its Muse models.
  - Apple hit a new high.
- **Bank earnings:**
  - Terranova: JPMorgan and Goldman trading revenue was lighter than expected. JPM equities were up 49% year over year, commodities and currencies were weak, and net interest margin guidance was better. He bought JPM in June and has held Goldman about three years.
  - Sechan: combined net interest income of $56.5B at JPM, Citi and Wells is positive in a rising-rate environment. He sees a good read-through to regionals.
  - Brown: nothing in the data points to deterioration, in the labor market or in credit metrics.
  - Wapner and the panel noted a less robust IPO market than expected.
- **Final Trades:**
  - **Joe Terranova:** West Pharmaceutical Services (steering away from energy and tech).
  - **Josh Brown:** JPMorgan (dipped from $363 in August to $335).
  - **Rob Sechan:** Meta (best mega-cap over the past month, still the cheapest, Muse upside; up almost 3% today).
  - **Shannon Saccocia:** Energy, specifically refiners, because "the Middle East is not going away."
