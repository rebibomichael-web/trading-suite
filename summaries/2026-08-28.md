**Fed Chair Kevin Warsh Puts the Rally to the Test: The Investment Committee Weighs in, 8/28/26**

**Overview:** Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole speech came across as hawkish, and markets put roughly 55% odds on a September rate hike. The committee mostly agreed that earnings, helped by Nvidia's blowout results, matter more than the Fed, though September seasonality and stretched positioning are worth watching. Dominic Chiu hosted in place of Scott Wapner. The segments also covered materials and gold miners, a Best Buy street fight, a Medtronic setup, and a first-on-CNBC interview with Chicago Fed President Austin Goolsbee.

**Market snapshot:** Dow +0.1% (~71 pts), S&P flat, Nasdaq -0.25%. The 2-year yield was ~4.325% and the 10-year just under 4.71%. Stocks dipped and then recovered after Warsh spoke.

- **Jenny Harrington:**
  - Doesn't think the Fed matters much for stocks. The 10-year went from 4.2% at the start of the year to 4.7% while the market kept rising, so she expects steady rates and a focus on earnings.
  - Warns of an "air pocket" between now and October earnings. Wants investors to focus on *earnings reliability*: she'd dig deeper into Caterpillar (up on its large-turbine queue) and prefers steadier names like Sabra Health Care REIT.
  - Clients in Providence, Boston and Portland told her the market "seems like a peak." She agrees there's an AI boom but dislikes the word "craze," since the spenders have cash.
  - Cyber stocks are off-limits with a value tilt ("it kills you"). She used to own Palo Alto for its free cash flow yield.
  - **Best Buy (BBY):** owns it, added in March at $65, and bought more after yesterday's ~4% drop. It trades at ~12x P/E with a 4.6% dividend yield and reliable earnings. She calls Goldman's sell rating "sour grapes."
  - **Medtronic (MDT):** reports Tuesday. Aging demographics are the story. It's still down ~5% YTD despite the run since June, at 15x earnings with a ~7% FCF yield and 8-9% expected earnings growth.
- **Bill Baruch:**
  - Called Warsh's remarks more hawkish. He noted two lines: inflation expectations "look durable until they don't," and summer inflation data beat expectations but underlying trends haven't meaningfully improved.
  - Thinks the 55% September-hike probability is "off-sides." Warsh needs to build a cohesive 12-voter committee, and he expects Warsh to turn incrementally less hawkish unless a hot CPI forces his hand.
  - Took industrials exposure down over the last 1.5 months, and cut Vertiv (transcribed "Verdict") in half in late May. With tech at 35-37% plus communication services, he says an industrials overweight makes a portfolio "basically all AI."
  - **Marvell (MRVL):** owns it, bought ~$220 a month ago. It's down ~10% post-earnings, hurt by semiconductor-tariff news. He likes FY27 growth guided to ~60% (up from 50%) and the CXL/data-center connectivity theme.
  - Runs a gold and silver miners portfolio and sees miners as a complement to bonds over the next 12 months. Favorites: Newmont, Agnico Eagle and Coeur Mining. He also likes midstream energy as a bond complement in an 80/20 portfolio.
- **Jim Lebenthal:**
  - Used Warsh's own figure that AI is exactly half of corporate capex. The other half is non-AI, including reshoring, so he sees the AI build-out as additive without creating undue price pressure. PCE at 3.7% is well below where it was four years ago.
  - On retail, "price matters," citing Walmart and TJX selling off. The consumer is employed and consuming, so cheaper names like Best Buy look like "a great stock," and the recent dip is consolidation after a strong year. He doesn't own it.
  - Healthcare: prefers pharma on valuation and dividend yield. He owns AbbVie, AstraZeneca and Vertex (no dividend, pricey, but great growth) and would add with new money.
- **Kevin Simpson** (newly minted CNBC contributor):
  - Said the market got two opposing messages this week. Inflation ran hot (PCE 3.7%, core 3.3%), and odds of a September hike went from about one-third to one-half. He expects no move in September and a rate hike in December.
  - On Nvidia: revenue doubled to $96B, data center was $89B, and next-quarter guidance is $108B. He says the market can support higher yields as long as earnings deliver. He owns AMD and Intel in his growth portfolio but says "you don't need to go any further than Nvidia," which he puts at ~15x forward on 2028 numbers.
  - Likes market breadth (energy, discretionary, materials, healthcare/pharma). Flagged a capital-markets risk: if the funding window closed, the AI build-out would suffer, with Anthropic, OpenAI and Anduril expected to raise. He sees the July deleveraging as a healthy setup.
  - **CF Industries (CF):** initiated last week and added yesterday and this morning. It's a North American nitrogen producer, 9-10x forward earnings, with a dividend boost and reindustrialization support.
  - **Medtronic:** owns it. The stock has been on a three-month run, and he wants to see cardiovascular strength, especially cardiac ablation.
- **Market notes:**
  - BofA's Hartnett says positioning is in extreme bull territory. Goldman says flows are solid and technicals support a grind higher, though the levered community has cut exposure.
  - The Wall Street Journal ran a piece on AI investment keeping the global economy afloat.
  - The next Fed meeting is Sept 16.
- **Mike Santoli (midday word):**
  - There was mechanical buying after the speech, which removed a catalyst. Mag 7 names (Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet) are up ~2% while the Russell 2000 is down ~1.2%.
  - Higher yields and macro stress typically mean the market can't broaden. Big growth stalwarts are keeping things orderly. If August stays this calm, "we'd have gotten off easy."
- **Calls of the Day:** Best Buy street fight. Goldman has a sell rating (14% downside) and Truist has a buy rating (20% upside). Jenny and Jim sided with the bulls.
- **Austin Goolsbee interview** (Steve Liesman, Jackson Hole; Goolsbee is a 2027 voter):
  - Agreed with much of Warsh's read: the labor market is stable and the problem side of the dual mandate is inflation. Recent benign readings are "not out of the woods."
  - His main worry is services inflation, which can't be blamed on tariffs or oil. It improved in June and gave a little back in the latest report.
  - Was comfortable holding at the last meeting to see whether the improvement is a blip. He is skeptical of "transitory" claims, noting the supposed inflation peaks keep slipping. He dissented in December against front-loading rate cuts.
  - Open to six vs. eight meetings a year without a strong view. Favors scaling back explicit forward guidance and doesn't want to give a precise "if X then Y" reaction function.
  - Denied the Fed and Treasury are working at cross purposes: the Fed isn't coordinating with the administration or giving the stock market what it wants.
- **News update (Brandon Gomez):**
  - Congo's Ebola outbreak spread to 60 health zones, with 5,700+ cases and ~2,800 deaths. It has no approved vaccine, and the WHO says it is out of control.
  - Walmart settled the DOJ opioid lawsuit.
  - Virginia's Justice Kelly was crowned Miss USA.
- **Final Trades:**
  - **Kevin Simpson:** Take-Two Interactive (TTWO). The first Grand Theft Auto in over a decade is expected to be the biggest launch ever.
  - **Bill Baruch:** Nvidia (NVDA), his largest holding, which he sees as a gift to buy a little lower.
  - **Jim Lebenthal:** Cheniere Energy (LNG), a long-term LNG exporter, and a good time to buy.
  - **Jenny Harrington:** Pfizer (PFE), with a ~6% dividend yield at ~9x earnings.
