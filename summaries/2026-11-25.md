**Halftime Report: Market Sell-Off, AI Trade Retest and Fed Mistake Debate (Nov 26, 2026)**

**Overview:** Stocks sold off hard again, with the S&P 500 below every Wall Street year-end target and the Nasdaq 100 negative for the year. By the end of the hour the indexes had recovered part of their losses. The committee argued over whether this is a correction (a double-bottom retest) or the end of the AI cycle. They also covered rising credit stress in AI names and whether the Fed is about to make a policy mistake.

- **Jim Lebenthal:** Called this "still a correction" and a double-bottom retest of the low. He said Friday's low may already have been hit.
  - His case: GDP was "stellar," jobless claims are low, and S&P estimates have risen since the start of the month.
  - He said analysts have been mostly right for four years.
  - On the CoreWeave debate, he noted Oracle's CFO said financing is basically done. He also said the 15–17% CoreWeave yields are secondary-market sentiment, not actual issuance.
  - He bought more Alphabet (GOOGL) after it hit a 52-week low. He had trimmed it about 20 points higher and likes its "many shots on goal": search, chips, Gemini, YouTube and Waymo. He said he already owns plenty of Oracle.
  - He said the market can rally even if AI stocks don't, citing RSP.
- **Steve Weiss:** The bear on the panel. He said "the facts have changed dramatically" because AI financing costs are astronomical and the debt is trading at distressed levels.
  - He argued stocks usually move before estimates get cut, and that this is how cycles end.
  - He sold Micron (MU) and Caterpillar (CAT) in July and has lightened up. He advised protecting cash and said it is "still early to buy."
  - His concerns: Jensen Huang's departure and how much of Nvidia's revenue really comes from China, Democrats coming into office, extended valuations, and weak-handed capital from the V-shaped rebounds.
  - He expects hyperscalers to be fine but sees the next leg down coming from over-ordering, like stockpiled toilet paper, roughly a quarter out.
  - He grudgingly bought some QQQ and Google near the 52-week low, saying it "could have been a better time."
  - He sold a retail stock (name unclear in the transcript) to ring the register. He had bought it in the $120s after a 20% post-earnings drop and said he'd return on a pullback.
- **Joe Terranova:** Listed the semis' declines from 52-week highs: Micron −55%, Marvell −63%, KLA −60%, Lam −58%, AMD and Broadcom about −43%.
  - He said no company has reported any AI demand weakness, so the market is prioritizing liquidity.
  - Strong reports from Analog Devices, HP and Dell drew no buyers.
  - He wants stabilization and follow-through in the AI trade before an "all clear." His own ETF is rotating defensive toward staples, and he is trading equity futures.
  - He said to watch the Mag 7 as a leading indicator. He is building Apple (AAPL) and won't sell it, and he expects Meta to lead.
- **Brynn Talkington:** Bought Alphabet, Tesla (TSLA) and the Nasdaq 100 premium income ETF (GPIQ) on Friday's panic and today's dip, and will keep adding Microsoft (MSFT).
  - She said the bond market is flagging a Fed mistake and a growth scare. The 2- and 10-year yields fell despite hot inflation and rate-hike talk.
  - She compared this to Q4 2018: a new Fed governor worried about inflation, followed by a growth scare. She expects better regulatory structure for data centers under a Democratic Congress.
  - She cited Michael Dell's record backlog and no customer slowdown, with memory prices staying elevated through 2027.
- **Steve Liesman (guest, CNBC senior economics correspondent):** Said Fed Chair Kevin Warsh is more attuned to markets than any chair he has covered, and is watching AI as the main driver of the economy.
  - The 2-year yield has fallen about 20–25 bps since mid-November, from 4.80% to 4.60%. The odds of a January hike dropped from 77% to a coin toss, about 50–55%.
  - Scott raised the Greenspan 1996 analogy, when the Fed held off hiking despite "irrational exuberance." Liesman said Warsh faces a real inflation problem now, much of it supply-side (oil, tariffs). He called the AI build-out inflationary now and disinflationary only later. He also said the wealth effect is bigger than ever.
- **Mike Santoli (guest):** Called this a retest sequence, noting it was not a washout, since upside volume was around 30% and quality was working.
  - He said the S&P needs to clear about 7,400 to leave the trough and wants the VIX below 30. There were about 700 Nasdaq 52-week lows.
  - He sees Anthropic's IPO as part of the pile-on, not a leading indicator. It reversed from −9% to up about 2.5% on a volume spike.
  - He said the Dow is acting like a "wartime consigliere": defensive, with value working.
  - Marvell reports Tuesday at about 37x earnings with 53% expected revenue growth, a good test of whether good earnings finally get rewarded.
  - He noted the market keeps rejecting companies' "it's still early" message about the AI build-out.
- **Calls and market color:**
  - Michael Burry said he is "temporarily almost entirely long" after selling the rest of his Nasdaq 100 puts.
  - CoreWeave bonds are yielding about 17%, with credit default swaps (CDS) widening, and Oracle was up 7%.
  - Nvidia, SoftBank (−15% at one point), Nebius and CoreWeave were all down sharply.
  - Dell was down more than 7% at the low despite strong numbers, then recovered some.
  - Goldman Sachs hit about $790, a new low, versus about $1,100 in summer.
  - The Nasdaq was down more than 4% at the low and off about 2.5% later. The Dow briefly pushed back above 50k.
- **Retail and staples segment:** Scott noted XRT is headed for its ninth positive week in ten. A panelist pointed to falling gasoline prices as a tailwind for retail and said they own Amazon (AMZN) plus adjacent plays like Disney, Wynn and Delta.
  - Scott listed staples revenue growth: Casey's 24%, Monster 16%, Costco 11% and Dollar Tree 6%, which rose after earnings.
  - Jim's top discretionary pick was Airbnb (ABNB).
- **Options (Oliver Renick):** Options flow is bullish on KWEB despite a 45% fall over 52 weeks. Bulls are buying the $22.50 calls for an 8% pop by next Friday, plus the $26 calls for mid-January. FXI sentiment is much weaker, with 85% of options trading in puts.
- **News update (Frank Holland):**
  - A US-backed investment group is buying Lukoil's international assets for $10B in securities, pending Treasury approval.
  - Judges urged prosecutors to drop the most serious case against Netanyahu.
  - A Pakistani astronaut arrived at China's space station.
- **Final Trades:** The transcript's end is garbled, so some attributions are uncertain.
  - **Brynn:** Robinhood (HOOD), selling the February $75 calls for about $4.
  - **Steve Weiss:** Defense stocks, which he said have been overlooked because the Iran issue "is not over at all."
  - **Disney (DIS):** Someone recommended it, pointing to the chart as "worth a thousand words," but the speaker is unclear.
  - **Anthropic:** Named as a final trade, but the speaker is unclear. I couldn't tell who picked Anthropic or whether Joe or Jim had a separate final trade.
