# NVIDIA Overhang, Anthropic IPO Demand and AI Trade Fallout, CNBC Halftime Report, Nov 26, 2026

**Overview:** The hour focused on NVIDIA after earnings. The numbers and guidance were strong, but the stock is weighed down by the Jensen Huang resignation and governance fight. The panel also covered the Anthropic IPO, which is pricing today and trading tomorrow on roughly $500B of demand, and what it means for the AI trade.

- **NVIDIA (stock ~$152, ~10x forward earnings):**
  - Wall Street: Goldman reiterated Buy, JPMorgan raised its target to $230, and Morgan Stanley trimmed its target. Dan Ives said "demand did not resign."
  - NVIDIA held an all-hands meeting while employees petition to bring Huang back.
- **Josh Brown:**
  - **NVIDIA:** "The numbers were great… the overhang is the problem with the stock." He thinks interim CEO Kress isn't the long-term answer. He doesn't see how Huang gets the votes to return, since shares are widely dispersed with no 10% activist, unless the stock falls below $100. He sees Huang's public testimony as a possible "clearing event."
  - **AI trade beneficiaries:** Amazon (Trainium, more leverage in GPU negotiations) and OpenAI/Anthropic (hard to trade until one is public). He also named AMD (Helios racks), Broadcom (custom chips) and Astera Labs/ALAB (~150% revenue growth, derated multiple, sells against NVLink).
  - **Trades:**
    - Trimmed Snowflake.
    - Cut Toast by 20%, but remains a long-term holder.
    - Bought more Netflix, bringing his average cost below $70. He argues it trades at ~16x 2027 estimates, down 50% from its high, with consensus at just 10.6% revenue growth and 4.9% EPS growth. Catalysts: NFL games on Thanksgiving Eve and Christmas, the Fury-Joshua fight, the Women's World Cup, and ad-tier expansion to 15 more countries in Q1. The wild card is a rumored bid for NBC/Universal.
    - Started a small Rocket Lab position, alongside Joby (Electron's 100th flight, larger rocket due in Q1/Q2, stock down about two-thirds since summer).
    - Sold West Pharma (WST) after a profit within a month, but it stays on his best-stocks list.
    - He is clearing the decks because he sees better opportunities in stocks down 30–40% from their highs.
- **Rob Sechan:**
  - **NVIDIA:** He said the call was "a step in the right direction." Positives he cited: no more China sales, clarified circular financing and customer guarantees, and hyperscaler revenue up 25% sequentially. He argued the board must act in shareholders' best interest and shouldn't rush to judgment.
  - **Anthropic:** He called it "generational" and a "next-gen hyperscaler," citing 75% of enterprise, 12,000 customers spending over $100K a month, 900% net revenue retention and an $81B run rate (about 1% of global IT spend). He'd be a buyer on the IPO. He bought Amazon as a final trade yesterday, partly for its stake.
  - **Netflix:** He agreed with Josh (16x, $13B cash flow, ad business not priced in). It is a tax-loss sale candidate for his firm, though he's unsure whether they'll act.
  - **Retail:** Lowe's raised its outlook while Home Depot narrowed its guide. He says Lowe's comps are accelerating in the worst housing market since '07 and has upside when rates fall.
- **Steve Weiss:**
  - **NVIDIA:** He's very bearish on the governance side. He said the board shouldn't bring Huang back, given the affair with a junior employee and his belief that Huang "probably lied to the board." He also criticized the family hires and said the vendor-financing liabilities are bigger than disclosed, and that the whole board should be replaced.
  - **Anthropic:** He indicated interest but said to ignore the headline book numbers, because "everybody's inflating their book." He'd price it at $85 or above with more shares, expects it to trade through the deal price, then reset on valuation. He noted Goldman has a lot riding on it.
  - **Retail:** He likes Dick's Sporting Goods as a unique offering, expecting a good quarter, though he called discretionary a tough space.
- **Joe Terranova:**
  - **NVIDIA:** He said the earnings aren't the story. The beat, guide and bigger buyback were all delivered, but unanswered questions on advanced chips reaching China keep the story complex. Near-term alpha lies elsewhere because Jane Street and Citadel are swinging the stock.
  - **Anthropic:** He doesn't usually play day-one IPOs, but this is an exception. He sees Apple and Anthropic as the two main engagement winners and expects enterprises to shift from Copilot to Claude.
  - **Copper/Freeport-McMoRan:** Copper sentiment turned negative after the midterms and Druckenmiller's comments.
  - **Retail:** TJX is up 11% but only a bounce from depressed levels, and he prefers Ross. He says staples are getting expensive: Costco ~53x and Walmart ~46x.
- **Anthropic IPO (Leslie Picker):**
  - Likely to price well above the range, north of $80 a share, and may be upsized. Final decisions are due this afternoon, and it trades tomorrow.
  - Institutional indications of interest topped $400B and retail orders are near $100B. That is about $500B for a $64B offering at the high end, roughly double SpaceX's oversubscription.
  - New Street rates it Buy with a $105 target. Amazon and Google will own nearly 30% after the IPO.
  - Andy Jassy said Amazon invested "not to turn around and make a quick buck" and expects to hold the equity for a long time.
- **Mike Santoli (Market Memo):**
  - The S&P is at its 200-day moving average with twitchy trading. Anthropic allocations could free up reserved cash and create an "upside air pocket."
  - He's reluctant to call a relief rally. AI enthusiasm now has to spread across another ~$1.5T of market cap, and the Fed is hawkish.
  - He sees a possible rotation from NVIDIA into big-tech platforms like Amazon.
- **Options Action (Oliver Renick):** Take-Two (GTA 6) options volume was above 40,000 contracts, 7x average. Calls and puts were roughly even. The two biggest trades were $1M put sales, at the $170 strike expiring in January and the $210 strike expiring Dec 31, a sign the weakness may be over.
- **Final Trades:**
  - Josh Brown: CrowdStrike (CRWD), which reports after the bell, on rising AI-driven cyber-crime.
  - Steve Weiss: Fortinet (FTNT), because "cyber is the one budget nobody cuts."
  - Rob Sechan and Joe Terranova: not captured in the transcript.
