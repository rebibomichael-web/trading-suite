**Shifting Sentiment and Sliding Semis: The Investment Committee's Strategy – 10/23/26**

**Overview:** Markets were under pressure. The Nasdaq was up 1.31%, the Dow down about 1% and the S&P off about 0.1%. The 10-year yield was just under 5%, oil was surging, and consumer confidence hit its second-lowest reading ever. The panel debated whether to buy the AI and semiconductor dip ahead of next week's mega-cap earnings and the FOMC meeting, or to stay cautious on financing, capex and positioning risks.

- **Stephanie Link:**
  - Bullish. About 25% of companies have reported, with earnings up 38%. Retail sales were up 5.1% ex-gas, ISM manufacturing and services are well above 50, and yields fell from 5.36% to a low of 4.95% this week.
  - The S&P equal weight is up almost 2% week to date while the S&P is down 0.5%. She calls this broadening, and says materials, financials and discretionary are each up about 6% month to date.
  - She expects strong monetization next week: SpaceX AI revenue up 754%, Meta monetization up 26%.
  - She bought more Amazon, which she says trades at 17x forward. She sees AWS accelerating from 37% to 42-43% growth on doubled capacity.
  - She also bought more Wells Fargo: 1.5x book, ROTCE targets of 17-18% that she thinks are conservative, net interest margin expanding for the first time in several quarters, and the stock down 11% YTD.
  - She sold Dover to raise cash and started a new Zscaler position: up only 9% YTD versus 120%+ for CrowdStrike and Palo Alto, at a 50% discount to its historical EV/sales, with a $15B TAM and margins heading to 27-30% by 2030.
  - She is a buyer of the beaten-down power names. GE Vernova is still up 26% YTD, Quanta up 29%, Vertiv up 21%, and she says fundamentals don't justify the pullback.
  - On SpaceX she praised 161% total revenue growth, a 22% sequential capacity increase and guidance raised about 50% versus expectations.
  - On SLB she called it a strong quarter and a consistent CEO, with big upside if the Middle East war ends.
  - On earnings ahead, she thinks Boeing could be fine if it guides to $4-5B of 2027 cash flow. She owns Coca-Cola (the only staple she holds) but wouldn't buy it into earnings at its valuation.
- **Jason Snipe:**
  - Wants to lean into the correction. He thinks the big prints next week will lift the market, and he is not in the "AI is a bubble" camp ("third or fourth inning").
  - His worry is that capex and backlogs have to be executed. Western Digital, Lam, Micron and Meta all had good earnings but bad price action.
  - Expects Google Cloud, Microsoft (30M paid Copilot users) and AWS (mid-30s growth, new training chip) to be strong.
  - Likes ServiceNow: 20-25% growth, 25x earnings, used by 90% of the Fortune 500, and positioned to govern AI agents. About 50% of its business is consumption-based.
  - On cybersecurity, he likes Zscaler, CrowdStrike, Palo Alto and Fortinet, with CISO budgets expanding. He said he wished he had added to Fortinet when it was beaten up.
  - Still bullish on Eaton and the infrastructure names, citing about $800B of capex this year and the electrification theme. Eaton is up 16% YTD, down from about 30%.
- **Jim Lebenthal:**
  - Hopes the broadening is real but is wary of financing and the cost of capital. The risk is a higher weighted average cost of capital compressing margins and forward multiples, and he cited crowding out in the IPO window.
  - Likes Alphabet at a 1.3 PEG, with Gemini, chips, YouTube and search behind it, and says multiples have compressed more than growth justifies.
  - Doesn't like Meta's spend: expense growth is outpacing sales and he says the Muse monetization math doesn't cover the cost of capital.
  - Wants more specific ROI commentary. He called Jassy's comment that new data centers pay back in under three years a win.
  - Expects semis to be a source of risk-off pressure and described the market as "risk off." On Amazon he questioned whether the multiple belongs at 17x in a tougher margin environment.
  - Says staples, healthcare and utilities are money hiding in a risk-off trade, not a cyclical broadening. He thinks the staples are expensive and Coca-Cola is stretched.
- **Rob Sechan:**
  - Cautious on offense. Cites Goldman's desk (too many overhangs: FOMC, earnings, elections), Jefferies (tech the most "for sale" sector on their desk, with one of the most dramatic net-selling periods in recent years) and Deutsche Bank (tech allocations at the 25th percentile).
  - Says the real question is financing the AI buildout. Apple is at all-time highs because it isn't spending as aggressively.
  - Says semis are down sharply: Lam 47% off its high, KLA 52%, Hynix 31%, AMAT 48%. His firm owns a lot of them (Lam up 600%, KLA up 300%) and he won't double down yet.
  - Sees a possible catalyst if Anthropic goes public by the end of November and OpenAI closes its roughly $30B round.
  - Likes bonds as a "shock absorber," barbelled with healthcare and AI infrastructure. Bonds are down about 5% YTD.
  - Calls ServiceNow the "air traffic controller" of the corporate AI stack: an AI beneficiary, not a casualty. It was his final trade yesterday.
- **Calls of the Day:**
  - SpaceX was double-upgraded from sell to buy at Phillip Capital (compute demand running ahead of supply, contracts renewing at higher prices). The stock is still below its $201 high.
  - ServiceNow's price target was raised to $180 from $150 at Bank of America.
- **Market Memo (Mike Santoli):**
  - The S&P went back to the June highs, just above 7600.
  - Consumer cyclicals, banks, transports and semis are in full corrections. Staples and healthcare are going vertical because semis are down.
  - Positioning has reset, but the action is "squirrelly." The AI trade now rallies when bond yields rise.
  - Mega-caps are again being asked to rescue the tape.
- **Options Action (Oliver Renick):**
  - USO and XLE options volume was double the average, with bullish call buying.
  - USO saw 200,000+ contracts and over $90M in premium, including 210-strike calls that need a 24% rally.
  - One XLE buyer put about $3M into 75/69-strike calls expiring Dec 18 (the transcript is garbled here).
- **Final Trades:**
  - **Rob Sechan:** Devon Energy ("own the cash flow, own the discipline").
  - **Jim Lebenthal:** Lockheed Martin (18x earnings, 1.2 PEG).
  - **Stephanie Link and Jason Snipe:** Goldman Sachs (up 72%) and IBM. The transcript is unclear on who picked which, and Snipe's pick isn't clearly captured.
