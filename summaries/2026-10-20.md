**The Bull Market Backdrop in Question: The Investment Committee Weighs in – 10/20/26**

**Overview:** Stocks rose across the board, led by the Dow (up about 1%), as sectors outside tech, especially health care, kept leading. The panel debated whether this is a true broadening or just a defensive rotation. They also covered housing weakness, rates near 5.1%, and earnings from SpaceX, Netflix, and others.

- **Joe Terranova:**
  - Still sees the opportunity in the AI trade (semis, memory, optical), which has stalled since June. He thinks hedge-fund deleveraging is largely done and expects a November–December rally.
  - Calls the market defensive and says it swings between fear and greed. He notes low-volatility stocks are having their best month in 24 months.
  - Overweight health care, energy, metals, and agriculture, and underweight tech (about 25%).
  - Added to UnitedHealth for the third time in a month ("momentum and quality"; earnings, Medicare ratios, and guidance were all up).
  - Holds Eli Lilly at an all-time high and about $1.2T. Lilly is the clear GLP-1 winner, while Novo is under $200B. Goldman's target is $1,413 and BofA's is $1,397.
  - Owns Pulte as his housing exposure. It is down 7% over 52 weeks and was at a 52-week low five days ago.
  - Prefers Delta to United Airlines because of fuel costs. United would need fares 25–30% higher.
  - Likes CME on volatility, though he wouldn't necessarily buy it before earnings.
  - Bought Cboe in June at 263, and says it is breaking out.
- **Jim Lebenthal:**
  - The equal-weight and cap-weight S&P 500 are in a "dead tie" year to date.
  - His tech weight is 36%, versus about 40% in the S&P, which frees him to own energy and health care.
  - Holds half-weight Apple and sold it before the last two earnings releases at about 330. It is now 340.
  - Says 10-year yields near 5.1% are fine, and expects a 4.8–5.2% range. Cites the Atlanta Fed's Q4 GDP estimate of 3.5%. A drop below 5% would worry him.
  - Not selling Oracle. He thinks AI spending is not near its peak and says "this does not feel like 1999."
  - Prefers Steel Dynamics over Cleveland Cliffs: better balance sheet, $35B market cap, and better capital returns. Cliffs fell 4% today after rising 16% yesterday, and Steel Dynamics fell 1.3% on its aluminum business.
  - Called BlackRock a good long-term hold that has been "tepid" so far.
  - Holds Home Depot and Lowe's, which he calls a 2028 story, not 2027.
- **Josh Brown:**
  - Quotes UBS, which says the bull market is intact and keeps its June 2027 S&P target of 8,400. He cites Q3 S&P earnings growth of 24.6%, 79% of companies beating estimates, revenue up 12.3%, and profit growth in 10 of 11 sectors.
  - Argues that rotation keeps the bull market alive, and that high rates fuel the "K-shaped" rich via money-market income and push investors back to the Mag 7.
  - Bought West Pharmaceutical (new best-stocks name), a breakout versus its highs from nearly five years ago. High-value components grew 19% and are 49% of sales. GLP-1 exposure is 18% of revenue, and gross margin is up 200bp to 37.7%.
  - Added Waters to his best-stocks list: up 57% from the March low, Q2 revenue $1.65B, EPS $3.00, and raised guidance. Reports Nov 3. Stop at $420 for traders and $390 for investors.
  - Thermo Fisher is up 19% since his call last November. It hit a record $703. He suggests a $650 trader stop and a $600 weekly-close stop for investors.
  - Sold the rest of his Rocket Companies stub, which is down 42% year to date ("when you find yourself riding a dead horse, dismount").
  - Defended Live Nation against CFRA's cut to Sell with a $167 target (the stock is at 174): "a one-of-a-kind crown jewel," and he's a never-seller.
  - Sold half of his remaining Netflix position. He admits he was wrong on the stock after buying back down to an average cost under $80, and the stock hit $69. He is worried by the layoffs of about 1,000 people and the exits of Bela Bajaria and Dan Lin ahead of earnings.
- **Kevin Simpson:**
  - Says the healthcare move is more a flight to quality than a broadening.
  - Agrees there will be a split between profitable AI winners and leveraged "have nots." Tech is about 25% of his portfolio.
  - Owns SpaceX but not for 2026. He thinks the options market is right and expects a tepid move because the company has to prove monetization.
  - Expects Tesla to show the same narrative as SpaceX, and thinks a Tesla–SpaceX combination is likely someday.
  - Bought West Pharmaceutical in April at about 296 (a $27B market cap). He sees accelerating revenue from obesity drugs and peptides.
  - Likes Spotify (Morgan Stanley Overweight, target $640), but calls the target a bit extreme.
- **Market context and calls:**
  - Oil was around $100 and was $110 earlier. Housing data was a "disaster," Coreweave faced an HSBC call for another 30% drop, and Korean data-center firm Ellis fell 32% in its debut.
  - B of A warns defensives can become bubbles.
- **Options action (Oliver Renick):** The options market prices only a 6% move for SpaceX after earnings, about half the August move. Put buying was heavy at the 140 strike expiring tomorrow and Friday. The big-money put sellers who were the "tell" in August are missing.
- **News update (Dom Chu):**
  - Hegseth made a surprise Israel visit to discuss Iran.
  - Trump and Vance are announcing a $25M DOJ whistleblower program (up to $30,000 per tip).
  - Alberta voters rejected independence, with two-thirds voting to remain.
- **Final Trades:**
  - **Josh Brown:** Amazon. It has a pattern of higher lows and higher highs and should break through the summer high on earnings.
  - **Kevin Simpson:** UnitedHealth ("the turnaround will be worth the wait").
  - **Jim Lebenthal:** eBay ("what a beautiful chart"). He also personally bought Twilio at 258 yesterday.
  - **Joe Terranova:** The transcript gives no clear final trade for him.
