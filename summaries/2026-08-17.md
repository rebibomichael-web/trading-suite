**Too Bullish or Just Right? — 8/17/26**

Overview: The Investment Committee debated whether record highs and a three-week winning streak mean investors have gotten too bullish, with most panelists arguing the tape is "tactical" rather than complacent even as rising long-end yields (30-year at 5.29%) get largely shrugged off. Chips/memory names (Micron, SanDisk) and NVIDIA rallied hard into next week's earnings, while Alphabet keeps lagging on AI-talent-drain worries and Walmart/Target/Costco face valuation and inflation-language questions ahead of earnings.

- **Joe Terranova**: Sees S&P hitting $10,000 within 18 months; says universal bullishness isn't a sell signal — the market is becoming more "tactical," rebuilding/maintaining positions in memory, optical, refiners, and financials (BofA, State Street, Schwab, PNC at 52-week highs). On Mag7 rotation, notes NVIDIA (+12.5% MTD) awakening ahead of earnings while Alphabet/Amazon/Apple are down in August as money rotates into NVIDIA; says the CapEx/memory buildout is real and you "can put new money to work in NVIDIA right now." Owns Micron with Jim; likes the momentum factor (JOT at 52-week high) as a signal the market is comfortable.
- **Steve Weiss**: Bullish but flags risks — rates rising meaningfully (Fed tightening via oil or a weakening consumer) as the main threat to the bull case; otherwise sees no disaster ahead, AI trade still strong. On Micron, joked he sold too early ("finally above where I sold it... no regrets, I put the money elsewhere and I'm doing well"). Bought Cisco last week on a post-earnings dip and reiterated it as his final trade.
- **Shannon Saccocia**: Notes valuations look extended and multiples have compressed despite strong earnings; flags slowing/hard-to-rationalize AI CapEx and rising real rates (US and global) as the two real risks, though earnings strength (broad ~14% average, ~21% ex-one-timers) is offsetting concerns. Says curve-steepening gives investors room to add duration/lock in yields ahead of a possible December cut. Expects more sector dispersion into Q4 and says investors need to get tactical around "compute as an asset class" (memory, networking, hardware, semis) over software. Final trade: IDU (utilities), citing data-center-driven demand growth and self-funding balance sheets.
- **Jim Lebenthal ("Jimmy")**: Says the current equilibrium (~21x forward earnings vs. mid-20s% earnings growth) is sustainable through year-end but doubts a fifth straight year of double-digit S&P gains (only happened once, late-90s) given tougher 2027 comps (~14% growth expected). Flags the 30-year yield (5.29%) as underappreciated — worries it signals the 10-year could break above 5%, though says this isn't a reason to dump hyperscaler exposure now. Defended Alphabet's AI-talent-drain reaction as overblown initially but conceded Joe was right that the stock has stayed heavy since the Aug 5 chief-scientist departure news (fell from an all-time-high $384). On Regeneron/biopharma, picks Vertex as his favorite in a group (Amgen, Regeneron, Vertex) he says trades at reasonable, not extreme, valuations. Co-owns Micron with Joe, citing ~$130B in projected free cash flow next year (vs. ~$20B a few years ago) and heavy buybacks at ~6x earnings. Final trade: Cisco, echoing Weiss's call from last week.
- **Mike Santoli (Midday Word)**: Says the market may be near-term "air pockety" until NVIDIA earnings/Jackson Hole; worries earnings might be "too good" (a pull-forward) and that the market has paid close to the max multiple per dollar of earnings, though that doesn't preclude further gains. On the 30-year yield, says tens still matter more for pricing and remain contained in their multi-year range; sees the steepening as a "curiosity" for now rather than a valuation threat.
- **Kate Rooney (AI IPO segment)**: Reports Anthropic's bankers are valuing the company off projected 2028 revenue of $190–200B (vs. ~$47B current run-rate), with some investors eyeing a ~$2 trillion valuation; both Anthropic and OpenAI have filed confidential IPO paperwork. Notes pricing pressure from open-source models/big tech and plateauing spend on Anthropic's premium models. Cites Dario Amodei's rare weekend tweet pushing back on being called an AI pessimist, acknowledging fair criticisms (e.g., AI hasn't yet proven out drug-discovery upside) while flagging real risks like cybersecurity.
- **Oliver Renick (Options Action)**: SMH call open interest is at its most bullish vs. puts since April; SanDisk (+40% in a week) and Micron (+20%) options volume running well above 30-day averages; DRAM ETF flows skew heavily toward calls (62,000 calls vs. 24,000 puts). But flags a contrarian red flag: a massive ~$130M "synthetic short" — 20,000+ in-the-money SMH November $630 puts bought in a single trade.
- **Retail/Calls of the Day**: DA Davidson reiterated Walmart with a $150 target ahead of Thursday's earnings; Joe (owns/maintaining) wants to see ~30% e-commerce growth and ~40% ad growth to stay comfortable, expects lingering "inflation" language to cap the stock near a reflex bounce to the mid-$120s. Panel also discussed Target's 57%-plus rally off its bottom (still down ~37% over five years) vs. Walmart's outperformance, and flagged Costco's valuation-driven pullback; Shannon likes TJX and Ross Stores for off-price market-share capture. Regeneron's target was raised to $824 (from $641) on Sanofi collaboration momentum, with Jim grouping it alongside Amgen and Vertex as reasonably-valued biopharma plays.
- **Final Trades**: Steve Weiss – Cisco; Jim Lebenthal – Cisco; Shannon Saccocia – IDU (utilities); Joe Terranova – Merck (breaking out above its June 2024 high, sees $150 stock).
