The Nasdaq Surge: The Investment Committee's Strategy — 9/22/26

**Overview:** The Nasdaq hit a fresh record high as Mag7 names like Meta, Nvidia, and Apple roared back, even as the S&P and Dow lagged and market breadth turned negative (more 52-week lows than highs, echoing December 1999). Much of the debate centered on Meta's huge two-week rally following its "Muse" AI product launch, plus a lingering bear/bull fight over Netflix and worries about financials like Schwab facing AI disruption.

- **Joe Terranova:** Says the market flipped from risk-aversion to risk-on, but it's concentrated in tech/semis/memory, not broad-based; sees Meta's Muse launch as a genuine paradigm shift ("doubt Zuckerberg at your peril") and expects more upside once earnings confirm monetization. Final trade: **AMZN**.
- **Brian Belsky:** Argues big-cap tech quality names are being "rented" into quarter-end while true long-term "ownership" should favor broadening-out laggards (Microsoft, Alphabet); skeptical of Meta given Zuckerberg's inconsistent capital allocation history, prefers Microsoft's proven monetization; expects Microsoft/Alphabet to reaccelerate into year-end.
- **Josh Brown:** Says the "broadening trade" narrative got flipped — Apple, Meta, and Nvidia all had simultaneous narrative shifts (Nvidia "boring" at 98% earnings growth, Meta finally monetizing AI via Muse/Reels, Apple as the AI distribution gateway). Calls Meta's forward PE (24x) still cheap given its $450B market-cap add in 21 days and floats a re-rating to 30x if Muse scales. Best Stocks in the Market segment: highlights cybersecurity — CrowdStrike (stair-step pattern), Palo Alto Networks (weaker technicals, lower highs), Fortinet ("looks the best," breakout in progress), Cloudflare ("an absolute wombat"); off-list names to watch: Zscaler (golden cross forming) and SentinelOne (breaking above $25B market cap, bad "Sears ticker" karma joke). Final trade: **Micron**.
- **Jim Lebenthal:** Skeptical of Netflix's "value stock" framing given rising content competition (Paramount/Warner consolidation, YouTube); on cyber, likes Cisco as a hardware/software/security combo instead of pure-play names; questions Meta's earnings follow-through. Final trade: **Apple**.
- **Scott Wapner (host):** Frames the Meta rally around Jensen Huang's Super Bowl-week line "nobody does AI like Meta," runs a Feb-to-date Meta chart showing the stock up 22% since the Muse launch two weeks ago, with price target hikes from Jefferies ($875), JPMorgan ($820), and BofA ($810); also moderates the Netflix bear/bull debate (Wells Fargo cut to sell/$57 target on lack of hit content; HSBC cut to hold/$76; BMO outperform/$135; Piper overweight/$85).
- **Guest segment — Kate Rooney on Anthropic:** Reports Anthropic released its newest frontier model, Opus 5.5, performing at the level of its best model (Fable) on most work, but ~40% cheaper to run and ~20% cheaper for usage-based customers, using fewer tokens and generating answers ~30% faster; comes after CEO Dario Amodei's "pace the frontier" comments, with extra cyber/bio-weapon safeguards; Anthropic is reportedly on track to price its IPO in November.
- **Options Action (Oliver Renick, from Cibo/Chicago):** Schwab options volume running 5.5x average with heavy put buying (popular bet: Jan $90 puts, wagering another 13% downside) on fears Meta's Muse AI agent threatens wealth-management platforms; by contrast, Robinhood is seeing bullish call buying (~80% of premium in calls) as its own AI-agent rollout is viewed favorably versus Schwab/LPL.
- **Final Trades:** Josh Brown — **AMZN** (correction: actually Belsky said AMZN, see below); Brian Belsky — **AMZN**; Josh Brown — **Micron**; Jim Lebenthal — **Apple**.
