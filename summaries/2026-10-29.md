**Tech Earnings Weigh on the AI Trade, 10/29/26**

**Overview:** Alphabet fell sharply after its worst-ever post-earnings reaction. Search revenue missed, and the panel debated whether AI is eroding Google's core business. Microsoft's Azure acceleration, the software and cyber selloffs (ServiceNow, Fortinet), and Amazon's earnings after the close rounded out the hour.

- **Alphabet (GOOGL) debate:**
  - Search growth slowed to 12%, from 19% and then 17% in the prior two quarters. YouTube also missed. Google Cloud was a bright spot, up about 103%, though margins were flat. There were 19 price-target cuts.
  - **Josh Brown:** This is the first time Google search has had real competition in its 25-year history. Query growth is at a five-year high. AI Mode queries monetize at a lower rate than search queries. Google has to find a model that earns as much from delivering the answer as from sending the user to Expedia. He noted there has been no buyback announcement for two or three quarters. He also said the companies spent 10 years buying back stock and are now selling it.
  - **Liz Thomas:** She reduced to neutral early in the year. The market is shifting from rewarding AI spending to demanding proof of monetization, which she thinks is a bit early for Alphabet. Alphabet is the only company funding its buildout from its legacy business. She advises patience.
  - **Kevin Simpson:** He owns about 3% of Alphabet. He will wait out the next quarter. He worries that advertising deceleration is the real problem, even as users stay loyal.
  - **Jim Lebenthal:** He trimmed Alphabet again, to about 3.5% weighting versus about 5.5% for the S&P 500. He expects the negative narrative to last for months, and he cited negative free cash flow across the AI complex. He still thinks search is "very fixable". One idea is to truncate AI Mode so users click through to sources. He said another quarter of weak search extends the "penalty box", while about 14% search growth would forgive everything.
- **Microsoft (MSFT):**
  - Azure accelerated to 48% from 42%. There were 19 price-target hikes. D.A. Davidson's Gil Luria raised his target to $650 from $550.
  - **Rob Sechan:** He flagged earnings-quality questions, since about $10B of revenue is tied to OpenAI. Capex was a bit light.
  - **Liz Thomas:** She said Microsoft, Oracle and Meta are "reading the room" by not adding more capex for 2027. The capex delay was only $5B. She was thrilled with the report.
- **Amazon (AMZN)** (reports after the close):
  - **Josh Brown:** Amazon trades at about 22x earnings. He said Azure's number sets a high bar for AWS tonight. He noted revenue growth has accelerated for five straight quarters, and the focus is on AWS and capex. Gene Munster is worried that personalized bots will erode Amazon's role as the starting point for shopping. Amazon's answer for now is its Rufus AI.
  - **Rob Sechan:** He said the group bought more Amazon last week. He is running the ball on the AI theme. He argued contracted demand for data-center supply chains (fiber and similar) stretches "as far out as the eye can see." He also likes fixed income as a shock absorber.
  - **Liz Thomas:** She called the discernment investors are showing the right approach. AI is still early innings, but it is no longer enough to just say "AI." She noted momentum has faded while value, quality and low-vol are leading. She has been pounding the table on Treasuries.
  - **Options (Oliver Renick, Cboe):** Options imply a move of nearly 8%. Over 450,000 contracts had traded, more than 60% of them calls. One trader sold the June 220 put and the June 330 call.
- **Committee move, Kevin Simpson:** He bought Booking Holdings (BKNG) as a starter position in his flagship strategy.
  - It trades at 17x trailing and 13.5x forward earnings, with a dividend increase and buybacks.
  - AI disintermediation fears have hit the stock. He said it could go the other way and told viewers not to follow him in size.
  - It reports next week. Josh Brown backed the idea and prefers Expedia. He argued that hotel inventory is hard to replicate.
- **Software and cyber selloffs:**
  - **ServiceNow (NOW):** Down about 26%. Rob Sechan called it a case of "good not being good enough" rather than fundamental deterioration. The stock is up 60% over the period, and he thinks it's time to buy.
  - **Fortinet (FTNT):** Guidance was light and gross margins missed. The stock is up about 120% and needed a reset. Rob called it a valuation reset and a one-off, and said he is long CrowdStrike. Liz Thomas said she bought CrowdStrike. Both pushed back on shorting cyber and said to be selective. Rob had heard something stock-specific about Fortinet but didn't elaborate.
- **ETF Edge (Dominic Chew, with Bryant van Cronkite of Allspring):**
  - He finds opportunity in hyperscalers and said the Google reaction is overblown. He also likes defense and aerospace (Raytheon) and healthcare.
  - He sees a chance to be a stock picker again.
- **Josh Brown's Best Stock, Okta (OKTA):**
  - Identity is key to the agentic AI future. Security vendors have been buying identity companies, such as Palo Alto's $25B CyberArk deal.
  - Okta has 20,000 customers, including two-thirds of the Fortune 100. Its enterprise value is about $39B, so it could be a takeover target, though he has no inside knowledge.
  - The stock is at $217. A breakout above $235 would be significant. $190 is the line in the sand: a close below it on high volume means he's out. Okta fell only 2% while Fortinet dropped 23%, which he took as a strong sign.
- **Final Trades:**
  - **Liz Thomas:** eBay, a bright spot in consumer.
  - **Kevin Simpson:** Oncology play with room to run (name unclear in the transcript).
  - **Rob Sechan:** COWZ (Pacer US Cash Cows 100 ETF).
  - **Josh Brown:** Shake Shack (SHAK), citing upgrades and price-target lifts ahead of earnings.
