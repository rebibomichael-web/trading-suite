Historic U.S.-China Summit: AI & Trade in Focus — September 24, 2026

**Overview:** President Xi's White House visit dominated the day, with the panel focused on AI regulation/safety talk between the US and China, ahead of tonight's star-studded state dinner. Markets rallied as oil and the 10-year yield reversed sharply lower on a Reuters headline that the US and Iran discussed a phased deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz.

- **Brad Gerstner (Altimeter Capital, guest from the White House North Lawn):** Pushed back on the idea the US needs a global AI regulatory platform, defending the current framework (Commerce Dept.'s Center for AI Standards and Innovation reviews frontier models; cited the Mythos model pull as proof "there's a cop on the beat"). Said the US isn't ceding authority to bodies like the UN but will "coordinate and cooperate" with China. On chips, said he doesn't support exporting leading-edge chips to China but favors limited H200/H20 exports to keep Chinese developers in the Nvidia/AMD ecosystem. Argued US AI models are extending their lead over China's (distillation, not innovation) given 40+ gigawatts of compute coming online in 2027. Big bull on Meta (large position, up 34% this month, best month since 2013) — credits the Muse AI-agent launch as "the next 10x moment" validating the ~$110B capex. Announced his family foundation is giving $250 Trump-account contributions to ~140,000-150,000 Indiana kids born 2023-2024, part of a plan to expand nationally.
- **Stephanie Link:** Bought more Coinbase — likes the $3B of Bitcoin ETF inflows over two months boosting transaction revenue, plus the mix shift to higher-margin subscription/services; said regulatory clarity (CLARITY Act) matters less to her than crypto price direction. Sees market breadth narrowing (S&P up 1% in September, average stock down 4%, Qs up 4%) but still finds value in cyclicals given accelerating economy and strong earnings; expects a year-end rally.
- **Josh Brown:** Advising clients to trim equity exposure that's drifted from 60/40 toward 80/20 or 90/10, given a risk-free ~5% on 2-year Treasuries — calls it "hand-to-hand combat" with clients ahead of Q4. On cyber security's huge run (Okta, CrowdStrike, Zscaler, Palo Alto, Fortinet all up big), says be more discerning rather than chase laggards; favors CrowdStrike (Falcon platform) as the consolidation winner, holding rather than taking profits. Best Stocks in the Market pick: **Lumentum (LITE)** — consolidating below its May all-time high (~$1085) after a huge run; likes a small position now, adding on a breakout above the old high, calling it a key AI-datacenter demand play.
- **Malcolm Etheridge:** On Okta (BofA raised target to $220 from $200 but stayed Neutral), argued the bull case is that every AI agent will need its own machine identity, but cautioned investors to use buying discipline given the stock already up ~50% this quarter/doubled since January.
- **Shannon Saccocia:** Sees markets reading the Iran/Hormuz headline as reducing pressure for an October Fed rate hike; flagged the 10-year's lockstep move with oil. Expects institutions to trim risk/take profits over the next 3-6 months given tough 2027 comps, a declining savings rate, and lower tax refunds/withholding changes; still sees a seasonal post-midterm rally but a more defensive one.
- **Jim Lebenthal-style pushback (Steph vs. panel on consumer):** Steph argued the K-shaped economy narrative is overstated since gasoline is only ~3% of household budgets and wages are rising 3.5-4%; Malcolm countered that rising rates/oil are already showing up as weakness in discretionary stocks (down 5.5% in a month) and could prompt banks to tighten credit.
- **Macro/market move:** S&P turned green, oil fell over 2%, and the 10-year yield dropped off its ~5.12% multi-decade high after the Reuters Iran/Hormuz headline broke live on air.
