Title line: **Rising Yields Threaten the Market: The Investment Committee's Strategy — 8/18/26**

**Overview:** The Investment Committee spent the hour on the sharp backup in bond yields (10-year approaching 5%, 30-year near multi-decade highs) and the rotation it's forcing — out of momentum/growth (semis, memory, software) and into value, with financials still strong but flagged as vulnerable after an 11-week win streak. Housing data came in weak (mortgage rates ~6.75-6.8%, starts and pending sales down), pushing Steph to exit her homebuilders, while the desk also covered a landmark Meta child-safety trial, SpaceX's lockup, and previews of NVIDIA and retail earnings (Target, Walmart, Estée Lauder).

- **Jason Snipe** — Called the yield move a "real rate" story: 10-year approaching 5%, China/Japan pulling back as buyers, inflation expectations, and the Iran conflict all contributing. Stayed overweight banks; owns Capital One ("the best bargain out there") and Bank of America (15% revenue growth, 17% ROTCE, 660bps of operating leverage, IB fees +50%). Likes NVIDIA into earnings on AI-infrastructure investment (stock up 20% in a month). Final trade: **Elanco Animal Health** (doesn't own yet — down 12% from highs, insider buying, market-share gains; also owns Zoetis).

- **Joe Terranova** — Argued rising yields are the fundamental trigger for macro funds/algos to de-risk, driving a broad "basket rotation" (growth vs. value, semis vs. software, long duration vs. real economy, money-center banks vs. insurers) — momentum ETFs MTUM and the memory/DRAM ETF both snapping five-day win streaks. Sees it as one day in a "grand journey," not a regime change unless yields spike another 25-50bp. On housing: builders are protecting margins by pulling back construction, calling it a housing recession without resolution. Owns Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan (via JOT) as financial-momentum names; flagged NVIDIA's CDS up 100% YTD as worth watching, agreed private equity looks vulnerable like financials. Final trade: **Eli Lilly**, about to make a new all-time high.

- **Stephanie Link** — Added "better growth" as a fourth driver of rising yields alongside debt/deficits, AI issuance, and geopolitics, citing GDP and retail-sales trends. Sold **DR Horton and Toll Brothers** — took gains, cited the 30-year at 6.8% (needs ~5-5.5% for real demand) and Home Depot's "frozen" housing comment; would revisit on better rate visibility. Sold **Meta** on spending/leverage concerns (unrelated to the $1.4T child-safety trial overhang) and rotated proceeds into **Amazon**. Continues to add to **SpaceX** despite Thursday's 319M-share lockup (7% of shares) — cost basis down 28% from highs but likes the 91% revenue growth, 191% EBITDA growth, and AI-compute optionality. Owns **ServiceNow** (Capital One PT to $150) and **Snowflake**, both called out as software winners. Ahead of Target earnings: watching operating margin (currently 4.5% vs. 8%+ in 2022) as the key number. Final trade: **Netflix**, on ad revenue set to double again in 2026.

- **Bryn Talkington** — Pushed back on yield alarmism, noting the 10-year has repeatedly tested this range since 2023 and always retreated; called today's move "a little tantrum." Differentiated bank exposure — Goldman for trading/IPOs, Bank of America for the consumer, JPMorgan as diversified — and flagged Jonathan Krinsky's technical warning that financials look stretched after 11 straight up weeks. On NVIDIA into earnings, said $228 looks like tough resistance even though fundamentals argue the stock "should be higher." Liked Uber (Mizuho outperform, $112 target) above 76-77 resistance toward the mid-80s. Final trade: **INFL** (infrastructure ETF), for offense and defense via exchanges, metals, and low-capex energy names.

- **Calls of the Day / notable ratings**: Snowflake to $408 (outperform, Citizens); ServiceNow to $150 (overweight, Capital One); CrowdStrike to $250 from $230 (Benchmark), with Joe wanting a pullback before next week's earnings; Uber to $112 (outperform, Mizuho).

- **Meta trial segment**: 29 states suing over child-safety issues, seeking damages that could reach $1.4 trillion; no panelist currently owns the stock, and Steph noted it's now an overhang layered on top of already-heavy spending.

- **NVIDIA earnings preview** (via CIBO's Oliver Renick): options pricing implies a ~5% post-earnings move, though actual moves have averaged just 2.5% over the last four reports; call buying (2/3 of premium) outweighs puts on the stock even as the SMH sector ETF shows heavier put activity.

- **Mike Santoli's Midday Word**: Called the yield move a "yellow flashing light" that the equity market is managing to stay ahead of so far (S&P holding technical support), and said it raises the stakes for Powell's Jackson Hole speech given the Fed's unclear communication around the move.

- **Final Trades**: Bryn Talkington — INFL; Jason Snipe — Elanco Animal Health; Joe Terranova — Eli Lilly; Stephanie Link — Netflix.
