**Trading the Major Week Ahead: The Investment Committee's Strategy — August 21, 2026**

**Overview:** Stocks closed out a down week with attention pivoting to next week's twin catalysts — NVIDIA earnings Wednesday and Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole speech Friday — as the 10-year yield backs up toward 4.75% and semis, banks, and industrials all posted their worst weeks in months. The panel debated whether Fed messaging or NVIDIA's guidance matters more for the momentum trade, while healthcare and energy stood out as the week's relative winners.

- **Jenny Harrington** argued next week is largely a non-event — earnings are "predetermined" and both NVIDIA and Warsh will only confirm what's already known; she says Q3 earnings reports starting in November are what actually move the market, not next week's headlines. She likes Lilly (overweight, $1,410 target), Abbvie, and Coca-Cola (target ~$100, watching $77 downside line), and flagged Coke's 27x earnings as rich given a K-shaped consumer.
- **Josh Brown** pushed back hard, calling Jensen Huang "the AI Fed chair" whose commentary on 2027/2028 will move dozens of $100B+ stocks (Dell $300B, HPE $74B, Arista $235B, Marvell $200B, Broadcom $1.7T); NVIDIA's expected $92B revenue would be 97% YoY growth. He highlighted his "Halo stocks" Coca-Cola and Monster Beverage (Monster up ~230,000% since 2001 on a hypothetical $2,000 investment; 50-day support ~$47, stop near $41), Cisco's 5% free cash flow yield and AI infrastructure exposure, and CrowdStrike ($240–$250 targets from Loop/KeyBanc) as a buy-the-dip after its worst week since March.
- **Amy Raskin (Chevy Chase Trust)** said NVIDIA numbers will be great but she's more focused on the stock's reaction than the print itself, calling for proof of real AI "killer apps" beyond coding; she's long-time holder of NVIDIA and Broadcom (not overweight), plus Illumina (her top pick, now ~$30B market cap) and Vertex on healthcare's re-rating story. She flagged BMO's new NVIDIA coverage (outperform, $340 target, 57% upside), Micron (outperform, $1,300, 33% upside), Broadcom ($455, 25% upside), and AMD ($550, 17% upside).
- **Rob Sechan** framed the Fed's dilemma as controlling the long end via rate hikes or QE, neither politically palatable, and said Warsh's messaging (not NVIDIA) will drive the momentum trade — a weak-dollar/lower-rate scenario reignites momentum flows into crypto and long-duration names, while runaway rates reverse it. He favors midstream energy (Suncor, CNQ, Devon) over direct oil-price plays like Exxon/Chevron/Shell, arguing $86 oil isn't sustainable and cash-generative names are better positioned for a higher-for-longer rate regime.
- **Featured segment — Energy at record highs:** Josh noted the IEO (independent producers) up 54% YTD vs. XLE's 45%, with 30% of XLE names at 52-week highs, arguing the group isn't overbought despite Jonathan Krinsky's "running on fumes" caution.
- **Featured segment — Options Action (Oliver Renwick):** Covered the crypto/exchange trade after Trump's comments on regulating decentralized exchanges; Hyperliquid-linked perp stock surged 60% this week (owns $2B in HYPE tokens), while CBOE, CME, and ICE dipped on perp competition fears but fully recovered, suggesting exchanges may move toward embracing rather than fighting prediction markets/perps.
- **Featured segment — Mike Santoli's Midday Word:** Said Warsh matters more than NVIDIA next week since markets already know how to price NVIDIA's likely guidance, while Fed policy carries a wider range of outcomes explaining recent yield moves.
- **Final Trades:** Amy Raskin — Microchip preferred (5.75% yield); Rob Sechan — Cognex (CGNX); Jenny Harrington — Ittoo (formerly Vontier); Josh Brown — Toast (TOST), making new year-to-date highs.
