# Navigating the Potential for a Rate Hike: The Investment Committee's Strategy — 9/11/26

**Overview:** The committee debated whether next week's likely Fed rate hike would spook markets or actually stabilize them, with most agreeing the market has already priced in an 86% chance of a hike and would welcome the credibility it brings. The second half featured a heated debate between host Scott Wapner and Altimeter's Brad Gerstner over the AI safety controversy sparked by an ex-Anthropic researcher's public warnings, plus stock-specific moves on Meta, Apple, Oracle, and a new United Health buy from Kevin Simpson.

- **Jim Lebenthal:** Expects a Fed hike next Wednesday but thinks long rates will fall as the market reads it as the Fed reestablishing credibility, not panicking; doesn't think this is the hike that "does in" equities since it looks preventative rather than reactive. On Oracle: likes the 30% revenue growth and better-than-feared free cash flow (-$5B vs. -$10B expected), calls the post-earnings selloff a buying opportunity tied to rising rates/debt-load worries, not AI-bubble fears; also likes Delta Air Lines as the correction looks over with packed airports and rising airfares.
- **Jenny Harrington:** Disagrees the market will "shudder" on a hike — says 88% odds mean it's already digested; frames a hike as confirmation the economy is strong, which is fine. On Meta: still holds it, likes the cash generation (pre-cash-flow margin down to ~0% but "for the right reasons"), trades at only 20x, sees this year as healthy consolidation after last year's huge run, not a broken thesis. Final trade: Kimberly Clark, down ~10% in a month on no company-specific news, now yields 5%+ with "zero threat from AI."
- **Shannon Saccocia:** Frames the hike as restoring proper restrictiveness after Powell's three cuts anticipated labor weakness that didn't fully materialize; worries about long-end yields becoming "unanchored" from AI capex/buyback concerns but sees earnings season as a stabilizing catalyst. On Meta, agrees with Jenny that the market isn't just "consolidating" but genuinely questioning whether Zuckerberg's direction (selling compute) is core to the business. Final trade: energy refiners, still attractive even as energy sector cools.
- **Kevin Simpson:** New buy — United Health, re-entering after being stopped out in early 2025 (exited near $355-360, it fell to $235); likes that earnings look closer to a trough than a peak ($6.38 adjusted EPS last quarter, management guiding toward ~$20/share) and sees healthcare as an AI beneficiary via faster/cheaper drug research. Final trade: initiating Freeport-McMoRan on the recent copper pullback.
- **Rate-hike framing (Wapner):** Argued the market now wants/expects a hike and can handle it — the real risk is the Fed not hiking, which could spike yields and hurt stocks. Cited Bank of America (out-of-consensus call for 75bps of hikes this year starting next week) and Barclays ("better hike than wait"), plus Wharton's Jeremy Siegel telling Wapner the market may "shudder" first but the long bond would react positively to a credible Fed.
- **Meta discussion:** Stock up ~2% on the day (best comm-services performer) on strength since its Muse AI agent launch; panel split on whether the sideways 2026 price action reflects healthy consolidation (Jenny) or genuine market skepticism about Zuckerberg's compute-selling pivot (Shannon, Jim).
- **Apple debate (Jim vs. Wapner):** Apple up 4.5% this week (best since July 17) on strong reception to the foldable iPhone and new phone lineup. Jim holds a half-market-weight core position but won't add new money at 34x forward multiple; would need a pullback below $300 to buy more. Pushed back on bull case that AI-driven services growth (Siri AI) and the upgrade cycle justify the current multiple.
- **Halftime Headliner — Brad Gerstner (Altimeter Capital) interview:** Forcefully rebutted ex-Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxson's viral warning that AI could "kill us all by the end of the decade," calling it "ridiculous hyperbole" from someone who worked there six weeks; argued the AI industry is already deeply coordinated on safety (citing Anthropic's 154-page misuse transparency report, the Mythos shutdown by Treasury/Commerce, frontier-model review frameworks) and that unbalanced fearmongering risks repeating the mistake of shutting down nuclear power over exaggerated risks. Agreed with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent that AI companies have done a "horrendous job" explaining themselves and defended his capital-account/ownership initiative (9 million kid capital accounts created, Michael Dell's $6.25B contribution) as key to addressing wealth-gap anxiety. Flagged China's industrial-scale AI distillation (e.g., Alibaba) as a legitimate national-security risk needing government focus.
- **Oracle:** Shares turned red despite strong quarter (30% revenue growth, free cash flow beat) as rising interest rates raised concern about Oracle's growing debt load (~$80B heading toward $150B); Jim called it a buying opportunity, Shannon countered that any AI-spending contraction at the margin could hit highly-levered names like Oracle hardest.
- **Palantir:** Noted DA Davidson price target raise to $250 from $200 (reiterated buy); Shannon called it a top pick for AI sovereignty/deployment despite a stretched valuation around $166-167.
- **Final Trades:** Kevin Simpson — Freeport-McMoRan (new position on copper pullback); Shannon Saccocia — energy refiners; Jim Lebenthal — Delta Air Lines; Jenny Harrington — Kimberly Clark.
