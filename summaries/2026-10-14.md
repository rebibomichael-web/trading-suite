**Rising Yields & Rally Risk: The Investment Committee's Strategy, 10/14/26**

**Overview:** A hotter-than-expected CPI pushed yields to multi-decade highs (10-year at 5.25%, 30-year at 5.57%). Tech, chips, small caps and banks fell, and the S&P was down about 1% and the Nasdaq about 1.3–1.5%. The panel debated whether this threatens the four-year bull market, how worried to be about concentration in a few AI and tech names, and what the big bank earnings say about the economy.

- **Joe Terranova:** Called the market "unhealthy" because a small basket of stocks is carrying it. Said higher yields make it harder for AI companies to fund their build-outs in the debt markets. Pointed to CoreWeave, down 15% with its bonds near 80 cents on the dollar, and to spillover into Oracle. Said State Street (in the Joe T ETF) fell about 6% on a small miss after high expectations, heading toward its 200-day moving average near 156. On chips, said he isn't emotional and that positioning is crowded into AI. Thinks Mag 7 earnings at the end of October could revive the bull market, but there may be more selling first. Ahead of J.B. Hunt earnings (after the bell tomorrow), said the setup is positive because the 5–10% earnings decline was already guided. Called Expeditors the strongest logistics name, technically and fundamentally.
- **Carrie Firestone:** The S&P is up 19.5% since the late-March 5% drawdown, and Mag 7 names are up 30–40%+. Would welcome a healthy 5–10% pullback. Owns JPMorgan and is troubled by the banks' reaction to earnings. Noted that no bank CEO has described real revenue from AI, only the spending. Said chips could fall about 30% as in past cycles; trimming is fine, but the stocks will probably come back. Her call of the day was NextEra: Morgan Stanley trimmed its target but still sees about 30% upside, and she called it the largest builder of new power.
- **Liz Thomas:** Doesn't like how financials are trading. Called bank earnings mixed, with deal-flow concerns hitting Goldman. Says financials aren't confirming a cyclical economy, expects a rough patch through the midterms, and sees a possible relief rally late in the year. On chips, said long-duration stocks naturally fall when yields rise and fundamentals haven't deteriorated. Said ASML's order interest through 2030 supports that, and that running for the door in a sharp drop is rarely the right move. Likes gold on structural central-bank demand and fiscal indiscipline, and said she's back in after being out in the spring.
- **Steve Weiss:** Calls rising rates a headwind for Goldman and Morgan Stanley. Expects them to be tough holds for 6–9 months, though better than the market on a relative basis. Sees small caps (IWM) as vulnerable. Says 30% earnings growth is already priced in, so he isn't putting new money to work, and a 5–10% dip isn't enough to excite him. Still holds Taiwan Semi (his bigger position) and Nvidia, which he said gives him pause. He sold Netflix, a small position, calling it dead money in a slowing consumer environment.
- **Stephanie Link (by phone):** Trimming Bank of America "pretty big time" and adding to Wells Fargo. Bank of America's quarter disappointed: net income down 13%, trading revenue down 5%, FICC down 17%, versus JPMorgan equities up 49% and Citi up 14%. Likes Wells because net interest income and margin are stabilizing (NIM up 2.4 bps), average loans grew 11%, and it has a path to a 17–18% ROTCE. Called the weakness a buying opportunity.
- **Gold call:** Morgan Stanley reiterated gold as a top commodity pick. One panelist was skeptical, said they don't trust the precious-metals move, and noted December silver down 6% despite higher yields.
- **Mike Santoli (Market Memo):** CPI, rates and especially credit unease in the AI debt-dependent hardware chain put "sand in the gears." Small caps, semis and banks were already in nasty downturns, so the S&P within 1% of its record highs was unusual. Nothing is decisive yet, but rotation isn't rescuing the indexes except into pure defensives.
- **Other data:**
  - The SOXX chip ETF was on pace for a seventh straight down day, its longest losing streak since May 2025, and is still up over 70% year to date.
  - The 30-year mortgage rate jumped 10 bps to 7.56%, about 125 bps higher than a year ago. Fannie Mae raised its forecast for next year's rate from 6.8% to 7.5%.
- **News and CNBC exclusive:** OpenAI told a federal judge that a former employee who returned to Apple took about 100 confidential files on an unreleased device. Apple denies it and says it found no evidence. Morgan Brennan spoke with Lockheed Martin CEO Jim Taiclet and Under Secretary of Defense Michael Duffey at a new Texas missile facility:
  - Lockheed plans to raise Patriot missile output from about 600 to 2,000+ a year, as part of an $8B+ investment.
  - Funding still has to come through Congress.
  - Taiclet said long-term agreements will build backlog and allow margin expansion.
  - Defense stocks are lower since the start of the conflict.
- **Final Trades:**
  - Weiss: buy the 10-year Treasury, which he called a good entry point even if rates keep rising.
  - Thomas: aerospace and defense, which she says is down on expected congressional action while the war isn't over.
  - Firestone: Thermo Fisher, which broke out as research spending loosens.
  - Terranova: Pfizer, at a 52-week high for the first time since 2021.
