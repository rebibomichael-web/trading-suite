Debate: Is a Hike Coming? — September 16, 2026

**Overview:** With markets pricing in a ~93% chance the Fed hikes rates for the first time since July 2023, the committee debated whether Chair Kevin Warsh will go through with it and how markets would react either way. The show also covered Meta's AI safety stance, a failed Senate crypto cloture vote, and rising diesel/energy costs threatening earnings, plus a Thursday Night Football preview with Al Michaels.

- **Joe Terranova:** Thinks the Fed hikes today — bond market already positioned for it (extensive short positioning per JPMorgan/CFTC data), and consumer discretionary is trading awful (38 of 47 S&P discretionary names underperforming, 25% near 52-week lows), so the market should absorb it comfortably. Bought more Apple (6th purchase since late March) on technical strength — stock has recovered all its post-July-earnings losses and he's targeting a breakout above $345. Flagged JB Hunt's warning that $6 diesel and driver costs could cut earnings 5-10% as a sign of broader earnings degradation from energy costs to watch.
- **Steve Weiss:** Believes the Fed hikes but yields fall afterward — thinks the market has already priced in this hike plus another. Sees inflation as driven by a supply shock the Fed can't fix with 25bps. On banks: owns Goldman Sachs, says a steepening curve helps banks/insurers, but cautions rate hikes hit non-financial/non-AI earnings broadly. Bearish on crypto after the Senate cloture vote failed — sees no real business use case, outflows since the vote, and expects the trading vehicle bias to stay lower, especially given Trump's personal crypto wealth ($1.4B) creating conflicts for lawmakers. Final trade: UnitedHealth, plus FTAI Aviation (big buyback, stock beaten down).
- **Jim Lebenthal:** Also expects a hike, citing Warsh's Jackson Hole logic (inflation too high, Fed responsible, rates are the tool) plus strong economic activity (data center buildouts pushing up copper/steel). Predicts a hike pressures 10-year yields down, but no hike would spike yields and hurt equities. Reiterated bullishness on energy (Exxon, Chevron, Transocean, Shell) for the rest of the year as inventories refill post-conflict. On Cleveland Cliffs (called out by Al Michaels): says the company is turning around, projecting $3B+ EBITDA next year on strong hot-rolled coil steel prices (~$1200+), needs one more good quarter to exit the "penalty box." Final trade: Core Inc.
- **Jenny Harrington:** Sees herself between Jim and Steve — a no-hike would spook her as a sign the Fed/government knows something bad, but agrees the post-decision language matters most. Notes the 10-year yield actually rose from 3.73% to 5% over two years despite seven Fed cuts totaling 1.75%, questioning whether a hike even matters for real rates. Coined "yawn and wander" — expects the S&P to keep chopping around the 7,600 level until earnings season provides real direction. Owns and reaffirmed bullish thesis on Meta (free-cash-flow generative, strong earnings growth, moved past the AI-safety "wet rag" overhang); says Zuckerberg's founder mentality makes him best positioned to navigate AI trends versus "professional manager" rivals at Amazon/Alphabet/Microsoft. Also likes AstraZeneca after UBS said the recent pullback (from a failed heart-drug trial) is overdone — attractive valuation, good dividend, defensive regardless of rates. Final trade: Charles Schwab (14x earnings, 20%+ earnings growth).
- **Steve Liesman (senior economics reporter):** Argues the Fed hikes given inflation above 3% for a long stretch, surging oil/diesel prices that may be bleeding beyond energy into broader inflation, and precedent (citing Kashkari) that the Fed does respond to supply shocks. Coined "one and mum" — expects a single hike without commitment to further guidance, as Warsh tries to cool demand into the supply shock.
- **Mike Santoli (Market Memo):** Says markets are well-positioned to pivot either way; the "mosaic" of data (core PCE now above 3%, unemployment down from 4.4% to 4.1%) does support a modest hike, even though it'd only bring rates to 50bps below a year ago. Flags multiple asset classes sitting at "fight-or-flight" thresholds (5% 10-year, S&P at summer range highs, oil at $100). Doesn't expect a huge yield rally even on a hike, and thinks a no-hike scenario would read as "an act of hostility" toward market expectations, causing confusion.
- **Al Michaels interview:** Previewed Amazon Prime's fifth season of Thursday Night Football kicking off Wednesday night with a new stadium (Lions vs. host team), discussed skyrocketing sports franchise valuations (Cowboys near $20B, Angels sold for $4B, Padres for $3.9B), reflected on AI's use of his voice at the Paris Olympics, and — in the show's running bit — flagged Jim Lebenthal's ongoing Cleveland Cliffs position.
- **Options Action (Oliver Renick):** Options traders are notably bearish on energy — XLE puts outnumbering calls more than 2-to-1, heavy volume in $57 puts expiring in a month (implying a 12% XLE drop), though some out-of-the-money $65 calls suggest a few think today's oil selloff is overdone.
- **Final Trades:** Jenny Harrington — Charles Schwab; Steve Weiss — UnitedHealth (and FTAI Aviation); Jim Lebenthal — Core Inc.
