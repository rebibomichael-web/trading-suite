Inflation Risks to the Rally: How to Protect Your Portfolio — 9/18/26

**Overview:** The panel debated whether a hot week for oil, yields (10-year at 5%), and AI-buildout worries threatens the rally, largely concluding the market's resilience (barely off highs) signals underlying strength and rotation, even as inflation fears and Fed hawkishness raise the stakes. Big corporate news also drove discussion — Disney's AI/tech pivot, Apple's new iPhone/foldable launch, and Warren Buffett stepping down as Berkshire chairman.

- **Amy Raskin:** Notes oil is up 30% and the 10-year up 40bps in a month, yet the market has barely budged — a sign of resilience, though momentum is slowing and stocks are set for a churn until AI/Fed/war uncertainties resolve. Owns Apple but isn't adding at current prices; owns Berkshire and isn't worried about its underperformance, calling it a longer-term holding with a war-for-capital advantage. On On Holding/Nike: consumer is stretched, real income has been negative, not a great environment for discretionary brands. Final trade: Itaú (Itaú Unibanco) — cheap at 9x earnings, some risk tied to Brazil's election next month.
- **Jim Lebenthal:** Doesn't see the backdrop as bearish given strong profit growth, solid economy, and rotation into healthcare/energy/financials picking up slack from momentum names. Pushes back on "end of the world" narratives (AI bubble, yield curve control, dollar decline), calling it a wall of worry the market can climb. Owns Disney, intrigued but noncommittal on the new CTO/Character AI hire; still holds for cheap valuation. Owns Apple with a $15 cost basis, won't sell, argues you can't be underweight given the "application layer" opportunity even as he's short-term skeptical of pure infrastructure buildout. On Berkshire: agrees Buffett stepping down as chairman reflects good corporate governance, letting Greg Abel lead without the ex-CEO in the chair. Passed on re-buying On Holding despite the Mbappe/Nike news — needs to see earnings estimates rise first. Final trade: Cisco Systems, calling recent consolidation a setup for the next leg higher.
- **Jason Snipe:** Highlights 52% earnings growth last quarter with a ~28% guide next quarter, still strong even as AI's outsized earnings contribution fades; watching broadening into other sectors. Owns Netflix despite the Wells Fargo downgrade to sell — believes it remains the streaming winner, sees upside from ad revenue doubling and sports content. Owns Apple, happy with the iPhone 18 lineup. Owns Goldman Sachs and Apollo — likes Goldman's IB strength despite softer FICC guidance from CEO David Solomon, and likes Apollo's diversified insurance business despite the alt-manager sell-off. Final trade: Arista Networks (data center networking demand still robust).
- **Rob Sechan:** Argues inflation is "transitory," tied to Middle East disruption, and the Fed did the right thing by hiking; sees this as a healthy reset that lets cyclicals re-rate lower (AI/tech multiples down from 34x to 24x, some to 18x) while insisting AI capex won't derail the market. Says his firm has been "buying bonds like maniacs" as a portfolio hedge now that yields are attractive. Owns Netflix, sees room for multiple winners (Netflix/YouTube) in streaming/sports content. On Apple: says you "can't be underweight" given it "owns the customer" long-term, sparking pushback from Jim and Steve Weiss (who is underweight/trimmed the stock). On financials: says it's not monolithic — capital-markets-heavy banks (JPMorgan, Morgan Stanley, Goldman) should thrive on M&A/trading activity despite this week's dip. Final trade: Broadcom.
- **Bill Baruch (phone-in):** Trimmed Apple from a 14% to 8% weighting in a concentrated 10-name portfolio, citing valuation two standard deviations above its historical median after a 20%+ YTD run; rotating proceeds into Nvidia, Broadcom, and Amazon to lean into the next "risk-on thrust."
- **Julia Boorstin segment (Disney):** Disney is creating a new Chief Technology Officer role reporting to CEO Josh D'Amaro (referred to as "Tomorrow" in transcript), hiring Character AI CEO Karam Deep Anand (ex-Meta, ex-Microsoft) for the post — a sign of D'Amaro's early emphasis on AI/tech just six months into the job, following Monday's naming of a new DTC/Disney+/Hulu chairman.
- **Mackenzie Sagalos segment (Apple):** The iPhone 18 Pro/Pro Max go on sale today, starting at $1,200; Apple is staggering launches (foldable duo next month starting at $2,000 up to $3,200, budget phones in spring) to manage supply, margins, and seasonality; a new leasing program brings the 18 Pro to about $35/month; IDC estimates ~6 million foldable "Duo" units sold this year (~25% share of that niche market).
- **Mike Santoli's Market Memo:** The S&P is hovering right at the key 6,720-ish technical level from June's high after dipping below it post-Fed and recovering; cyclicals (consumer discretionary, industrials, banks) showed wear this week; unclear whether this is a healthy internal pullback before new highs or the start of a breakdown, with 10-year yields at 5% and oil staying elevated key swing factors.
- **Sharon Epperson segment (401k/AI exposure):** Five mega-caps (Nvidia, Apple, Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon) make up ~30% of the S&P 500, meaning even non-AI-themed 401k funds carry AI concentration risk; advises savers to check fund holdings, rebalance, use "bucketing" strategies, and focus on long-term goals over short-term volatility.
- **Final Trades:** Rob Sechan — Broadcom; Jason Snipe — Arista Networks; Jim Lebenthal — Cisco Systems; Amy Raskin — Itaú Unibanco.
