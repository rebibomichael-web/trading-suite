# Wall Street Firms Raise Stock Targets: Your Next Move — August 10, 2026

**Overview:** The S&P sits at record highs after its best week since April, with JPMorgan, Evercore, and Goldman all lifting targets (JPM to 8,000, talk of 9,000 next) on strong earnings, cleaner positioning, and reduced hedge-fund leverage. The panel debated a rare Apple downgrade, a resurgent Nvidia/software/semis trade, Berkshire's post-Buffett capital deployment, and a fresh energy rally tied to oil back above $80.

- **Joe Terranova:** Bullish on continued market strength driven by earnings and deleveraging; sees momentum rotating into energy (JOTE ETF at 10% energy weighting paying off today) and into software as the new "laggard with building momentum," while Apple and Alphabet lose momentum. On Apple: says it's vulnerable to a deeper pullback into moving-average support short-term but will buy the dip long-term, calling it the Mag7 name that stands out over time. Loaded up energy names across the board: Shenier, Conoco, Enterprise Product Partners, Exxon, Kinder Morgan, OneOk, Targa, Texas Pacific Land, Williams Companies — roughly 3x S&P weighting.
- **Kerry Firestone:** Bullish, citing strong earnings (up ~20%), broadening participation beyond the top names (financials, healthcare +15% in 3 months, industrials), and rising retail participation. On Apple: trimmed shares bought near $190, holds a smaller-than-7% position, calls the pullback a "normal cycle" tied to valuation (33x forward earnings) rather than fundamentals. Views energy as easy to analyze fundamentally (Exxon's refining exposure, Shenier's LNG exports) independent of oil price swings. Final trade: Twilio.
- **Anastasia Amoroso:** Notes real-time inflation metrics (Zillow rent data) suggest core CPI closer to 1.6%, doesn't expect the Fed to derail the rally; flags Wednesday's CPI and Jackson Hole as key catalysts. Sees value rotating from expensive memory/semis into hyperscaler free cash flow (~3.5% yield) and software as bottlenecks resolve. On oil: comfortable with prices in the $80s–mid-90s given strong quarter-to-date energy positioning even amid Strait of Hormuz deal uncertainty. Final trade: Blackstone (calls it "on a roll," +16.9%).
- **Jim Lebenthal (Farmer Jim):** Sold half his Apple position after the Jeffries downgrade, agreeing the thesis (memory-cost margin pressure, canceled all-glass iPhone, rising iPhone 18 Pro costs) has merit, though he still holds a low-cost-basis core position; says he'd buy back around $270 but not $200. On Berkshire: pushed back on Michael Burry's bearish take, arguing the operating businesses (BNSF, Berkshire Energy, Precision Castparts) trade near 13x forward earnings and Greg Abel's buybacks/deals (Alphabet secondary stake, Taylor Morrison ~$7B) show good stewardship, even as he concedes the "Buffett premium" has largely eroded since the May 2025 succession announcement. Bought Micron in two tranches (~$1,000 avg, now ~$877), citing an elongated AI chip cycle and cheap forward multiple. Final trade: Transocean.
- **Apple downgrade (Call of the Day):** Jeffries cut Apple to Underperform, target to $263 from $285, citing canceled all-glass iPhone plans for Sept. 2027, soaring memory costs pressuring margins, and reports the iPhone 18 Pro's build cost is up ~40% — one of only 5 sell-rated calls among 51 analysts.
- **Nvidia/AI financing news (Leslie Picker):** FT reported Apollo, Blackstone, Global Infrastructure Partners, Brookfield, Goldman, and KKR are in talks on a $500B AI financing partnership with Nvidia, possibly announced as soon as Monday; Nvidia dipped ~2.6% on the headline (unverified independently by CNBC).
- **Software/semis trade:** Panel highlighted IGV's 8.5% weekly gain, Palantir (+30%+), Atlassian (+40%+), ServiceNow, Snowflake, CrowdStrike, Palo Alto (record high), and Oracle; Taiwan Semi sales up 45%; Micron targets cited include Mizuho $1,375 and UBS $1,625. Options flow (Oliver Renick) showed heavy bullish put-selling in IGV and near-$1B combined premium in Palantir/Microsoft options.
- **Berkshire Hathaway:** Up 2% post-earnings; debate centered on whether the "Buffett premium" has faded since Greg Abel's ascension — UBS raised its target to $604 from $585, while Cowen and CFRA reiterated Hold.
- **ETF Edge (Dom Chu with John Davi, Astoria):** Recommended rotating into AI "adopter" plays outside pure AI — bank ETF KBWB and emerging markets ETF (EEM/IMG, up 20% YTD) as beneficiaries of the AI buildout.
- **Michael Santoli's Midday Word:** Rising yields and oil are capping market breadth even as hyperscalers re-engage to the upside; NASDAQ 100 outperformed the equal-weight S&P by 7 points over the prior week.
- **Final Trades:** Jim Lebenthal – Transocean; Anastasia Amoroso – Blackstone; Kerry Firestone – Twilio.
