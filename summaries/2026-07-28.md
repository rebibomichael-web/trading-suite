Title: Can the Rally Outrun Semi Weakness? — July 28, 2026

Overview: Semiconductors sold off sharply overnight on a South Korea/memory-related flush before staging a partial recovery, while the broader market rotated into financials, industrials, travel, and other non-tech names to hold near record highs. The committee debated whether this is a healthy deleveraging within a still-strong bull market or an early warning that the AI capex cycle is cooling.

- **Joe Terranova**: Called the semi selloff pure deleveraging/positioning unwind, not a fundamental inflection — noted 12% of the S&P is hitting new 52-week highs (JPMorgan, Visa, Target, Best Buy), not just tech. Got stopped out of a momentum Nvidia trade "by a few pennies" right before it reversed higher. Flagged industrial CapEx beneficiaries (Quanta Services, Eaton, Vertiv) with backlogs running ~30% and said he'd be adding on weakness. Bearish near-term on Corning after its guidance miss (down ~60% from its June high, broke the 200-day) — wants to see proof before buying back in. Final trade: Quanta Services (expects a beat-and-raise on its electrical business, better margins, book-to-bill of 1).
- **Stephanie Link**: Argued the capex cycle isn't over — hyperscalers spending ~$800B this year, could hit $1.6T next year given tight supply in memory, compute, copper, and aluminum. Runs a barbell in semis: added Nvidia (18x, cheapest since 2019), holds Broadcom (21x vs. 28x long-term average) and a small new Micron position after its 30% drop. Owns Union Pacific and thinks the Norfolk Southern merger gets approved despite a 2027 timeline, aided by Canadian National's endorsement. On IBM: bought more after the post-pre-announcement 25% drop, arguing the ~18x forward multiple (down from 26x) overprices the guide-down. Overweight discretionary via housing and Dick's Sporting Goods (likes its ~6% comps and Foot Locker integration). Final trade: none explicitly separate — reiterated IBM buy as her committee move.
- **Josh Brown** (joined later): Said the market's ability to absorb a "bubble burst" in one leadership group (every SMH stock below its 50-day, average 30% drawdowns, RSI cratered to 39) without denting the broader uptrend is a hallmark of a healthy bull market. Framed Apple's rally to a ~$5T cap/35x valuation as the "no-brainer pivot" for large-cap/growth managers nervous about hyperscaler CapEx ROI, ahead of Thursday's earnings ($1.89 EPS est. on $108.9B) and Tim Cook's final call as CEO. Cited real-world evidence of AI ROI already showing up off-tech (e.g., Allstate using AI agents to triage inbound emails), arguing this capex cycle may avoid a 1999-2000-style bust. Best Stocks in the Market: Marriott (asset-light, testing the 50-day at $376, sees new highs by year-end) and Howmet Aerospace (gas-turbine/data-center power plus defense/jet exposure, up 41% YTD, approaching $300). Final trade: Netflix ("hanging on for dear life").
- **Calls of the Day**: CrowdStrike initiated buy by Loop Capital, $230 price target (Josh: pole-position mind share among Fortune 500 boards, up 55% YTD post 4-for-1 split, could be a decade-long story; Joe: too consensus, and the real trade right now is rotation into cybersecurity laggards). Steph (holds the stock): thinks $1–3B FCF guide is conservative and the $10B long-term FCF target stays intact as 737/787/777 production ramps.
