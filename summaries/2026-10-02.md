**Trading Stocks as Yields Rise: The Investment Committee's Portfolio Strategy, 10/2/26**

**Overview:** Bond yields eased after a soft jobs report and a light PCE reading. The 10-year was still about 5.26%, and the Nasdaq hit 52-week highs. The committee debated whether to be bullish going into Q4 and traded portfolio moves: trims of big winners, buys in tech, banks and healthcare, and a look at Nike's rough year.

- **Markets/Fed backdrop:** The Dow was up about 176 points and the S&P up about 0.75%, roughly 1% from a record. The Nasdaq and Nasdaq 100 were up over 1%. The CME FedWatch tool showed a 77% chance of a hold, up from 36% a week ago.
- **Jim Lebenthal:**
  - Bullish into year-end. He thinks the 2-year, which is pricing about four hikes, is "way ahead of itself", and that the Fed would be unwise to hike a week before the election. Oil only has to stop rising for inflation to cool.
  - Cited Atlanta Fed GDP at 3.7% and jobless claims under 200K for two months. Agreed with Citadel's Scott Rubner "Q4 reload" note.
  - Buying more Oracle on the Project Jupiter power-delay pullback, calling it a high-beta AI data center play. Buying more Micron: "one heck of a super cycle", about 6x earnings, no debt, buybacks of up to ~10% a year once restrictions lapse at year-end. Micron is up 275% YTD.
  - Still likes CRH (multiple down from 21x to 14x), but it is a 2–3 year hold. Likes Citi for its services business and credit quality. Owns Own Delta and called the airline setup "higher highs and higher lows".
  - On Nike and On: China is the number one cause, as homegrown brands get promoted.
- **Bryn Talkington:**
  - Expects the Fed on hold through December and the 2-year to settle toward Fed funds. Suggests dollar-cost averaging into names or ETFs through midterm volatility.
  - Calls it an "energy" market: XLE is up 40% and QQQ up 21%, versus Microsoft, Google and Meta each up about 8%. She thinks Hormuz tensions will persist.
  - Tesla is one of her bigger positions. Q3 deliveries were 486,532. She called 370 key support and sees upside from a possible SpaceX merger, robots and TeraFab.
  - Owns QQQ and GPIQ, so is overweight tech. Praised Meta's Muse app but "can't own all tech". Holds a Bitcoin ETF and an Ethereum ETF. Thinks crypto's downtrend is over (Bitcoin went from $62K to $85K in six weeks) and likes selling calls on MSTR. Owns CBRE (earnings growing about 25% a year).
  - Sold out of Nike. Said she doesn't buy retail stocks because consumers have too many choices.
- **Amy Raskin:**
  - Cautious longer term: rates stay high without an economic slowdown, which eventually pressures the S&P multiple.
  - Sold BWX. Trimmed Alnylam (roughly tripled since last year, still her largest position), Natera (up about 150%) and CrowdStrike (up over 100% YTD) because of position size.
  - Bought Berkshire Hathaway (a defensive play with capital), Vertex (type 1 diabetes drug, CF franchise), Thermo Fisher, Cognex and Major Drilling (about a $1B market cap, biggest drilling equipment company in North America).
  - Doesn't own Meta because of harm to children, and is unconvinced about monetization.
  - Goldman worries her because last quarter's call was all AI. She prefers European, Japanese and Brazilian banks and just bought Itaú. Owns Snowflake (trimmed too early, up more than 50% YTD, "expensive"). Sold Nike years ago.
- **Malcolm Etheridge:**
  - Expects "playing the old hits" through October, with AI doing the heavy lifting and the S&P 490 not mattering much. Expects an Anthropic IPO in mid-November.
  - Bought Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley after 10–12% pullbacks: "rate agnostic", with wealth management at 45–50% of Morgan Stanley's revenue and an IPO rebound ahead.
  - Owns Digital Realty as one of the safest data center plays. Owns almost nothing tied to the consumer. Warned that Nike's chart looks like his Peloton mistake.
  - Skeptical of Meta's Muse monetization, calling it "a fun toy" while it is free.
- **Stephanie Link (guest):**
  - Bought Meta. It is up 37% off the lows, still 6% below its highs and flat over the past year. She likes Muse (five million downloads in 22 days versus 56 for ChatGPT), its four billion users, the new enterprise hire (ex-MongoDB CEO) and a roughly 20x earnings multiple.
- **Mike Santoli (Market Memo):**
  - The stock market is "outsourcing its worry to the bond market". September was a decent reset in sentiment and positioning. Nvidia hit a record as the "quality defensive semi", and the rally is broader than recent ones.
- **Oliver Renick (Options Action):**
  - IBIT saw over 100K calls bought versus about 50K puts. In MSTR, about $90M of $160M in call premium was seller-initiated, and seven of the ten biggest trades were call sales.
- **Nike segment (Brandon Gomez report):**
  - The stock hit a 13-year low, down about 5%, on a revenue miss and weak outlook. The turnaround is pushed to early fiscal 2028, with big savings not until 2029–30. Morgan Stanley called the profit beat "low quality". Consumer sentiment this week was the lowest since 2014.
- **Other news and calls:** Tesla was up over 4% and SpaceX about 6%. RBC's Q4 list included CRH and Snowflake, and a Wolfe note on REITs was cautious. Bank of America's top money-center pick is JPMorgan. Oppenheimer said bank "trading may be dicey".
- **Final Trades:**
  - Bryn Talkington: Nvidia (a hold above 235 and a breakout of the six-month trend point higher).
  - Malcolm Etheridge: Okta (Muse makes identity security more important).
  - Amy Raskin: Cognex (an acquisition expands its addressable market in machine vision).
  - Jim Lebenthal: Delta Air Lines (expects a new high by year-end, earnings next week).
