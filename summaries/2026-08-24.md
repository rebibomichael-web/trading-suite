Trading the Big Week Ahead 8/24/26 — August 24, 2026

**Overview:** The panel previewed a pivotal week for markets, with NVIDIA's Wednesday earnings and Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Friday Jackson Hole speech seen as the two key catalysts against a backdrop of rising Treasury yields and Trump/Bessent bond-market intervention chatter. Discussion also covered gold, crypto, steel, homebuilders, and Tim Cook's final week as Apple CEO.

- **Jim Lebenthal:** Mocked the "military intervention" bond-market remarks (joked about "Operation Anti-Bond Vigilante"), said it's time to "stop the bluster" and focus on getting inflation/rates under control; wants clarity from Kevin Warsh Friday. On steel, defended his Cleveland Cliffs position as "coming out of the penalty box" after a strong Q2, versus Joe's preferred Steel Dynamics. Praised Tim Cook's quiet handling of politics and operations; called the 2200% stock gain "a hell of a chart" and credited Apple's deliberate, late-to-AI approach as possibly a virtue.
- **Steve Weiss:** Sees a "wall of worry" — Warsh potentially at odds with Bessent on policy, doubts Bessent's measures calm oil markets given Iran's willingness to strike infrastructure. On NVIDIA, expects beat-and-raise but worries about opaque financing commitments, double/triple ordering, and thinks "we've seen the peak in AI euphoria" for now even if highs are revisited later. Bought Moderna pre-market on its news, sold much of it after the stock nearly tripled intraday, watching the EU oncology conference (Oct 23-27) for the next catalyst. Bullish on Bitcoin's rebound (up 14% in a week) given likely pre-midterm legislation, sees room toward ~$125K resistance. Final Trade: **Dick's Sporting Goods (DKS)** ahead of earnings — high-risk but sees value after a beaten-down stretch.
- **Joe Terranova:** Says the AI/memory semiconductor trade needs "a shot of adrenaline" this week from NVIDIA or Jackson Hole; flagged AI capex increasingly funded by debt as a new risk for Treasuries. Cited Wolf Research's Gilead call (target $157, breaking out toward February highs) and named Vertex, Amgen, Lilly as areas of healthcare/biopharma momentum. Bullish Marvell (agrees with Wolf it's the top AI-infrastructure name to own, replacing "copper's position" in the build-out) alongside Stephanie Link's existing position. Disagreed with the Pulte upgrade, citing decelerating revenue growth (+3% trailing year to -12%) tied to higher mortgage rates and rising incentives. On Delta, said Cowen's $105 target implies ~25% upside at ~12x earnings/~7% FCF yield — achievable in 12 months. Bullish on Newmont Mining, says miners are ahead of gold itself and about to break out above January's $134 high. Final Trade: **BlackRock (BLK)** — quietly rallying, close to a 52-week high.
- **Shannon Saccocia:** Said NVIDIA can't just "beat and raise" on data center — needs to address gross margins (memory/input cost inflation), financing-related revenue share, and the open-model question, which could set direction for AI names over the next 4-6 weeks. Flagged data-center political backlash as a growing overhang but ultimately sees prevention of overbuilding as long-term positive for pricing power. Noted the elongated/secular nature of the memory cycle, with Micron near 6x forward earnings looking "way too cheap" if the cycle isn't turning negative; extended the same cheap-cycle argument to NVIDIA (~19x forward, ~35% growth, 0.5 PEG). On Airbnb (Bernstein target $217, "acceleration is here to stay"), noted it's the only one of nine consumer discretionary names in her book with positive momentum, boosted by World Cup travel strength.
- **Christina Partsinevelos (CNBC correspondent):** Previewed NVIDIA's Wednesday report — beat is assumed, so focus shifts to guidance; buy side wants next-quarter revenue guide "well above $109 billion" and the first real read on Rubin chip ramp. Noted NVIDIA has fallen the day after earnings four straight quarters despite beating every time, and shares are down nearly seven straight sessions into the print. Cited reported 15-17% price hikes on Blackwell/Rubin platforms, and flagged NVIDIA's circular financing web (~$500B+ across six Wall Street financiers, backstopping up to $105B of an OpenAI Ohio data center) as a key scrutiny point. Also noted reports of a possible Perplexity investment (30B+ valuation) and $6B toward a U.S. alternative to Chinese AI.
- **Call of the Day / Options Action (Oliver Renick):** A large options trade in GLD (sold $420 calls, bought $430 calls expiring Sept 18, net ~$60M position, breakeven $425) signals a bet that gold's rally is running out of steam even as call-buying flow has otherwise been heavily bullish (4:1 calls over puts).
- **Mike Santoli (Midday Word):** Said with bonds behaved for now, market "dispersion" is the story — semis (SMH) testing technical support while Mag 7 broadly (ex-semis) and an "Apple contra-trade" pick up the slack; thinks last week's bond-market chatter got "a little too loud," bond market was arguably due for a bid, and Bessent's comments haven't fundamentally changed the stock/economy equation, though internal rate pressure remains high stakes heading into NVIDIA and Jackson Hole.
- **Final Trades:** Steve Weiss – Dick's Sporting Goods (DKS); Joe Terranova – BlackRock (BLK).
