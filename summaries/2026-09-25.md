The Run in Rates 9/25/26

Overview: Rising rates dominate the conversation — the 10-year hit 5.17% and mortgage rates topped 7.49%, the highest in two-and-a-half years — while oil prices stay elevated near a possible Iran deal. Despite the rate pressure, panelists largely stay constructive on mega-cap tech and AI spending, even as market breadth narrows and the consumer shows signs of strain (Target, Nike, McDonald's all weak).

- **Steve Weiss**: Sees rates as real competition for equities, not just background noise — thinks the Fed keeps tightening and won't stop after one hike. Not putting new money to work here, though he's nibbled at beaten-down names. Owns Dick's Sporting Goods (added more this week) over Nike, citing better buying power and destination-store appeal; flagged China/tariff risk as a lingering concern but doesn't expect escalation before midterms. Skeptical on bonds as a hiding spot, prefers equities for long-term compounding (~12% avg vs. <3% for bond funds). Bullish long-term on Ethereum as base infrastructure for financial transactions.
- **Kevin Simpson**: Says "resilient" isn't "immune" — quant funds could rotate out of equities into risk-free yields (30-year near 5.5%). Raising liquidity, writing more covered calls to prep for volatility. Trades: added Sentinel-1 (cybersecurity, ~$23, entering in tranches); bought Netera at $355 (now ~$410) on its cancer blood-test franchise, sold half the position for an ~18% three-week gain; sold covered calls on half his Meta position (770 strike, $20/share premium); trimmed McDonald's from 5% to 2.5% of portfolio, citing consumer weakness and no plans to add; holds Costco, notes under-40 membership up 60% since COVID but stock's expensive.
- **Rob Sechan**: Thinks the Fed won't risk breaking the market since it would break the economy; believes mega-cap earnings growth offsets multiple compression, giving the Fed room to tighten. Sees value in bonds now as a portfolio ballast (cites muni tax-equivalent yields north of 10% for NYC taxpayers) alongside — not instead of — equities. Bullish on Meta/the "app layer" owning the consumer, compares to Apple's 36x multiple; watching Meta's push toward the $785.08 market-cap milestone (~$2T).
- **Macro/rates debate**: Panel broadly agrees the 10-year and oil are the key gauges to watch; narrowing market breadth (S&P stocks above 200-day MA fell from 73% to 51% since July 28) framed as "rational," not alarming, since mega-caps (up 16% over the month) are absorbing the pressure better than small caps.
- **Meta/Muse consumer AI segment**: Panel discussed Meta's new consumer AI app driving the stock to near-record highs before a ~3.5% pullback; Piper raised its price target to $875 from $785. Simpson flagged competitive risk from OpenAI/other LLMs following with consumer products.
- **Breaking news (Megan Casella, White House)**: Unconfirmed CBS/Al Jazeera report that US-Iran talks moved into a "technical phase," seen as increasingly positive in Tehran — moved markets (stocks up, oil down) but not verified by the White House; Iran's Foreign Minister separately floated a 7-day phased deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz.
- **Mike Santoli's Market Memo**: Compared the market's reaction to Iran headlines to a cornerback "biting on pump fakes" repeatedly; says any relief trade would mainly help yield-sensitive and median stocks, and doubts oil drops back to $60-70 quickly even with a deal.
- **Options Action (Oliver Renick)**: Heavy bullish call buying in crypto proxies IBIT and MSTR despite the $350M BitGo hack — MSTR call volume 2.5x average, one trade bought 20,000 in-the-money calls for $30M; IBIT traders buying the $52 strike for late October expiry.
- **Healthcare segment**: Simpson likes healthcare/biotech as AI monetization plays; Weiss reiterated bullishness on UnitedHealth (praised CEO Steve Hemsley) and private holdings Timecare (100% revenue growth) and Digital Diagnostics (90%+ accurate diabetic retinopathy screening).
- **Consumer stocks roundup**: Costco beat on Q4 profit but trades at a rich multiple (Simpson); Target cut at Goldman ($1,159→$1,134) and downgraded to equal-weight at Wells Fargo, also cut to $950 from $1,000; Nike downgraded to sell at BofA amid new-CEO uncertainty; McDonald's on a seventh straight losing week (worst since August 2014).
- **Breaking news**: Trump posted that Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent will not be the "AI czar," staying at Treasury instead.
- **Final Trades**: Rob Sechan — Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE); Kevin Simpson — Netera; Steve Weiss — White House (no comment/joke, no ticker given).
