**The AI Trade in Focus: Altimeter's Brad Gerstner joins "Halftime Report" live in San Francisco, 10/15/26**

**Overview:** A special live episode from San Francisco on the AI trade. Dow up big while AI stocks (semis, CoreWeave) fell on worries about whether AI revenue can justify the CapEx, plus higher rates and debt. The panel and Brad Gerstner debated Anthropic's investor-day numbers, AI safety and regulation, and the future of software with Snowflake's CEO.

- **Brad Gerstner (Altimeter):**
  - Says the market's key question is whether AI revenues justify the CapEx. He calls the current move consolidation and says churn avoids bubbles.
  - His exposure is the lowest in three years. After the April–May semis surge he cut AI exposure and moved about 10% of the book each into Meta and Tesla. He is positive on 2027–28 and may start buying in the next couple of weeks as prices come in.
  - On Anthropic: he attended investor day but couldn't share specifics. The "vibe check" was 9–10 out of 10 bullish, and he is more bullish on the IPO. He expects demand over $500B for the offering (SpaceX drew about $350B for a roughly $100B deal) and says valuation (1.5T, 1.75T or 2T) is irrelevant over a 1–2 year hold.
  - Jensen Huang told him on his podcast that labs will do at least 2x revenue next year. Gerstner attributes the Q3 slowdown to Anthropic cutting back on distillation (Chinese models querying frontier models), which would have moved billions from Q3 into Q2. He thinks the frontier labs' lead is extending, not shrinking, because of recursive loops and the most compute. He expects frontier labs to keep pricing power at the top, with cheaper Haiku and Sonnet tiers competing against open source.
  - Run-rate: it's useful but not ideal as a public-company metric. He would rather see customers added, spend and Fortune 500 penetration.
  - CoreWeave: he doesn't currently own it. He says most coverage is misinformation. The 15% yield is the public mark on about 20% of the debt, and the roughly 80% secured by GPUs is cheap. The stock is about $60 vs. a $30 IPO and a roughly $180 peak, and he says "I'm a buyer of CoreWeave today."
  - Safety: he likes Satya Nadella's call for a human-controlled emergency brake. He described a "three-legged stool" of regulation: the Commerce-led frontier model review, the White House Accord on Superintelligence (a non-binding, Sarbanes-Oxley-like approach) and an AI task force led by Jay Clayton. He opposes an "FDA for AI" and says "there is no safety off the frontier" if China gets there first. On Alexander Wang's softer view of China: "trust but verify."
- **Josh Brown:**
  - Says rates rose from June because of reverse crowding out, with heavy AI-related debt issuance. Bonds rallied yesterday when it looked like fewer bonds would come from CoreWeave and the neoclouds.
  - Healthcare, staples and housing plays are working today. His advice is to look elsewhere instead of adding a fifth semiconductor name.
  - Bullish on Snowflake (agentic flows running directly in the data).
  - Best Stocks in the Market: Palantir. The stock has been a roller coaster, with a record high last November, cut in half into June, then doubled. Revenue growth accelerated for nine straight quarters, from 27% in mid-2024 to 93% last quarter. The key technical level is 166, where both September lows held, and RSI is about 63.
- **Stephanie Link:**
  - It's both tech and the other 493 stocks. She is adding to tech on declines and says the economy is growing above 3%.
  - Data points: retail sales were strong, jobless claims four-week average was 198,000 (the lowest since 1969), and the Philly Fed and Empire Fed were good.
  - She called bank earnings a little sloppy but says the rest of their businesses are humming.
  - She says the $1T hyperscaler spend is intact. CoreWeave carries $50B in debt and Oracle $167B, but cash generators like Nvidia, Broadcom and Micron are caught in the selloff and offer opportunity.
  - Long-term Snowflake shareholder.
- **Malcolm Etheridge:** Sees a possible changing of the guard, with value shifting to companies that deploy AI (software and beyond) rather than the chips, neoclouds and hyperscalers. He cited Brian Moynihan saying Bank of America expects about $700M in AI savings starting next year.
- **Kate Rooney (CNBC reporter):** Reported Anthropic's Q3 revenue at just under $17B, up about 45% sequentially (vs. about 140% quarter over quarter earlier this year). It was reportedly profitable on an adjusted basis, with 80%+ margins cited. A roughly $2T valuation has been floated, with an IPO targeted before Thanksgiving. The Street reaction was negative (D.A. Davidson, Piper Sandler).
- **Sridhar Ramaswamy (Snowflake CEO guest):**
  - Calls this the "agentic enterprise" and says software creation has become industrialized. Data platforms get more powerful, and there will be fewer, more effective software players.
  - Edge vs. Databricks: an integrated, enterprise-grade product where agents automatically obey data access rules, with fast time to value (Kraft Heinz example).
  - M&A: the bar for big deals has gone up, so Snowflake favors small deals like Natoma (MCP).
  - News: a Lumen partnership expansion for a private, secure lane that moves on-prem data into Snowflake without the public internet.
  - Gerstner says the stock is up 50% this year and is one of his biggest personal holdings.
- **Coming later (Closing Bell, 3pm ET):** Palo Alto CEO Nikesh Arora, Reflection AI CEO Misha Laskin (new open-source model), a Nobel-winning economist on jobs, and tech investor Jeff Richards.
- **Final Trades:**
  - **Brad Gerstner:** Anthropic IPO, which he expects in "weeks, not months."
  - **Malcolm Etheridge:** CrowdStrike.
  - **Stephanie Link:** Wells Fargo, bought after earnings (turnaround beginning, stock cheap).
  - **Josh Brown:** Netflix as a risk/reward trade at 67 (double bottom, defensive on red days), risking 5 points ahead of earnings next week.
