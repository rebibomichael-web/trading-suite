**Snowflake Surges on Q2 Earnings: The Investment Committee's Software Strategy — September 3, 2026**

**Overview:** Stocks rallied to session highs after Fed Governor Waller signaled he's likely to hold rates steady this month, easing yields and rate-hike fears. The real story was a software/tech earnings rotation — Snowflake's blowout quarter sent the stock up ~21% to a new 52-week high and validated a broader "Sasspocalypse" reversal in SaaS and cybersecurity names, while Broadcom fell on a light revenue guide and semis lagged software for the first time in months.

- **Josh Brown**: Long Snowflake, flagged it on Best Stocks in the Market in late May at $205; UBS raised its target to $500 today. Cited 120–130% net revenue retention and 9,000 customers running agentic workflows in Snowflake's data-governance layer as the thesis. Also pitched Deere (Best Stocks) as an AI double-beneficiary — data-center equipment demand plus AI built into products (8R autonomous tractors, "See & Spray" computer vision) — sees $800 price target, record high today. Called cybersecurity (CrowdStrike, up 4x post-split lows) the most underestimated software comeback, citing the CrowdStrike/NVIDIA Nemotron LLM security partnership and Anthropic's "Project Glasswing."
- **Jason Snipe**: Highlighted Snowflake's product revenue growth accelerating from low-20s to 37% with expanding op margins; net revenue retention 126%. Reiterated buy on Costco (B of A) post sales beat, wants to see focus on younger/value consumers. Likes Microsoft post-earnings: Q4 sales +18%, Azure a $100B business, 30M Copilot paid seats, commercial RPO +84% to $678B.
- **Bryn Talkington (Brenda "Bryn")**: Argued the software collapse ("Sasspocalypse") narrative got the fundamentals wrong; sees IGV heading back to highs. Bought/added Toast, ServiceTitan, Snowflake during the selloff, arguing SMB verticals (restaurants, contractors) won't build their own software and will instead buy AI from these vendors. On Microsoft, prefers its "open model" approach (Anthropic in Copilot) over Oracle's all-in bet on OpenAI. Added to Tesla last week at $360, sees $400 as next resistance ahead of tonight's Cybercab event. Final trade: Capital One (target $227).
- **Jim Lebenthal**: Debunked both the "Sasspocalypse" and the parallel private-credit systemic-risk narrative (Blue Owl, Apollo both up ~30% off lows). On Broadcom's pullback, cited "recency bias" — software historically deserves higher multiples than cyclical semis, and the market is normalizing back to that. On Adobe (reports Sept 10, Barclays raised target to $295): says only "medium hopes" needed given 10x forward earnings, 11.8% projected quarterly revenue growth, and consistent buybacks (share count down 10% in 18 months). On financials strength (XLF at all-time high): driven by IPOs, data-center financing activity (City, JPMorgan, BlackRock). Final trade: ServiceNow.
- **Seema Modi (guest)**: Noted Morgan Stanley says Snowflake's print softened the AI bear case; also positive on ServiceNow and Atlassian for durable moats and rising agent usage. Flagged Palantir as a top IGV gainer over the past month. IGV up ~24% since June lows vs. semis down ~17% over the same period — called it still a "show me" story requiring continued growth acceleration.
- **On Uber**: Wedbush reiterated outperform, $91 target; called it the "cheapest growth stock in the market" at 17x forward earnings, downplaying Waymo competition risk given Uber's own AV-partner investments.
- **Options Action (Oliver Renick)**: Tesla and SpaceX led options volume with bullish flows — Tesla calls outnumbering puts 2:1 (~$900M of $1.3B premium in calls) ahead of tonight's Cybercab event; SpaceX saw large put-selling (e.g., $10M of Jan 2028 175-strike puts sold) alongside call buying.
- **Best Stocks in the Market (Josh Brown)**: Revisited Deere for the fourth time — record high today, Evercore ISI upgraded to outperform; thesis centers on data-center-driven equipment demand plus AI features (autonomous tractors, See & Spray) becoming recurring-revenue drivers.
- **Mike Santoli (midday word)**: Said the market goes into the September Fed meeting essentially 50/50 on a hold vs. cut; downplayed the odds of a hike, noted long-term yields haven't historically fallen just because hike odds rose.
- **Final Trades**: Bryn Talkington — Capital One ($227 target); Jim Lebenthal — ServiceNow; Jason Snipe — Nvidia; Josh Brown — Spotify (approaching a Golden Cross, 50-day crossing above the 200-day).
