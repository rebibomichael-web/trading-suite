**The Investment Committee Prepares for a Pivotal Week, 10/26/26**

**Overview:** Markets were green to start a big week: Alphabet, Microsoft, the Fed and Amazon all report or decide in the next few days. Anthropic's S-1 (ticker ANT, Nasdaq) dropped, and the panel debated what it means for AI financing and the hyperscalers. The show also covered Apollo's roughly 10% stake in Mubadala Capital, two Calls of the Day (EQT, Amgen) and a Lockheed upgrade.

- **Joe Terranova:** Said the week is tied directly to the AI trade, specifically hyperscaler capex and how the stocks react, since good earnings are already expected. He is bullish into year-end and expects a strong November push, but admitted the price action is poor. He said the S&P went from about 77.50 on Wednesday to about 76.20 within 48 hours. He thinks the equal-weight S&P is rotating from defensives toward financials and industrials, and he called the economy "hopping" (GDP tracking 3.5%, unemployment 4.2%). Risks to the market:
  - Oil spiking to $150 on Middle East escalation, which he sees as the real black swan.
  - Financing for AI buildouts. He said the market is up today because that risk eased, with SpaceX issuing $40B of debt and Meta heading to market for about $95B.
  - On the Apollo deal, he said he owns the shares and called the Mubadala stake a smart deal.
  - Large-cap pharma is working (Pfizer at 52-week highs, plus Gilead, AbbVie and Amgen). Amgen reports Nov 3 and Lilly on Thursday, and he sees tech helping healthcare revenue.
- **Steve Weiss:** Cautious and "on guard", leaning with the RBC and HSBC camp calling for a 5-10% pullback. His main worry is the Middle East. He said an oil strike would be a "disaster" and that diplomacy has never paid off. He repeated that stocks peak a year before capex peaks and that tighter financing hurts the weaker neoclouds (Lambda is a survivor). He is still positive on megacaps, calling Alphabet "destroyed" at about $315 and extremely cheap. He likes Meta, and he would buy it if it sells off. He is skeptical of AMD's earnings and noted Broadcom has been "decimated". He pushed back that under the surface the market "looks like garbage" (the average stock is below its 200-day average). On Anthropic, he said he has passed on the private rounds many times for discipline reasons, but he has bought other private names (such as Nibir Robotics, up tenfold).
- **Anastasia Amoroso:** Optimistic on this week's hyperscalers as long as they don't lean on bigger capex numbers. She said investors want to see the cloud backlog (about $2.3T expected) and debt sustainability, and she noted interest coverage ratios are strong. Her view is that the market is becoming discerning about who has debt under control. She also said large cap is where the AI opportunity is. Some of her comments on private markets and Gulf investment, including her firm's offices in Dubai, Abu Dhabi and Kuwait, were not clearly attributed in the transcript.
- **Jim Lebenthal:** Bullish on Lockheed Martin, citing the order book (850 F-35s over 10 years), missile production and the market looking through margin pressure from the production ramp. He said he doubts the $835 target is reached. On Adobe, he called it an "open wound" in his portfolio. It has traded in a $250 ± $25 range since the first-quarter software selloff and recently set a higher low. He will decide after the Dec 9 earnings whether to tax-loss harvest it. He is watching Datadog, Palantir (looks like it's breaking out) and Twilio (reports Thursday) for software outperformance.
- **Anthropic S-1 (Kate Rooney, CNBC):**
  - **Financials:** First-half revenue was about $16.3B (up more than 1,000% year over year), with an operating loss just under $700M that narrowed sharply. It was adjusted-profitable, with margins above 80% and a net loss of $81B, mostly convertible-debt accounting. An amended S-1 with Q3 numbers is expected.
  - **IPO terms:** It could be one of the biggest IPOs ever. The first lockup date is 27 days after listing, with about two dozen more through year one.
  - **Governance:** Founders keep 50.1% of the vote through a Class F share, there are five voting classes, and it is a public benefit corporation.
  - **Risks (about 100 pages):** Model behavior (resisting shutdown, deception, blackmail-like behavior), compute and debt access, and customer concentration (two customers were nearly 25% of revenue). Others are $270B+ of committed spend with Amazon, Google and Microsoft, open-source and China pricing pressure, and conflicts of interest with the hyperscalers.
  - **Scott Wapner:** He highlighted the chart showing revenue per dollar of compute rising faster than spend.
  - **Price cuts:** Kate said Anthropic is cutting token prices but argues usage growth offsets it.
  - **Customers:** A panelist cited 12,000 customers as of Sept 30, up from 950.
- **Michael Santoli's Market Memo:** The S-1 is "at the margin probably a bit reassuring". The market is in waiting mode on the Fed and on how the cloud platforms are rewarded or punished for their results.
- **Apollo/Mubadala:** Apollo is taking a roughly 10% stake in Mubadala Capital, the asset-management arm of Abu Dhabi's sovereign wealth fund (Scott's exclusive). Robert Frank said Mubadala has about $60B in core alternative assets and Apollo has over $1T in AUM. Alternatives are expected to grow from $20T to $32T by 2030. The panel framed it as scale ("capital as a moat") and said asset managers are consolidating. Apollo was up about 2.3%.
- **Calls of the Day:**
  - **EQT:** It is buying Range Resources for about $13B in an all-stock deal at a 20% premium. A panelist who owns it tied it to Appalachian gas feeding Northern Virginia "data center alley". The Amgen call is covered under Joe's bullet: its drug combo cut the risk of death by 58% in aggressive lung cancer.
  - **Lockheed Martin:** Morgan Stanley upgraded it to overweight and raised the target to $835 from $690. Jim and another panelist liked the multi-year US contracts and the reindustrialization push.
- **Options Action (Oliver Renick):** Bullish put selling in IGV (software ETF), which rallied 5% last week ahead of Microsoft and ServiceNow earnings. About $1.5M of Dec 18 $107 puts were sold, plus about $800K of mid-January $105 puts and about $100K of $130 calls.
- **Final Trades** (attribution is partly unclear in the transcript):
  - Weiss: Lockheed Martin (transcribed "Lightos"; he said the defense trade has legs).
  - Exxon Mobil ("excellent mobile", reports Friday). The Scott "Farmer Jim" banter suggests this is Jim's pick.
  - A data center name that is down a lot with only 1% vacancy, a turnaround after the midterms. The name was garbled in the transcript.
  - eBay, into the end of the week after earnings.
