**Amazon in Focus as the Nasdaq Slides – Friday, October 30, 2026**

**Overview:** Tech sold off hard (Nasdaq down roughly 2.5–3%, a three-month low) after Amazon and Intel earnings. The panel debated whether AI spending is peaking or the market is only pausing. Healthcare and biotech hit record highs, and a hawkish Fed comment added to the cautious tone.

- **Jim Lebenthal:**
  - Trimmed Alphabet yesterday, expecting it to stay in the "penalty box." He wouldn't be surprised if the money goes back into it.
  - Isn't flinching on Amazon. He cites Jassy's view of AWS as a $1 trillion business and sees valuations as forgiving, with no sign capex is letting up.
  - Thinks the search and ad worries are solvable, for example by giving half an answer and making users click through for the rest.
  - Says Microsoft's edge is positive free cash flow. It recently did a $25B debt deal that was quickly absorbed, unlike Oracle's and Meta's.
  - Sold Caterpillar; the stock is down roughly a third since. He also says he "almost threw in the towel" on Disney before its last earnings report and is glad he didn't.
- **Jason Snipe:**
  - Owns Amazon and would "lean in." He cites AWS growth of 39% (the whisper number was in the low 40s) at a record 13.9% margin.
  - Backlog grew from $497B to $578B, and Graviton and Trainium are 20x last year's level. The 2026 capex guide of about $200B was reiterated.
  - Notes Jassy gave roughly two years of AWS guidance, with 40%+ growth.
  - On the market, says the defensive rotation (staples, financials, energy) is mixed. He advises checking multiples and reshuffling the deck.
  - Says power and grid names like GE Vernova (upgraded to buy, HSBC target $1,110) still have a long way to build.
- **Steve Weiss:**
  - Sold most of his Nvidia today. He argues the AI cycle's equity growth has peaked and ROI is "elusive."
  - Sees over-ordering in memory, chips and data centers, and calls take-or-pay contracts "a figment of people's imaginations."
  - Dislikes Nvidia's circular and vendor financing. He says private markets are struggling to raise capital.
  - Argued with Lebenthal over Microsoft's subscription model versus free cash flow.
- **Bryn Talkington:**
  - Says skepticism is healthy because it dampens the bubble. Much money will be wasted, but the cloud businesses at Amazon, Google and Microsoft remain leveraged to AI.
  - Notes Nvidia is about to hit its 200-day moving average and says the sell-offs look overdone.
  - On Apple, expects a strong quarter (revenue up 10%+) but no big stock move. It's John Ternus's first report as CEO, and she wants news on the Google–Apple Siri partnership.
- **Joe Terranova:**
  - Was stopped out of Twilio at $188 (stock down about 17%) after a disappointing outlook. The loss erased a decade of gains.
  - Admits he was wrong that positioning was clean and that earnings would bring a return of optimism. Supply constraints mean more spending, so he plays defense.
  - Still thinks November could bring a return to optimism, and sees the market rotating among sectors rather than into cash. He sees it rotating toward healthcare and energy.
  - Breadth is weak: about 60% of S&P constituents are at least 20% off their highs, while the S&P is only about 3% off its high.
- **Healthcare/biotech:**
  - Healthcare is having its best year since 2021, and the IBB (biotech ETF) hit a record high. Biotech is up 29% year to date, and M&A is picking up.
  - Snipe sees continued juice in the trade. Biotech offers growth investors can't get in AI right now.
  - Talkington had dinner with Moderna's CEO and cites 39 pipeline programs and strong combo-vaccine data. The stock is having its best year ever, and Moderna reports next week.
  - AI could help drug trials skip early phases.
- **Fed (Steve Liesman):** St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem said further policy firming "will be necessary and soon," calling policy accommodative. He sees inflation as too high and broad, with risks to the upside. Hikes are most likely, and AI should remain a tailwind. This was the first Fed speaker after the blackout period.
- **Calls of the Day:**
  - Visa: Macquarie raised its target to $465 from $420, overweight. Talkington likes its 60%+ margins and consistency.
  - JPMorgan: Goldman raised its target to $405 from $392. Lebenthal calls it the best of the bunch, worth its premium.
  - GE Vernova: upgraded to buy, HSBC target $1,110.
  - Disney: UBS buy, $133 target.
- **Mike Santoli's Market Memo:** The AI worries have been in the market for 4–5 months. The S&P is down about 0.3% on the week and is testing the bottom of its range, which goes back to June 2. The Nasdaq 100 is down 9%. Defensive leaders like Lilly, Walmart, Costco and Berkshire mean the market is supported but capped. Equal-weight consumer discretionary is up 2.5% this week. He asks whether a washout is needed, and expectations for a post-election relief rally are high.
- **Options Action (Oliver Renick):**
  - There is no slam-dunk hedge for the AI trade. VIX is near 20, and VIX options volume is about 80% above average with heavy call buying.
  - TLT calls are outpacing puts 2 to 1. Energy is working, while gold and silver are not. Flows are still leaning bullish in GLD and SLV.
  - Bitcoin is down 4.5% after the Binance hack, and IBIT options are leaning bearish.
- **Market movers:**
  - Nvidia is down almost 5%, AMD down nearly 12% and Marvell down more than 10%.
  - Dell, Arista and Qualcomm are lower, and Intel had its worst day since 1974 on a weak guide.
  - Amazon is down about 2–2.5%, well off its pre-market low of about 7% down. About 10 price-target cuts and a few hikes came in today.
  - An FT report that Anthropic may not secure an investment-grade rating after its IPO, per Blackstone, may have driven the last leg down.
  - Apple is the best mega cap over the past month, up more than 4%, and reports Monday.
- **Final Trades:**
  - Jim Lebenthal: eBay
  - Bryn Talkington: GPIQ
  - Jason Snipe: "ATV" (garbled in the transcript; ticker unclear)
  - Steve Weiss: unclear. The transcript has a garbled "FT AI" remark.
