**Will the AI Trade Resume? The Investment Committee Weighs in, 10/21/26**

**Overview:** The panel debated Citadel's Scott Rubner's view that the midterms will clear the way for AI stocks to outperform again. Terranova wants to buy the pullback, while Weiss, Harrington and Firestone want a selective approach based on valuation and earnings. Meta's earnings after the close were the main near-term test. Netflix's sharp drop, a Circle CEO interview and Harrington's new book rounded out the hour.

- **Joe Terranova (bullish on AI):**
  - Agrees with Rubner: hedge funds and PMs have "run for cover," positioning is light, and they will have to rebuild it in early November. He expects a strong close to 2026.
  - He plans to add a semiconductor-equipment name tomorrow.
  - He wants a narrower, quality-led AI trade: Vertiv, Quanta Services, GE Vernova, KLA, Lam Research, Marvell, Corning and Micron. He avoids CoreWeave and other high-beta names.
  - Says Vertiv's revenue growth has been consistent (about 22% over three years, 26% over two, 30% over the last year, 24% last quarter).
  - On the pullback: "I'm recognizing you're getting a correction opportunity in the AI trade."
  - Alphabet: Gemini 3 and Google Cloud justify the spending. He expects a catch-up phase in 2027 after a year of lagging the other Mag 7 names.
  - Valero (call of the day): reports tomorrow. Refining margins and diesel and gasoline supply are the drivers.
    - It trades at a high-teens multiple with historic cash flow.
    - Stay in it and trail a stop. He thinks $500 is plausible.
    - Exxon is mainly a function of crude prices.
  - Zoom (Oppenheimer upgrade to outperform, PT $130):
    - He says longs should move to the sidelines, since software is challenged in 2026.
    - The upside case is its 2023 ~$50M Anthropic stake, now worth billions, to be realized if Anthropic goes public.
  - Emerging markets: skip China. South Korea's Kospi has been the place to be but is volatile, and he wants semis to bottom first.

- **Steve Weiss (cautious, selective):**
  - Disagrees that everyone has fled AI. He sees rationalization of position size and quality instead.
  - Doesn't expect the election to clear things up. Anthropic's IPO, rates and "circular funding" matter more. He cited Anthropic needing about $750B over the next few years.
  - He prefers cash flow: Nvidia is about 10x next-year EBITDA, while CoreWeave, Anthropic and OpenAI have no EBITDA multiple. He avoids those three.
  - He trimmed or traded out of Vertiv ("I just made my money in it," bought $45, now ~$240) and some other AI names. He says capex peaks about a year out and markets discount that a year ahead.
  - Meta is still one of his largest positions. He doubled it at $550 (it had been over $800), then traded out of the add-on and kept the core. He expects a capex increase tonight. He also owns Moderna, which has more than doubled.
  - Taiwan Semi remains a holding. He called shaving it six months ago a mistake.
  - Netflix: he sold in the $80s and avoids all the streamers.

- **Jenny Harrington:**
  - Says "AI trade" is too broad. Investors have grown more discerning: Palantir and Nvidia are up only about 14% YTD, Micron is up ~200%+, and Oracle is down ~30%.
  - The S&P trades below its 10-year average multiple because of skepticism about earnings.
  - Meta: no one uses AI better. She expects ~24% earnings growth this quarter at about 21x earnings, with double-digit growth ahead.
  - If Meta's report is good, she sees AMD as a follow-on trade, since about 5.5% of AMD's revenue comes from Meta.
  - Marriott (Morgan Stanley target $403, stock at ~$405): a permanent compounder she's owned since 2013. At 31x earnings and a 2.5% FCF yield she wouldn't add new money, but she's holding.
  - Northrop (Baird outperform, PT $675):
    - It trades at 18x earnings with a near-5% FCF yield and is down ~5% YTD.
    - She expects high-single-digit earnings growth and cites a $110B pipeline. She likes the reliability.
  - Co-authored the book *Investment Wisdom for Our Friends and Loved Ones* with Rebecca Patterson. It has 25 chapters and 23 contributors, and comes out Nov 17. Author profits go to the Council for Economic Education and the Boys & Girls Clubs.

- **Kerry Firestone:**
  - Sees a pullback and retest, not a clean "clearing event." There's about $30T of market cap in the top 10 S&P names, so it takes a lot of money to move them.
  - Watching Meta's capex, which has weighed on the trade. P/Es have come down: Nvidia is in the teens, Google in the low 20s, and Apple is the only big name with a 30s multiple.
  - Says the market is stuck between those expecting a "day of reckoning" and those who would buy a 20% drop. The S&P has been flat for months.
  - Netflix: its revenue and cost squeeze is real, and the ad business needs content. Wrote a book chapter on sensible risk-taking rules for new investors.

- **Meta and Alphabet:** Meta reports after the close. Mizuho named Alphabet its favorite near-term internet pick, and Truist raised its target to $420 ahead of earnings.

- **Netflix:**
  - Shares are at their lowest since August 2024 and are down 47% over the past year. It is the sixth straight quarter of falling on earnings.
  - The Q4 revenue outlook was weak, and 2026 ad revenue guidance was cut to $2.7B from $3B.
  - Wells Fargo (underweight) warned that falling engagement leads to fewer logins and then cancellations, and called the chief content officer change a "canary in the coal mine."
  - Panel view: it's broken, and even at 18-19x earnings it isn't cheap given the growth direction.

- **Guest, Circle CEO Jeremy Allaire (interview by Seema Modi):**
  - New OCC rules implementing the GENIUS Act, which takes effect in January, give digital dollars a clear federal framework.
  - He doesn't expect a Democratic House to reverse it, since it is bipartisan law (70 Senate votes).
  - He doesn't follow Bitcoin's weakness, and says stablecoins are payment tools, not speculation.
  - He says an AI "agentic economy" needs machine-built infrastructure like Circle's Arc blockchain. Circle was up ~6% after a Needham target raise.

- **Options Action (Oliver Renick):**
  - The KWEB China ETF fell 2.5% to near one-year lows, but traders bought about 24,000 calls against under 4,000 puts, on 2.5x average volume.
  - The most popular contract was the $24 December call, which needs about a 6% bounce to break even. The $28 call costs 14 cents and needs an 18% rally.
  - Renick reads it as hope for support at the lows, not a trend reversal.

- **Other news (Frank Holland):**
  - Envoy Steve Witkoff drew criticism for calling Putin a "humanitarian" after Russia freed an American teacher.
  - Riyadh's King Khalid airport reopened for domestic flights after Houthi strikes.
  - A chemical tank explosion in Toledo, Ohio forced evacuations.

- **Final Trades:** The transcript doesn't label speakers for these, so attribution is inferred from order.
  - **Weiss:** FTAI Aviation, which a broker just initiated and sees doubling in a year.
  - **Harrington:** Albertsons, a 5.1% yield that looks cheap, with a couple of potential catalysts.
  - **Firestone:** NextEra, a great utility. A Democratic House could revive the "sustainability" theme, which would help it.
  - **Terranova:** active brokers. Q3 lacked trading volume, and he expects it to return in Q4.
