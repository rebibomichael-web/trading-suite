AI Safety Concerns Push Tech Stocks and the Market Lower: Your Next Move — September 14, 2026

**Overview:** Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's weekend essay proposing a slowdown in AI development to prioritize safety rattled tech and semiconductor stocks, compounding pressure from the 10-year Treasury yield briefly crossing 5% for the first time since October 2023. The committee debated whether this marks real rotation risk or just noise ahead of Wednesday's Fed decision, with oil-driven inflation, AI infrastructure spending, and midterm-election politics all complicating the picture.

- **Kate Rooney (correspondent segment):** Detailed Amodei's essay calling for third-party evaluators, safety standards, and international coordination on AI, citing fears about faster-than-expected AI progress and an OpenAI agent-hacking incident at Hugging Face. Elon Musk and Sam Altman both publicly agreed with Amodei; Altman told Fortune an OpenAI IPO now would be "ill-advised," eyeing 2027, though sources say a 2026 listing is still likely. Trump slammed Amodei, blaming a "sick conspiracy" against AI/data centers benefiting China. Investors told Rooney the safety push could actually help Anthropic's IPO positioning (lower regulatory/liability risk) and that Anthropic has posted back-to-back adjusted-profitable quarters.
- **Joe Terranova:** Sees a rotation story, not a game-changer — SMH never reclaimed its June high and momentum stalled, pushing money into Mag7 (Apple near its all-time high ~$340) and software/healthcare. Flagged the 10-year's sharp intraday reversal off 5%. Called broken-momentum AI-power industrials (GE Vernova, Verda, Quanta, Eaton) "in a much more perilous position" than three months ago; sold Caterpillar in the last rebalance. Wouldn't buy Lululemon near a 52-week low after Morgan Stanley cut its target to $450 from $650. On the Fed: expects hikes to be followed quickly by market pricing of cuts.
- **Stephanie Link:** Doesn't think Amodei's essay changes the game; sees the market's real driver as muscle-memory rotation back into Mag7/hyperscalers as market-cap-weighted S&P catches up to equal-weight. Says cybersecurity is the long-term buy out of AI-safety jitters given persistently high demand.
- **Steve Weiss:** Argues oil is the bigger story — a supply shock (not overheating demand) that Fed hikes can't fix, and one that will hit margins and earnings broadly, not just semis. Skeptical of Trump's repeated claims Iran is close to a deal. Says he won't put new money in here; cited Broadcom's post-earnings breakdown (bounced to $410, now back to $346) as a cautionary tale on high semis valuations despite owning Netflix (raised back a position, likes it as a "value player" among streamers, not expecting $180 soon).
- **Jim Lebenthal:** Notes his mega-cap holdings (Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft) are acting as ports in the storm; thinks the market ultimately "crescendos into year-end" on strong profit growth and a solid labor market, though midterms are a headwind and Bessent's "grow into" the deficit rhetoric is dubious. On Delta: still likes it despite an oil-price offset to strong travel demand — planes are packed, ticket prices rising per CPI. Sticking with Disney despite Rothschild's price-target cut to $125 from $160, citing 14x forward earnings for a strong brand.
- **Fed/rates debate:** Weiss and Liz Thomas (Link) sparred on hike mechanics — Weiss and Terranova expect a Wednesday hike is priced in and good for banks (Wells Fargo's Mike Mayo bullish on JPMorgan, Bank of America, Citi); Link argued a hike would be a "mistake" reacting to markets rather than the economy, comparing Kevin Warsh's committed 2% inflation stance to the 1999 hiking cycle rather than 1997's one-and-done. Lebenthal thinks the Fed would be "crazy" to hike the week before midterms but expects hikes anyway given persistent above-target inflation and diesel-driven cost pressure with no near-term supply relief.
- **Calls of the Day:** Citigroup bullish on gold, eyeing $4,800/oz in three months — Link (owns iShares Gold Trust/IAU) reiterated bullish stance below $4,300 as a geopolitical hedge, while Terranova is skeptical of the recent bounce, seeing risk of a fall back toward June/July lows. Morgan Stanley cut Lululemon's target to $450 from $650 (Terranova: broken momentum, avoid until it stabilizes). Rothschild cut Disney to $125 from $160 (Lebenthal: buyer at that level). Evercore raised Netflix to $110 from $100 (Weiss: downside removed, upside limited near-term).
- **ETF Edge (Dominic Chu w/ Justin Schach, Rosenblatt Securities):** Retail trading is concentrated in leveraged/inverse and single-stock ETFs plus Bitcoin products — 8 of the top 10 most-active ETPs in August were leveraged/inverse, and 5 of the top 10 most-active stocks overall were leveraged single-stock ETFs or Bitcoin.
- **Options Action (Oliver Renick, Cboe):** VIX popped to 18 with call-buying volume more than double the 30-day average, including $2.7M in VIX 31-strike calls expiring mid-November — traders treating the AI-safety story as more of a "crash insurance" trigger than the bond selloff was on Friday.
- **Mortgage update (Diana Olick):** 30-year fixed rate jumped to 7.17%, the highest since January 2025, up 29 basis points in a week as the 10-year crossed 5%.
- **Final Trades:** Jim Lebenthal — AstraZeneca; Steve Weiss — Lockheed Martin; Stephanie Link — long the 10-year Treasury at a 4.94% yield; Joe Terranova — Expeditors International (EXPD), at a 52-week high.
