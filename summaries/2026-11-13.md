**CNBC Halftime Report: "NVIDIA Fallout, Chip Volatility and the Case for 60/40" (Friday, Nov. 13, 2026)**

**Overview:** Frank Holland sat in for Scott Wapner. The show centered on Nvidia's sharp drop after CEO Jensen Huang's resignation and the FT report that the board has approached Arm CEO Rene Haas as a successor. The panel also covered chip-sector volatility, next week's retail earnings, and whether higher bond yields strengthen the 60/40 portfolio. The S&P was down about 1% on the day but up about 2% for the week, and the 10-year yield was about 5.13%.

- **Market and news backdrop:**
  - Nvidia was down about 3.5% on the day and more than 14% on the week. The stock is off about 30% from its high and down nearly 20% this month.
  - The FT reported the board approached Rene Haas. Interim CEO Colette Kress, the CFO, is in place, and earnings are Tuesday.
  - Kalshi odds on Haas rose from 35% after the report.
  - Analysts cut price targets sharply. Truist went from $346 to $174, and HSBC went from $365 to $170.
  - AMD, Broadcom and Micron were all down on the day.
  - Bank of America said U.S. equities saw their largest inflow since June.
  - Mike Santoli said the market remains in a "soggy zone" and is still waiting on a clearing signal, possibly the Anthropic IPO. He dislikes the retail buying of Nvidia and would prefer capitulation.
  - Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, speaking with Jamie Dimon, called the roughly $900B compute commitments large but said Anthropic buys "on the low side" and can buy more on the spot market.

- **Jim Lebenthal (bull on Nvidia):**
  - He is not selling and is putting new money into Nvidia. He cites free cash flow above $300B and a forward multiple of about 10.
  - He said the stock will see a bull-bear tug-of-war for the next week or two, and more pain is likely. He does not call $159–160 the bottom.
  - China is about 11% of revenue by one estimate (others say 20%), and export controls will curtail it. He expects the bulls to win long term.
  - He sees a slower data-center build-out as healthy, because it lets hyperscaler cash flow catch up to capex. He expects Anthropic's $60–80B raise to flow through to chip names.
  - On Cisco, he said the stock fell because the market wanted a bigger guide-up. He argues it is a high-teens-multiple stock rather than a mid-to-high-20s one. He has held it since 2015 and said it beats the S&P without much drama.
  - On Apollo (UBS top pick), he said the private-credit and data-center-financing worries are overblown.
  - He used "punkish" as a running joke all show.

- **Jason Snipe:**
  - On Nvidia, he cited $115–117B of quarterly revenue, 70%+ margins and a roughly 10x forward multiple. He said it would be a buy if you removed the name, and he has held it since 2023.
  - He expects volatility from the investigations and open questions about government complicity.
  - He likes Arista Networks (ANET) as the clearest beneficiary after Evercore's upgrade. His one concern is customer concentration, with Microsoft at 26% of revenue and Meta at 16%.
  - He likes Palo Alto (KeyBanc upgrade to Overweight, $380 target, about 18% upside). The stock is down about 20% in a month, partly on rumors that CEO Nikesh Arora could go to Nvidia. He thinks the identity-security push and $30B of acquisitions give it a long runway.
  - On Apollo, he said private credit is "totally overblown." The stock is down about 15% year to date. He also likes the Athene insurance business.
  - He owns AutoZone, which is down about 3% this year. He is excited about commercial sales.
  - He owns Costco, up about 28%, with a $4B buyback and a 90% renewal rate. Bank of America raised its target to $1,257 from $1,095.

- **Kevin Simpson:**
  - He sold half of the firm's Nvidia position on a price-based rule, entering near $200 and exiting in the $160s, for a roughly 20% loss. He said he likes the fundamentals but follows rules, not emotion. If the price falls further, they will probably sell the rest.
  - He thinks the street would prefer an outsider as CEO but expects departures either way. He said "where there's smoke, there is fire."
  - He owns Broadcom and AMD in the growth portfolio, and said Broadcom's target of just under $400 may be a little low.
  - He owns Apple at a roughly 37x multiple with a full allocation. A $350 call expired unexercised, and the stock is near $360.
  - He added to Caterpillar at $581 as an AI-adjacent power, mining and industrial play. He has owned it more than a decade, and it is up about 6.5% on the week.
  - He likes Robinhood at about $77 (Deutsche Bank buy), expects a return to triple digits, and sells calls against it.
  - He sees little to be enthusiastic about at Home Depot, which is up about 19% in a month, and wants lower rates or a spending super-cycle. Walmart (Wells Fargo target raised from $120 to $135) is a quality name but expensive to chase.

- **Liz Young:**
  - On Nvidia, she respects Kevin's discipline and notes the opportunity cost. She sees an overhang for at least a few weeks and more news to come, plus "damage control" for AI and semis generally.
  - She said the SMH and SOXX are positive on the week, but she does not see a catalyst to reclaim summer highs. She thinks the market would view a government-forced capex slowdown negatively.
  - She notes the 12 months after midterms are usually positive, but the third year of the presidential cycle is typically tough for tech. She calls 2027 a "transition" year with tighter policy, a later economic cycle and hot inflation.
  - She is bearish on discretionary retail because consumer sentiment is poor and oil is high. Value-focused retailers may hold up better. She cited the highest PPI in four years.
  - On the 60/40, she likes treasuries and an equal-weight S&P for the 60. For the 40, she would limit investment-grade and high-yield credit and add commodities or gold. She warned that 60/40 would struggle in stagflation.

- **Other segments:**
  - **Committee move:** Kevin added to Caterpillar (above).
  - **Calls of the Day:**
    - Palo Alto: KeyBanc upgrade to Overweight, target $380.
    - Robinhood: Deutsche Bank maintains buy.
    - Apollo: UBS top pick.
    - Cisco price targets: Citi $139 to $120, Goldman $125 to $115, Wells Fargo $150 to $130.
  - **Retail earnings setup:** Home Depot reports Tuesday and Walmart Thursday. Bernstein likes TJX and AutoZone.
  - **News:**
    - The FBI seized about $1.2B of crypto stolen from Binance.
    - Baker McKenzie is moving toward value-based billing because of AI.
    - SpaceX is scheduled to launch its 15th Starship test flight today.
    - Swiss dental company Vivardis opened on the NYSE about 21% lower at about $14.30.

- **Final Trades:**
  - **Liz Young:** the 10-year Treasury, which she would take at 5.13%.
  - **Kevin Simpson:** Caterpillar. "AI needs more than chips. They need power."
  - **Jason Snipe:** Amazon, on 39% AWS growth.
  - **Jim Lebenthal:** eBay. "This is a stock that wants to go higher."
