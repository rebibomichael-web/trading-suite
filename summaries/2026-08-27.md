**Trading Nvidia's Mega Moment: The Investment Committee Weighs In — 8/27/26**

**Overview:** Nvidia's blowout report, including a 70% revenue growth guide for 2028 against Street estimates near 44%, sent the stock up about 9%. The panel debated what that means for the AI trade, since the rest of the chip and mega-cap group was muted, and whether the data-center backlash ahead of the midterms is a real risk. Software and cybersecurity had a big day (Salesforce, CrowdStrike, Okta), and the panel also covered Blackstone, Regions Financial, Nike and Airbnb.

- **Scott Wapner (host):**
  - Said the Street was at 44% for 2028 growth versus Nvidia's 70% guide. Gross margins were in line, and the guide could have been higher without supply constraints.
  - Noted 16 target hikes, with Raymond James at $515, apparently the Street high (up from $352).
  - Said the stock had been down 8 of 9 days going in.
  - Pushed the Apple-style installed-base and buyback analogy. Jensen Huang told Jim Cramer he expects buybacks to rise and called the stock "a tremendous opportunity."
  - Raised the data-center backlash: a WSJ poll found 71% would oppose a local data center versus 53% for a nuclear plant. He also cited Shapiro, Abbott, and Rogers, who backs a one-year moratorium in Michigan.
  - Asked whether Caterpillar and similar names need rethinking if build timing shifts.
- **Josh Brown:**
  - **Nvidia:**
    - Highlighted "visibility" (a huge installed base plus a replacement cycle of roughly 3–4 years) and "fungibility" (Nvidia compute works across hyperscalers, sovereign clouds, neoclouds and AI labs). He said the Amazon deal shows GPUs stay central even as ASIC, TPU and Trainium competition grows.
    - Credited CUDA as the real moat, with the Apple-like installed base predating AI.
    - Noted $26B in Q2 buybacks and dividends, a $99B authorization in place that could go to $200B, and about $50B a year of free cash flow. If the stock drifts back to $211–212, Nvidia is buying it below the market multiple.
    - Said a buyback isn't the reason to own it. He would rather see free cash flow reinvested in the next growth wave, and the two aren't mutually exclusive.
  - **Data centers:** The backlash "is not an existential threat." Moratoriums are about incumbents getting reelected. Data centers will get built, perhaps in different places and more slowly, which he called bullish because it lengthens the cycle and avoids a chip and photonics bubble. Supply constraints matter more than politics for the drawdowns. His analogy was cell towers a generation ago.
  - **Salesforce:** It checked every box. It announced Cloudforce, an AI CRM built with Anthropic, and free cash flow rose 81%. It also won major customers (Booz Allen, Mercedes-Benz, the World Cup, Robinhood). The stock got "too cheap."
  - **Private equity:** Owns some Carlyle but isn't big in the space. The group is now one big pro-cyclical trade and less of a diversifier. Equity investments would fall harder in a downturn.
  - **Best Stocks in the Market: Airbnb (new name).**
    - A breakaway gap out of a 2–3 year base after the first-week-of-August earnings.
    - Management says AI is "the best thing to ever happen to Airbnb." The AI assistant closed 45% of support issues without a human, support cost per booking fell 16%, EBITDA margin is 35%, and free cash flow was $1.25B in the quarter.
    - His stop is the top of the gap, about 20% below. He expects it to rise above its 200-day moving average.
    - It was flat or down in 2024 and 2025, while Expedia is up 223% and Booking up 166% over three years.
- **Malcolm Etheridge:**
  - **Nvidia:**
    - Said the key proof was Nvidia becoming less reliant on Meta and Microsoft. Hyperscale revenue fell from 59% to 55% of the total, which is material for a $5T company.
    - Said he expected the decoupling to take longer than it did.
    - Pointed to receivables of about $63B versus inventory of about $31B, which restores the old 2:1 ratio. He read that as sold out for the next 12 months.
  - **Data centers:** About $800B of spending commitments from four companies this year may be a pull-forward ahead of the backlash, with November 2026 as the wall.
  - **Cybersecurity:**
    - Identity is everything as AI agents multiply. Palo Alto bought CyberArk for roughly $25B last year.
    - He expects consolidation, with only 4–5 standalone cyber names surviving besides Microsoft. He named CrowdStrike and Okta, and said Okta could be an acquisition target.
    - CrowdStrike is up about 90% year to date.
- **Jason Snipe:**
  - **Nvidia:** Said this is the inflection point. Nvidia "widened the tent": hyperscalers grew 13% sequentially and 102% year over year, while neocloud, sovereign and enterprise grew 25% and 138%. He also cited the $500B private-equity investment Jensen architected, which makes semiconductors an investable asset.
  - **Market:** Mega caps and chips were mixed (AMD, Micron, Marvell and Western Digital down). He said memory costs and supply chains are short-term issues but the long-term tailwinds are intact, and noted cybersecurity is working.
  - **Snowflake:** Up 89% in three months and about 50% year to date. Last quarter EPS was up 68% and revenue up 33%.
  - **Calls of the Day: Blackstone.** Oppenheimer reiterated its call and raised its target to $171 from $140, about 20% upside. The stock is up about 7% year to date and 8% in the last month. AUM is $1.35T with $263B of inflows this year. Private credit and private equity are improving, and AI infrastructure investments are starting to yield. He also likes Apollo.
- **Brian Belski:**
  - **Nvidia:** Stressed the consistency of the company. He pointed to the stair-step chart and the annuity-like base, and noted that Jensen has said buybacks will continue.
  - **Data centers:** The midterms won't change the build-out trajectory, given backlogs of tens of thousands of projects worldwide. His own play is "OG utilities" (NextEra, FirstEnergy, Southern). He acknowledged utilities trade at a premium to history but said free cash flow is up and debt to equity is falling.
  - **Calls of the Day:**
    - **Regions Financial:** Deutsche downgraded it to hold at $34. He likes it as a value and dividend-growth name at 12.5x earnings with a 3.5% yield, a strong credit record and a favorable region.
    - **Nike:** Having its worst year since 1993 and down five straight years. He sold it about six months ago as a "broken growth stock."
    - **Dick's Sporting Goods:** Fell on Foot Locker comps down 4%, weak guidance, and operating margins more than 500 bps below plan. He still likes the assets and expects a turnaround.
  - **Airbnb:** Prefers Marriott, Hilton and Hyatt (recently added, which he expects to be next on momentum) for travel exposure.
- **Mike Santoli (Midday Word):**
  - Nvidia is up almost 10% and is accounting for nearly all the index upside.
  - The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) is up only 1.2%. Micron opened around $967 and traded near $912.
  - The S&P 500 is about 1.2% below its all-time high, with banks and consumer down.
  - Moves in software and cybersecurity: CrowdStrike up about 18%, Palo Alto up 12%, Salesforce up 21%, Okta up 27%.
  - He warned of possible whiplash for investors who are long semis and short software.
- **Options Action (Oliver Renick, SpaceX):**
  - Implied volatility is down to the mid-50s from above 110–120 going into earnings and the first lockup expiry. The one-month implied move is just over 10%.
  - It is the cheapest it has been to make a directional bet. 8 of the top 10 most-bought contracts are calls, led by the $144 strike expiring tomorrow, which needs another 3.5% gain to break even.
- **Final Trades:**
  - **Brian Belski:** Marriott. The transcript has no attribution, but this fits his Marriott comments earlier. He cited staying in the JW brand and called it the best-in-brand name.
  - **Jason Snipe:** American Express. It's down this year, but travel and entertainment spending remain strong.
  - **Malcolm Etheridge:** BUG (cybersecurity ETF), the "smartest way to play" the coming consolidation. He joked that one more quarter like CrowdStrike's might get him the logo tattooed.
  - **Josh Brown:** Not captured in the transcript.
