Navigating the Risk of a Market Correction: The Investment Committee Weighs in — 9/9/26

**Overview:** The committee debated whether rising oil prices, backing-up yields, and seasonal headwinds (buyback blackout, September opex, quarter-end) mean correction risk is genuinely rising, even as strong earnings and a semis-led momentum rotation keep the market near highs. Apple's foldable iPhone launch and a resurgent Meta (on its new Muse AI agent) were also major talking points.

- **Joe Terranova**: Understands the bearish case (oil, yields, buyback blackout) but won't abandon his bullish bias; sees asymmetric risk if oil/yields stay elevated into midterms. Sold Costco (bought 7/7 at $965, exited on a 60-day time stop, down 5-6%) — says it lost both quality and momentum characteristics, with 3-year revenue growth moderating to 6%. Aggressively adding to Apple, dismisses the "biggest bull" skepticism (only 58% buy ratings vs. 90%+ for MSFT/NVDA/GOOGL/AMZN), not concerned about Apple's ~34x forward multiple given its ecosystem/balance sheet/buybacks. Final trade: stay with Nutrien.
- **Kari Firestone**: Sees a plausible September correction after a strong earnings summer, expects hawkish Fed noise given oil prices and a stretched consumer (tariff rebates not showing up in retailer profits); Treasury market already pricing 10-year toward 5%. Notes B of A's client flow data showed the 6th-biggest inflow week since 2008 — bullish longer-term because earnings (MAG7-led) are too good to ignore. On Apple: pushes back hard on Joe, says the 33.7x forward multiple (vs. historical ~23x) is the number that matters, and premium is unwarranted given App Store growth just turned negative. Final trade: Thermo Fisher, picking up momentum (clarified she doesn't currently own it).
- **Steve Weiss**: Says risk/reward has changed but wouldn't sell long-term quality positions to avoid taxes; also wouldn't deploy new capital unless something's truly on sale. Is short some QQQ and VOO but sitting on cash; sees no "off-ramp" for lower oil prices given geopolitical checks, calling sustained high oil the biggest market risk (worst case: higher rates AND higher oil). Notes AI mega-caps aren't energy-consumers, so oil spikes hurt them mainly via higher cost of capital, not energy costs. Bought a small Apple position as a "safe place to hide" into the launch but is skeptical — compares to the iPhone X launch (a dud) and flags negative App Store growth as a red flag against paying such a premium multiple.
- **Bryn Talkington**: Views midterm-year drawdowns as historically reliable (every midterm since 1962 has seen a 7-28% peak-to-trough decline) but also reliably followed by ~95% positive returns Nov-Feb — favors dollar-cost-averaging into strength given the earnings backdrop. On Apple: calls the foldable event a "Vision Pro moment" — a niche luxury nothingburger (~$2,000-3,000 phone) that won't drive multiple expansion or push the stock to new highs before midterms; sees a head-and-shoulders pattern forming. Final trade: TQQQ-adjacent play — actually IEFA/INFL up 25%, thinks it has more room (doesn't own it, cites Wells Fargo).
- **Market backdrop**: Citadel's Scott Rubner flagged high conviction that "the highs are in for the month" given the buyback blackout starting the 12th, opex on the 18th, and quarter-end on the 30th — RBC echoed 5-10% pullback risk from the same calendar/geopolitical/oil setup. Mike Santoli's Market Memo: bonds keep getting cheaper and nobody's buying despite attractive yields — a sign investors don't see bonds as competition for stocks despite the "stocks vulnerable to higher yields" narrative.
- **Rotation/momentum theme**: Panel highlighted a rotation back into semis (AMD, Micron, SK Hynix, Marvell all up sharply) after software/IGV outperformance faded post-earnings; JPMorgan noted the momentum-factor unwind looks complete with the S&P only ~2.3% from all-time highs.
- **Apple event coverage**: Mackenzie Sigalos previewed the "Surprise and Shine" event — the first foldable iPhone (reportedly "iPhone Duo," ~$2,000-3,000+) plus iPhone 18 Pro/Pro Max camera and battery upgrades, new Watch/AirPods health features; Barclays/TD Cowan see limited near-term financial impact from the foldable, with ~$100 price hikes on Pro models. Options Action's Oliver Renick reported unusually high implied vol (85) for a zero-day Apple options expiry, pricing in a 1.5%+ move — call-heavy flow centered on the $320 strike.
- **Meta surge**: Up over 6% on the day (and >10% over 30 days, outperforming the rest of the Mag7) after unveiling its Muse AI agent; Morgan Stanley (Kari's firm) reiterated a $775 price target, Mizuho called it "more promising than expected." Panel credited Zuckerberg's "startup mentality" for continually finding new growth levers versus peer mega-caps run by "second-generation" operators.
- **Retail red flags**: Dick's Sporting Goods down 33% post-earnings (blamed partly on Footlocker), BMO initiated underperform with a $110 target; Joe added more in the $120s but says he wouldn't be surprised to exit soon. FTAI Aviation also flagged as "broken" and cheap but with no clear catalyst.
- **Final Trades**: Bryn Talkington — INFL (up 25%, more room to run, doesn't own it); Kari Firestone — Thermo Fisher (picking up momentum, doesn't currently own); Joe Terranova — stay with Nutrien.
