**Fed and Tech Earnings in Focus — July 29, 2026**

**Overview:** Stocks sold off hard into a huge afternoon — a Fed decision under new(ish) leadership, plus earnings from Meta and Microsoft after the bell. Semis and AI-capex-linked industrials (Caterpillar, KLA) got hit especially hard as the panel debated whether hyperscaler spending has peaked and what investors are actually getting for it.

- **Joe Terranova:** Neither Meta nor Microsoft qualifies as a momentum name on any timeframe now; Microsoft is making lower highs/lows, below its 250- and 100-day averages. Sees the CapEx cycle as "seventh inning," not peaking but decelerating — appreciation continues but not exponentially. On Caterpillar's downgrade (Street cut to Neutral, target to $900 from $1200): agrees with the call, thinks $900–1000 is fair, keeps it in the ETF as "not a one-trick pony." Called KLA's quarter "okay but not enough" (foundry/logic revenue +7% vs. needed high-teens), noting the stock is down 38% this month (worst since 1987) but still +50% YTD. On Disney: added to the ETF end of April, calls it "guilty until proven innocent," still a losing position heading into Friday's rebalance. Likes Coca-Cola's beat-and-raise and the recent CVR/Coherent-type add as "quality earnings... proving itself more innocent than guilty."
- **Bryn Talkington:** Argues "for what?" is the key question for hyperscaler spend — sees frontier AI models commoditizing and doubts Microsoft's Copilot/Teams stack can compete with cheaper Chinese open models or rivals like Claude/Perplexity. Warns not to be lured by low hyperscaler P/Es since these companies have flipped from capital-light/high-margin to capex-heavy and free-cash-flow negative — history-based valuation lenses no longer apply. Bought more Netflix (~$70) as part of her "growth strategy" bucket, betting on a turnaround within six months to a year despite few top shows currently on the platform. Skeptical of medical devices broadly (cites Intuitive Surgical's struggles) given hospital administrators delaying purchases.
- **Steve Weiss:** Admits he violated his own rule of never getting in front of a CapEx cycle on both Meta and Microsoft, though he's not losing money, just underperforming. Still has faith in Zuckerberg as a visionary who admits mistakes quickly; likes Meta's core ad business (Instagram/Facebook, Ray-Ban expansion) but wants them to focus there rather than uncertain cloud-leasing ambitions. Not worried about CDS widening as a Lehman-style signal, more a read on investor sentiment. On Netflix: has traded in and out, worried about elevated streaming costs and live-sports bidding wars, but thinks it could hit $80 "on no news." Highlighted his COHR-type position (referred to as "CBR" in transcript): entered around $130s, core earnings +30%, data-center revenue +68%, just crossed the 200-day moving average, sees a path to $170.
- **Kevin Simpson:** New to the Fed chair seat ("give Kevin some rope"), expects low probability of a hike today and more Greenspan-style opacity between meetings, which he thinks reduces volatility overall. Bought more Visa ahead of/around earnings — called it the "haves vs. have-nots" story of earnings season: payment volume +10%, processed transactions +10%, cross-border volume +13%, revenue +14%; BofA raised its target to $430 from $410. Sold Netflix, mostly "out of boredom," wants more cash and flagged that the NFL deal hasn't driven subscriber growth despite rising costs.
- **Calls of the Day:** Disney cut to $135 (Citi) and $123 (Morgan Stanley) while both maintain Buy/Overweight ahead of next week's earnings, on guide-risk concerns. Coca-Cola hit a new high on a beat-and-raise (global unit sales +5%, Coke Zero +16%). CVR/Coherent-type name (data-center revenue +68%) called a potential $170 stock. GE Healthcare/medical devices ticked up on earnings but the desk stayed skeptical of the group; Garmin hit a record high, up 19% on the year on product diversification (wearables, aviation, marine, auto, golf).
- **Options Action (Oliver Renek, CBOE):** Options flow leans bullish into Meta and Microsoft earnings. Microsoft's implied move is 6% (vs. a typical 4%), with heavy put-selling at the 392.5 strike suggesting traders are fading downside; top call volume ranged from the 400 to 460 strikes. Meta's implied move is ~8%, smaller than its 11% average swing, with bulls targeting 590/595 calls but some bearish bets on 500 puts (needing a 14% drop).
- **Michael Santoli (Midday Word):** Notes the market has been broadening out to offset semi weakness, though he's skeptical broadening alone "saves" the market given semis are down ~25% in four-five weeks while the S&P sits near highs; today's bank weakness looks like a distinct, Fed-related story. Doubts the Fed's logic supports a hike today despite the market pricing in real odds, though he notes most Fed chairs historically hike within two months of taking office.
- **Final Trades:** Joe Terranova — Steel Dynamics (STLD); Bryn Talkington — the 10-year yield/bonds (sees a good entry point even if yields aren't done rising); Steve Weiss — Coherent/"CBR" (target $170); Kevin Simpson — Apple (AAPL), ahead of tomorrow's earnings and Tim Cook "going out on top."
