**Halftime Report: NVIDIA Overhang, AI Trade Reset and Market Bounce (Nov 25, 2026)**

**Overview:** After last week's sharp drop and yesterday's big bounce on Jensen Huang's reinstatement as NVIDIA CEO, the panel debated what comes next. Semis were down again, with SMH off about 9%, while defensives and the broader market held up. The main arguments were over AI capex timing, Fed rate-hike risk, and whether the Mag 7 or the chip stocks are the better trade.

- **Scott Wapner (host):** Said yesterday that "it gets harder from here" for the AI trade. He noted good news isn't getting a good price reaction and the market "wants liquidity." He cited JPMorgan's view that removing the Huang uncertainty is a clear positive, but governance and political uncertainty is worse. He also cited Goldman's sentiment and positioning gauge, at its lowest since Liberation Day, which is historically favorable for returns. He was skeptical of Dan Ives' NVIDIA target move.
- **Stephanie Link:**
  - **Market view:** Said waiting for certainty means missing bargains. Fear & Greed is at 10, the S&P oscillator is around -8 to -9, and there was record de-grossing. Redbook sales were up 11.3%, the second best week of the year. S&P forward P/E has compressed from 23 to 17. Earnings growth likely slows from about 30% to 10–15%, still above trend. She thinks a 75 bp hike path through March is too aggressive and sees oil under $80 (down 28%) as helping inflation.
  - **AI capex:** Thinks the NVIDIA news removes an overhang for the whole food chain. 2027 spend is already locked, new data center projects will come under pressure, and the capex cycle gets extended rather than boom-and-bust.
  - **Marvell:** Bought more. The stock is down 60% from its highs. Management raised its revenue target through 2031 from $20B to $70–90B, with mid-50s gross margins and possible EPS power of $30. She likes the Lumentum optical deal, which lifts the TAM to $550B. She wouldn't have bought without Huang's return. At $30 EPS, she says the stock trades at about 4x earnings.
  - **Capital One:** Trimmed it. It's having its worst month in over three years. She's worried about a Trump-backed credit card rate cap, from about 27% toward 10%. She still likes the Discover synergies ($2.5B) and the 8.5x P/E, and kept a big position.
  - **Other:** Dick's Sporting Goods raised guidance, posted a 17th straight quarter of positive comps, trades at 13x, and bought back $1B of stock. She is adding to Wells Fargo (asset cap lifted, JPMorgan executive hires, turnaround, attractive valuation). She isn't alarmed by bearish bank options, saying loan losses are where cracks would show first, and delinquencies and charge-offs are falling.
- **Jenny Harrington (name inferred from the transcript):** Her fear is cost of capital and inflation more than earnings deceleration. She thinks the Fed is fixated on the 2% target, and that the "50 bp" talk may have set a low bar for a 25 bp hike rally.
- **Josh Brown:**
  - **Market view:** The AI trade is bifurcating, and the "everything rises together" capex trade has been over since July. He prefers hyperscalers and model makers over semis. SMH is down 9% today after closing at 460 yesterday, versus about 410 last week. The Mag 7 cap-weighted forward P/E is 18.8, the cheapest since Q4 2018, versus 17.5 for the S&P. He called hyperscalers the "fat pitch." He is long NVIDIA and said Ives' $250 target won't be seen soon.
  - **Anthropic:** Bought more, below the IPO price. He accepts the compute-contract and competition risks, and sees enterprise adoption as "Peter Lynch 101." His firm has 93 employees on enterprise Claude, and he thinks it could become a leading software company over 3–5 years.
  - **SpaceX:** Initiated a small position via limit order, filled under $125. He expects Musk to fold Tesla into SpaceX and thinks SpaceX holders get the better deal, given Musk's 83% voting control.
  - **Rocket Lab:** Bought more despite a new underperform initiation. The stock is down two-thirds, Electron has launched 100 times, the larger Neutron rocket is due in H1 next year, and it is acquiring Radium. He called it speculative.
  - **Robinhood:** Likes it as a longer-term fintech holding, though it is volatile.
  - **Financials ETF:** His ETF now holds six names: Visa, Mastercard, Nasdaq, Cboe, CME and TradeWeb. It moved out of regional banks and insurers.
  - **Best Stocks in the Market updates:** All three are winners.
    - **Shark Ninja:** At an all-time high. Sales were up 21%, with 14 straight quarters of double-digit growth and guidance raised for the third time this year. It was written up Aug 20 at $181.
    - **Target:** Net sales were up 7.1% with a beat and good guidance. He owns it for clients, and suggests a $152 stop, about 8% below.
    - **eBay:** Guided full-year EPS to $6.25–6.30, about 18x earnings. He calls it reliable.
- **Kevin Simpson:**
  - **NVIDIA:** Sold half his position yesterday on the pop, after an earlier trim in the $160s. He got in around $200 and took a 15–20% loss. He says the problems remain: election, cost of capital, and vendor and circular financing. SpaceX raised debt at 8%, and Anthropic got junk ratings, so NVIDIA's customers are paying more. He expects pressure for 2–3 months, but holders at low cost bases needn't sell.
  - **Other:** Boeing (Mizuho outperform, $225 target) is owned in his growth portfolio, and the backlog is the draw. He likes Interactive Brokers, with Cboe the best financial play near a 52-week high.
- **Joe Terranova:**
  - **Anthropic:** Established a new position at $181 via a resting bid. He thinks the stock is under pressure because it is a pure-play AI developer on a bad AI day, and because of its credit rating.
  - **Lockheed:** Likes Mizuho's outperform call with a $765 target. The stock is up 21% year to date, and he cites its diversification into cyber and drones.
  - **Other:** Has owned eBay for 18 months. Calls Target a momentum name with a 12-month street target of $175, and says only 32% of analysts rate it a buy, which he likes. Notes Shark Ninja joins the S&P 500 on Dec 2, replacing AES.
- **Guests and segments:**
  - **Dan Ives:** Raised his NVIDIA target to $250 from $225, and says $300 is still the bull case. He joins the Closing Bell at 3.
  - **Rick Rieder (BlackRock):** Said there is more to come in this story, with uncertainty around midterms, trade, chips and data center build.
  - **Aiman Javers:** Reported that AG Todd Blanche backed the President's statement that Huang was "exonerated," but gave no details of any DOJ investigation.
  - **Seema Modi:** Reported that NVIDIA's first all-hands since Huang's reinstatement started at 12:30 ET.
  - **Mike Santoli:**
    - **Market read:** Respects yesterday's move but doesn't treat it as final. Rotation out of semis held, and the VIX came in. Positioning is light, which gives a slight upside bias, but it is a holiday week. Yesterday's 4.7% S&P gain left it up 5.7% year to date. He sees 7,200 as the line in the sand, with 7,350 above it.
  - **Oliver Renna (Options Action):** Bearish bets in bonds and banks. About $11M of $20M in call premium traded near the bid, mostly 79-strike January calls sold. In XLF, there was heavy buying of 52-strike December 18 puts, which need a 7% drop.
  - **Other analyst calls:** Mizuho initiated Boeing (outperform) and Lockheed (outperform). Goldman reiterated Interactive Brokers and Robinhood.
- **Final Trades (attribution garbled in the transcript):** Silver, which is back at $40 after a run to $125 and was discussed as a reversal of an earlier "bubble" call. CrowdStrike, because a 10% drop was too much. Amazon, going into Black Friday and AWS. Quest Diagnostics, at a new all-time high and on the Best Stocks list. One more name sounded like "Ways to rule," which I couldn't identify.
