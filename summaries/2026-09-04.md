**The Return of the "Magnificent Seven": The Investment Committee's Strategy — September 4, 2026**

**Overview:** The committee dug into Big Tech's reasserted leadership, crediting strong earnings (especially Microsoft, Meta, and NVIDIA) and disciplined AI-related capex for reversing the MAG7's summer underperformance, even as September's seasonal reputation and a possible Fed rate hike loom as risks. Consumer and retail names told a split story — Walmart and energy majors drew buy interest while Adobe, Lululemon, and Amer Sports faced skepticism — and Tesla's cybercab event landed as a disappointment.

- **Jim Lebenthal**: Called NVIDIA the "clearing event" that restarted MAG7 outperformance, citing Amazon's Jassy (data centers pay back in <3 years), Alphabet cloud +80%, Azure +40%. Expects the iPhone price hike (reportedly $1,319 for the 18 Pro) to be absorbed by consumers as telecoms keep subsidizing. Defended Adobe against Weiss's "financial engineering" charge, noting 15% annualized net income growth and >10% free-cash-flow growth funding buybacks (share count down ~10%); says the stock trades as if AI poses an existential threat that hasn't materialized. Likes FTAI (formerly owned, cited pullback to $184 on data-center jitters). On energy: "if you're gonna own anything in the energy patch... start with Exxon Mobile" (outperformed S&P 500 2x over six years); also likes Chevron (production +20%, ~$1.5B in Hess synergies captured). Trimmed TJX after Marmaxx/TJMaxx/Marshalls comps slowed to 1% from 6% (still holds a 3% position); called Lululemon a "no touch," having exited in April.
- **Kevin Simpson**: Added to Freeport-McMoRan and Agnico Eagle on the metals pullback, continuing last week's CF Industries/hard-commodities theme; flagged Citi's bullish gold/silver call into year-end but noted a Fed rate hike would blow up the gold thesis. Cautious on chasing Robinhood after its bitcoin-fueled rally (Deutsche Bank raised its target to $136 from $115); likes Robinhood's tokenization push and sees it outlasting bitcoin long-term. Trimming Suncor, calling it "the bright spot" of energy and a good rate hedge but says it's "rounded" enough to take some chips off the table.
- **Rob Sechan**: Sees Microsoft as the biggest driver of the MAG7 comeback (stock +27% since earnings) due to quality, scale, and margin resilience amid weakening market breadth. Bullish on Meta after its Muse Spark 3.1 model launch (+6% on the week); flagged B of A's reiterated buy and $810 target, calling it the cheapest hyperscaler at 18.1x forward earnings — a buy into weakness. Personally believes the Fed will hike in September (not his firm Serity Partners' house view), citing Kevin Warsh's comments and political risk of hiking near midterms. Owns SpaceX both privately and publicly, calls the options market's caution "too much too soon" and sees an "evangelical" support level that could spark a big move on positive news.
- **Steve Weiss**: Sees MAG7 strength as investors rotating into names with proven AI ROI (Microsoft, Meta) rather than the group broadly; noted Meta's pivot toward an AI model business helped sentiment, and cited low relative debt costs as a safety cushion. Skeptical on Adobe's new insider CEO pick ("the market wanted an outsider"), argued buybacks are financial engineering that masks underlying growth questions, and cited mixed analyst reactions (Viva underperform, Citi neutral, JPMorgan overweight). Called Tesla's cybercab event "a big fizzle" despite still holding the stock, noting NHTSA is reviewing the rollout. Prefers Walmart over Costco after Costco's price target was cut to $1,144 from $1,194.
- **Bill Baruch (phone)**: Sold Amer Sports (bought near $19, exiting after strong long-term gains) as China stimulus tailwinds faded and tariffs compressed margins across Wilson, Arc'teryx, and Salomon. Bought Walmart as a "trade-down" beneficiary, citing 94% of the U.S. population reachable in a 3-hour delivery window, elevated same-day fees aiding margins, and Sam's Club competing directly with Costco; sees technical support near $100 (200-week moving average near $80 as worst case).
- **Mike Santoli's Midday Word**: Called the market's reaction to the jobs report "fatigued" rather than assertive, noted the market is pricing a coin-flip on a Fed hike, VIX sits below 14, and next week's CPI/PPI prints take on outsized importance.
- **Options Action (Oliver Renick, Cboe)**: SpaceX options are unusually cheap given the stock's tight 10-point range over the past month; puts outnumber calls 11-to-10, with thin hedging below $142 — a break below could accelerate losses since bears would need to move faster than bulls to profit.
- **Morgan Stanley energy call**: Sees value in Exxon, Chevron, and Suncor as oil pushes back toward $100/barrel — discussed above by Jim and Kevin.
- **Final Trades**: Steve Weiss — FTAI (data-center selloff concerns are "misplaced," stock is cheap); Rob Sechan — KLA (still off 30% from its June peak), NVIDIA (likes the Hugging Face acquisition), and Oracle (good momentum).
