# Positioning for Big Tech's Next Move — 8/12/26

**Overview:** CPI came in line, yields fell, and stocks rallied across the board, with the AI trade getting fresh validation from strong CoreWeave and Super Micro earnings and Ed Yardeni raising his S&P target to 8,400. The committee debated whether tech/AI names still have room to run into Nvidia's earnings in two weeks, while also covering financials, data-center power stocks, cash on the sidelines, and a shocking Lakers ownership flip.

- **Joe Terranova**: Called Yardeni's 8,400 call "the roaring 20s" driven by earnings growth and margin expansion toward 17%. Expects a break above the August 5 high (~7,793 SPX) to draw systematic funds back in, pushing toward 8,000. On Nvidia (owns it), walked through the setup into earnings exactly two weeks out: 96% buy ratings, $303 average PT, revenue growth reaccelerating to a consensus 96%, EPS growth of 114%, $47B free cash flow, next-quarter guide toward $100B revenue — expects a retest of the prior $236 high but warned to "be a little careful" on the other side of the print. Also holds Vermeer, Quanta, GE Vernova, Eaton (data-center power) — recently trimmed Caterpillar over debt-to-equity concerns; argued regulatory noise (Texas grid pause, NY moratorium) is a reason to avoid utilities, not the industrials he owns, given >20% revenue growth. Financials: JPMorgan making new highs, likes Bank of America's wealth/Merrill franchise. Final Trade: J.B. Hunt.
- **Brian Belski**: Sees Yardeni's target as directionally sound given the S&P/Nasdaq's changed composition (Broadcom, Micron now top-10 alongside Nvidia). On Nvidia, called it "the cheapest it's been in seven years," with a technical ceiling near $230-236 that should open "the races" once breached; flagged SpaceX committing its chip spend to Nvidia as an unmodeled tailwind. Likes Cisco (10-year holding) as an AI/optical play alongside Lumentum, Corning, Coherent, Applied Opto, noting Nvidia's $2B optical deals and potential FCC limits on Chinese optical imports as tailwinds. Cybersecurity (CrowdStrike upgraded to $230, Palo Alto to $415 at Citizens) remains the strongest software pocket; broader software needs proof from Salesforce, Oracle, and Adobe reports before he'll turn positive — sees Oracle as "the number one problem child." On McDonald's: owns it, likes the U.S.-traffic turnaround story despite BofA's underperform/$124 target. On Hilton: upgraded to buy, $365 target, prefers it to Marriott. Cut targets on Lowe's ($262) and Home Depot ($344), preferring Home Depot's integration execution. Final Trade: General Dynamics.
- **Shannon Saccocia**: Noted the S&P is actually underperforming other global equity markets and tech P/E has compressed even as AI's "next phase" broadens participation (Nvidia's financing MOUs, investment-grade and private-credit issuance). Argued the U.S. economy is no longer consumer-driven, insulating earnings leaders from labor-market/Fed jitters. On software, said non-tech Fortune 500 firms will keep relying on existing software stacks enhanced by AI rather than ripping them out — a bullish read for incumbent software. On bonds/cash: correlation between stocks and bonds has broken the classic 60/40, but attractive absolute yields exist without going out to the 10-year. Owns CNP despite Texas data-center political risk, calling the demand story durable ("Shawshank" quip: "get busy living or get busy dying"). Final Trade: Healthcare.
- **On Nvidia earnings (broad panel discussion)**: Consensus 96% buy ratings, $303 average price target (Goldman's Brent Goldman reiterated buy, $285 target); stock cheapest relative to earnings growth in seven years; Steve Weiss/Josh Brown not present this episode but panel broadly bullish into the August 26 print, with Terranova cautioning on post-earnings volatility given the recent run-up.
- **Financials (unnamed Humilis/Terranova-linked note referenced by Belski/panel)**: Called financials the next big earnings winner after tech, citing moderating rates and widening spreads; flagged Bank of America and Goldman Sachs as top broker-dealer picks; one panelist trimmed Fifth Third (up 30%) on loan-book concerns while staying ~32-33% weighted financials.
- **Cash-on-sidelines segment**: Discussed WSJ report on $3T+ sitting in retail money markets. Panel agreed investors are anchored to higher pre-2022 bond yields and an inverted-curve mindset; noted attractive yields now available in 2-4 year duration without taking on 10-year volatility; one panelist emphasized municipal bonds and covered calls (6-9% annual yield) as ways to generate cash flow given long-duration bonds' poor total returns over the past five years.
- **Data-center power stocks segment**: Vermeer (+81.5% YTD), Quanta (+63%), GE Vernova (+58.5%), Eaton (+46%), Caterpillar (+50%) highlighted amid backlash risk — Caterpillar downgrade cited state/local regulatory limits on hyperscaler capex; New York's data-center moratorium and Texas's grid-connection pause flagged, plus a poll showing 71% public opposition to local data centers. Consensus: buildout continues regardless of where sited.
- **Lakers sale segment (Alex Sherman, CNBC Sports, and guest Mark Ganis, Sportscorp)**: Mark Walter is selling the Lakers for $12.5B (bought for $10B less than a year ago) to a consortium led by Josh Kushner and Bob Iger — financing and Jeannie Buss's future role still unclear; speculation ties the sale's timing to an ongoing federal probe into Walter's insurance companies. Ganis called it "stunning" given Walter's history of buy-fix-hold (Dodgers, Cadillac F1), noted NBA's looser private-equity/sovereign-wealth ownership rules versus the NFL enable higher valuations, and said trophy sports assets (citing the Seahawks' ~$10B sale to Vinod Khosla, Apollo's Yankees stake) will keep appreciating as an AI/globalization-resistant asset class.
- **Final Trades**: Bill Baruch — LPL Financial; Kevin Simpson (referenced, "Bryn") — buy DRAM, sell October $60 calls; Shannon Saccocia — Healthcare; Joe Terranova — J.B. Hunt; Brian Belsky — General Dynamics.
