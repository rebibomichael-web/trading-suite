**The Rally and Rising Rate Risk – CNBC Halftime Report, 10/8/26**

**Overview:** Hawkish Fed talk pushed short-end yields higher, oil and the dollar jumped, and the panel debated whether rate risk can derail a record-setting rally near S&P 7,800. Late in the hour, an FT report that OpenAI's annualized revenue is about $20B below earlier signals hit AI-linked names, and the S&P fell about 0.5%. The panel also covered Starbucks–Chipotle takeover chatter, Microsoft, energy, and Williams-Sonoma.

**Who said what** (the transcript has no speaker labels, so some attributions are best guesses)

- **Market backdrop:**
  - Fed Governor Waller said he anticipates additional hikes, not necessarily at consecutive meetings.
  - Oil was surging and the dollar was at an 18-month high.
  - The VIX is about 15.5, down roughly 30% since the war began in late February. The MOVE bond-volatility index is up about 30%.
  - UBS and Wells Fargo said to stay invested. Ray Dalio said rates can rise without sinking equities while earnings growth is strong.
  - Morgan Stanley data: 89% of S&P stocks have had a 10% drawdown since June 1, and 75% fell in September.
  - Five stocks make up about 28% of the index, a record.
- **Josh Brown:**
  - Expects a "haves and have-nots" earnings season. Good results won't rescue the most rate-sensitive sectors, and the broadening trade is harder.
  - The S&P can still rally into December.
  - Flagged BDCs (private credit) at multi-year lows as the hidden stress point.
  - Said rate volatility is being ignored by stocks.
  - Doubts Starbucks–Chipotle: "I think both of these stocks will lose another 20" if the deal gets serious.
  - On energy, XLE is having its third-best year ever, and when it's up double digits into October it finishes higher 8 of 10 years. He argued the sector's winners are heavily tied to AI power demand (Chevron's Microsoft data-center deal). Jenny pushed back that the top XLE winners are refiners and that AI runs on natural gas and electricity, not oil.
- **Jenny Harrington:**
  - Argued for selectivity within sectors and sub-industries: strong balance sheets and free cash flow, and hyperscalers can still drive earnings.
  - Said real estate and REITs are less rate-hurt than assumed. Cited the CNBC piece by Diana Olick: REITs are up 6% YTD but down 8% in a month, and the dividend ETFs are still up 9–19%.
  - Noted Starbucks traffic is down 15% from 2019 and called the deal idea a "Martingale strategy." Said Stephanie Link, a Starbucks holder, "would be shocked."
  - Owns Best Buy: ~$31, under 10x earnings when bought, now ~12x, 5%+ dividend yield. She likes it better than Williams-Sonoma.
  - Sold JetBlue earlier, saying it ended up "in no man's land," and might buy it back if it gets cheap.
- **Malcolm Etheridge:**
  - Said the VIX/MOVE split shows where sentiment sits.
  - Backed Microsoft. The OpenAI renegotiation let it unify Copilot and add Anthropic models, and the Morgan Stanley call is incrementally more positive.
  - Flagged Williams-Sonoma technicals: resistance at $248–252, a breakout above $250 on volume is a buy, and support at $215 (July and September lows, rising 200-day).
- **Shannon Saccocia:**
  - Yields at these levels are attractive for investors with three strong equity years.
  - A typical hiking cycle runs about 300bp, which she doesn't expect this time.
  - Her firm cut its long-term small/mid-cap overweight to target. She cited rate sensitivity, thin margins and housing weakness, and prefers large caps.
  - Likes the opportunity in insurance brokers and real estate.
- **Rates and positioning debate:** The panel agreed it's hard to tell clients to take profits after four double-digit years. Suggested locking in ~5%+ short-end yields and using January for tax-aware rebalancing. Krinsky's note showed a historic Nasdaq–Russell 2000 divergence.
- **Starbucks–Chipotle (FT report):**
  - The panel was skeptical; one called it AT&T–Time Warner-like.
  - Another saw possible synergy in the afternoon day-part.
  - The Stevens note said the possibilities are "worth exploring."
- **Breaking news, Megan Cassella:** Trump said the U.S. won't attack Iran before the Nov. 3 midterms, citing "productive discussions." The blockade stays. WTI is above $90 (up ~3% on the day) and Brent is ~$105.
- **Williams-Sonoma (Best Stocks in the Market):** AI shopping assistants on its sites reportedly lead to orders about 9x larger on average. Retail is bifurcated: Lululemon is down 56% YTD, Nike down 46%, Tractor Supply down 35%.
- **Options Action (Oliver Renick):**
  - Delta ahead of earnings: options volume is 2.5x average, put-heavy, implied move ~4%, and one $1.5M put seller bet on $80 holding through January.
  - JetBlue is in free fall with heavy put volume.
  - A panelist who owns Delta said they're staying long. Delta CEO Ed Bastian is on Squawk Box at 7 a.m. tomorrow.
- **CNBC Financial Advisor 100:** Robert Frank reported Howland Capital (Boston, $4B AUM, 59 years) ranked #1, then Heritage, Baird, Harris and ZWJ. Peter Dixon, Howland's president and CIO, said the firm is family-office rooted and independent. It uses AI for committee prep, research idea generation and admin, but not for decisions.
- **Mike Santoli (OpenAI report):**
  - The FT said OpenAI annualized revenue is ~$20B below prior signals. Oracle fell ~5.5% and Nvidia ~2%, with AMD, Broadcom, Marvell and chip equipment also lower.
  - He said it hits the durability of AI demand and the order-book story.
  - Non-debt-financed AI platforms have held up better.
  - Top S&P contributors were Apple, Exxon and Philip Morris ("defense, defense, defense").
  - The 5.3% level on the 10-year has drawn buyers four times in the last week.
  - He expects a market tantrum, then a narrative that AI demand is more distributed, citing rival products.
- **Final Trades** (speakers unclear): Industrials, Visa, Disney, and Netflix (possible double bottom).
