**Countdown to the Fed Decision 10/28/26**

**Overview:** Leslie Picker filled in for Scott Wapner. The show centered on the Fed's expected hold today and a possible hawkish dissent, plus after-the-bell earnings from Microsoft and Alphabet. The panel also discussed the AI and memory-stock rebound, and Bill Baruch's portfolio trims.

- **Steve Liesman (CNBC):** The Fed is expected to hold, but a CNBC survey of 30 respondents found 18 expect at least one dissent in favor of hiking, 10 expect two, and 5 expect three. JPMorgan notes Chair Warsh must explain holding on an expected 0.3% core PCE after hiking on a 0.2% print in September. PCE is due tomorrow, and 0.3% would lift the year-over-year rate to 3.1%. The market prices one more hike and as many as two in 2027, with the 2-year yield around 4.77%. Warsh may hint at more hikes to win over dissenters but wants to avoid forward guidance. He cautioned against "wishful thinking" that 2027 hikes go away.
- **Joe Terranova:**
  - *Fed and bonds:* Falling yields over the past two weeks helped momentum and the AI trade. He expects a hold, with one more hike priced in. An extended hiking cycle into 2027 would force a recalibration. The bond market is now more price-sensitive, since hedge funds and speculators have replaced pensions and central banks as marginal buyers. That means more volatility in both directions.
  - *Earnings:* He favors Alphabet on expectations, sentiment and positioning. It is up about 5% since the July 22 report, after a 116% run, and it is trading at support. Microsoft is up 36%+ since the end of July and needs to beat. Meta has reversed all its Muse gains amid extreme bullishness and a roughly $100B equity raise.
  - *Market drivers:* SK Hynix is lifting memory stocks, with Seagate turning higher. Vertiv is up 13% on a blowout quarter, and GE Vernova is recovering despite its $9B Trench deal. Teradyne's weak outlook is idiosyncratic, not a referendum on semi-equipment. Goldman's momentum basket has had 39 moves of 5% or more this year, the most since at least 1999. He personally owns Cboe.
  - *Gold:* He is skeptical. CTA capital is flowing to energy (heating oil, gasoline, Brent, WTI) amid the Middle East conflict.
- **Brynn Talkington:**
  - *Fed:* Rate hikes won't affect energy-driven inflation but are hurting housing and commercial real estate. About $8T of debt refinances yearly, which makes interest costs sensitive to short rates. She expects at most a December hike, then a pause.
  - *Earnings:* Expects blowout Microsoft and Alphabet cloud numbers. She says Copilot is finally working, and the cloud gives them cover for their spend, unlike Meta.
  - *Memory:* She owns the DRAM ETF, since Samsung and SK Hynix are about 45% of it. SK's revenue rose 32% and operating profit 38% quarter over quarter. Half its bookings are contracted through 2028 and some run to 2030. She calls this "peak skepticism" and says the numbers contradict the price action.
  - *Other holdings:* She owns the BUG cyber ETF long term and doesn't need to trim it. She also owns INFL (inflation beneficiaries), which gives energy, exchanges and metals exposure that doesn't look like the S&P or Nasdaq.
- **Bill Baruch:**
  - *Earnings:* He expects Microsoft to break out above $550 on the report. Azure is expected at $34.5B with a guide above 45%. Microsoft is a top-five holding he is sitting tight on. Meta's capex and free cash flow set a low bar, so he expects "less bad" or better results. Alphabet is at support.
  - *Trims:* He trimmed Meta from 2.5% to 1.5% of the portfolio. He cited 2026 capex of $135–145B, 2027 estimates near $200B, negative free cash flow and resistance at $700. He also shaved 50 bps each from Micron (a 5%+ position, cost basis around $70), Broadcom (back above support at 333) and Nvidia, plus CrowdStrike (nearly 4%). The semis trims are risk management ahead of the Fed and the midterms. SMH is down about 20% from its October peak and up 7% this week. He still loves CrowdStrike and cybersecurity.
  - *Rotation:* He sold the CQQQ China tech ETF, "a dog all year," and bought XTN (equal-weight transports) over IYT.
  - *Futures trade, gold:* Gold fell below $4,000 last week. He sees support there and resistance at $4,150 (21-day moving average) and $4,180, with $4,200 the breakout level. He cited seasonal strength from mid-December through January, central bank buying, and producers closing hedges.
- **Mike Santoli (Market Memo):** He sees the Fed, PCE and the election as potential clearing events. He cited strong end-of-October and early-November seasonality and tighter hyperscaler credit spreads. A go-slow Fed is generally good for stocks. The week is partly mean reversion, with the worst 15 October stocks averaging a 6.5% bounce in two days. Stocks have been weak on Fed days.
- **Halftime Headliner, Andy Sieg (Citi head of wealth):** Wealth revenue is up 15%, with 10 straight quarters of growth, record net flows of $38B+ and a 10%+ organic flow rate. The new Premium Boost relationship-based savings rate drew 25,000+ clients in two days. The CitiSky AI tool is rolling out more slowly than expected to build trust. He called private credit and private equity key long-term allocation building blocks, with the emphasis on discipline and diligence.
- **CNBC News Update (Frank Holland):** Spain's high-speed train crash has killed at least 68, with 58 more treated in hospital. Dolores Huerta, the United Farm Workers co-founder, died at 96. The MV Odyssey cruise ship suspended operations after fuel costs jumped because of the Iran war.
- **Final Trades:**
  - **Bill Baruch:** RTX, putting half of the cash he raised into it. It is a high-quality defense name back above its 200-day moving average.
  - **Brynn Talkington:** Tesla, selling the February $450 calls for about $20. She is also selling out of Nutrien, bought at $75.67 on September 1, as a losing trade.
  - **Joe Terranova:** His final trade is not in the transcript.
