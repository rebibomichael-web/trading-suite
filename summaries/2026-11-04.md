**Post-Midterm Playbook – Halftime Report, 11/4/26**

**Overview:** Stocks surged the day after the midterms. The Dow was up almost 3% and the Nasdaq more than 2%. The panel agreed it was a "clearing event" with a clean Democratic sweep, lower oil and easing rate hopes. They split on whether the rally lasts, and on what the apparent data-center backlash (Texas governor flipping, local bans) means for the AI trade. Hyperscalers, financials, healthcare, housing and consumer names rallied, while chips and AI-power names sold off.

- **Joe Terranova:** Called it a clearing event and said "inflation lost last night," with the "fever broken" in energy. He would be surprised to see oil back at $100 and thinks it's time to pare energy. He sees a Democratic Congress as supportive of the Fed in fighting inflation. He is long the industrial power names via an ETF, now down more than 10%, and doesn't expect a quick recovery. He expects broadening into discretionary and housing: Ford, GM, Pulte, Lennar, and Nike up about 6%. On the earnings calendar, he said Cummins (before the bell) looks poor, sitting near a one-year low and punished as part of the AI power story. He was not captured in the final trades.
- **Steve Weiss:** Cautious. He said Trump has about two months to act on Iran and that an aggressive move is "more likely than not." That would push oil and rates up, a "black swan" for the rally. He argued the market isn't saying "all clear" on AI. Capex is constrained by a possible data-center moratorium, notably in Texas where about 30% of projects sit. That leaves Nvidia and TSMC down and suppliers hit, while Micron nears $800. He thinks the market is "too high," doubts how long the clearing trade lasts, and says he doesn't use technicals. He likes FTAI Aviation (FTAI), which he's buying more of. The core engine business is dirt cheap, with about $2.5B of confirmed power-generation orders. Ex-power, it trades near 10x next year's earnings. He owns Moderna as a big position and calls it "ahead of itself," driven by biotech momentum. He called Marvell's $115B stock acquisition a defensive deal that dilutes holders and leaves it in "no man's land" until close. Parker Hannifin reports before the bell tomorrow.
- **Bill Baruch:**
  - He said the market is "once again rotating" and that S&P futures closing above 7,800 would mean "we're off to the races." He noted the S&P was down 2% last night, with the Nasdaq testing its 200-day average.
  - He bought more Marvell on the decline. He had trimmed about 3% of tech (Nvidia, Micron, Broadcom, Meta) and cut ARM earlier. Marvell is down 30% from last week's high, its multiple has compressed from 45x to 24x, and it's growing EPS about 40%. He called the deal a headwind to Broadcom over time and said it won't close until 2028.
  - He bought Rocket Companies (RKT), up 8.5%, on Tuesday as a refinance and lower-rate play. The stock was down about 30% on the year. His homebuilder alternatives are PulteGroup, NVR and LPX.
  - He called a bottom in McDonald's, citing sentiment, a rotation into staple-like names, and real estate and rate tailwinds. He thinks Host Hotels (HST) is a K-shaped winner, up 50% since last December. It has a dividend and buyback and reports after the bell. He called Moderna and Edwards Lifesciences (EW) healthcare-revival plays.
- **Rob Sechan:** Argued the market sees Democratic control as a check on capex. Existing, contracted compute is favored over new builds, so hyperscalers win (Apple and Microsoft up almost 7%, Amazon, Alphabet up about 4%) and chips and neoclouds lose. He said Apple has not spent on data centers and now holds a moat. He called Amgen's raised full-year outlook and record high a net positive for healthcare. He said managed care could see more subsidies under Democrats. He also noted the Suncor beat and dividend raise, with oil not yet low enough to pressure the company. He said momentum was down 2.5% and quality up 2.5% on the day. He warned that rates may not keep falling, and that a rollover in the biggest index names is the likelier way yields drop.
- **Mike Santoli (market memo):** The rally is an exhale from a sold-down, hedged-up market. Mag 7 names are seen as net beneficiaries of a data-center slowdown. Equal-weight consumer discretionary (RSPD) is at seven-month highs and overbought, but in a good way. The S&P has stalled near 7,700 several times today. The Nasdaq 100 started the day at its 200-day average. The rally is "textbook" seasonally, but he is "suspicious" it can last with semis sitting out.
- **Calls of the Day / stocks on the move:**
  - McDonald's rallied despite weak US traffic.
  - Amgen hit a record high on its raised outlook.
  - Suncor beat and raised its dividend.
  - Lockheed got a Guggenheim buy with a $735 target.
  - Edwards Lifesciences hit a 52-week high. Truist has a buy with a new $113 target, up from $95. It reports after the bell.
  - AMD had a strong report and Q4 guide, but the panel disliked its intraday price action.
  - Marvell and Lumentum were weak.
  - Digital Realty and Vistra were hit by the AI-power selloff.
  - Alt managers jumped: Blackstone and KKR about 6%.
  - JPMorgan rose about 4% and Goldman about 3.5%.
- **Options Action (Oliver Renick):** Rocket Companies (RKT) is up 8.5% to its highest since July. About 71,000 of 81,000 contracts traded were calls, on roughly double normal volume. Bulls favored the 14 and 16 strikes expiring Nov 20 and the December 16 strikes. One large trader sold some 17-strike calls.
- **Earnings ahead:**
  - Conoco before the bell. Record earnings are likely, but oil is falling.
  - Parker Hannifin before the bell. Revenue is expected up nearly 20%, with aerospace supply constraints a risk.
  - Cummins before the bell.
  - Host Hotels after the bell.
  - Edwards Lifesciences after the bell.
- **Other:** Lockheed agreed to a $30M DEI settlement with the DOJ. The Ukraine strike death toll rose to 40. Climate X estimates $2.8B in California flood damage.
- **Final Trades:**
  - **Rob Sechan:** Amazon ("existing compute capacity is going to be more valuable tomorrow").
  - **Bill Baruch:** Coeur Mining (CDE), a gold and silver play that reports after the bell, with $20 mentioned. This pick was hard to read in the transcript.
  - **Steve Weiss:** FTAI Aviation, "buy low, sell higher."
  - **Joe Terranova:** Not captured in the transcript.
