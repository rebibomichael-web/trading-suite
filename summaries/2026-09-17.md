Trading the Rate Hike Rebound: The Investment Committee's Strategy — 9/17/26

**Overview:** Markets rebounded sharply the day after the Fed's hawkish 25bp hike and Kevin Warsh's press conference roiled stocks and sent the 10-year yield toward 5%. The committee debated whether the bounce is durable or just a one-day reflex, with rising oil/diesel prices, hyperscaler debt issuance, and AI-safety jitters all flagged as risks to watch into year-end.

- **Joe Terranova:** Sees the market as having priced in the hawkish outcome; highlights bounces in consumer discretionary and memory/optical names, and Mag7 building "clear bullish momentum" vs. semis. Likes insurance names (Allstate, Travelers, Chubb, Progressive) as a durable higher-rate trade over banks. Timing a 90-day stop on CBOE (bought 6/23 at $263) since trading volumes/tight ranges make bank trading revenue questionable this quarter. On J.B. Hunt (bought late July at $271, down since): "if you don't own it, I don't think you step in and buy it" — wants stabilization first; flags rising diesel/fuel costs as an early-earnings-season margin theme across truckers.
- **Malcolm Etheridge:** Says the moment "matters a ton" — wishes the Fed had acted earlier since the bond market is now straining to absorb ~$75B in hyperscaler debt issuance since July. Sees rotation coming back into "value" mega-cap tech/hyperscalers (funding data-center buildout) away from crowded semis. Worried about OpenAI/Anthropic IPO pacing and whether AI spending promises hold up, especially after an AI-lab CEO raised safety concerns about an upcoming IPO. On cybersecurity: thinks the group (CrowdStrike, Okta) is "us finally catching up," flags Okta (~$35B market cap) as a takeover target.
- **Kevin Simpson:** Manages $21B for clients prioritizing capital protection; getting modestly more defensive — more liquidity, more covered-call writing, tighter stop losses — without turning bearish or timing the market. Says the "bar for risk has moved higher." Owns Valero (bought last July at $144, big profit) on the diesel/refined-products supply shock story (Russian refinery outages, gasoline-to-diesel switching), also likes Phillips 66 and Marathon. Owns Robin Hood, bullish on its tokenized-stock ambitions post-SEC exemption news despite voting-rights/dividend conditions.
- **Jeff Gundlach (referenced clip):** Said he would have dissented and voted for a 50bp hike — "stun and done" — to give the market more true-up on Fed funds rates.
- **Halftime Exclusive — Brookfield CEO Bruce Flatt (with Leslie Picker):** Says 25bp moves don't matter for long-term owners of great businesses; many Brookfield assets are fixed-rate or real-return (inflation-linked pricing power). On Westinghouse: supplies fuel/engineering to 60% of the world's nuclear fleet, sees a 25-30 year nuclear buildout trend, unfazed by the Oklo/nuclear IPO pullback. Says power availability — not AI model demand — is the real bottleneck (nuclear takes 5-7 years, gas 4 years, solar 2.5 years to build). On Nvidia's $500B financing plan: says chips are becoming a financeable "investment asset class" like real estate/infrastructure, still early days.
- **Calls of the Day:** J.B. Hunt upgraded to Outperform at Citizens (PT $305) despite margin/fuel-cost pressure — Joe owns it, cautious near-term.
- **Options Action (Oliver Renick):** Heavy call buying in CrowdStrike (100K contracts, $120M premium, 75%+ calls), popular trade the 250-strike call expiring next day; Bernstein flagged caution/crowding in cyber (downgrades on Palo Alto, Okta, SentinelOne).
- **Final Trades:** Kevin Simpson — UnitedHealth; Malcolm Etheridge — Goldman Sachs; Joe Terranova — Oklo (risk to the 200-day moving average at $323).
