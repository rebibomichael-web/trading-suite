**More Volatility on the Way? 11/3/26**

**Overview:** The committee debated Elliott Management's "FOGO" (fear of getting obliterated) warning about bear-market risks, and whether the washout in AI names has gone far enough ahead of the midterms. Most of the panel stayed constructive on earnings but said valuation and caution matter more now. Other topics were Apple's services and margin worries, AMD's earnings after the close, and a new Teradyne buy.

**Who said what** (the transcript has no speaker labels, so some attributions are inferred from context):

- **Elliott letter / macro backdrop:**
  - Per the WSJ, Elliott says nearly every bear-market alarm is ringing. It compares today's market to the Roaring 20s and cites deficits, inflation, private credit, AI hype, 0DTE options and meme stocks.
  - Joe Terranova: the headline is "apocalyptic," and you would need an earnings recession to get there, which isn't happening.
  - Josh Brown: you can always catalog scary things. The same arguments were made 11 years ago by Icahn. He wants a plan, not a reaction. His firm's tactical strategy cut equity from 100% to 90% at the November rebalance and could go to 70% next month. The Porter House portfolio is raising cash systematically.
  - Josh also made a fundamentals case:
    - Nvidia should generate about $300B of free cash flow over the next 12 months.
    - Meta's and Oracle's negative free cash flow is another company's positive.
    - Low unemployment, strong GDP and strong profit growth support the market.
- **Jason Snipe:**
  - Don't get too bearish on AI. Q3 earnings growth is about 30%.
  - Discretionary and regional banks are working, helped by oil's biggest one-day drop in some time.
  - Post-election, data-center capex noise should quiet down and "that money has to find a home."
  - On Apple, he is focused on the consumer-AI/Siri ramp, which is still early.
  - Holding Uber for now, citing headwinds: bookings need about 20% growth, and DoorDash may acquire Instacart.
- **Joe Terranova:**
  - Earnings are strong, but reactions to them have been awful. Industrial AI is the bright spot: Vertiv rose about 15% on earnings, with similar strength in Quanta, PWR and GE Vernova. He sees that as a possible sign of a washout.
  - Apple: he still expects $400 before he is stopped out at $275. His worry is memory and diesel costs passing through. He now says he is more aware of valuation than he used to be.
  - New buy: Teradyne.
    - It is a semi-equipment name at an upper-20s multiple.
    - It tests AI chips and memory/hard-drive hardware.
    - Revenue grew 82% last quarter.
    - He bought it on a big pullback from around $500, after it was above $400 recently.
  - Energy: the committee owns 13 energy names, with the weighting above 10%. He is holding and not adding, because the Middle East is unclear.
  - AMD: it was hit by sympathy selling with Intel (-10% Friday, -7.5% Monday). Meta is about 5.5% of its revenue.
- **Jim Lebenthal:**
  - The market-clearing event will be an Anthropic IPO raising roughly $80–100B+. He sees it as a sentiment signal that the money is still there. He also pointed to OpenAI's raise, cut from $30B to $20B.
  - He expects volatility to come down over three to six months.
  - Apollo: he is sticking with it despite the 5% drop. The Q3 net loss and a write-down in the retirement unit are "annoying," but AUM rose to $1.074T on $53B of inflows. He sees it as the financing play on the AI buildout.
- **Josh Brown (more):**
  - He argued that Anthropic, with about $146B in cash, could raise $100B privately. What it really needs is public liquidity and legitimacy.
  - His semis case: the market topped in July and the SMH is about 24% off its high. DRAM is down 41%, and Nvidia trades at 14x forward earnings versus 17.9x for the S&P value index. "Let's not act like people are frothing at the mouth."
  - He criticized Uber as a poor stock to own into earnings. It has dropped after six of its last seven reports, and he says no one believes the autonomous-vehicle strategy.
- **Scott Wapner:**
  - He pressed on Apple's 36x forward multiple against services growth that has slowed to about 10%, and on the gross-margin miss.
  - He cited JPMorgan's cautious stance: the S&P is pressing support at 7,500 and could shift regime below it. HSBC expects more pain first, while UBS says the midterms won't change the main market drivers.
  - He noted that big drawdowns in KLA, AMAT, Marvell, Lam, Micron and Alphabet suggest much of the AI reset may already be done.
- **Stocks on the move:**
  - Vertex: a mixed report with a light guidance raise. The stock is priced for perfection and the sell-off is shaking out recent longs. The cystic fibrosis franchise is still dominant.
  - Expeditors: a record high yesterday. It is a broker, so it carries no ownership cost, and it benefits from hyperscaler spending and tariffs.
  - D.R. Horton: Raymond James cut it to underperform after an earnings miss and guide, with mortgage rates and incentives squeezing margins. It has been "in review" for three months.
- **Best Stocks in the Market (Josh Brown, energy):**
  - ConocoPhillips (COP): already broken out, RSI 74. Wait for a pullback to the breakout level on lower volume.
  - Devon (DVN): the Coterra merger closed in May. It pulled back to support. Watch for a breakout above $52–54, and it is a Porter House holding.
  - Occidental (OXY): Berkshire owns 26.5% and holds in-the-money warrants. RSI is 64, and the March high of $67 is the level to watch.
  - Risk: a negative oil supply shock.
- **Santoli's Market Memo:**
  - The Elliott letter is from someone fighting the trend, and the market has been de-risked.
  - The Nasdaq 100's RSI is where it was at the late-July low. He expects it to respond after the election.
  - A 10% rally in the SOX is needed to be more than a dead cat bounce, versus about 5% for the Nasdaq 100.
  - AMD tonight and Nvidia are the next catalysts.
- **Options Action (Oliver Renick, Uber):** About 50K options traded, with puts outnumbering calls more than 2 to 1. The options market is pricing a move of almost 7%. The top contract is the $60 put expiring Friday at $0.35, which needs an 11% drop. The stock is down about 20% this year.
- **Final Trades:**
  - Josh Brown: Amazon, up 19 points off its post-earnings low.
  - Jim Lebenthal: BlackRock, which is building a big base ready to break out. He said he could have picked any large-cap financial.
  - Jason Snipe: a name the transcript garbles ("clean beaten rays"), which I can't confirm. He pointed to a nice recent move.
  - Joe Terranova: Pfizer, a surprise since Jim doesn't own it.
