**The Setup into Q4 – September 30, 2026**

**Overview:** With Q3 ending and Q4 starting tomorrow, the committee debated a hotter-than-expected PCE print, 30-year yields at their highest since 2002, and a market concentrated in AI and mega-cap tech. They also covered Micron's earnings after the close, a new FTC probe of AI labs, and how to balance growth, income and capital preservation. Jenny announced two portfolio moves, and the hour ended with worries about speculation and market structure.

- **Market backdrop (John Fort, filling in for Scott Wapner):** The S&P was up more than 0.5%, the Nasdaq up more than 1% and the Dow slightly negative. Yields stayed stubborn, and small caps and equal-weight gave back gains as money went back into big-cap tech.
- **Liz Thomas:**
  - She never thought the Fed needed to hike. She expects inflation to ease, which would end the hiking cycle and help equities.
  - She noted the 30-year yield is at its highest since 2002, and the 10-year and 5-year at their highest since 2007. She sees opportunity at such extremes, especially in Treasuries.
  - She splits portfolios into preservation, income and growth and says to weight all three about evenly now. She stays all in on AI (semis, software, cybersecurity), balanced with Treasury income and gold if the Fed has to backtrack.
  - She thinks AI regulation will become a real market story before the 2028 election.
  - On capital preservation: hold a bond to maturity and you get your money back. A 50% stock drawdown needs a 100% gain to recover.
- **Steve Weiss:**
  - He stays cautious. Yields are heading toward 6% (last seen in 2007), but shorting is too dangerous.
  - He expects narrow, AI-driven breadth to continue and says AI spending won't pause for rates or oil. Even Caterpillar's good month is part of the AI trade, not a real-economy signal.
  - On Micron, he says about 6.5x earnings is fair value, and only 40–50% of forward business has guaranteed pricing. He thinks the cycle will be longer this time but doesn't call it "different."
  - He exited Micron after a 300%+ gain in under a year and has been trading SK Hynix. He would re-buy Micron if earnings knock the stock down.
  - Most investors are better off in indices than individual stocks.
  - On retail speculation: "the house always wins," and people will blame the banks when the market falls.
- **Jenny Harrington:**
  - Her real-economy holdings (Clorox, Comcast, Conagra, NextEra, Kimberly-Clark) have been "crushed" since Aug 21. Clorox is down 24% with a 6.3% yield. She sees a buying opportunity and says the real economy will still exist in 2030.
  - **Sold** the last of Whirlpool: repeated earnings misses and poor capital allocation.
  - **Bought** Albertsons. It has a 5.8% dividend yield and trades at about 6.5x earnings. EPS of $1.78 covers the dividend, which was raised 13% in April. She sees mid-single-digit earnings growth, JPMorgan rates it outperform, and CEO Susan Morris is a strong operator. Meg Whitman just became board chair. The 4-year, 60%+ decline stems from the failed Kroger merger.
  - **Northrop Grumman:** Expected EPS growth is 10% this year, 5% next year and 8% the year after. It has a 4.5% free-cash yield and trades at about 16x. The stock is down about 10% this year, and she bought it 13 years ago at under $100. She is unsure whether drones will disrupt incumbents.
  - **Meta:** She owns it and is unsure how durable the excitement around the new Muse AI product is. She noted the stock went from about $630 to $700+ on Muse.
  - She expects a correction and sees "funny money" out there. She cited Sorkin's 1929 book and the line that the market "transfers money from the impatient to the patient."
- **Joe Terranova:**
  - **Positioning:** Q3's theme was "bent but didn't break." Small caps and equal-weight lagged while mega-cap tech led. He bought SPY at 764 on the pullback with a tight 760 stop, below the Sept. 18 low. He is owning Nvidia and Apple, which are breaking out, and Meta, which is up 26% month to date. He expects Q4 to lean further into concentration.
  - **Micron:** He expects mid-80s gross margin and a historic profit quarter. The stock is up 541% over 52 weeks and 276% year to date (he holds it in his Joe T ETF). He thinks the real story is the read-through to semi-cap equipment, since Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron underinvested in 2022–23. He sees a capex catch-up as a 2027 story, and says supply has to come from SK Hynix and Samsung too.
  - **Eli Lilly:** Positive results on a new GLP-1 drug. Lilly is up 10% year to date and 56% over 52 weeks, versus Novo down about 24% and 31%. Lilly has done about $20B of acquisitions this year.
  - **AI policy:** On the new FTC probe, he says the market itself won't react much. He sees CrowdStrike and Palo Alto, near all-time highs, as the hedge.
  - **Market structure:** A correction is possible as market structure encourages speculation and binary prediction bets. He said "safe" isn't the word he'd use.
- **News segments:**
  - **FTC:** Chairman Andrew Ferguson opened a probe into Anthropic, OpenAI and other AI labs over consumer harm and product safety. It is unclear what information was requested or when. It came a day after the White House AI meeting.
  - **AMD:** The committee mentioned AMD buying Fei-Fei Li's World Labs for $8.2B.
  - **Moderna:** Downgraded to sell at Citi. The transcript is garbled on who owns it.
  - **Ken Griffin interview (Sarah Eisen):** He gave $3B to Carnegie Mellon, the largest individual higher-education gift ever. $2B goes to a new CMU Miami campus and $1B to Pittsburgh. Griffin joins the board. He described Citadel's rescue of Situational Awareness, buying about $16B of its portfolio in July, and worked about 40 hours straight on it. He said AI enthusiasm is tied to the need for productivity growth against $40T of national debt.
  - **Defense:** Boeing beat Northrop for a $20B Navy next-generation fighter deal. Defense stocks are heading for their worst quarter in a year.
  - **Options Action (Oliver Renick):** The XLF financials ETF has a −0.92 30-day correlation with the 10-year yield. More than twice as many calls as puts were bought. Cboe was up 6% on binary-contract news, and Robinhood options skewed bullish. One trader bought 130-strike HOOD calls that need a 27% rally through March to pay off.
- **Final Trades:**
  - **Joe Terranova:** Cboe, which needs to move higher for him to keep the position.
  - **Liz Thomas:** 10-year Treasury, noting yields could go higher but it is still a good place to be.
  - **Jenny Harrington:** Millrose Properties, a 12.2% dividend yield that was raised 2.5% last week, and the stock has been crushed by rates.
  - **Steve Weiss:** Meta, which he thinks has come down enough. The transcript is garbled here, so this attribution is likely rather than certain.
