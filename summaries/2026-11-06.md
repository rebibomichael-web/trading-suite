**More Tech Trouble Ahead? – 11/6/26**

**Overview:** Tech tried to rebound after Thursday's brutal chip selloff, the worst day for the SOX since early COVID. The panel debated whether the AI and data-center trade has been washed out or is still deleveraging, after the midterms raised the prospect of data-center slowdowns. They also covered Nvidia and AMD, the neoclouds, Amazon, Tesla/SpaceX, a TripAdvisor downgrade, and a report that Microsoft may pull back from Oracle capacity.

- **Scott Wapner (framing):** Cited Schwab: the Philly Semiconductor Index was up 106.6% YTD in mid-June and is now up 27.2%. On Thursday Micron and AMD fell more than 17% and Nvidia fell 9.5%, briefly going negative on the year. Noted many chips are 45–60% off highs (Marvell ~60%, Lam 55%, Teradyne/KLA/Applied ~56%, Micron 45%). He floated that the "2000 moment" may already have happened in the more vulnerable names.
- **Jim Lebenthal:**
  - Said Thursday's drop was part political (new Democratic governors and legislatures will slow data-center buildout, not stop it) and part hedge-fund deleveraging and pod closures. Expects strong labor, growth and profits to lift markets after a week or two of volatility.
  - Called the semis' move a possible final throe of a bear market, with Nvidia at a low forward multiple.
  - Bought Quanta Services (QUAN, ~33–35% off its high, mid-20s multiple) and says it's not a bubble. Cited Google Cloud growth of 100%+ and Azure growth of 40%+, and said power supply is the bottleneck.
  - On Micron: no debt, big free cash flow, a $100B buyback against a ~$750B market cap, and ~4x next year's earnings.
  - Added to Amazon, the cheapest in years, with AWS, Trainium and retail drivers. Called this "a consolidation of pause, not the beginning of the end" and said "this is not December 31st, 1999."
  - On Oracle's Microsoft headline: "nothing to like." Wants updates from the company before its December report.
- **Brian Belski:**
  - Called Mike Wilson's "final stages of the correction" note brilliant. Thursday's downside volume was only ~5:1, versus the 8–10:1 typical of washouts, so he wouldn't be surprised if the market turns negative Friday.
  - Owns Nebius at a small ~50 bps weight versus Nvidia at ~700 bps. Defended the neoclouds: 50–60% of capex is prepaid by customers, Nebius has ~$10B of debt versus CoreWeave's ~$50B, and the group is small relative to chips and memory.
  - Holding Nvidia into earnings (Nov 17), not trimming, and still thinks it's cheap. Expects less post-earnings pain than in prior quarters, but lower data-center expectations "change the economics" for every company in the chain. Thinks Micron's peak in sales is probably near.
  - Warned that the credit market, not rates, will drive the next market move.
- **Kevin Simpson:**
  - Said the 90s comparison fails: today's leaders are cash-rich and can withstand state, municipal or federal pressure. The have-nots are the capital-markets-dependent, more speculative names.
  - Likes the hyperscalers. Thinks a large part of the correction has already happened but says it may not be over. Nvidia is cheap and he's not selling.
  - Holds Tesla and SpaceX in his growth portfolio, bought at ~$120 and ~$150, and would add on volatility. Called the Cybertruck wind-down no big deal if it makes room for robotaxis, and said the stock "is going to be a rodeo." Position sizing is deliberately modest.
- **Jenny Harrington:**
  - The bearish voice. Doubts the data-center names have had their full "2000 moment" and noted they could go sideways for years. Cited her Ciena shares bought in 1999, which sat flat for 25 years.
  - Floated "maybe not an AI bubble but a data center bubble." Microchip's CEO twice said data centers are in a bubble.
  - Said Micron being up only ~6% after a 17% drop isn't reassuring, and asked what its 2027–29 earnings growth looks like.
  - Sold TripAdvisor (owned since ~$14) at about $10 on Wednesday, on the thesis that agentic AI is changing travel booking. It then fell ~28–30% on an earnings miss and a new CEO. She called it luck. Bank of America's double downgrade (to Underperform, target cut to $8 from $15) came after the drop.
  - Owns Tesla at under 1% of the portfolio and is "hanging on for the merger." Said Tesla looks more like an industrial or tech name than consumer discretionary.
- **Malcolm Etheridge (guest):** Trimmed Nvidia for the third time this year ahead of earnings. AMD reported 97% y/y data-center growth and showed contracts with Anthropic, OpenAI and Microsoft, plus its Helios system, which pairs software and hardware much as Nvidia's CUDA does. Nvidia's data-center revenue is ~$90B per quarter versus ~$9B for AMD, but AMD is now a credible No. 2 after four years without real competition. Calls the trim risk management, since the market has sold off after recent Nvidia reports.
- **Mike Santoli:** The rebound is tentative. Thursday looked close to a washout in tech, but the rotation and dispersion trade (equal weight S&P versus Nasdaq) broke. The key question is how much positioning stress remains. He wants the Anthropic deal done on schedule, probably not as a big down round, to refill coffers and calm credit markets, though it could mark a final panic.
- **Seema Modi (news):** Bloomberg reports Microsoft may be winding down compute capacity arrangements with Oracle, and is consolidating neocloud partnerships. Oracle shares fell. The worry is Oracle's reliance on Microsoft's investment-grade rating for off-balance-sheet loans.
- **Oliver Renick (Cboe options):**
  - EEM options volume was ~4x average, with ~190K contracts traded and ~180K of them puts. The most popular was the 54 put expiring Nov 20, about 9% lower.
  - FXI/China shows heavy call buying despite a year-long decline. He's skeptical unless something monumental has changed for China.
- **Pippa Stevens (news):** Bomb threats hit flights over Europe (fighter jets escorted three planes over Hungary, nothing found). Mississippi and Alaska Senate races are still too close to call. Pope Leo begins a 12-day tour of Uruguay, Argentina and Peru.

  - Amazon, with a strong balance sheet despite credit-market worries. Probably Jim Lebenthal.
