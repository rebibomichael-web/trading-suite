Time to Be Cautious on Stocks? — September 2, 2026

Overview: The Investment Committee debated whether September's historically rough seasonality and rising bond yields warrant genuine caution, generally concluding investors should stay selectively bullish rather than turn bearish. Much of the discussion centered on the momentum factor's worst quarterly stretch in 25 years, AI/data-center political backlash, and a wave of earnings reactions (Palo Alto's plunge, Dell's surge, ahead of Broadcom).

- **Joe Terranova**: Says be "selective," not bearish — foundationally still a bull market despite stretched valuations. Sees the momentum factor as washed out but wants proof before stepping back in; cites Broadcom as the real-time test of AI momentum psychology (stock down 23.5% over 3 months, cheaper at 21x forward vs. 32x in June); references David Tepper's Q2 SanDisk exit as an example of "tacit knowledge" investing. Final Trade: Valero.
- **Liz Thomas**: Argues today's volatility is about the Fed/Treasury mixed messages, high yields (10-year at 4.80%), and an AI theme maturing into its next phase — not the calendar. Says a pullback would be healthy, keeping valuations in check for the bull cycle to extend. Highlights BofA data: clients net buyers of U.S. equities last week ($5.3B inflows, tech among sectors bought).
- **Jim Lebenthal**: Thinks the Fed likely hikes soon (per Chairman Warsh's speech); not selling wholesale but liquidating "idiosyncratic" names he's soured on — sold PG&E this hour over California wildfire liability/political risk and a new strategic review. Bullish on Vertex Pharmaceuticals (owns it; cites Chronetics $10B acquisition) and Eli Lilly (JPM reiterated overweight, $1,400 target, ~$1.1T market cap, GLP-1 dominance). On Wynn Resorts (initiated outperform at Wolfe, $128 target): defends the recent weak chart as UAE/Strait of Hormuz-related, expects recovery. Final Trade: Oracle ("compute constrained... will benefit").
- **Steve Weiss**: Says don't sell stocks now, keep a "shopping list" and dry powder; trimmed some AI plays into cash. Long-time Taiwan Semiconductor bull (initiated at $515 target by Stiefel) — calls it possibly a better "must-own" than Nvidia given its foundry monopoly, though clarifies he's not saying swap one for the other; still very bullish Nvidia too, thinks it could hit new highs in September. Defends Adobe (owns via Seritage-style logic — cites his firm re-upping its Adobe enterprise contract) against Jenny Harrington's bearish "vibe coding" replacement thesis; cites Dell's blowout guidance/City and BofA $600 targets as bullish read-through for Oracle's AI capex story. On Palo Alto Networks' historic ~10.5% drop despite record earnings: says valuations can't be justified and competitive threats are rising from OpenAI/Alphabet/Anthropic cyber tools. Final Trade: Alphabet ("too cheap not to own").
- **Data center/AI political backlash segment**: Sam Altman (OpenAI) warns of "unsustainable silliness" — random NeoClouds building capacity without revenue support, though he's confident in OpenAI's own compute plans. Treasury Secretary at the G20 says AI companies/data-center builders have done a "horrendous job" explaining themselves to the public and will "have to take some of the blame." Panel discusses growing backlash (Pennsylvania scrutiny, Missouri recall vote, Senator Ted Cruz pushback) and draws a fracking-industry-messaging parallel; Jim Lebenthal calls it a real, underpriced multi-year risk hitting stocks like GE Vernova, FTAI, and Vertiv.
- **Options Action (Oliver Renick)**: Huge options volume in Brazil's EWZ ETF — 300,000+ contracts (6x average), 94% calls, ~$30M premium; most popular trade is the November $45 call (needs 20%+ upside); roughly half the call premium is being sold, suggesting traders are fading the rally as much as chasing it.
- **Calls of the Day**: Vertex Pharmaceuticals reinstated overweight at Morgan Stanley, $665 target; Eli Lilly reiterated overweight at JPMorgan, $1,400 target; Wynn Resorts initiated outperform at Wolfe, $128 target; Taiwan Semiconductor initiated at Stiefel, $515 target.
- **Final Trades**: Steve Weiss — Alphabet; Jim Lebenthal — Oracle; Liz Thomas — Gold; Joe Terranova — Valero.
