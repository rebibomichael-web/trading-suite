# Trading the Tech Surge: Your Next Move — 8/7/26

**Overview:** Tech and semis powered the Nasdaq toward its best week since mid-April, with software, chips, and megacaps like Microsoft, NVIDIA, and Palantir all bouncing hard even as the jobs report missed. The committee largely shrugged off weak payroll data because inflation is cooling and earnings/guidance remain strong (48% S&P earnings growth), with several strategists raising S&P targets toward 8,000+.

- **Stephanie Link** — Called the market "close to Goldilocks": soft jobs but slowing wage inflation, lower unit labor costs, and rising productivity are good for the Fed and disinflation; sees no Fed hikes through year-end. Bought **NVIDIA** (new position, ~$218, calls it a long-term investment not a trade), owns **Broadcom** (18x earnings, cheaper than Staples names, 85% revenue growth, mid-70s gross margins), added a small/volatile **Micron** position ($100B in bookings/take-or-pay contracts), and bought more **SpaceX** on the post-lockup, post-earnings 40% pullback (92% revenue growth; likes the long-term despite heavy capex). Final trade: **Stanley Black & Decker (Stay Lauder — Stanley/Black & Decker referenced generically as "Stay Lauder")**... [garbled in transcript] — see Final Trades below.
- **Jenny Harrington** — Torn between near-term bullishness (fully invested, loves market breadth: Dow Dividend Index +16%, Russell +21%, S&P +14%) and longer-term worry that productivity gains could eventually pressure jobs and consumer spending. Owns **Microchip Technology preferred** (bought at $41, now $71; 5.3% yield, stock trades at 20x earnings) citing strong industrial/data-center/aerospace segment growth. Likes **Charles Schwab** at new highs, still only 14.5x earnings. Holds **NextEra convertible preferred** (7.9% yield) as a hedge on the NextEra/Dominion merger fight with Virginia's governor. Trimming **Roche** on valuation discipline (down to 3% yield after strong run). Final trade: **Amcor**, 5.4% yield.
- **Kevin Simpson** — Sees the tech rally driven by encouraging jobs revisions (12-month average job creation near 36,000) reducing rate-hike risk. Bought more **NVIDIA** at $218 ("this is not a trade, this is an investment," now pays a dividend). Doubled down on **Amgen** post-earnings (beat/raise, Rapata/Avenidae ramping, Maritide in pipeline). Final trade: **Amazon**, cited for demonstrating returns on AI investment.
- **Brent (Bryn) Talkington** — Highlighted Microsoft's ~8.5% weekly surge as the epicenter of a software/cloud re-rating (models becoming "swappable," cloud revenue guided up to 43% from 38-40%), and framed last week as a healthy reset after the "situational awareness" leverage blowup. Bullish on Palantir's short-covering rally (+37.5% this week) and Snowflake (+11% this week, 30-34% product revenue growth guided). Noted Palantir options flow (calls funding stock purchase, fading near-term vol but not bearish). On gold, likes exposure via **INFL** (diversified basket) rather than direct precious metals given yen-intervention macro risk. Final trade: **Netflix**, sees drift to mid-$80s by year-end.
- **Calls of the Day / Notable Upgrades:** CFRA raised S&P target to 8,050 (was 7,400, sees 8,650 next year); Tom Lee sees 8,000 possible in August; Netflix rated buy at Redburn/Rothschild ($93 PT); NextEra/Dominion merger political fight from Virginia governor; Quanta Services upgraded to overweight at KeyBank; Allstate downgraded to underweight ($250 PT, Wells Fargo); Fiserv target cut to $78 from $90 at Mizuho despite reiterated outperform (Steph plans to exit within six months, citing lost management credibility); Eli Lilly target raised to $1,300 at Argus; Natera raised to $320 at BTIG; Roche upgraded to overweight at Morgan Stanley ($63 PT).
- **Options Action (Oliver Renick)** — Palantir saw over $600M in options premium (nearly 90% calls), 4x average volume, the day's second-biggest single trade: selling $101M of Sept $155 calls while buying $110M of Dec $175 calls — a bet that fades short-term volatility without being bearish.
- **Final Trades:** Bryn Talkington — Netflix; Kevin Simpson — Amazon; Stephanie Link — Amcor (5.4% yield); Jenny Harrington — Amcor (also referenced/discussed).
