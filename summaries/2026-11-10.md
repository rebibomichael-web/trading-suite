**NVIDIA Fallout, Anthropic IPO and Hot CPI Hit Markets, Tuesday, November 10, 2026**

**Overview:** Stocks fell after a hot CPI report and a jump in yields. NVIDIA dropped again on the fallout from its CEO Jensen Huang's conduct, and Microsoft passed it in market cap. Anthropic's IPO terms landed and drew strong demand.

- **Macro:** CPI came in hot. The 2-year yield rose about 20 bps. Fed Governor Waller opened the door to 50 bps in December, and Logan was hawkish too. Oliver Renick (Options Action) saw heavy bullish call buying in TLT. In credit, LQD and HYG saw heavy put volume, and IWM traded about 2x as many puts as calls.
- **Josh Brown (NVIDIA):**
  - He is a 12-year holder who has been through 70% drawdowns. Unless a bombshell shows Huang knowingly broke rules, he doesn't think Huang should be removed from the board.
  - He expects the stock to be "disgusting and ugly" for a while, with investigations and politics ahead, and said funds are de-risking on a business basis. Leverage and programmatic trading turn a 4% move into 7%.
  - He argued the AI mega-caps are not one trade. Year to date, MSFT is up 12%, GOOGL up 8% and AMZN up 17%, while NVDA, AVGO (-5%) and TSLA (-16%) are down. Energy is the best sector, up 37%, and 69% of S&P 500 stocks are above their 50-day.
- **Rob Sechan (NVIDIA):**
  - He called the drop a "governance discount, not a demand discount." He bought NVDA last night in his personal account, and his portfolios are slightly underweight.
  - CUDA can't be replaced if the CEO leaves, and the stock is down about 11% over two days.
  - He argued that a hyperscaler procurement problem isn't a power problem, and that Vistra at 13x earnings is healthy.
- **Joe Terranova (NVIDIA):**
  - Discretionary and retail holders aren't the sellers. Quant and high-frequency selling is driving the move. He expects more of it if the stock breaks its 52-week low of $164.27.
  - 78% of analysts still rate it a buy, and the average target moved from $324 to $320. Seaport has a sell with a $180 target, Citi cut its target to $285, and Wells cut to $300. BofA removed NVDA from its US 1 list.
  - He said NVDA is no longer a price momentum stock: it is down about 20% in six months and flat since July 2025.
  - He called Broadcom a beneficiary through its custom chips. AVGO is up 11% on the week and AMD up 14%.
- **SoftBank/GE Vernova:**
  - SoftBank took a $3.7B writedown tied to power equipment procurement, and GE Vernova fell about 7%. Joe said that shows sovereign wealth funds backing off, and he tempers his enthusiasm for 2027 AI spending.
  - Rob countered that 80% of Vernova's slots are sold through 2032.
  - Josh said the AI hardware trade is unwinding after the midterms. Micron is still up 150% this year, Dell up 163% and SanDisk up 250%.
- **Anthropic IPO (Leslie Picker report):**
  - Anthropic will market shares at $70–$78 for about $64.4B. Strategic investors (AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC) are adding a $26B private placement. With the greenshoe, the total is about $100B.
  - The high end implies a $1.4T fully diluted valuation. Pro forma cash is about $210B. The book is reportedly already covered, with demand stronger than SpaceX at the same stage.
  - About 20% goes to retail. Pricing is next Wednesday and trading starts Thursday on Nasdaq.
  - Josh said a lower valuation than the $1.5–2T chatter is positive, because it leaves room for a pop. He called the IPO the best barometer for the AI trade.
  - Rob cited 75% enterprise revenue, about 12,000 customers spending $100K or more a month, and 900% net revenue retention. He would buy it, and Josh said he would too.
  - Josh and Rob both liked the setup; one noted $81B in run-rate revenue and that Goldman sees free cash flow positive by 2030.
  - Later, Claude announced Sheets, Whiteboards, Data and Meetings tools. Microsoft, Alphabet and Zoom dipped. Josh said Anthropic is coming for SaaS and that Adobe and Intuit aren't in the clear. Another panelist said earlier "Claude scare" selloffs, like the cyber one, proved fleeting.
- **Calls of the Day:**
  - **Carlyle:** UBS downgraded it to neutral with a $47 target, down from $60. A panelist agreed. Private equity and credit face rate and wealth-channel headwinds, and he is holding but not adding.
  - **Ross and TJX:** Both were initiated, Ross at overweight with a $276 target and TJX at neutral with a $139 target. Joe owns both but prefers Ross and Target.
  - **Mastercard:** Citi raised its target to $800 from $675. A panelist is up 33% since adding in May and calls it a toll booth and a sneaky inflation hedge.
  - **Delta:** Rothschild reiterated buy. Josh sees a breakout above $92.93 and a possible triple-digit stock, and he is long. Joe agreed.
  - **Walmart:** Mizuho raised its target to $135 from $125. Joe said Walmart and Costco are taking share and that WMT looks set to make a new high.
- **Josh's Best Stocks in the Market update:**
  - **Palantir:** Down 20% since the Oct. 15 write-up, so the $185 stop was hit.
  - **Okta:** Down 24% since Oct. 29, now 28% below its $235 breakout high. It is off the list for a while. Earnings will come Dec. 1.
  - **HPE:** Written up at $22, now $46 after trading in the 70s. The lesson is to trail stops.
  - Josh said both Palantir and Okta fell on price action, not fundamentals.
  - Rob likes HPE as enterprise AI plumbing, trading at 10x earnings.
- **Mike Santoli's market memo:** Half of the S&P decline is still NVIDIA. Last Thursday may mark the low in correlation. NVIDIA is in a buyback blackout, and earnings are Nov. 17. He called Anthropic's numbers net positive for the AI trade.
- **Final Trades:**
  - **Rob Sechan:** Broadcom, noting semis trade at one of their widest discounts to the S&P in two decades. He owns NVDA personally.
  - **Joe Terranova:** Amazon. He thinks it will make a run at its $275 high.
  - **Josh Brown:** The transcript is garbled and mentions LQD and natural gas, so his pick is unclear.
