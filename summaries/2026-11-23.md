**NVIDIA Rebounds as Jensen Huang Returns; AI Trade Rally Debate. CNBC Halftime Report, Nov 24, 2026**

Overview: NVIDIA's board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO. The stock jumped about 33% and the Nasdaq rose nearly 7% on a huge AI and semiconductor rebound. The panel debated the "Trump put," how fragile a market this dependent on AI is, and whether this rally is more than a reflex bounce. CrowdStrike's roughly $44B acquisition also sparked a cybersecurity consolidation discussion.

- **Josh Brown:**
  - He had predicted Huang would return if NVDA fell toward $100. He said the president's role was "definitely so," because trillions in retirement accounts were exposed to AI.
  - He is more bullish than Joe into year-end. Nasdaq 100 P/E is down about 40% this year, one of the five worst derating episodes ever, while S&P tech earnings estimates rose from $100 to $160. He calls it the opposite of 2000, since "nobody believes" the growth.
  - Tech is expected to grow about 40% in 2027, twice the overall market. The risk is that booked chip sales get pulled.
  - On CrowdStrike's deal, he called it a coup: it buys the best-in-class scaled inline zero-trust network (8,000 customers, about $4B of new ARR), paid in stock and immediately accretive.
- **Joe Terranova:**
  - His JOTE ETF dropped NVDA in last week's rebalance, and he's "great" with that. It's equal-weighted and holds Astera Labs, and it was up nearly 5% today. The rebalance was off-schedule because of a new NYSE partnership, and the strategy stays rules-based and momentum-leaning.
  - The market is still in a "precarious position." He says you won't get back the first half of 2026, and regulatory headwinds from the election remain.
  - He sees continued cyber consolidation and named Okta as a name to watch.
- **Liz Thomas:**
  - She is horrified that $80T of US equity value hinges on one person. If one person can erase $8T and add back $4T, the market is "extremely fragile."
  - She argues the Fed is in a hiking posture and the cycle is aging. She prefers value and quality over momentum, calls 2027 "a tough year," and sees a near-term bounce as likely.
  - She also noted the stronger dollar as a defensive signal. She is bullish on cyber, with the cyber ETF down about 11%.
  - The Evercore note read on air, which she did not write, calls today the most consequential AI development since ChatGPT and "AI's first bailout." She agreed this shows a "presidential put" but said it doesn't erase the overhangs: a Democratic Congress, CapEx questions, and cyber issues. She doesn't expect the summer highs soon.
- **Jenny Harrington:**
  - She said she is spooked by market fragility. She is not ready to say Goldman's first condition for a bottom is met, since many questions remain.
  - Her discipline-growth strategy once owned Palo Alto but dropped it on the 5% free-cash-flow hurdle. It holds CrowdStrike, not Okta or Palo Alto.
- **Brad Gerstner (Altimeter, guest):**
  - He wrote to the board urging it not to rush to judgment. He credits the board for reversing course after the administration and DOJ found no evidence of a national security violation. He did not speak to the president directly and declined to discuss other contacts.
  - He isn't calling for board changes now, but suggested adding export-control and compliance expertise. He defended Huang's 60 direct reports.
  - He called Huang "humble" and "apologetic" about his personal mistake.
  - NVDA was $174 before the resignation, down from $230. The next leg up needs labs to show durable revenue growth ($5B/month to $10–15B). He wants Anthropic's run rate to go from about $82B to $200B by the end of next year. NVDA's multiple implied negative growth for 2028–29, which he calls "nuts." The rally is "not a get out of jail free card."
  - On Anthropic's IPO: priced at $83 and now about $87. About 300M employee shares could be sold on day one, and there was heavy hedging. He expects $30–50B of index-inclusion buying within three weeks and a $3T valuation by the end of next year (10x forward revenue). Altimeter added over $1B and was one of the top 10 allocations.
- **Mike Santoli (CNBC):**
  - The rally stopped where expected, with the S&P near 7,300. The index was already set to open up more than 2% before the Huang news, and Friday was a tactical low.
  - The AI trade has a few percent more room before it's more than a reflex bounce. You can have a Trump put or a Fed put, "not both."
- **Featured items:**
  - Breaking news from Megan Cassella: Huang will testify publicly as sole witness before Senate Banking on Dec 3. The hearing is titled "Protecting America's AI Advantage: Oversight of U.S. Export Controls." A closed-door House session was scheduled for Dec 1.
  - Chip names also surged: Micron up almost 20%, SK Hynix about 20%, plus gains in Marvell, Oracle, Palantir, CoreWeave and Nebius.
  - Mike Wilson (Morgan Stanley) called this "the fat pitch."
  - Goldman's three conditions for a correction bottom: AI earnings confidence, a dovish Fed signal, and a positioning washout. The panel judged only the third was plausibly met, on Friday.
- **Final Trades:**
  - **Josh Brown:** Bought some Anthropic for his dividend strategy.
  - **Jenny Harrington:** Kimberly Clark, with a 5% yield, as a defensive holding.
  - **Liz Thomas:** Cybersecurity, because hacks and outages are a feature of this cycle.
  - **Joe Terranova (attribution inferred, as the transcript doesn't label it):** Amgen, "cautiously bullish."
