**Wall Street's "Most Exciting Era"? The Investment Committee Weighs In — 8/20/26**

**Overview:** Stocks slipped as long-end yields, oil, and Iran headlines all ticked up, even as BlackRock's Rick Rieder called this one of the most exciting investment environments ever. The committee debated whether rising yields threaten the rally, whether memory chips (Micron) are now a secular rather than cyclical bet, and dug into a batch of earnings movers (Walmart, Lowe's, Alibaba) plus fresh moves in Spotify, Netflix, and crypto.

- **Josh Brown**: Long Micron but pushed back on calling memory "no longer a commodity" — still cyclical, just an unusually long cycle from the AI buildout; flagged that momentum ETFs like MTUM are ~48% tech/AI-concentrated and prefers non-AI momentum names (JB Hunt, Marathon Petroleum, Incyte, Expeditors, Valero, East West Bancorp); not worried about CrowdStrike's CTO departure ("in George Kurtz we trust"); doesn't see a Walmart-driven consumer breakdown, would buy the dip; **bought Spotify** (best media business, ~31% off highs, stop near $419, entry ~$520) and separately flagged **SharkNinja** as a Best Stock (added to list 8/5, $182, ~28x adj. EPS, buy level ~$155 on a pullback to the 50-day).
- **Jim Lebenthal**: Agreed the AI/data-center demand story is a genuine reset (cited data-center CEOs saying builds are "first inning" and 12x larger than prior generations); on Lowe's/Home Depot, no rush to move given rate-sensitive housing backdrop, staying long both; final trade: **his own suit**, quietly having a nice year.
- **Rob Sechan**: Sees yield backup as largely AI-issuance driven (Treasury Secretary flagged "asymmetric" impact of hyperscaler debt on long rates); called rising yields a buying opportunity for duration ("I'll buy a 5% 10-year"), citing starting-yield as the closest thing to an "iron law" in investing; long Alibaba despite the 75% net-income drop, likes the cloud/AI/e-commerce combo at a reasonable valuation; owns Marriott, likes travel stocks broadly, no plans to sell despite the target cut to $395 (UBS); trimming SpaceX around the lockup expiration (~300M+ shares); final trade: **ServiceNow**.
- **Stephanie Link (referenced discussion, via Rieder framing)**: Not present as a distinct panelist bullet beyond Rieder's remarks — folded into overview.
- **Rick Rieder (BlackRock, cited quote)**: Called this the "most exciting, most fun investment environment" ever, citing AI, healthcare (Moderna/Merck) and cross-currents; said the market could add another 5–10% this year.
- **Micron CEO Sanjay Mehrotra (clip)**: Argued memory is now "strategic infrastructure for AI," not a commodity — sparked the panel's cyclical-vs-secular debate.
- **Moderna/Merck update**: Moderna gave back some of Tuesday's massive gain (still up huge post skin-cancer phase 3 data); Merck upgraded to overweight at Morgan Stanley (cancer pipeline) but downgraded at RBC on valuation (PT raised to $150 from $142).
- **Walmart/TJX/Target**: Walmart fell ~9.3% on soft comps despite an earnings/revenue beat and raised guide; TJX downgraded on an "execution misstep"; panel largely says the consumer is fine, sees this as stock-specific/valuation-driven overreaction, contrasts with Target's better execution.
- **Netflix (Bill Baruch discussion via chart)**: Framed as a possible bottom after a >50% correction from June 2025 highs — 42% expected EPS growth this year, 21x earnings, five straight weekly gains (best streak since Oct '24); wildcard is a potential Universal bid.
- **SpaceX lockup / CrowdStrike / Walmart / Lowe's-Home Depot / Micron**: covered above by speaker.
- **Options Action (Oliver Renick)**: Bitcoin up ~15% this week; heavy call buying in IBIT (5x average volume) and especially Hyperliquid-linked PURR (16x average), with bullish bets targeting PURR above $10 by Friday and possible +50% move by mid-October.
- **Mike Santoli's Midday Word**: Attributed the pullback to a mix of yields, oil, and rotation out of banks/industrials (each down ~3% week-to-date) as semis stalled; not alarmed by the 10-year near 4.7%, noting it's within the 3-year 4–5% range; called Walmart's report the informal end of earnings season, with focus shifting to Nvidia next week.
- **Final Trades**: Rob Sechan — ServiceNow; Jim Lebenthal — his suit (a joke, playing on his quietly strong year); Josh Brown — Uber (Apollo Go robotaxi with Baidu launching in Dubai).
