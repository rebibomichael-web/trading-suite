**Managing Risk in a Volatile Market – Halftime Report, 10/19/26**

**Overview:** The panel debated whether the neocloud and semiconductor selloff, set off by stress in CoreWeave's unsecured debt, signals a coming peak in AI spending or is a healthy shakeout. Nasdaq bounced about 1.67%, with hyperscaler earnings and Meta on Wednesday as the next catalysts. Other topics were Cleveland Cliffs' surge, a Datadog upgrade, and Bill Baruch's portfolio moves.

*The transcript has no speaker labels, so some attributions below are inferred from context.*

- **Joe Terranova (likely):**
  - Expects an AI capex slowdown in 2027, so investors should moderate earnings expectations. He cited Nvidia, which has gone from triple-digit growth to a consolidation phase.
  - Is avoiding CoreWeave equity. The unsecured debt is thinly traded, and that volatility is feeding the stock.
  - Still sees the AI trade as intact, but says positioning had become extreme and is being worked off.
  - Likes hedge funds lightening semi exposure, because Nov–Dec is seasonally strong and positioning looks better. Says the next sell-off is the time to add risk, citing Mike Wilson. Expects momentum to lead into year-end.
  - Is not convinced last week's "broadening" was real. He called it defensive, citing oil at $102 and high yields.
  - Datadog: upgraded to Outperform at Bernstein with a $350 target. The ETF bought it in April at $132, and it hit an all-time high of $300 on Oct. 12. Earnings are Nov. 4.
- **Steve Weiss (likely):**
  - Says stocks anticipate a spending peak about a year ahead, so he has trimmed AI exposure. Micron is gone, he took profits in Vertiv, and Nvidia is small.
  - Taiwan Semi is his largest position. He added Meta this morning when it dropped $15, and it is his second largest. He praised Zuckerberg as a founder-operator. He also owns Alphabet, Goldman Sachs and Apple.
  - Believes some compute demand is double-ordering typical of late cycles. Says that if he is wrong, he is "prepared to miss it."
  - Notes Meta targets: Morgan Stanley $820, Ives $900.
- **Jim Lebenthal (likely):**
  - Argues it's "97, not 99" and that this isn't the peak. Unlike the 1990s, there is no dark-fiber glut, and hyperscalers say they'd use more capacity if they had it.
  - Bullish on Micron. It peaked in June, but he says demand hasn't slowed, there's no debt, ~$100B cash and ~$130B free cash flow, and it is buying back shares. Also bullish on semi equipment and is watching Lam, which reports this week. Lam is down 37% from its June high but up 62% year to date.
  - Expects hyperscaler earnings to show spending continuing, compute supply constraints, and fast data-center payback. Cites Anthropic's possible IPO before Thanksgiving and OpenAI's private round as catalysts.
  - Called Cleveland Cliffs the "Call of the Day" case:
    - Two straight strong quarters. 2027 EBITDA guidance was raised to $4.5B (from $3B last quarter), while the Street sits near $2.5B.
    - Enterprise value is about 3x that EBITDA. At a trough multiple of 5x, he says the equity roughly doubles.
    - Leverage should reach 2.5x by mid-2027.
    - Tariff risk: hot-rolled coil is $1,300/ton vs. $800 a year and a half ago, and the forward curve is above $1,200. He doesn't expect tariffs below 25%, and tariffs have also hurt via Stelco. Weiss pushed back that falling tariffs could remove the benefit. Weiss had suggested Steel Dynamics in August.
- **Other panel points:** Semis are coming off their worst losing streak in about 20 years, and the Mag 7 have outperformed semis for 10 straight days (Jefferies). Individual stock volatility is 3x the market's. One panelist called AI and tech a buying opportunity on valuation and earnings. Jamie Dimon told the WSJ the economy is "booming." Neocloud stress is tied to Treasury yields.
- **Bill Baruch (portfolio moves):**
  - **Bought McDonald's:** trading three standard deviations below its long-run multiple and down ~25% this year. He likes its "Next" growth strategy and says GLP-1 and rate worries are priced in.
  - **Added Walmart:** a new buy around Labor Day, now back above its 200-day moving average. He cites margin expansion and Sam's Club.
  - **Sold Arm (60 bp position) and Amkor:** he calls the AI trade still early innings and remains overweight semi manufacturers and core hyperscalers, but is trimming at the margins after OpenAI's revenue shortfall and a hot CPI.
- **ETF Edge (Dom Chu with Valerie Grimba, RBC):** Investors are buying the dip, with the biggest ETF flows into semis, the hardest-hit area. They are selective and price-sensitive. Fixed income flows are more uncertain.
- **Options Action (Oliver Renick):** SpaceX options imply a ~6% move after earnings. About 190k calls vs. under 80k puts traded, and the Friday $170 call was the most popular strike.
- **Market Memo (Mike Santoli):** Sentiment is tentative, but positioning has reset and the bar is lower for earnings. The S&P has crossed 7,700 four times in four sessions. Meta is just below $800 resistance, and he thinks hardware names will drive the AI-trade outcome.
- **Final Trades** (speakers not identified in the transcript):
  - UnitedHealth, which has executed well and should keep moving higher.
  - Twilio, a low-risk position trade with a $225 target. The speaker said it is not in the ETF and not currently personally owned, but will be at today's close.
  - Transocean, whose Valaris merger has cleared all regulators, plus good contract news.
