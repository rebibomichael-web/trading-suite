### [WTF did Cathie Wood and Gwynne Shotwell Just say about SpaceX?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gV-7O6Rl9CQ)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Sep 20

Cathie Wood and SpaceX president Gwynne Shotwell dropped some eye-popping numbers this week. Wood's team calculated each Starship launch could carry $1 billion worth of Starlink capacity, and if Elon Musk hits his goal of 10,000 flights a year by 2030, that's $10 trillion in annual revenue — making SpaceX's $1.75 trillion valuation look like a bargain in hindsight; Musk himself said it's "not impossible." The next big test is Starship Flight 14, targeted for Tuesday Sept 23, which will be the first orbital flight and will deploy the first 26 Starlink V3 satellites (each with 10x the capacity of older satellites); Musk says V3 will eventually deliver 100x today's total bandwidth. Shotwell also talked up putting data centers in space (rebranded "supercompute"), arguing orbital real estate and cooling are free versus the years-long permitting and rising land costs of Earth-based data centers. She announced a new Starlink mobile deal with Japan's KDDI for broadband and voice calls in dead zones by end of 2027, and took some jabs at Boeing over its slow, costlier Starliner capsule program. The video's guest, VC Larry Goldberg, is broadly bullish, calling the 10,000-flights/$10-trillion scenario plausible given SpaceX's execution track record, though he's skeptical of hitting the full 10,000 flights by exactly 2030.

### [FSD v15 Is Tesla's Most Important Catalyst Right Now](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fBQ8-WnyRog)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Sep 20

Tesla bulls should watch FSD version 15, not the Cybercab or regulation—both are basically solved. Version 15 is a full architectural overhaul (~10 billion parameters vs. 1 billion in v14), and Tesla's robotaxi fleet is already running early v15 builds, with about 40% of its planned improvements merged as of the July earnings call. AI chief Ashok Elluswamy said v15 will be "ridiculously safe" and told a fan in September that the next merge (expected around October) should finally enable 24/7 robotaxi service, up from the current 6am-10pm window. The robotaxi fleet went from 380,000 to 1 million unsupervised miles in about six weeks (roughly 17% weekly mile growth), and the video's author thinks that could jump to 20-25% weekly once v15's software cap lifts. Key things to track going forward: whether 24/7 service actually launches in October, whether Tesla's Texas DMV vehicle registrations (currently flat at 439) start climbing again, whether Florida's tiny pilot fleets (3-4 cars each in Miami/Orlando/Tampa) scale up, and whether Ashok reports well above 40% of v15 tracks merged on the late-October Q3 earnings call. Regular customer cars are expected to get v15 by end of 2026 or early 2027.
