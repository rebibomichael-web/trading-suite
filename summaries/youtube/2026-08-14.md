### [Wait… Tesla Built HOW Many Cybercabs?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qmoVVZKoLwI)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Aug 13

Tesla's Cybercab is showing up everywhere: spotters counted 38 units in Fort Worth, 85 in Houston, and rows in Franklin, TN, with VIN numbers suggesting Tesla may have already built over 2,300 of them (up from an estimated ~1,000 about a month ago—implying roughly one built per hour). Details confirm it's purpose-built for robotaxi service, not a repurposed consumer car: it uses Continental "A Contact" tires made specifically for autonomous EV fleets (not sold to consumers), has an RGB front light bar (possibly for rider pickup identification, seen going red-white-blue at a Florida event), and the first Starlink-equipped Cybercab was just confirmed by Tesla to handle connectivity gaps during rides. Florida Governor DeSantis publicly test-rode a robotaxi through simulated hazards (a child running into the street, a staged crash) and called it impressive, part of Florida positioning itself as an AV testing hub alongside Waymo. The video also compares Tesla's pace to Waymo, which is upfitting ~1,000 Hyundai Ioniq 5 minivans at a Magna factory in Arizona; the hosts argue Tesla's Cybercab is roughly 3x more energy-efficient per mile than Waymo's Jaguar I-Pace and that Tesla could out-produce Waymo's entire fleet in about a day of Cybercab output within a year. Overall takeaway: Tesla is scaling Cybercab production and real-world testing fast, with mounting evidence it's a purpose-built, cost-optimized robotaxi rather than a modified passenger car.

### [Is Something Holding Tesla Back?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cwU-R-5ojFU)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Aug 13

Tesla stock is stuck around $340, way off its recent $400 high, while SpaceX shares have recovered above their $135 IPO price, and the panel (Jeff Lutz, Alexandra Mertz, and James from Invest Answers) says this is the slowest post-selloff recovery in Tesla's history, mostly because the SpaceX IPO is sucking liquidity away from Tesla. They debunk several "conspiracy theories": Elon's pay package didn't secretly let him dodge milestones (that clause was known months ago and only kicks in on a change-of-control merger scenario), the SpaceX index-fund inclusion wasn't a backroom deal (FTSE Russell confirmed it was investor-driven), and robotaxi isn't being deliberately "sandbagged" — it's dealing with real software/convenience issues (like long pickup/dropoff loops) while safely scaling toward roughly 90-100 unsupervised cars and pursuing state permits (e.g., a new but limited 10-vehicle Nevada approval). Alexandra flags a SpaceX options deadline around end of August tied to Elon's ~$8.40 strike shares, and speculates (with heavy caveats) that a Tesla-SpaceX merger announcement could come soon, possibly even "Monday," driven by political timing concerns. James pegs Tesla's fair value around $495-499 and argues Tesla is currently the cheaper buy versus SpaceX given expected merger premiums (40-60%), while Wall Street price targets sit near $500 for Tesla and imply ~58% upside for SpaceX from current levels. The group agrees the long-term thesis rests on Optimus (~$30T TAM) and Cybercab robotaxis (~$10T TAM), with no clear near-term threat to that story, though nothing is guaranteed and much depends on merger terms and execution timelines.

### [Tom Lee: S&P 8,000, Then a 10% Pullback](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rt7qeyOkpyU)
**Fundstrat** · Aug 13

Tom Lee still sees the S&P 500 reaching 7,900-8,000 by end of August, and thinks that milestone could set up a roughly 10% pullback shortly after—not because fundamentals turn bad, but due to bloated margin debt, uncertainty over Fed nominee Kevin Warsh's policy framework, midterm election jitters, and a looming SpaceX stock unlock. He notes 2027 S&P earnings estimates have already climbed to around $410-425, which at a 20x multiple implies close to 9,000, and other strategists like Ed Yardeni see 8,400 by year-end. Robinhood's Stephanie Guild agrees rate risk and AI-driven "speed crashes" (parabolic runs followed by consolidation) are real threats, while Payne Capital's Courtney Garcia is more bullish, pointing to broad sector participation over the past month (financials +4%, healthcare +4.7%, industrials +2.5%, materials +3%, tech +3.25%) as evidence the rally isn't just AI mega-caps. Lee also argues that despite strong earnings growth, valuations have actually compressed by two turns since March, and widespread skepticism about an "AI bubble" or "earnings bubble" is itself a healthy sign that could let the bull market run longer, drawing a parallel to the 18-month, 35% rally after the 1998 LTCM crisis.
