### [Tesla Just Made A Move China Won’t Like](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oX39Za67KyM)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Nov 21

Tesla filed plans for a $3.1 billion polysilicon refinery near Corpus Christi, the first step in making its own solar supply chain in Texas, alongside the $10 billion Crystal Sun solar factory near Houston. China makes more than 90% of this material today. Guest Basher (a CFA) estimates the refinery would make 200,000 tons a year, worth about $4.2 billion at the new $21/kg minimum import price, and that the real cost could be roughly five times higher. He cites an analyst who thinks $3.1 billion buys only 30–40k tons a year. Tesla and SpaceX each want 100 GW of annual solar capacity by 2028, which needs about 200,000 tons of polysilicon a year. The same silicon, at higher purity, could also feed the $16.8 billion Terafab chip plant, which would need far less of it.

The refinery isn't approved yet. Tesla says it isn't economical without a tax abatement of 100% for 2028–2030 and 50% for 2031–2040, and the governor's signature probably comes after Gina Hinojosa takes office on January 19. Basher argues these abatements aren't really tax breaks. They are meant to smooth lumpy tax payments, so that counties don't have to raise property taxes later.

At SpaceX, Cameron County voted 3–2 against hearing a tax break for its $24 billion Starbase AI data center, a project that would have paid about $58 million in year one (roughly 25% of the county's $265 million budget). Starbase's own commission, where two of its three members work for SpaceX, votes December 9. Basher expects both deals to eventually go through, and says construction at Starbase continues regardless.
