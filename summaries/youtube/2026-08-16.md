### [Jensen Just Solved Elon’s $119 Billion Problem](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8Bs7opzk0-M)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Aug 15

Jensen Huang and Nvidia are helping Elon Musk fund massive AI infrastructure by turning compute into a financeable asset class, similar to how airlines lease planes instead of buying them outright. Nvidia has recruited major finance firms — Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Goldman, KKR — to provide over $500 billion (potentially trillions) in outside capital so companies like SpaceX and Tesla don't have to fund AI data centers entirely from their own cash or debt. This directly addresses Tesla's ~$119 billion Terafab chip-factory buildout (a 100M+ sq ft, 10+ gigawatt facility in Texas), suggesting Wall Street financing — not just Tesla's balance sheet — will cover much of that cost. SpaceX's AI data centers reportedly pay back in under a year, making them attractive to these big capital pools, and the guest speculates Nvidia may end up taking a revenue share (not just chip sales) from Musk's ventures. The video also floats that robotaxi could become its own Wall Street-financed asset class, and that a spot/futures market for AI "compute" (like oil or commodities) may emerge as usage scales.

### [Tesla Is Hiding Robotaxis All Over America](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EM9Mm9OzGdY)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Aug 15

Tesla's robotaxi fleet is clearly bigger and more built-out than the app suggests — trackers count roughly 90 active Model Ys and 140+ total logged in Austin, plus DMV registrations around 181-186, but no Cybercabs are registered yet even though 200+ have been spotted staged across Houston, Fort Worth, Vegas, Chicago, Orlando, San Antonio and Austin. Nevada just granted Tesla an interim permit capping it at just 10 autonomous vehicles on the Las Vegas Strip corridor (of the 5,000 requested) while 42 Cybercabs already sit parked there. The video's take: Tesla isn't deliberately holding back — it's finishing real prerequisites (fire-department safety training, Starlink connectivity now standard, supercharger depot buildouts like a 124-stall SF filing) with the last big blocker being pickup/dropoff precision, which the host believes FSD v15 (adding location memory) will fix. Waymo still leads with 691 registered Texas vehicles vs Tesla's much smaller count, and current robotaxi fares (~$5-11/ride) are rounding-error revenue, but the thesis is that once v15 ships and driverless Cybercabs start carrying paying riders (watch for this in Austin this fall), the staged fleet could scale into service quickly through 2027 — though Tesla dates have slipped before, so treat the timeline as directional, not certain.
