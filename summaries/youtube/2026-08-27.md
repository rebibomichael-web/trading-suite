### [Elon's Next Move Is Medicine. Here's My Proof](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TAtMrl9TIPs)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Aug 23

Elon replied to Moderna/Merck's Wednesday news that a personalized mRNA cancer vaccine (with Keytruda, for melanoma) passed phase 3—cutting recurrence/death risk by about 49% and distant spread by about 60%—by saying artificial RNA turns curing disease into "a software problem," echoing something he first said back in 2020. The video argues this, paired with his Thursday post that "Optimus and Grok will provide incredible medical care to all the people of Earth," signals Tesla/SpaceX/xAI moving toward medicine, but not by buying a pharma company (he only pays cash for things others spent a decade building, like the $60B Cursor deal or the $44B Twitter buy — both stock/talent grabs, not tech buys). Instead he's betting on infrastructure he already controls: the $10.1B "Project Crystal Sun" solar-panel plant in Texas (filed August 11, construction targeted 2026, operations Q1 2029) and the $16.8B Terafab chip plant in Grimes County, both aimed at building the massive compute needed for individualized cancer therapies (which require a fresh sequencing/AI run per patient, unlike normal drugs). The host's prediction: expect a compute-for-pharma partnership (like Nvidia-Eli Lilly) before any Tesla-branded drug, and watch whether Elon buys a patient-data/diagnostics/imaging company as the real "tell" — no Tesla drug is being predicted here, just Elon positioning as the compute supplier to oncology.

### [SpaceX Just Had An Insane Few Days](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7JzEcU7XDYA)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Aug 24

SpaceX had a huge stretch: it hit its 100th launch of the year (24 more Starlinks), crossed 11,000 Starlink satellites (up from 9,000 just months ago), and filed with the FCC for approval of 100,000 more, plus a new 30,000-satellite proposal to beam Starlink directly to phones in India. On the infrastructure side, Louisiana Governor Jeff Landry is set to announce a multi-billion-dollar SpaceX spaceport on 130,000+ acres (SpaceX's third US launch site, aimed at polar orbits), and SpaceX/xAI struck a 51-acre land swap in Mississippi likely tied to a data center/power plant. Elon says SpaceX now launches over 80% of the world's mass to orbit and is targeting a roughly 1,000x jump to 2.25 million tons/year by 2032 (from ~2,200 tons in 2025), driven by Starship mass production. On AI, Elon's January prediction of 100x intelligence gains at fixed model size has already been hit (cited via Qwen's efficiency breakout), and he's now calling for another 100x via specialist models; token costs have halved since May per Cathie Wood/Ark data. He's skeptical Nvidia's $105B-backed SoftBank/OpenAI Ohio data center (targeting 10GW) will come online as fast as planned, while pushing SpaceX's own compute target of 5-10GW next year. Also: the "lost" Starship from flight 13 was recovered floating at sea after 24 days for heat-shield analysis (won't be reflown), and flight 14 is expected early September with a possible booster catch, full ship reuse likely late this year or early next.

### [The CyberCab Launch Everyone Said Would Never Happen](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_CZrR8Tj31c)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Aug 24

Tesla's Robotaxi program hit several milestones this weekend: the Cybercab officially launches September 3rd in Austin (23 months after its October 2024 unveiling, silencing skeptics who called it vaporware), and Nevada approved Tesla for up to 5,000 robotaxis statewide (Tesla says it'd be happy with 2,500 by next year), while Waymo got approved for 1,000 in the same state. Tesla's fleet is now at 908 robotaxis total (672 Bay Area, 154 Austin, 32 Dallas), with Houston wait times down to single digits and near-full utilization — signs the service has notably improved over the past month. Sentiment is also turning: the Wall Street Journal's Dan Neil called FSD "the best thing about the new Model Y" and said he was "bewitched" by hour two, prompting Elon to reply "feels like magic." On the competitive/cost side, Waymo claims it cut its robotaxi hardware cost from $115K to $20K per vehicle using a new custom chip, but the guest (supply-chain exec Jeff Lutz) argued Tesla's multi-year, millions-of-vehicles chip/manufacturing scale gives it a lead of at least a couple of years on robotaxi cost/volume, and up to five years before others match Tesla in personally-owned autonomous vehicles. Also of note: Tesla is hiring vehicle operators in Frankfurt (an early sign of German/European expansion), and a widely-reported "3 million vehicle recall" in China is actually a minor free over-the-air software/label fix, not a hardware recall.
