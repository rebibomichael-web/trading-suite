### [Elon Just Confirmed the Cybercab Bet Paid Off](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Msyehavhwyk)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Sep 09

Tesla's new steering-wheel-free Cybercab launched publicly in Austin last week, and while Wall Street reacted negatively (Wells Fargo called it "underwhelming," stock fell ~6% Friday), several commentators argue investors are grading it wrong by comparing it to a normal car. Elon confirmed his 2022 decision to cut the steering wheel/pedals was deliberate and paid off, and separately killed Tesla's planned cheap $25,000 consumer car in 2024 specifically to force full focus onto Cybercab and self-driving software. The bull case, illustrated with rough numbers from analyst "Cern," is that Cybercab should be valued like industrial equipment on cost-per-mile over its lifespan rather than sticker price: at a $30,000 build cost, cost-per-mile drops from 10 cents at 300,000 miles to 3 cents at 1 million miles (the durability target Tesla engineers reportedly cited to Dave Lee), meaning spending more upfront on durability can still lower total cost. Elon has cited an operating cost target around 20 cents/mile and rider price of 30-40 cents/mile, though real-world Austin rides currently show 15-25 minute wait times and only 45 Cybercabs deployed (versus 420+ total robotaxis), so the network/software side remains unproven even if the hardware economics look promising. A NHTSA audit into how Tesla self-certified a car with no steering wheel/pedals is also underway and could slow the rollout if it drags on. Bottom line for investors: watch wait times and rider-miles-per-car over the next 6-12 months as the real test of whether Cybercab's economics translate into revenue, since a future cheaper 4-6 seat Tesla built on the same platform is possible but years out and unconfirmed.
