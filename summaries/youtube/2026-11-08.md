### [SpaceX Has A Business Worth More Than SpaceX](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0LHOeEIq-yY)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Nov 04

The video relays one investor's claim that SpaceX's computing business alone is worth about as much as the whole company, which the market values at roughly $1.83 trillion. The math: about 3 GW of compute (around 1.2 million Blackwell chips) comes online by December. Keep 10% for Grok training, rent out the rest at market rates, and apply Anthropic's expected IPO multiple of about 13.6x revenue (a $1.5–2 trillion valuation on roughly $110 billion of run-rate revenue). That gives about $1.84 trillion, so Starlink (which did $5.2 billion last quarter, up 69% a year), launch, Starship and Grok would all be priced at zero. Anthropic is itself a major SpaceX customer, with up to $120 billion of compute purchases through 2029, and one customer was 34.5% of SpaceX's revenue last quarter. Reports say that customer is Anthropic.

Guest Joe Baguley calls the revenue-multiple comparison weak and argues for earnings instead. He expects a run rate of about $150 billion by December and $450 billion or more next year, with $200–250 billion in earnings. At a 40x P/E he gets $10–15 trillion, and he calls it "very likely" the stock more than triples in 12 months. These are bullish personal forecasts, not established facts, and the host stresses that none of it is a price target.

They put the recent stock weakness down to IPO lockup unlocks, merger talk, AI-bubble fears and a broad momentum sell-off. They argue SpaceX is the one AI-infrastructure player that can build data centers fast enough to survive any shakeout.

### [Every Tesla Investor Needs To Watch This](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9rlva6saGbE)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Nov 04

The video argues that Tesla's huge valuation gap (ARK Invest's $4,400 per share by 2031 versus Wall Street's average 12-month target of about $410) mostly reflects which year's earnings you price. Tesla trades at about 230x trailing adjusted earnings (about 440x on GAAP) and about 185x 2027 and 125x 2028 consensus. The host estimates that on the consensus path it's roughly 70x 2030 earnings, versus about 20x on Canaccord's more bullish path ($442 target, $11 of 2028 EPS). Analyst targets range from $25 (GLJ) to $600 (New Street). Goldman, at $360, calls valuation full, while Piper Sandler ($475), Morningstar ($475), Barclays ($450) and others are higher.

The host says roughly 60–70% of the stock price is already a bet on robotaxi and Optimus, since the current car and energy business is worth only about $100–150 a share. Optimus is treated as nearly a free option in most models. ARK has robotaxi at about 80% and Optimus at about 15% of its 2031 value. Ron Baron is more conservative, at $2,000–2,500 in 10 years.

On a possible SpaceX merger, Polymarket gives about 18% odds of an announcement by year-end. The stock has treated it more as a floor than an overhang. The exchange ratio and any premium (guesses of $420–550) are what matter. The bear case, from Gary Black, is that autonomy becomes a commodity and estimates are slipping, so Tesla stays expensive even on 2030 numbers unless it far exceeds consensus. The host's advice is to match the P/E to your holding period: about fair value on a one-year view, but a bet on far-above-consensus results over five-plus years. Key things to watch are Elon's fireside chat with Baron on Nov 6, merger committee news, 2027–28 estimate revisions, and the Dallas Cybercab rollout. The video is also sponsored by InvestingPro.
