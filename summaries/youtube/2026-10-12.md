Anthropic ($1.25B a month) and Google ($920M a month) currently rent compute from SpaceX.

For Tesla holders, the host says Grokbot matters because it is already in Tesla cars, it is the voice for Optimus, and Tesla owns a small stake through its $2B xAI investment. He calls Musk's "I've won" claim far too early. No public figure exists for Grokbot's own revenue, Grok 4.8 is overdue, and Musk's data-center dates have slipped before.

### [Every SpaceX Investor Needs To Watch This](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NLlWSRZ-QMw)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Oct 08

Brian Wang of NextBigFuture argues that SpaceX's key edge over the next two years is building AI data centers fast: about 5 months per gigawatt-scale site, against 2 to 4 years for everyone else. He says that speed lets SpaceX charge Anthropic and others roughly $50–60 billion per gigawatt per year (Deutsche Bank cites $60–61 billion on recent leases), versus about $2 billion for Amazon's deal, which he says was partly paid for with equity. He expects "Minihard" (a 220,000-chip GB300 site worth about $26 billion a year in revenue) to land in November and another site in December. That could put SpaceX at about 2.6 GW by year-end, and he thinks it could build 8 to 12 or more such sites in 2027, including Rubin-chip versions he values at about $75 billion each. His 2027 target is a revenue run rate of about $500 billion, up from about $100 billion this year, and with 50% margins and a 30x P/E he gets a stock price of $850 to $1,100 (versus about $389 now). He also says SpaceX is already ahead on power and land in Memphis and Mississippi, that chips and memory are the remaining bottlenecks, and that SpaceX could hold 40–50% of all data centers completed next year. The host admits the numbers are almost too big to believe, and everything depends on SpaceX actually delivering a new site every month or two.
