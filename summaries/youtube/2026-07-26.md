### [What Elon Musk and Ashok Elluswamy Actually Said](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RNYELfWv-GU)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Jul 25

Tesla's Q2 call had more signal beneath the headlines than most coverage captured. Ashok Elluswamy said Robotaxi has logged 380,000+ unsupervised miles across six cities with zero notable incidents, unsupervised mileage is compounding at double-digit rates week-over-week (Elon said "at least over 10%"), and expansion effort per new city is shrinking toward zero, with Tesla eventually aiming to go state-by-state rather than city-by-city. The early version of FSD v15 (10x more parameters, described as "ridiculously safe") is already running in some Robotaxi vehicles, and Cyber Cab production is being paced to match unsupervised-mile growth, hinting at a public rollout in coming months. Elon's broader message was that demand is no longer Tesla's constraint — growth now hinges on manufacturing capacity for cars, Optimus robots, and chips (hence the Terafab investment), plus new bets like digital Optimus, Megapack-powered AI data centers, and Starlink connectivity in vehicles. The analysts' take: Tesla isn't being overly cautious given regulatory/political risk (especially in Europe) and Waymo's own struggles, and slower near-term Robotaxi scaling could set up a faster, "double-exponential" ramp once v15 and validated Cyber Cabs are ready — but Optimus mass production and the semi remain multi-year-out bets, not near-term catalysts.

### [Elon Just Told The Economist His Real Endgame](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_bP0huIx4z4)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Jul 25

This is a normal video-summary task, not related to trading-suite. I'll just write the summary directly.

Elon Musk sat down for a wide-ranging Economist interview the day after a rough Tesla Q2 earnings report, and the video pulls five key moments. He praised retail investors as more insightful and long-term focused than institutional fund managers, framing his push for voting control as protection from Wall Street's 90-day mindset. He described his real goal as a "quasi infinite economy," where humanoid robots (Optimus) paired with AI (FSD, Grok) remove the labor cap on producing goods and services, eventually making work optional. On competing with China in AI, he said China has more electricity generation than the US, Europe, and India combined and is closer than people realize to solving advanced chip lithography — his proposed fix is putting solar-powered data centers in space via Starship to sidestep the grid bottleneck entirely, directly tying Tesla Energy and SpaceX into the AI race. He also broke down his taxes, saying he pays roughly 45% in income tax and will lose another ~45% to estate tax at death, leaving him with only about a quarter to a third of his wealth, framing this as proof money isn't his real motivator. Finally, in a viral exchange, he told the Economist's editor that journalists are more despised than he is (citing ~15% trust in media), reinforcing his stance of not needing public approval. For Tesla investors, the takeaway is long-term thesis alignment (robots, AI, energy) even though near-term fundamentals like margins and free cash flow remain weak.

### [Cathie Wood: "The Most Important Company In History" / Cathie Bullish (TSLA To $2600)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v2BJySePmjY)
**Matt Pocius on Tesla Stock & Money** · Jul 25

Cathie Wood told Fox Business she's bullish on both Tesla and SpaceX, calling SpaceX potentially "the most important company in history" thanks to its satellite/global communications network (75% of all satellites), orbital data centers, and reusable-rocket lead—she suggested a possible $1,000/share target for SpaceX over five years, alongside her existing $2,600 Tesla price target. The video's host, Matt Pocius, then recaps SpaceX's Starship Flight 13: it successfully deployed real V3 Starlink satellites for the first time, restarted a Raptor engine in space, and showed a much-improved heat shield, but the Superheavy booster failed its ocean splashdown (5 of the engines meant to slow it didn't relight, similar to a prior May failure). He calls it a technical win but only "neutral to slightly bearish" for the stock near-term, and says he's happy to keep dollar-cost-averaging into SpaceX shares (down ~33% from its peak, trading around $123) while it's cheap. He also flags rising prediction-market odds (74%) that Tesla and SpaceX merge within a year, and personally predicts a combined entity could eventually be worth $20 trillion.

**Mr. FIRED Up Wealth** · Jul 25
