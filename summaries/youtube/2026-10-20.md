### [Tesla Roadster Event Reaction](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TWC0CYGZZKI)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Oct 16

Tesla and SpaceX unveiled the new Roadster in a live demo, and the Brighter with Herbert panel called it a huge win. The car flipped, flew on cold-gas nitrogen thrusters (10,000 PSI carbon-wrapped tank, emptied in about 3 seconds) and landed cleanly. There are three tiers: a ducted-fan "performance" version with a trunk (0-60 in about 1.6s), a thruster version (0-60 in about 0.85s, roughly 1,000 units, about $2M hypercar territory), and a limited invite-only rocket version (about 0.55s to 60, near 5G average). The panel says the car is light (1,600-1,700 kg target). They also say its motor is 50% more powerful than the Plaid unit at 30% less weight, which could carry over to the Cybertruck, Semi and other models. Their main investment takeaway is that it works as a "halo" marketing coup and engineering showcase, and strengthens the case for a Tesla-SpaceX merger. They don't see it as a volume product, and they doubt Tesla will do flying cars. They expect drones to be more likely, and they note that Optimus got little airtime at the event. No price targets were given. The only other figure mentioned was Tesla's roughly $40B cash pile, which they said is going into big capital projects such as Giga Texas and the Optimus factory.

### [SpaceX’s $180 Billion Patch of Texas Dirt](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DN65KcPJjpk)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Oct 17

SpaceX has confirmed a 444-acre site outside Brownsville, Texas for a third data-center cluster, which guest CERN Basher calls "Colossus 3". The land was removed from city limits in August, so Brownsville's planned data-center moratorium doesn't apply. Aerial photos show one "Mini Hard"-style building going up and a second foundation beside it. About 400 MW of gas turbines (reportedly rising past 500 MW) are already on site, with a gas pipeline being laid in. Basher thinks the first building will be finished around March 2027.

Each building is estimated to hold about 220,000 Nvidia chips and earn roughly $22B a year on current GB300 pricing, or about $30B with newer Vera Rubin chips. Six buildings, which he considers a conservative fit, would bring in $132B–$180B a year. He compares that with Tesla's roughly $100B in annual revenue. Basher says the site could eventually hold 10 to 20+ buildings if SpaceX uses its adjoining land.

Basher's main point is that SpaceX has turned data centers into a repeatable product: simple shells built in about 5–6 months. He says this makes it the fastest, cheapest builder of AI compute. He estimates SpaceX's AI compute could add about 1.4% to US GDP growth next year (7.5–10 GW) and 12% by 2031, based on ARK Invest's projections. The tie to Tesla is stated only briefly: Tesla owns a small piece of SpaceX, and a Tesla-SpaceX merger would make these data centers relevant to Tesla shareholders. The $180B figure is theoretical and assumes every chip is rented at today's premium prices.

**Brighter with Herbert** · Oct 17
