### [The Biggest Threat To Tesla Robotaxi](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iQDZvz0smz0)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Jul 28

Tesla's robotaxi push is being challenged more by politics than tech: Uber reportedly lobbied in New Jersey for a rule forcing driverless services to keep human drivers in 85% of rides for three years, aimed at blunting Waymo, Zoox, and Tesla, and Uber's CFO defended it as necessary to avoid a total AV ban — sparking backlash from Tesla-friendly investors. Waymo, meanwhile, is reportedly planning to split from Uber in Austin and Atlanta once their contract expires in January 2028, launching independently as the two increasingly compete rather than partner, with both sides blaming each other for cleanliness, routing, and economics problems. Separately, Waymo has racked up over $9,325 in Austin parking tickets since 2024 with just 300 cars, a sign of scaling pains even as its safety record stays strong. The guest, supply-chain veteran Jeff Lutz, argues the real fix for Tesla is staying focused on flawless, error-free rollout and securing a federal AV standard (via Elon Musk's White House ties) rather than a patchwork of state rules, and predicts Uber's aggregator role will get squeezed out as robotaxi platforms scale and vertically integrate. The video's third topic shifts to legacy automakers (BMW, Mercedes, Volvo, etc.) increasingly badging and exporting China-designed, China-built vehicles to compete globally, which Lutz calls a red flag that offloads engineering jobs to China and could eventually extend to self-driving tech licensing, though he thinks Chinese FSD-style systems are still far from export-ready.
