### [Tesla's Next Product Is a Subscription, Not a Car](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pz9RWQJXk8w)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Sep 13

Tesla's next big move might not be selling more cars—it could be a monthly subscription (like Amazon Prime) that bundles ride credit, garage access to a Tesla when you need one (a Model Y for a road trip, a Cybertruck for hauling, a Cybercab for commuting), plus insurance, FSD, connectivity, and charging all in one bill. The evidence: FSD went subscription-only in February at $99/month and now has 1.48 million subscribers (~$790M/year); Tesla Insurance is live in 13 states; and Tesla already has the "swap" logistics problem solved via self-driving cars repositioning themselves, which is exactly what killed every past car-subscription attempt (Cadillac's Book, Volvo's Care, BMW's Access, and the startup Autonomy all failed because swapping cars required human drivers and ate margins). For reference, AAA pegs true all-in car ownership at about $965/month, so any Tesla bundle priced under $1,000/month would directly compete with owning a car. Nothing here is officially announced—Tesla's current Cybercab signup form is only for commercial/fleet buyers, Elon has only described an "Airbnb-style" model where owners rent out their own car, and Austin's robotaxi rollout is still small (45 cars) and had a regulatory audit opened day one. The video's bet: watch for language changes in Tesla's fleet FAQ, a membership tab in the robotaxi app, and whether Tesla starts reporting "recurring mobility revenue" as its own line—if that happens, it argues Tesla should be valued more like a software/subscription company than an automaker.
