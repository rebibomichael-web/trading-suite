### [Every Tesla or SpaceX Investor Needs To Watch This](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IrbWZt7D7MA)

He's skeptical of near-term hype around SpaceX hitting $100B run-rate or $1 trillion revenue by 2029 (versus Anthropic already near $100B/month and projected near $1T next year), and expects Optimus, robotaxi scale, and the Tesla-SpaceX chip/AI fab buildout to take years, not months — his advice to investors is basically to expect a long "no-man's-land" period and not anticipate a quick 30% pop from an announcement.

### [SpaceX: From $19 Billion to $250 Billion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PBfoQi09ouc)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Aug 09

SpaceX just held its first earnings call, and the numbers point to explosive growth ahead: management guided to a $100 billion annualized revenue run rate by December (on contracts already signed), up from just $19 billion for all of 2025, with independent models like Brian Wang's projecting $130-250 billion for 2027 if AI compute pricing holds. The biggest driver is AI compute leasing to companies like Google and Anthropic, which signed $14.1 billion in cloud deals last quarter alone and swung the AI segment from a $600 million loss to $1.1 billion positive EBITDA in one quarter, backed by Nvidia exclusivity and plans to scale from 1.4 to ~10 gigawatts of compute by end of 2027. Starlink is also compounding fast — 12 million subscribers (up 2x year-over-year), $66/month pricing holding, and a new V3 satellite launching this month that's ~10x more capable with 10x more units planned, potentially delivering 100x more bandwidth; enterprise/government revenue grew 108% with zero customer churn. A new "Starlink mobile" phone service is coming too, using 65 MHz of newly-approved EchoStar spectrum (13x more than today) to potentially challenge AT&T, Verizon, and T-Mobile's $600 billion market without building traditional cell towers. Starship's heat shield problem appears solved after a clean Flight 13 splashdown, which is critical since V3 satellites and the mobile constellation both depend on Starship launches. The key risk flagged: all upside above the $100 billion floor depends on AI compute pricing (~$35/watt/year) holding rather than decaying as supply catches up with demand.
