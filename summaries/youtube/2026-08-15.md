### [Tesla’s Biggest Auto Milestones Just Hit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5oFNH80sQOM)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Aug 14

Tesla dominates the Herbert-Brian White auto rundown: an S&P Global study puts Tesla's customer loyalty at 64.3%, the highest of any brand and ahead of Ferrari, driven largely by FSD (one reporter traded a six-figure Lincoln Navigator for a Model Y specifically for it). Battery-life fears look overblown—Tesla batteries are engineered to retain 80% capacity after 200,000 miles, versus a $13,000 engine/transmission repair on a comparable gas car—and an MIT study found EVs cut lifecycle emissions 40-60% even on the dirtiest grids. The Model Y outsold Mercedes and BMW combined in Korea in July and remains Switzerland's top-selling EV. The Tesla Semi is gaining fleet traction (freight carrier Bali Express called a 3-week trial a "strategic commitment"), with the Nevada factory now producing weekly, though 4680 battery cell supply may be capping output before a targeted ramp toward 50,000 units/year. Cybertruck has passed 100,000 VINs, but production has hit parts delays tied to a legal dispute with supplier Angstrom Automotive (which Tesla won in court, though the tooling equipment hasn't yet been recovered), so the earlier $59,000 base trim isn't expected to return at that low a price.

### [Tesla Just Won](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5-FPxInLE6g)
**Matt Pocius on Tesla Stock & Money** · Aug 14

Tesla and SpaceX had a big news day: Tesla finally won its three-year labor dispute with Sweden's IF Metall union, which ended all strikes as of August 19 — a major relief since escalation risked spreading unionization to Germany and other European countries where Tesla operates. Tesla also got a Nevada robotaxi network permit and is requesting approval for up to 5,000 robotaxis in its first year, with Vegas seen as a huge advertising opportunity given tourist traffic. SpaceX/Tesla broke ground on "Terra," a massive AI chip factory project requiring $16.8B in initial capital, and SpaceX officially closed its acquisition of Cursor AI to bolster its Grok AI efforts (Grok 4.6 jumped to 4th on leaderboards). SpaceX's CFO said the company is on pace for $100B in annual recurring revenue by year-end, driven by AI compute deals with under one-year payback periods, and Morgan Stanley's bull case puts SpaceX at $600/share within 12 months. Speculation is growing (via analyst Gary Black) that SpaceX could make an all-equity offer to acquire Tesla this year, and the host reiterates his long-term view that Tesla and SpaceX will eventually merge into a combined entity worth $20 trillion. Michael Burry has also closed his short position on Tesla, and both stocks have been climbing off late-July lows (Tesla near $300, SpaceX pre-IPO shares around $140).
