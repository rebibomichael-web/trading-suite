### [What The Election Means For Tesla And SpaceX](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xqN2PlbptsE)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Nov 06

Tesla and SpaceX both sold off after the midterms, with SpaceX at about $123 (below its $135 IPO price) and Tesla at about $343 (below $350). The fear was that a Democratic Texas governor and Congress could slow AI data centers, robotaxis and the SpaceX–Tesla merger. The three hosts called that overblown: Texas permitting boards (TCEQ, PUCT, DMV) have staggered six-year terms and a Republican Senate confirms their members. The legislature meets only about four months every two years, and the new governor says already-permitted data centers can finish. SpaceX is also bringing $3.6B to its 6 GW Arizona project to cut local power rates 10–40%, and its plan to grow from 3 GW to 13 GW of compute was seen as largely intact.

On robotaxis, they expect no new headwinds, since Texas already has statewide preemption for autonomous vehicles (passed in 2017) and Waymo is expanding unimpeded. On the merger, Alexandra argued that pricing is anchored to October 20, when the special committees were announced, so the post-election drop matters little. She said Tesla's shareholder vote needs about 7 of 10 voting holders just to reach 50% of outstanding shares, so a generous premium and ideally a floor on SpaceX's price are needed. The main risks they flagged are noisy subpoenas and headlines, Elon's political fight with Democrats, and possible delays, but none of that was treated as a thesis-breaker.
