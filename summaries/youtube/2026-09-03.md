### [NEW INTERVIEW: Elon Musk On The $30 Trillion Prize](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_Dz64hXIkkA)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Sep 02

Elon Musk told the G20 that AI could add $20–30 trillion a year to the global economy, and predicted that by the end of 2027 AI coding will be "Stockfish level" — so good that no human can compete with it in writing software. He also warned of a looming power crunch: AI chip capacity is growing 40–50% a year while power outside China grows only 10–20%, creating an estimated 15-gigawatt shortfall by 2027; this is why Google and Anthropic are now leasing compute from SpaceX, which built its own power plants (and is even making its own gas-turbine blades) to get ahead of the shortage. Meanwhile the OpenAI-Musk feud escalated: OpenAI is cutting off Cursor's access to its models on November 12 after SpaceX bought Cursor, but Cursor's CEO says OpenAI is only ~5% of its traffic and Anthropic is stepping in to add more compute for Cursor instead. Economist guest Joe Bacti argued we're in the middle of an exponential "singularity" curve toward near-human-replacing AI by 2027-28, raising the specter of mass white-collar job loss with no real transition plan yet (universal high income is discussed as a hoped-for fix, not an actual plan). Bottom line for investors: this is being framed as a bullish power/compute story for Tesla and SpaceX specifically, since SpaceX's ability to build its own energy infrastructure is positioned as a major competitive edge in the AI buildout.

### [Tesla’s Biggest FSD Moment Just Came From Washington](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C-QIkogKpZ8)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Sep 02

This week's most important Tesla news actually came from Washington: Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the biggest obstacle to autonomous vehicles is the state-by-state patchwork of rules and is pushing Congress for one federal standard, even questioning whether autonomous cars need steering wheels or pedals — a big deal for Tesla's robotaxi/Cybercab push. Meanwhile Tesla open-sourced European FSD safety data ahead of an October 6th EU-wide approval vote: across 100 million km in 5 approved countries (Netherlands, Denmark, Belgium, Estonia, Lithuania), FSD Supervised showed 4x fewer major crashes, up to 84% fewer emergency-braking events, 99% fewer minor collisions, and it drives at or below surrounding traffic speed 98% of the time. Sweden's infrastructure minister rode in an FSD car with hands off the wheel and now wants his town to become a robotaxi pilot city. FSD has now crossed 14 billion total miles — the last billion took just 28 days, versus 3.5 years for the first billion. Separately, Waymo opened paid robotaxi rides to the public in Denver, San Diego, and Tampa (14 cities, ~4,000 cars total), and Zoox is expanding to Houston and San Diego — but Tesla is still behind both on actual paid, unsupervised robotaxi miles.

### [Why the Wall of Worry Points Higher in September](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fOMlh1gq33g)
**Fundstrat** · Sep 02

Tom Lee is bullish and contrarian on September, arguing that widespread caution—falling stocks, rising oil, higher yields—actually sets up a rally rather than a warning sign, since real tops happen when investors are bullish, not fearful. He expects inflation fears to fade with this week's jobs report, next week's August CPI, and the September FOMC meeting, potentially dropping the odds of a rate hike to zero; he specifically flags that "flash memory" costs are distorting core PCE, masking otherwise tame inflation, and a CPI print near 2.4% would ease market anxiety. Margin debt shrank in July and stayed low in August, meaning positioning isn't excessive despite the S&P near 6,800-6,900, and he sees the recent crypto rebound (which historically leads stocks by about a month) as a bullish signal for equities too. He does expect an index-level correction in AI/Mag7 names due to churning between semis, memory, and downstream plays, but says that pullback needs to coincide with actual bullish sentiment first, which hasn't happened yet. Other panelists (Young Yumat, Malcolm Ethridge) push back partially, citing sticky inflation, oil near $100, and stretched valuations (Nvidia's multiple still capped in the 20s despite strong guidance to 2028), but Ethridge agrees the recent caution is likely temporary given AI fundamentals remain intact. Separately, the segment flags Dell earnings after the bell as a key catalyst, with options pricing an 8-10% move, and notes software/cybersecurity stocks (Palo Alto reporting tonight) just had their best month since May despite some profit-taking ahead of the print.
