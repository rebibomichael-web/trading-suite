### [Tesla's AI Infrastructure Is Exploding](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B6PVM0BUPQI)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Jul 26

Tesla is now running an enormous, simultaneous buildout — Optimus lines, Cybertruck, Robotaxi fleet, a chip fab (Terafab), the Semi factory, Megapack 3, lithium/cathode refineries, battery cells, solar manufacturing, and AI data centers — funded by roughly $25 billion in CapEx this year plus a newly secured $30 billion credit line. Guest Larry Goldberg argues Tesla is far more capital-efficient than peers like Amazon or Microsoft (spending ~$200B each) and expects Tesla's CapEx to actually exceed guidance this year and roughly double, with long-term debt eventually settling around $50 billion (possibly folded into a rumored Tesla/SpaceX merger). He says robotaxi revenue has already started (visible in the "services" line) and will scale from hundreds of thousands of dollars now to hundreds of millions next year, funded largely by cash flow (~$3-4B/quarter now, rising to $5-6B) rather than heavy new borrowing. Cortex 2 data center capacity is doubling by year-end and is currently being used for FSD v15 (a 10-15x parameter jump), not yet Optimus training. Tesla also locked up priority chip supply from Micron and Nvidia despite tight memory pricing, and Elon's stated philosophy is "speed over maximum capital efficiency" — moving fast lowers total cost even if per-unit spending is higher. Bottom line from the guest: Tesla isn't underspending, it's spending just-in-time, and impatient investors selling now (like in 2017-2018) will likely regret it once robotaxi and AI revenue scale.

### [Go Big Or Go Home: Tesla’s Strategy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RqsSFONG8Fk)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Jul 26

Tesla's Q2 call revealed why its robotaxi fleet is still tiny (just dozens of cars across seven metros in Texas, California, and Florida): four executives explained that Tesla is deliberately solving regulatory, operational, and AI-generalization problems at small scale before scaling up, rather than flooding cities like Waymo does. The safety record is the key asset—Tesla claims 380,000 fully unsupervised miles with "zero notable incidents," versus Waymo's 220 million miles but roughly 1,400 NHTSA incident reports and two fatal crashes (neither Waymo's fault). The payoff for waiting: Tesla says it aims to eventually get approval to launch robotaxis state-by-state (not city-by-city) once regulators are convinced, with Texas and Florida the likely first candidates given mile accumulation there. FSD v15 is now live in the fleet with camera-only autonomy reaffirmed as validated. Optimus is following the same "go big" playbook — training generalized robots via an "Optimus Academy" data flywheel rather than shipping simpler pre-programmed robots like competitors are doing; the video's author theorizes the V3 humanoid (production starting now at Fremont) is a training generation, with the real wide-scale version being V4 paired with the AI5 chip (targeted for next year). Bear case: Tesla has missed its own robotaxi guidance three quarters running, spending is at record highs while profits compress, Waymo already has a large mileage/revenue lead, and any single bad autonomous-vehicle incident industry-wide could freeze regulatory progress for a year.
