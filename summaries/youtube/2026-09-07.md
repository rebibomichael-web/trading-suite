### [We Hailed a Tesla Cybercab… It Actually Came!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Jz2b_4YM0M)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Sep 05

Herbert and guest Sern Basher took Tesla's Cybercab for its first public robotaxi ride in Austin on launch day, with the service opening to the general public that evening at 5pm Central. There's no steering wheel or pedals, top speed observed was 30 mph, and an engineer told them Tesla aims to build Cybercabs at a rate of 10,000 per week, versus Waymo's roughly 400-car fleet in Austin (about 800-850 Waymos total in Texas) and a claimed Waymo production cap of 10,000 per year. The car has reclining bench seats, adjustable ambient lighting, in-cabin games/movies/music via a touchscreen, Grok voice assistant, Starlink hardware (though passenger Wi-Fi access isn't available yet), and automated cabin cleaning "coming soon." No pricing or Robotaxi business-model details were shared at the launch event, and Elon Musk did not attend. The speakers frame this as a bigger story than competing with Uber/Lyft/Waymo — the goal is replacing car ownership outright, which one guest estimated would need a fleet of 30-50 million vehicles nationally.

### [Tesla’s Cybercab Math Is Getting INSANE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YhxLUwzFAY8)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Sep 06

Tesla's Cybercab officially launched to the public in Austin as of Friday evening, and the data shared at the event shows real self-driving miles growing 17% week-over-week, hitting 1 million cumulative unsupervised miles — a pace that, if sustained, would put the fleet around 2,000-3,000 vehicles and roughly 14 million cumulative miles by end of 2026 (constrained mainly by a ~125,000/year Cybercab production ceiling). Tesla also opened a form gauging interest in fleet purchases of Cybercabs, though a broad consumer sale (needed for Elon's bet with Marques Brownlee) still seems unlikely this year. Key engineering points: no rare-earth-metal drive motor, brake-by-wire with no hydraulic lines, and a design philosophy prioritizing lowest total lifetime cost (targeting 500,000+ mile durability) rather than lowest sticker price, plus upcoming Starlink V5 connectivity and always-on active collision avoidance rolling out across Tesla's fleet. Speakers also pushed back on reports suggesting Tesla needs a special NHTSA exemption to sell Cybercab without a steering wheel — they say it self-certifies under existing standards — and argued each Cybercab's efficiency/range roughly equals three Waymo vehicles in service capacity.
