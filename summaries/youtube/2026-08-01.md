### [What Finally Reignites Tesla Stock?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LqAuJ_pg0n8)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Jul 31

Tesla stock cratered from around $420 in early July to about $308-$316 now, and the panel says it's tangled up with a brutal market-wide momentum-stock selloff (the worst monthly momentum crash in 30 years, worse than dot-com or the 2008 crash) plus Tesla's own soft earnings call, where management gave no timeline pressure on scaling robotaxi and signaled spending will exceed free cash flow without clearly explaining the ROI payoff. Bulls argue the drop is mostly mechanical/sentiment-driven rather than fundamental — institutions have actually been buying (institutional ownership just hit an all-time high near 2 billion of 3.95 billion shares, about 70%+, while retail's stake fell to roughly 20%), and they see steady on-the-ground progress: driverless Cybercabs now spotted on public Austin roads (60-68+ sightings, increasingly unsupervised), which they read as a slow-but-real robotaxi rollout rather than a stalled one. A big wildcard is the rumored SpaceX-Tesla merger: SpaceX's AI/compute business (Starlink data centers, "Colossus" sites) could reportedly generate around $100 billion in revenue this year, which would be huge for a combined entity, but the panel flags major uncertainty around SpaceX's August 6 share unlock (up to ~30% of float), a Cybertruck parts-supplier legal dispute threatening near-term production, and unresolved China/Europe regulatory risk for robotaxi. Bottom line from the bulls: they expect Tesla to track a broader AI-sector rebound and see robotaxi scaling toward roughly 1 million+ vehicles by 2030, but caution the stock stays volatile until the SpaceX unlock, Colossus contract announcements, and a merger structure become clearer.

### [AI Just Got Profitable. Tesla Might Be the Winner](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i8hPENPr5tM)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Jul 31

Tesla and SpaceX may team up to build a satellite/mesh phone network using Tesla's existing superchargers, power walls, and cyber cabs as network nodes, potentially costing around $20 billion versus what carriers spend just maintaining current infrastructure — futurist Brian Wang says this could roll out over the next 2-4 years, hitting real competitiveness by 2028-2029, eventually offering an AI-focused device (not quite a "phone") monetized through X subscriptions, Grok, and X Money rather than carrier fees. The bigger near-term story is that SpaceX's pending acquisition of Cursor (the AI coding tool) is already profitable at roughly $4 billion ARR with about 2.1 million developer seats, unlike OpenAI (losing $10-20B/year on $40B revenue) or Anthropic — Cursor's cost of building software is collapsing, with a demo SQLite-class database dropping from $20,000 to about $1,300 and heading toward hundreds of dollars as AI "agent swarms" scale from 1,000 code commits/hour toward 1,000/second. Wang also notes SpaceX's Starship recently survived reentry with a damaged heat shield and was recovered from the ocean, suggesting even imperfect heat shields still allow reuse, which could push launch costs toward $50-100/kg. Overall framing: Tesla's existing hardware becomes a new revenue layer, and Cursor's profitability shows AI monetization is already working, not just a future promise.
