### [How to Actually Value Tesla Before Robotaxi Pays Off](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XuPrR1SgQQE)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Oct 24

Sern Basher (Brilliant Advice) argues that Tesla shouldn't be judged on forward P/E, because that only captures next year's earnings, while most of the value comes from what a company grows into over the next 25-30 years. In his generic examples, discounted at 12%, a flat $1 a year for 30 years is worth $8.06 and forever is worth $8.33. Growing that dollar 5% a year forever is worth about $14.29, and growing it 10% is worth about $50. He treats new bets like Robotaxi and Optimus as call options: a business paying $1 in year 5 and growing 50% for 20 years is worth about $625, so a "crazy" P/E is just the math of future growth. A one-year delay cuts that value about 10-11%, but if the delay lets the company grow faster (60% instead of 50%), the value rises to about $1,480-1,530, and a 50% success probability cuts that to roughly $790. His practical method is to estimate unit economics (profit per Robotaxi or Optimus times the number deployed), discount it back, apply probabilities, and then work backwards from today's price to see what odds the market implies. His example is that a price implying only a 5% chance of Optimus hitting its numbers is an opportunity if you think the odds are higher. He says even Terafab should get a small non-zero value rather than zero, and that stocks can stall for years (Microsoft took about 14 years to make a new high), so don't over-concentrate. None of this is a Tesla price target. The numbers are illustrative examples, not a Tesla valuation.
