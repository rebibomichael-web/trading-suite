### [Tesla Just PROVED Everyone Wrong!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6vj1-lKdGL0)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Sep 26

Tesla kicked off high-volume production of the Tesla Semi at a new factory built to make 50,000 trucks a year, and the numbers beat expectations: 500 miles of range fully loaded (600 miles at half load, 325 for the standard-range variant), independent fleet tests showing 465 miles between charges, and 60% charge recovery in 30 minutes on Tesla's Megachargers. Pricing is estimated around $260K (standard range) and $290K (long range), undercutting rival EV trucks like the Peterbilt 579EV ($428K, 200-mile range), Volvo VNR ($436K, 275 miles), Freightliner eCascadia ($460K, 220 miles), and Kenworth T680E ($514K, 150 miles) on both cost and range. Fuel savings versus diesel run roughly $50,000-$95,000 per truck per year depending on diesel/electricity prices, meaning a 10,000-truck fleet could save $500M-plus annually and the truck can pay for itself within 1-2 years. Analyst Cernig Basher estimates the semi business could be worth ~$14 billion a year at full 50,000-unit production before counting future autonomy software revenue, which he thinks could ultimately be worth even more than the truck hardware itself. The video also notes a teased Tesla Roadster reveal set for October 1st.

### [Is AI driving inflation?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6FPt1i_dwt8)
**Fundstrat** · Sep 26

AI spending is fueling real inflation pressure in two spots—memory chips and energy—because demand for AI infrastructure is straining supply in both. The speaker argues the Fed hiking rates won't slow AI capex at all, since that spending isn't rate-sensitive the way consumer borrowing is. He also notes flash memory costs don't really flow into the consumer CPI basket, so this inflation is more of a corporate/infrastructure story than a consumer one. Today's market selloff was tied to strong PMI data, which he frames as a reminder that the market's current psychology is fixated on "good news is bad news" (strong data = fear of higher rates).
