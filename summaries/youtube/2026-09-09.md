### [Cybercab’s First Week Changes the Story](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=byKcYJjVbko)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Sep 07

Tesla's Cyber Cab robotaxi had a strong launch week in Austin: the fleet grew from roughly 270 to 432 registered autonomous vehicles (45 of them Cyber Cabs), with confirmed 17% week-over-week growth—well above Wall Street's estimates and putting ~2,000 robotaxis by year-end and 50,000 by 2027 within reach. NHTSA opened a probe into the Cyber Cab's certification right after launch, but the hosts argue (citing analyst Sern Basher) this is likely a routine, even favorable, step toward formalizing new rules, similar to what happened with Amazon's Zoox years earlier—and Musk/Ashok Elluswamy haven't shown concern, still promising customer deliveries and a sub-$30K price. Public reaction has been strongly positive: Texas DOT's director praised the rollout, Google search interest hit 100/100 in both the US and China, and MKBHD reaffirmed his bet to shave his head if Tesla delivers a $30K Cyber Cab before 2027. The guest (manufacturing exec Jeff Lutz) argues Tesla engineered the car for long-term lifetime economics (e.g., through-colored reaction-injection-molded panels, unboxed modular assembly) rather than just a low sticker price, aiming for an operating cost near 20-25 cents/mile versus ~$2.80-3.16/mile for Uber/Lyft/Waymo today—a cost reduction the hosts believe will expand the total transportation market, not just take rideshare share. Overall framing: Tesla's robotaxi strategy (skipping a cheap compact car in favor of Cyber Cab) is compared to Steve Jobs' iPhone leap—a multi-year technical and business-model lead that rivals like Waymo and Zoox will struggle to copy quickly.
