### [Is Physical AI Tesla's Real Moat? Jeff Lutz Says Yes #Tesla](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4epcCHWC_vY)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Sep 30

Jeff Lutz argues that Tesla's real moat is "physical AI," which is much harder to copy than chatbot AI. He says AI token usage has already moved from 80/20 closed models to 80/20 open models, and he expects the frontier models to follow, so everyone will end up with their own set of agents. In his view, people will pick the AI tied to the physical products they use every day. That makes Tesla sticky: a Grok chatbot in the digital world, Grok in the car, and Grok in a humanoid robot (Optimus). He says no other company has both chatbot AI and physical AI, or is delivering physical AI with the same clarity and breadth. The clip gives no price targets or numbers, and the call is bullish on Tesla (TSLA).

### [SpaceX Just Did What Experts Called Impossible](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2s2ZnurhLao)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Sep 30

Brighter with Herbert and guest Joe Bacti (an economist and investor) argue that SpaceX's Starship flight 14, its first orbital flight, which deployed 26 V3 Starlink satellites and re-entered cleanly, proves the system works. They say this puts SpaceX on a path to a potential $100 trillion market cap and sets up space-based AI compute (Star Mind satellites) starting in 2028. Bacti models a gigawatt of orbital compute at $68B all-in, falling to $43B by 2035, with $30-35B of that being Nvidia GPUs. Wall Street puts it far higher: BofA about $165B, Wood Mackenzie $170B, and a range of $160-180B. He says that's roughly 10-15% above the cost of a terrestrial build, with no permits or maintenance. He sees Starlink as a business capped near $1T and compute as the far bigger prize, while the video's intro cites Mach 33's estimate that 151 Starship launches could carry about as much traffic by 2028 as all internet demand in 2025.

On the bank side, TD Cowen started coverage with a Buy and a $200 price target. It calls AI compute leasing SpaceX's fastest-growing revenue stream (customers include Google and Anthropic) and expects it to be the majority of revenue by 2027. Bacti notes SpaceX made about $28B on under half a gigawatt, the CFO says they're on track for a $100B run rate, and he thinks the stock, at roughly a $2T valuation, is bullish but not cheap. The tone is very promotional, with big claims such as a launch every hour and "limitless" demand, so treat the forecasts as one side's opinion.
