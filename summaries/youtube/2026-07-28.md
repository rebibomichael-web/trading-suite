### [Elon Just Connected Tesla And SpaceX](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tt18gRSPTLc)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Jul 27

Elon was asked directly on Tesla's earnings call about combining Tesla and SpaceX, and instead of shutting it down he leaned into all the overlaps between the two companies: Grok running in cars and orchestrating robo-taxis, Starlink built into the Cybercab (and eventually other Teslas) so robo-taxis never lose connectivity and can stream 4K sports in the back seat, Optimus/"digital Optimus" using Grok as its brain, SpaceX buying Megapacks for power, and the big one — a shared chip fab ("Terafab") building custom AI silicon (AI5, AI6, and SpaceX's D3 chip) instead of paying Nvidia's roughly 400% markup. Guest Larry Goldberg argues this isn't a financial deal but a product-design argument: one company could design the perfect product across cars, robots, and rockets in a way two separate companies can't. He predicts a merger announcement (likely just an exploratory committee) sometime between Tesla's Q3 earnings call and year-end 2026, with actual completion by mid-2027, and doesn't think anything will be announced at SpaceX's first-ever earnings call on August 4, 2026. No specific price targets were given in this segment.

### [Tom Lee: Crypto Is Having Its 1934 Moment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PN6l0QTFKuw)
**Fundstrat** · Jul 27

Tom Lee (Fundstrat) says he sees low odds the Fed hikes rates this year, arguing underlying inflation is cooling as shelter costs weaken, and any remaining pressure (tariffs, oil) is more likely "temporary" than a reason for a hiking cycle—he suspects the Fed could lean on quantitative tightening (balance sheet shrinkage) instead of rate hikes. On crypto, he flags something unusual: crypto (Bitcoin, Ethereum) has been rallying even as hawkish Fed odds and high oil prices would normally pressure it down, and the ETH/BTC ratio is rising despite the Clarity Act's odds of passage this year dropping to around 30%. He calls this a "1934 moment" for crypto—comparing it to the creation of the SEC to unify fragmented state securities rules—arguing that as money becomes software, a single federal regulator (the CFTC) is needed, and that major asset managers (Schwab, Franklin Templeton, Fidelity Digital) support this. He notes the US risks falling behind as Japan, Russia, and Europe pass similar crypto clarity legislation, which he thinks is actually a tailwind for crypto right now.
