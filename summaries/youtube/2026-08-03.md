### [Tesla Is Running Amazon's Secret Playbook](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OZtIaRfyySw)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Aug 02

Tesla's revenue mix is starting to look like Amazon's did in the 2000s: heavy upfront spending that hurts earnings now but builds hidden, recurring businesses that eventually get re-rated by the market. Tesla stock is flat around $389, roughly where it traded in late 2020, and Q2 revenue was $28.2B (up 26%) but EPS missed (33 cents vs. 49 expected) because capex jumped 142% to $5.79B, largely funding Optimus and autonomy. The bull case: just as Amazon's AWS was buried in the numbers until 2015 (then re-rated the whole stock), Tesla's FSD subscriptions (1.5M, up 56%), Services ($4.58B, up 50%), Energy (13.5 GWh, up 40%+), and Robotaxi (live in Austin, Dallas, Houston, three Florida cities) aren't broken out yet but are compounding underneath the "car company" label — watch for a robotaxi line item, services+energy margins rivaling automotive, or Optimus shipping externally as the re-rating trigger. Tesla's competitive edge isn't robotaxi tech itself (which will commoditize, like Waymo/Chinese rivals), but the "Amazon-style" moat around it: personalization, spotless cars via automated service-center washes, wireless charging on Cybertruck, and the already-built coast-to-coast Supercharger network keeping cost-per-mile low. Bear case flagged: Michael Burry is short Tesla/Nvidia/semis betting on a 30-40% crash by March 2027, arguing AI spenders are "barely up" while chip sellers ripped 200%, and Tesla trades at ~156x forward earnings, so skeptics say much of any re-rate may already be priced in. The video's suggested homework: track the ratio of (services + energy + robotaxi gross profit) to automotive gross profit each quarter — when that trends toward 1, the "car company" narrative breaks.
