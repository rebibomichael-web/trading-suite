The video gives no price targets or stock calls. The tone is bullish on Tesla.

### [800 Million SpaceX Shares Unlock. Sell Or Buy?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c8NFqVRaSJU)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Nov 24

SpaceX has about 800 million shares (roughly $100 billion, or 6% of the company) unlocking on December 8th. They belong to pre-IPO employees and early investors, and it's the last of nine unlocks. Nearly 4 billion shares have already become sellable since August, so the host argues the unlock is the smaller part of the sell-or-buy question. December would grow the tradable float by only about 18%, versus more than 100% in August and about 45% on October 22nd. History is mixed: the stock rose 6% on the August unlock day, but fell 9% on October 22nd. Elon's own shares stay locked until about June 12th of next year.

The bigger question is price. At about $128 (down almost 8% on Friday and just under the $135 IPO price), SpaceX is worth about $1.7 trillion. That's roughly 17 times the $100 billion December run-rate target and about 12 times the Q1 pace. The host's view is that the market has already priced in the guidance, and the debate is now about what comes after it.

The bull case rests on these points:
- Q3 revenue was $14.6 billion, up 161% year over year.
- Backlog rose from $47.5 billion to $63 billion.
- Q1 2027 revenue guidance is $33–37 billion.
- The average analyst target is about $260.
- Catching a Starship upper stage was a milestone.

The bear case rests on these points:
- The company lost about $3.4 billion over the past 12 months and burned close to $40 billion of free cash flow.
- It just sold $32 billion of chip-backed bonds, with the largest tranche yielding 8.259%.
- About $1.6 billion of Q3's $4.4 billion profit was non-operating.
- Anthropic may account for more than a third of the $100 billion run rate, and it can cancel on 90 days' notice after its first commitment periods end in January and March.

The host gives no buy or sell call. December 8th is also the first day of the Fed meeting, where a rate hike is priced as near-certain, so any move that day may not be about the unlock. The Q4 report will show whether the $100 billion run rate was actually hit.
