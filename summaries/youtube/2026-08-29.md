### [Elon's Forgotten Product Just Got a Launch Date](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MBHZx0Xxj_w)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Aug 27

Tesla just set September 24th as a "rollout" celebration for its Sparks, Nevada Semi factory, which has actually been building trucks since April and can eventually make 50,000 units a year (about $13B in revenue, roughly 18% of the North American Class 8 truck market). Real orders are landing: Einride bought 500 semis (~$125M), WattEV bought 370 (~$100M) after a competitive bid against Volvo and Freightliner, and Canadian carrier Bison is now trial-running trucks in Chicago — 870 trucks total from real fleet customers, not old 2017-2020 reservations. The economics work for buyers: the 500-mile Semi costs ~$290K but runs about a third the energy cost of diesel, saving 50-65 cents/mile, with payback in roughly 4 years, plus big maintenance savings. The bigger long-term story is autonomy — every Semi already ships with full self-driving cameras/hardware, Elon says semi FSD should work by end of 2026/early 2027, and self-driving would let a truck run nearly 2x the daily miles since it isn't limited by driver hours-of-service rules. Real risks remain: Tesla's battery cells are the same constraint limiting Model Y/Megapack production, charging infrastructure is only 2 sites live today (60+ planned), gross margin on the truck is unknown, and Tesla's self-driving timelines have missed repeatedly before. Near-term this is a rounding error for the stock, but by 2028-2029 a ramped Nevada line could become a real ~$13B/year business line, and if autonomy lands, it turns Tesla into a company selling freight capacity rather than just trucks.

### [SpaceX Is BEATING OpenAI At This](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sTdv2tFRNHs)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Aug 27

SpaceX currently has more AI compute actually running (1,286 MW) than either OpenAI (421 MW) or Anthropic (910 MW), even though all three are announcing plans to hit 10 gigawatts by next year. SpaceX's edge comes from owning its own data-center campus (Colossus, built in just 122 days) rather than renting like the others, and it can lease that fast-turnaround capacity to others for $30-50 million per megawatt/year — Anthropic alone reportedly pays SpaceX $1.25 billion/month through May 2029. Analysts (semianalysis) think SpaceX's 10GW target by end of 2026 is plausible given its land, power (turbine deals with Caterpillar and APR Energy), and Nvidia chip commitments, while OpenAI and Anthropic's bigger numbers are mostly "aspirational" partnership announcements (Microsoft, Oracle, SoftBank, Google, Amazon) that haven't broken ground yet. The video's bigger thesis: OpenAI and Anthropic are vulnerable because they aren't yet profitable and depend on selling LLMs that risk becoming commoditized, whereas SpaceX/xAI can be its own customer (like Starlink), monetizing compute directly and building a vertical stack (Grok, Grok bot, Cursor-like coding tools, eventually robotics) that gives it staying power even if it's not the clear #1 model. Bottom line for investors: watch actual "energized" megawatts, not announced gigawatts, when judging SpaceX/Tesla-related AI infrastructure claims.

### [How the market stayed afloat despite the massive tech correction.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z6Mwr5-74Ng)
**Fundstrat** · Aug 28

The market avoided a real correction this year even though tech got hammered — software, then Mag 7 hyperscalers, then semis, then memory stocks all took turns dropping 20-40%.
