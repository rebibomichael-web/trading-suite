### [We Finally Know Who Owns SpaceX](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M1jHSmLJdu8)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Aug 18

SpaceX made two big splashes this week: filings revealed the ownership structure for the first time, with Elon Musk holding 48.7% (though much of that only vests on aggressive milestones), Google holding roughly $77 billion, Nvidia $17 billion, AMD $463 million, and Saudi Arabia's PIF disclosing a new $25 billion stake as its largest Q2 purchase. Separately, SpaceX closed its $60 billion all-stock acquisition of Cursor (Anysphere), folding the team into SpaceX AI to build out Grok, Grok Build, and Grok Bot — and Grok 4.6 is reportedly now near frontier-level intelligence (comparable to GPT-5-class models) but priced far cheaper than Anthropic's Claude Opus, which the hosts think could pressure Anthropic and OpenAI on pricing. The conversation ties this to Elon's broader compute thesis: a forecasting model projects AI compute infrastructure could be a $3.2 trillion/year market by 2040, and Musk argues orbital data centers will become necessary to keep scaling by around 2029 due to land power/permitting limits, tying into SpaceX's in-house "Terrafab" chip/memory manufacturing push with Nvidia. Bottom line takeaway from the hosts: with Anthropic potentially IPOing near $2 trillion, and Grok/Cursor now roughly comparable, they argue the rest of SpaceX (launch, Starlink, orbital compute) is effectively "coming for free" in current valuations — though both hosts caution Grok/Cursor still have a long way to go to match Anthropic's enterprise and OpenAI's consumer adoption.

### [Tesla Announces CyberCab Launch Event!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3jcMqzkaROc)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Aug 18

Tesla just announced a CyberCab public launch event is coming soon: anyone who takes a robotaxi ride before August 23 gets a shot at an invite, with winners announced by August 25, suggesting the actual launch event lands roughly a week later. Drone footage from Giga Texas shows the largest fleet of CyberCabs yet on the test track, plus new Superchargers being installed there. NHTSA data for mid-June to mid-July shows zero at-fault incidents for Tesla's robotaxis — the only two reported crashes were caused by other human drivers hitting stopped or parked robotaxis. Two new Tesla patents for wireless charging pads (covering both 400V and 800V vehicles with overheat cutoff) were also published, though they were filed back in 2024 and there's no confirmed rollout timeline. Separately, Waymo got approval to expand into Sacramento and San Diego, and Rivian says it's targeting a limited point-to-point self-driving rollout later this year with a wider 2027 release, though the hosts are skeptical Rivian has the fleet data/scale to hit that.

### [TLT Just Broke a 3-Year Trend — What Comes Next](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OB8ItiTRULM)
**Wicked Stocks** · Aug 18

TLT (the 20+ year Treasury bond ETF) just broke a roughly 3-year trendline support that had held since the October 2023 low, and Carrie Artekch sees this as a clear long-term sell signal for bonds — meaning rising interest rates ahead, since bond prices and rates move inversely. Near-term support sits at 80.42, with a drop into the upper-79 handle likely by early September; a close below 80.42 (and especially below 80.06 by late August) would trigger an accelerated decline toward the mid-70s by year-end. Using Fibonacci wave counts, she projects two downside targets: 75.05 and 74.41, with a longer-term objective in the low-70s/high-60s (around 69-70) over the next year, roughly equating to a 1% rise in long-term interest rates. She cites bearish drivers like the record ~$1.2+ trillion fiscal deficit, above-target inflation, Middle East uncertainty, and rising oil prices, but notes that if higher rates trigger a recession, rates could eventually fall again.
