### [Elon's Doubters Keep Making the Same Mistake](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CCBKV3_WYVY)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Oct 04

This video argues that big-company CEOs keep doubting Elon Musk and keep losing the bigger argument, so investors in Tesla and SpaceX should ask what each doubter has riding on their claim. This week AT&T's CEO called SpaceX's plan to turn Starlink dishes into home small cells "not a viable strategy." T-Mobile's CFO said you "can't break the laws of physics." Verizon's CEO said he sees zero impact from Starlink. A reported (unconfirmed) Delta CEO quote said "we do not want to be with Elon Musk," though Delta picked Amazon over Starlink while United already has Starlink on 560+ planes. Herbert compares this to past doubts that aged badly. Daimler used the same "laws of physics" line about the Tesla Semi, which began volume production in September 2026 with a 50,000-truck-a-year factory, and AT&T underestimated mobile phones in 1980 and later paid $12.6 billion to buy its way back in. Other examples: Starship reached orbit last Monday, SpaceX quarterly revenue was close to $8 billion (almost double a year earlier), and Tesla's CyberCap launched driverless rides in Austin. He admits Musk's timelines are often years late (the Semi by 7 years, coast-to-coast FSD by 8, the 2020 "million robotaxis" never happened, and the Roadster demo is now set for Oct 15), and that Starlink's full phone service isn't due until the end of 2027. His takeaway is that doubters are usually right about *when* but wrong about *whether*. Next to watch: the AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile earnings calls in late October, a Starship tower-catch targeted around end of October, and V2 direct-to-phone satellites launching next year.

The guest expects SpaceX to win most of that spectrum. Starship reached orbit on Sept 28, 2026, exactly 18 years after Falcon 1 did. It deployed its first revenue-generating Starlink V3 satellites, each said to add about 0.1% to constellation capacity.

SpaceX also flew three launches in one day: a Falcon Heavy on its 14th flight, a Falcon 9 carrying about 300 satellites, and Crew 13, which reached the ISS in under 8 hours. Musk says he is aiming for hourly launches in two to three years. The guest thinks Starlink's capacity could grow 30–40% next year and that prices could fall to roughly 20% of today's. The tone is very bullish and no stock calls are made. Several of the claims are the guest's own estimates.
