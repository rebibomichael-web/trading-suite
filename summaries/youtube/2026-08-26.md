### [The Real Number That Decides Robotaxi's Future](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Et-pObpDcHQ)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Aug 22

This video features Sern Basher, a CFA, breaking down the economics of Tesla robotaxi safety and liability as the fleet scales. His core point: even a self-driving system 10x safer than human drivers would still cause about 1,190 deaths per trillion miles driven, versus roughly 10,000 for human drivers over the same distance — so accidents are inevitable at scale, but the real business metric isn't crash count, it's liability cost per mile. At 100 billion miles a year, even 3 cents/mile in liability costs $3 billion annually, which is manageable if those miles earn tens of billions in revenue (implying liability could stay in the low single-digit cents range as FSD improves). The upside of scale cuts both ways: more miles means more accidents in raw numbers, but every accident also trains the whole fleet via software updates, so one bad algorithm is a fleetwide risk but also a fleetwide fix — unlike individual human drivers who don't learn collectively. Tesla currently has about 13.6 billion supervised FSD miles and roughly 650,000-700,000 fully autonomous robotaxi miles, still too small a sample to statistically prove safety levels to regulators, which is why the rollout has been deliberately slow (small geofenced areas) before scaling aggressively. Basher's bottom line for investors: watch "Tesla-at-fault collisions per million miles" and liability cost per mile as the real signals, expect negative headlines when a bad accident inevitably goes viral, but the statistical case — assuming FSD keeps improving — favors robotaxi being a net positive for both safety and Tesla's margins.
