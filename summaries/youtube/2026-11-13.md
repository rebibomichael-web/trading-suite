### [Wait… This Model Gives Tesla HOW Many Robotaxis?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6xCPFD9o8HQ)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Nov 09

Larry Goldberg ran 20,000 Monte Carlo simulations of Tesla's robotaxi business. His median case has the fleet growing from about 1,100 Tesla-owned cars today to roughly 50,000 by Q4 2027. That's about 17,500 Tesla-owned cars plus about 32,000 customer-owned cars, and the fleet roughly doubles every quarter. His 2027 revenue is only about $900 million in the middle case, with a range of $348 million (bad case) to $2.5 billion (good case). Quarterly revenue reaches about $500 million by Q4 2027 and nearly doubles each quarter after that. He says the business breaks even on network costs around mid-2026, turns profitable in Q3 2027, and exits 2027 at a run rate near $3 billion a year. The high case (90th percentile) is about 110,000 cars. The biggest driver by far is how fast cars get into the fleet, with a 0.85 correlation to revenue. He treats fares ($2 a mile) as the model's weakest assumption, since current Austin robotaxi and Uber fares run $4–6+ a mile. The host thinks 50,000 sounds low but is probably closer to right than wrong. Both argue that the growth rate and the high margins matter more than the absolute 2027 numbers.
