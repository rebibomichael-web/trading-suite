### [Modeling Tesla's Robotaxi Growth Curve](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0w07vrFPre0)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Aug 01

Tesla robotaxi analyst Sern Basher argues Wall Street is thinking linearly about a business that's actually growing exponentially, based on Ashok Elluswamy's Q2 comment that unsupervised miles are growing at a "high compounding rate" week-over-week — likely 11-17%, not just the 10% Elon mentioned. Modeling three scenarios (11%, 14.5%, 17% weekly growth), Tesla's robotaxi fleet could grow from ~65 vehicles today to 500-1,400 by end of 2026, generating an annualized revenue run rate of $65M-$180M (at a conservative $2.50/mile). Extending to end of 2027, the fleet could reach 80,000-450,000 vehicles and $15B-$80B in annualized revenue, though this would require Cybercab production capacity to scale to 500,000-2,000,000 units/year — a real potential bottleneck. Both analysts agree these are illustrative scenarios, not predictions, and caution that a standard P/E valuation misses the point entirely for an exponential business like this — a discounted cash flow approach matters more, and the key unknown is whether Tesla's upcoming FSD v15 solves enough edge cases to sustain or accelerate the current growth curve.

### [Tesla Is Where Nvidia Was Before The Boom](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9W3Bgolpcv8)
**Brighter with Herbert** · Aug 01

Tesla is compared to Nvidia's early history: both companies had founders (Elon Musk, Jensen Huang) who poured billions into unproven technology while the stock went sideways for years before eventually paying off massively. Nvidia's stock was flat from 2009-2015 while Jensen invested over $1 billion building the CUDA software platform into every GPU chip before AI even needed it—then AI arrived in 2012 and Nvidia returned roughly 26,000% over the next decade. The video argues Tesla is in a similar "flat but building" phase: the stock has traded sideways around $389 since 2020, Q2 revenue hit $28.2 billion (up 26%) but EPS missed at $0.33 vs $0.49 expected because capex surged 142% to $5.79 billion, funding Optimus and FSD. Underlying usage metrics are compounding: 1.5 million FSD subscriptions (up 56% YoY), robotaxis running in Austin, Dallas, Houston and three Florida cities, and 13.5 gigawatt-hours of energy storage deployed in one quarter. The video also highlights that Musk gets priority access to Nvidia's newest chips (like the $1.08 billion GB200 order and the 122-day Colossus supercomputer buildout), giving Tesla's AI roadmap a structural edge in the compute queue. Bottom line: the bet is that Tesla's "October 2019 moment" (a surprise profit that ended its prior 5-year flat stretch) could come from robotaxi or Optimus revenue showing up in earnings, but the video cautions this pattern isn't guaranteed and requires a multi-year time horizon.

### [The Coldcard Disaster Has Kaspa Holders Wondering If Their Wallet Is Next](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tz1mRCiUug0)
**Kaspa Silver** · Aug 01

Kaspa Silver's video breaks down the Coldcard hardware wallet disaster, where a flawed random number generator produced predictable seed phrases, leading to over $88 million in confirmed Bitcoin losses across three waves of attacks identified by Galaxy. He explains the concepts of RNG (random number generator) and entropy (a 256-bit key should require ~2^256 guesses to crack) to show why Coldcard's weak randomness left seeds far easier to guess. While this doesn't directly affect Kaspa since Coldcard only supports Bitcoin, he warns that any wallet with an RNG chip (Ledger, OneKey, Ellipal, Tangem) is theoretically at similar risk, since you can't verify a closed-source chip's randomness. His personal move: he's dropped Ledger entirely and now uses Tangem (seedless, with a passphrase) plus the fully open-source, airgapped Cassigner (a Kaspa-focused clone of Bitcoin's Seedsigner) for cold storage, and recommends anyone using Tangem go seedless and always add a passphrase for extra protection. He also plugs donating to a "Kasa fund" to help get Cassigner properly audited, and offers a Tangem affiliate discount code ("Kaspa Silver") in the description.
