**Tim Cook's Final Earnings Call as Apple CEO — Halftime Report, July 30, 2026**

**Overview:** Microsoft's blowout earnings (40%+ Azure growth, reaffirmed cash-flow-positive guidance) triggered a massive Nasdaq rebound and renewed conviction that AI capex is finally paying off, while Meta cratered nearly 9% on capex/ROI concerns despite a solid ad quarter. Attention now turns to Apple (Tim Cook's final call as CEO) and Amazon reporting after the bell, against a backdrop of bond-market turmoil after Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's confusing press conference.

- **Malcolm Etheridge:** Shocked by Microsoft's 40%+ Azure growth after a year of the stock being punished; says the market is rewarding MSFT's capex discipline specifically, "we may not be buying the capex from Alphabet and others." On Amazon (reporting tonight), feels better after MSFT's reception — expects AWS acceleration helped by open-model access (Anthropic, Codex) and Trainium chip improvements; the only numbers that matter are AWS growth vs. ~31% and the ~$200B capex guide.
- **Bryn Talkington:** Flipped bullish on Microsoft overnight — cited the 43% Azure number (guided higher to 45%), Copilot users doubling from 15M to 30M after opening the platform to Claude and other models, and Nadella's "swappable model" strategy; watching for a breakout above the 200-day MA. Also made the key call that MSFT raised the *floor* of its capex range without raising the *ceiling* — the reason the stock popped ~15% — contrasted with Meta, who offered no similar revenue-generating capex story. Noted MSFT quietly extended useful life of data centers/offices to 25 years from 15, a nod to depreciation-schedule concerns. On her own holdings: owns KKR (sees it creeping to ~$115 despite bad private-credit sentiment, trimmed Apollo), owns SoFi (calls Mizuho's $22 PT cut wrong given 40% revenue growth, though Muddy Waters short report still overhangs), owns CBRE (Evercore raised PT to $175 on five straight quarters of 18%+ earnings growth), and owns Live Nation ("bulletproof," least-worried holding reporting after the bell).
- **Shannon Saccocia:** Cited Street upgrades on MSFT (Citi, Goldman PT $640, Barclays) and noted it was the worst Mag 7 stock YTD heading into the print; said easing OpenAI-concentration fears (90% of cloud revenue from non-frontier-model customers) was "just what the doctor ordered." Sees the IGV software basket building higher lows since April, with real dispersion emerging — Snowflake/Microsoft strong, Salesforce/Oracle still in question given OpenAI ties.
- **Josh Brown — Snowflake (new buy):** Bought a new long position on a technical breakout following the strong May earnings gap-up; highlights the renamed "Coco" (formerly Cortex) agentic AI product turning Snowflake into "AI infrastructure as a service" rather than a plain software name; stock +34% YTD.
- **Malcolm Etheridge — Palantir:** Noted Rosenblatt reiterated with a $225 PT, but expects the stock to stay range-bound near-term given the valuation reset (market cap roughly cut in half).
- **Stephanie Link (phone-in) — Meta:** Sold her remaining Meta position; called the quarter itself decent (revenue +27%, ad impressions +14%, pricing +12%) but flagged operating margins falling 1,200 bps, far worse than expected. Estimates 2027 capex could exceed $250B with spending continuing into 2028 and no visibility on ROI outside advertising (no detail on developer APIs, agents, or compute monetization). Calls the stock "dead money," not expecting much downside but redeploying into AI food-chain beneficiaries instead. (Note: most analysts still rate Meta Buy/Overweight despite PT cuts — Citi to $800, Barclays to $780; JPMorgan/Wells lowest at ~$640.)
- **Bryn Talkington — Apple:** Reiterated her bullish call (target $400) ahead of Tim Cook's final earnings call as CEO; argues price hikes are a positive (elevated prices plus component-cost efficiency gains expand margins); consensus revenue $108.8B (+16% YoY), EPS $1.88 (+19.8%); sees Apple's on-device AI and App Store as the lowest-capex, highest-profitability way to monetize AI across the installed base of 2.5B devices. Notes stock is up 2,370% under Cook's tenure, with John Ternus seen as the successor.
- **Steve Liesman (economics reporter):** Recapped widespread criticism of Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference (Roger Ferguson, Greg Ip, FT, Reuters, Ed Yardeni) for being evasive about what bond-market signals — including the 30-year yield spiking above 5.20% — mean for policy; raised a credibility/independence concern that Warsh is reluctant to acknowledge market signals pointing to a rate hike. Noted conflicting Street calls (Morgan Stanley: Fed on hold; JPMorgan: pulled forward next hike to December; Deutsche Bank: sees 50bp of hikes this year); Gundlach thinks the Fed likely hikes in September.
- **Josh Brown (Best Stocks in the Market / trades):** Bought Delta — ~8% off its 52-week high, RSI 54, target $100 with a stop-loss in place, citing resilient upper-income travel demand (record 153,000 US flights on July 23) and shares holding up through the recent oil spike. Also bought Reddit ahead of tonight's earnings, citing last quarter's 69% revenue growth and a 63% EPS beat, strong ad trends, and AI data-licensing deals with OpenAI and Google (expects the Anthropic lawsuit to end in a paid licensing settlement). Pitched utility Eversource (ES) as a Best Stock in the Market — up only 1% annually versus the sector's 9% after shedding its wind-energy and water-utility businesses, now hitting new 52-week highs at 16x forward earnings with a 4.2% dividend yield, expecting a breakout.
- **Final Trades:** Malcolm Etheridge – Financials (INFL); Josh Brown – Amazon. (Shannon Saccocia's and Bryn Talkington's final trades were cut off on air by the live Jersey Mike's IPO opening-bell segment.)
