Live From Future Proof: AI's Regulatory Divide — September 15, 2026

**Overview:** Halftime broadcast live from the Future Proof conference in Huntington Beach as markets sold off with the 10-year yield topping 5% (highest since 2007) and crude above $100, ahead of Wednesday's Fed decision under new Chair Kevin Warsh. A parallel storyline dominated tech: a widening rift among AI leaders over safety, regulation, and whether to slow AI development, following Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's weekend essay calling for a slowdown.

- **Josh Brown:** Says the bond and oil stories are two sides of one global phenomenon — UK gilts, French and German yields, and Japanese yields all at multi-decade/multi-year highs, so blaming the U.S. deficit alone is wrong; rising inflation expectations tied to oil (WTI back above $100) are the real driver. Thinks the market shrugs off a 25bp hike either way and only a hawkish statement would hurt stocks (puts that odds at 1-2%). Says cheap multiples (S&P sub-20x forward) alongside accelerating earnings mean the market can absorb a hike. Highlighted his "Best Stocks in the Market" list now has 15 energy names (Marathon, Phillips 66, Schlumberger, ConocoPhillips, Chevron, LNG, FTI, Fang) as a hedge/rotation play versus AI/tech, and named CrowdStrike (up 104% YTD, a long-term holding he's up 600%+ on) as the built-in cybersecurity winner regardless of which AI/LLM wins. Bought more Target for his firm's concentrated momentum strategy (16x earnings, 33% expected growth); final trade: Target.
- **Stephanie Link:** Now expects the Fed to hike Wednesday after last week's hot core PPI (4.6) and CPI (2.4), calling it likely a "dovish hike" with no signal of an aggressive campaign; believes the economy and earnings (~20% growth) can absorb it. Disagrees with Wells Fargo's S&P target cut to 7,700 and multiple-compression call. Bought back Wells Fargo (WFC) as a top position, citing the lifted asset cap, accelerating investment-banking/M&A fees, and bottoming/improving net interest margins; stock trades 1.4x book, 11x earnings and has lagged big bank peers by 14% YTD. Still owns Bank of America despite its worst day since April on soft Q3 IB fee guidance, calling the selloff overdone (Morgan Stanley reiterated overweight). Added to Netflix (38% below all-time highs, cites Mark Mahaney's $110 target) and Delta (buying the oil-driven dip). Final trade: Wells Fargo.
- **Shannon Saccocia:** Expects a brief relief rally on a hike but sees the market falling back into a choppy pattern through mid-October dominated by elections, oil, and rate anxiety, with AI-related noise "creating a ceiling" on stocks; worried Warsh won't clearly telegraph a dovish tone. Sees value rotating into non-tech sectors (financials, industrials, energy) given broad earnings strength beyond mega-cap tech. Final trade: managed care/health care, citing improving margins and a possible bump if Democrats take the House.
- **AI regulatory divide segment (Kate Rooney reporting):** Anthropic's Dario Amodei sparked the debate with a weekend essay urging an AI slowdown; Sam Altman voiced support, and OpenAI said it's coordinating with Anthropic and Google on model oversight/safety testing. Elon Musk agreed with Amodei and, at the All-In Summit, called for AI labs to "peer review"/cross-test each other's models rather than "grade their own homework." Nvidia's Jensen Huang countered that slowing down could cost the U.S. its AI lead over China and called extinction-risk rhetoric "irresponsible." President Trump (dialed into the All-In Summit) called AI safety fears a "hoax." Pentagon tech chief Amil Michael argued existing laws already provide adequate policing tools.
- **Panel reaction to AI divide:** Joe Terranova argued the market has already "spoken" — cybersecurity names (CrowdStrike, Fortinet, Palo Alto Networks) have all roughly doubled or more this year because AI spend can't happen without cybersecurity spend, regardless of which model/hyperscaler wins. Stephanie Link noted hyperscalers (Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft) traded green despite the weekend AI-safety negativity, pointing to real cloud-revenue acceleration (Azure, Google Cloud, AWS) as proof of ROI; flagged Meta as the exception she's more cautious on. Cited BlackRock-style CapEx figures: ~$7.1 trillion on data centers and $1.7 trillion on power buildout through 2030.
- **Rick Reader (BlackRock CIO of Global Fixed Income) interview:** Now expects the Fed to hike tomorrow given how far markets have priced it in (~90%+) and the credibility position the committee has put itself in, even though he personally wouldn't hike since a 25bp move won't meaningfully address rate-insensitive inflation drivers (war, energy, insurance, healthcare) and raising rates costs the U.S. government roughly $100B per 100bps. Expects the yield curve to flatten post-hike and thinks the long end holds in; BlackRock has started "dabbling" in long-end Treasuries after being significantly underweight, while favoring the front end (his "BINC"-style ETF running ~7.2% yield, A- average rating, under 3-year duration). Downgraded his overall stock/bond environment assessment from "A-plus" a year ago to "B-minus" now, citing peaking earnings growth comps, higher-for-longer real rates making fixed income a real alternative to equities, and rollover financing risk emerging in real estate and private credit. Said Treasury's recent buyback intervention isn't about manipulating the long end but signaling it has tools available.
- **Josh Brown — Anthropic/Future Proof segment:** Ritholtz Wealth Management named a design partner for Anthropic's newly announced Claude-based AI tools for financial advisors (unveiled at Future Proof, with ~12 partner logos including Schwab and BlackRock). Called it a potential "Holy Grail" intelligence layer unifying the fragmented wealth-management software stack (portfolio accounting, rebalancing, CRM, reporting) so advisors spend less time on manual data-wrangling and more on client-facing planning conversations; stressed clients care about advisor attention and empathy, not which software executes trades.
- **Final Trades:** Josh Brown — Target (TGT); Stephanie Link — Wells Fargo (WFC); Shannon Saccocia — managed care/health care.
