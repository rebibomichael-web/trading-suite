Playing the Rise in Oil and Yields: Your Next Move — 9/23/26

**Overview:** Stocks slipped as the 10-year yield hit 5.08% and oil climbed on fears the Fed may need more hikes, even as Deutsche Bank stuck with its bullish 8,000 S&P target on strong Q3 earnings growth. The panel debated whether the market's return to mega-cap/growth leadership (led by Meta's Muse AI launch) is healthy concentration or a warning sign, while also weighing energy, streaming, and consumer-discretionary names caught in the crosscurrents.

- **Joe Terranova:** Turned near-term cautious after two bullish days; sees 7640 on the S&P as the level that would neutralize his bullishness. Says the 10-year sustaining above 5% is unprecedented in decades, and the buyer base for Treasuries has shifted from price-insensitive central banks/pensions to price-sensitive institutions/individuals amid heavy hyperscaler-driven supply. Favors momentum/MAG7 names over equal-weight/value, is aggressively buying municipal bonds (NYC muni tax-equivalent yield "over 10%"), and dismisses the Krinsky/BTIG "1999-style" divergence stat as just a symptom of a top-heavy market. On Meta: owns it, calls his position oversized after the run but is letting it ride, citing Zuckerberg's "founder mentality" and Jensen Huang's comment that "nobody does AI better than Meta." Final trade: Shenier (LNG).
- **Jason Snipe:** Notes breadth deteriorating — SPY dramatically outperforming RSP, MAG7 up ~8% this month — with real estate/staples/industrials oversold and tech/comm services overbought; sees the pullback as a buying opportunity into earnings despite expecting "murky" near-term catalysts. Final trade: Microsoft.
- **Steve Weiss:** Sold Valero entirely after a 35% one-month gain, wary of staying too long in a commodity/energy name given his skepticism on long-term energy stocks. Bought more Netflix on the dip (calls the chart "terrible" but likes the contrarian setup after the Wells Fargo sell downgrade), citing 330M subscribers and accelerating ad growth. Also added to Dick's Sporting Goods (bought more after his earlier "disastrous" pre-earnings buy, likes its Meta/Muse partnerships), likes FTAI Aviation (engine leasing/MRO disruptor, dismisses bearish oil/flight-hours short thesis as "crap"), and is bullish on Moderna's cancer-drug pipeline and flu-COVID combo vaccine. Final trade: FTAI.
- **Rob Sechan:** Sees the market pausing but longer trend higher; favors fixed income (munis) barbelled with high-growth/AI capex names that "don't care" about higher rates. Flags Devon Energy after Faber's scoop that activist Tom's Capital is pushing for a sale/alternatives — Sechan owns it, calls it cheap (~9x P/E), best-in-class Permian operator, sees Eagle Ford divestiture ($2–3B) as a value catalyst. Also weighed in on Royal Caribbean's ~$3B Sandals-related stake acquisition and its poor stock performance (down 17% YTD, 31% over 52 weeks) tied to lower-end consumer weakness.
- **Meta/Muse segment:** KeyBanc raised its Meta price target to $900 from $780 ("Muse's moment"), reiterating overweight after Muse hit #1 on the app store with 2.5M subscribers in its first week (vs. ChatGPT's slower 2022 launch). Panel debated "consumer inertia stock" disruption risk (Goldman basket: LPL Financial, Allstate, Schwab, Expedia, Charter, H&R Block, Huntington Bancshares, TripAdvisor, Netflix) — Sechan flagged missing the positive read-through to Twilio (up sharply on AI-driven communications volume) while insurers/financials (Allstate, Schwab, Progressive) sold off.
- **Streaming debate:** Panel split on Netflix (Weiss buying, Terranova sold in June and it's down 17% since); Terranova sees streamers facing margin pressure from costly live sports/entertainment content and predicts consolidation around Netflix, Disney, and YouTube as consumers narrow subscriptions; Disney's 13% price hike to Disney+ cited as evidence of pricing power tension.
- **Mike Santoli's Market Memo:** Flagged the 10-year's rise to 5.08%+ (up ~40bps since Jackson Hole) as the market's key focus, questioned whether earnings strength can offset bond-market worries, and noted the equal-weighted S&P has barely moved since mid-June even as the market absorbs the moves reasonably well (VIX under 15).
- **Options Action (Oliver Renick):** TLT options skew toward call-selling/bearish-neutral bets; GLD seeing heavy put buying (380 strike, Oct 16 expiry); Bitcoin options evenly split; USO call buying (185 strike) is double puts, including a speculative bet on a 24% oil rally.
- **ETF Edge (Dom Chu w/ Tyler Rosenlich, Cohen & Steers):** Argued a "regime change" favors natural resources (energy, copper, metals) into the 2030s on strong demand, constrained supply, digitalization, decarbonization, and deglobalization/nearshoring trends.
- **Final Trades:** Joe Terranova — LNG (Shenier); Jason Snipe — Microsoft; Rob Sechan — FTAI; Steve Weiss — FTAI (as stated on air).
